### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Agricultural Geography of India

##### Land-use / intensity
- **Net Sown Area (NSA):** area sown with crops/orchards; land sown more than once counted once.
- **Gross Cropped Area (GCA):** total cropped area counting repeated sowings each time.
- **Cropping intensity = (GCA / NSA) × 100.** >100 means multiple cropping on part of NSA.
- **Current fallow:** left uncultivated for one agricultural year or less.
- **Fallow other than current fallow:** uncultivated for >1 and up to 5 agricultural years.
- **Culturable wasteland:** cultivable land left uncultivated for 5 years or more; not the same as barren/non-culturable land.
- Total cultivable land conceptually = **NSA + fallows + culturable wasteland**.

##### Moisture-based farming geography
- **Irrigated farming:** irrigation supplements/replaces rainfall uncertainty.
  - **Protective irrigation:** limited water spread over larger area to protect crops against moisture stress.
  - **Productive irrigation:** higher water input per unit area to maximise productivity.
- **Rainfed farming:** primary dependence on rainfall.
  - **Dryland:** broadly <75 cm annual rainfall; hardy/drought-resistant crops → ragi, bajra, moong, gram, guar; moisture conservation/rainwater harvesting central.
  - **Wetland:** rainfall exceeds crop moisture need during rainy season; flood/erosion risk; rice, jute, sugarcane and aquaculture typical.

##### Indian regional logic
- Northern/interior India shows clearer **Kharif–Rabi–Zaid** seasonality; southern India can grow the same tropical crop multiple times where moisture is available.
- **Upper Gangetic / western-central UP:** high irrigation and cropping intensity; Green-Revolution imprint is a recurring UPPCS association.
- India–China elimination fact from UPSC 2023: **China > India in arable area; India > China in irrigated proportion; Indian per-hectare productivity is not higher than China.**
- Do not fossilise current land-area rankings: treat comparative acreage data as dated unless the question gives a reference year.

##### Legume / origin / use traps
- **Legumes:** groundnut, horse gram, soybean, gram, pea, green gram, black gram, arhar etc. → pea/legume family; root nodules commonly host **Rhizobium**.
- **Do not memorise “all pulses except arhar fix N”** — pigeon pea/arhar is itself a legume capable of symbiotic N fixation.
- `UPSC-CSE-2012-GS1-Q044`: cowpea and green gram satisfy pulse + fodder + green-manure use in the tested combination; do not universalise every pulse as a green-manure crop.
- **New World origin trap:** tobacco, cocoa and rubber are American-origin crops.
- **Crop by-products:** coconut shells, groundnut shells and rice husk can serve biomass-gasification feedstocks.
- **Guar/cluster bean:** vegetable/fodder traditionally; guar gum is used as a thickening agent in hydraulic fracturing fluids for shale-gas extraction.
- **Maize:** starch + oil/biodiesel feedstock + alcoholic beverage feedstock.

##### Statistics / trade — permanent handling rule
- Memorise **definitions and comparison logic**, not floating ranks.
- Dated PYQ facts:
  - UPSC 2019: rice had the highest Kharif crop area in the referenced five-year period; cotton area > sugarcane area.
  - UPSC 2019: India was the largest rice exporter in the referenced five-year period; vegetable oils led agricultural imports by value.
  - UPSC 2025 on **2022–23 turmeric:** all three statements were accepted — India largest producer/exporter; >30 varieties; Maharashtra, Telangana, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu major States.
- These are **replaceable examples**, not permanent node identity.

#### C. Farming Practices, Technology & Allied Sectors

##### Farming-system anchors
- **Mixed farming:** crop cultivation + livestock rearing on the same farm/system.
- **Intensive subsistence:** small fragmented holdings, high labour input, high output/ha but low output/person; multiple cropping common.
- **Extensive commercial grain:** large mechanised farms, low population density, lower output/ha but high output/person; export orientation.
- **Plantation agriculture:** large estate, capital/management intensive, processing linkage, often export-oriented; tea/coffee/rubber classic.
- **Shifting cultivation:** vegetation cut/burned, short cultivation followed by fallow/shift; **jhum** NE India; regional names are UPPCS-style recall but low UPSC priority.
- **Nomadic herding vs commercial ranching:** mobility/subsistence dependence vs fixed, scientific, market-oriented livestock production.

##### Conservation agriculture / zero tillage
- Conservation agriculture core = **minimum soil disturbance + permanent/continuous soil cover + crop diversification/rotation**.
- UPSC 2018 trap: **avoiding plantation crops is not a defining principle**; “avoiding monoculture” is too loose unless expressed through diversification/rotation.
- **Zero tillage:** seed placed without conventional prior ploughing.
  - allows wheat sowing into previous-crop residue without residue burning;
  - enables direct-seeded rice without nursery/transplanting in the tested context;
  - can increase/retain soil organic carbon → carbon sequestration;
  - helps conserve soil moisture.
- UPSC 2017 canonical key: **zero/reduced tillage + retaining crop residue** were accepted water-conservation practices; generic pre-irrigation gypsum application was not.

##### Micro-irrigation / fertigation
- **Drip:** frequent low-volume delivery near root zone → reduced evaporation, weed growth and nutrient loss; enables precise water/nutrient scheduling.
- Micro-irrigation is **not the only** irrigation option for dryland farming; in some settings it can reduce pressure on groundwater.
- UPSC 2016 accepted drip advantages: reduction in **weeds + soil salinity** in the framing; did **not** accept soil-erosion reduction in that item.
- **Fertigation:** soluble fertiliser delivered through irrigation (especially drip).
  - can adjust/control irrigation-water alkalinity;
  - improves nutrient availability/timing;
  - reduces nutrient leaching;
  - **trap:** rock phosphate / every phosphatic fertiliser is not efficiently suitable for fertigation because low-solubility materials clog/are poorly deliverable.

##### SRI — System of Rice Intensification
- Core: **alternate wetting and drying**, lower seed/plant density, improved root-zone aeration/soil management; avoids continuous flooding.
- UPSC 2022 accepted outcomes: **reduced seed requirement + reduced methane production + reduced electricity consumption**.
- Mechanism: less continuous anaerobic flooding → less methanogenesis; lower pumping requirement where irrigation is used.

##### Sugarcane technology
- **Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative (SSI):** lower seed material, nursery-raised bud-chip/young settlings, wider spacing, efficient irrigation, scope for intercropping.
- UPSC 2014: low seed cost + drip suitability + greater intercropping accepted; **zero inorganic fertiliser** is not an inherent SSI condition.
- UPSC 2020:
  - bud-chip settlings → substantial saving in seed material;
  - tissue-culture settlings can be used;
  - single-budded direct setts do **not** automatically outperform multi-budded setts in germination/survival under bad weather.

##### Inputs / soil–nutrient process
- Excess/inappropriate **N fertiliser** → soil acidification + nitrate leaching to groundwater; does not imply proliferation of N-fixing microbes.
- **Neem-coated urea:** slows urea transformation/nitrification and N loss → better nitrogen-use efficiency; not “nitrogen-free” or a substitute for nutrient balance.
- **Biochar:** stable carbon-rich material from biomass pyrolysis; can improve water retention, provide habitat/support for beneficial microbes incl. N-fixers, and function in growing media including vertical systems.
- **Tensiometer:** measures soil-water tension → irrigation scheduling; eco-friendly through better water-use efficiency.
- **Legume intensification/crop diversification:** improves biological N contribution, breaks pest/disease cycles and reduces monoculture risk.

##### Organic / permaculture
- **Organic farming:** relies on biological/organic inputs and ecological nutrient/pest management; synthetic input exclusion is a production-system rule, not proof that all organic systems are automatically high-yield or impact-free.
- **NPOP:** national organic production/export certification architecture; **APEDA acts as Secretariat** for NPOP in the UPSC-tested framing.
- **Sikkim:** safest durable wording = **India's first fully organic State**; avoid converting a dated exam superlative into a universal “world first” claim.
- **Permaculture vs conventional chemical farming (UPSC 2021):** discourages monoculture; mulching important; chemical farming can contribute to salinity; permaculture is not inherently impossible in semi-arid regions.

##### Agriculture and emissions
- Flooded **paddy/rice** → important anthropogenic methane source; rice also tested as major source of both CH₄ and N₂O among crop options.
- Crop/biomass-residue burning can release **CO, CH₄, ozone-forming pollutants/ozone in UPSC framing, and SO₂**.
- Agricultural soils emit nitrogen oxides; cattle emit ammonia; poultry systems release reactive nitrogen compounds.

##### Allied sectors
- **Kharai camel (Gujarat):** adapted to coastal/mangrove environment; can swim in seawater and graze mangroves; **domesticable** — “lives wild and cannot be domesticated” was the trap.
- **Rashtriya Gokul Mission:** indigenous cattle + buffalo development/conservation, genetic improvement/productivity, breeding technologies/AI coverage; strong small/marginal farmer + landless-labour relevance.
- **Recirculating Aquaculture System (RAS):** recycles treated water; biofiltration converts toxic ammonia → nitrite/nitrate through nitrification; solids/uneaten feed require waste-removal stages; phosphorus enrichment is not the purpose of biofilters.
- **FAO Blue Transformation:** durable “Four Betters” formulation tested in 2026 = **better production, better nutrition, better environment, better life**.

#### D. Agricultural Biotechnology & GM Crops

##### Core distinctions
- **GM/transgenic crop:** genetic material altered/introduced using biotechnology; ordinary budding/grafting is **not** transgenesis.
- **Genome sequencing:** identifies markers for disease/drought tolerance, speeds marker-assisted breeding/selection, helps analyse host–pathogen interactions.
- **RNA interference (RNAi):** sequence-specific gene silencing; can engineer virus-resistant crops.
- **CRISPR–Cas9:** Cas9 = programmable nuclease / “molecular scissors” for targeted gene editing; gene editing is not synonymous with inserting a foreign Bt gene.

##### Bt crops
- **Bt = Bacillus thuringiensis**, a bacterium producing insecticidal Cry proteins; **not a fungus**.
- **Bt brinjal:** objective = insect resistance (especially fruit-and-shoot borer in the technology context).
  - UPSC 2012 traps: source is not “soil fungus”; Bt brinjal is not inherently a terminator-seed technology.
  - health/allergenicity and biodiversity/non-target/gene-flow concerns are legitimate **biosafety questions**, not proof of harm.
- **Bt cotton / Bollgard:**
  - Bollgard I / MON 531 → **cry1Ac**.
  - Bollgard II / MON 15985 → stacked **cry1Ac + cry2Ab**.
  - trait logic = protection against target lepidopteran bollworms; resistance management/refugia remain conceptually relevant.

##### Golden Rice
- GM/biofortified rice engineered to accumulate **beta-carotene (provitamin A)** in the grain/endosperm → intended to help address Vitamin-A deficiency.
- Trap: it is a nutritional trait, not herbicide tolerance or insect resistance.

##### GM mustard / barnase–barstar system
- **DMH-11 (Brassica juncea)** uses **barnase + barstar + bar** transgenes.
- **Barnase:** bacterial RNase expressed in anther tapetum → pollen degeneration → **male sterility** in female parent.
- **Barstar:** inhibitor of barnase → **restores fertility** in the F1 hybrid.
- **bar:** selectable-marker/herbicide-resistance gene used to select transformed lines; do not confuse its role with barnase/barstar hybridisation control.
- Purpose of the barnase–barstar system = enable controlled cross-pollination/hybrid seed production in a predominantly self-pollinating crop.

##### Biosafety / regulation — durable architecture
- India's GE biosafety regime is anchored in the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** and **Rules, 1989** for manufacture/use/import/export/storage of genetically engineered organisms/cells.
- **GEAC** functions under **MoEFCC**; appraises large-scale use/release of GE organisms/products and experimental field trials from the environmental angle.
- **NBA ≠ GEAC:** NBA's biodiversity role includes access/benefit-sharing and protection against biopiracy of indigenous/traditional genetic resources; it does not directly supervise all GM crop research.
- GM-food imports require approval of the competent authority; do not infer that every imported food commodity is GM.
- Biosafety dimensions worth testing: food/feed safety, allergenicity/toxicity, gene flow/crossability, weediness, non-target/beneficial organisms, biodiversity, resistance evolution.
- **Current approval status is a replaceable layer** and must not be hard-coded into permanent notes.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Agricultural Economics: MSP, Procurement, Marketing & Credit

##### 1. MSP–CACP–Government chain

- **CACP** = Commission for Agricultural Costs & Prices → recommends MSP/FRP after examining production cost, demand–supply, domestic/international prices, inter-crop parity, terms of trade, economy-wide effects/resource use, etc.
- **Final fixation/approval is by Government**; for sugarcane FRP, UPSC’s tested authority is **CCEA**.
- MSP is fixed **for the country as a whole**, not separately by each State.
- Official 2026 architecture: MSP for **22 mandated crops**:
  - **14 Kharif:** paddy, jowar, bajra, maize, ragi, tur/arhar, moong, urad, groundnut, soyabean, sunflower seed, sesamum, nigerseed, cotton.
  - **6 Rabi:** wheat, barley, gram, masur/lentil, rapeseed & mustard, safflower.
  - **2 commercial:** jute, copra.
- **Sugarcane → FRP, not MSP.** FRP replaced the earlier Statutory Minimum Price framework from 2009–10; it is the minimum cane price payable by sugar mills under the central framework and is linked to sugar recovery. Some States separately announce **SAP**.
- **Trap:** MSP ≠ market-price ceiling. Market price may rise above MSP.
- **Trap:** announcement of MSP ≠ universal legal/physical procurement of every quantity offered by every farmer in every State.

##### 2. Procurement architecture

- Core purpose → price support to farmers + public stocks for NFSA/welfare/buffer/food-management functions.
- **Wheat/paddy:** procured for Central Pool through FCI + State agencies, subject to marketing season and prescribed **FAQ (Fair Average Quality)** norms.
- **Coarse grains:** procurement depends on approved Central/State procurement arrangements.
- **Pulses/oilseeds/copra:** procurement operates through separate price-support architecture and is **scheme/trigger/limit dependent**; details/caps are dynamic.
- **Cotton / jute:** specialised procurement agencies/frameworks exist.
- Permanent elimination rule: **crop-wise procurement architecture differs** → “all MSP crops are procured without limit everywhere” is false.

##### 3. FCI economic cost / price transmission

- **FCI economic cost = acquisition/pool cost (MSP + bonus where applicable) + procurement incidentals + distribution cost.**
- Procurement incidentals ≠ distribution cost; both are added beyond acquisition price.
- Rice/foodgrain market price can be influenced by:
  - MSP/acquisition policy;
  - government procurement/trading;
  - public stock accumulation/release;
  - consumer/PDS subsidy.

##### 4. Agricultural marketing: APMC and e-NAM

- **APMC regulation:** agricultural produce markets historically regulated under **State APMC laws**; agriculture marketing is substantially State-law institutional terrain.
- **e-NAM:** pan-India electronic trading portal that **networks existing APMC mandis** into a unified electronic market layer.
- Core functions/advantages:
  - wider buyer–seller access / inter-market trade;
  - transparent electronic auction;
  - real-time price discovery based on demand–supply;
  - quality assaying/quality-linked pricing;
  - online settlement/payment architecture.
- **Trap:** e-NAM does not by itself mean “APMC abolished”; it is an electronic integration/trading layer over participating market institutions.

##### 5. Agricultural credit architecture

**Institutional roles**

| Institution | Exam-useful role |
|---|---|
| Commercial Banks | Major direct institutional lenders; in UPSC 2011 context, highest share in agri/allied credit disbursement |
| RRBs | Direct rural credit; agriculture/rural-sector mandate |
| NABARD | Apex development/refinance/supervisory institution; **not ordinary direct retail credit to rural households** |
| PACS | Village/grass-root short-term cooperative credit interface |
| DCCB | District-level cooperative bank; major function = finance/support PACS |
| State Cooperative Bank | Apex of short-term cooperative credit structure at State level |
| Land Development Banks | Longer-term rural/agricultural credit; treated as direct-credit institution in UPSC 2013 PYQ |

- Durable chronology for UPPCS: **RRBs 1975 → NABARD 1982 → SHG–Bank Linkage 1992 → KCC 1998**.

##### 6. Kisan Credit Card (KCC)

- Introduced **1998–99**; model scheme formulated by NABARD in consultation with banks/RBI.
- Objective → adequate/timely credit through a simplified **single-window** mechanism for cultivation + associated farm needs.
- **Short-term credit component** includes:
  - crop cultivation requirements;
  - post-harvest expenses;
  - produce-marketing loan;
  - household consumption requirements;
  - working capital for maintenance of farm assets/allied activities.
- **Investment credit** for agriculture/allied activities is a **long-term component**.
- Hence purchase of tractor/combine etc. should not be casually classified as the short-term crop-credit component.
- Eligible architecture extends beyond owner-cultivators to tenant farmers/oral lessees/sharecroppers and farmer SHG/JLG structures under RBI scheme design.

##### 7. Public investment vs subsidy/support

- **Public investment** → creates productive/institutional/social capital or durable public assets/capability.
  - PYQ anchors: PACS computerisation; social-capital development; government cold-storage facilities.
- **Price/transfer/subsidy measures ≠ public investment** merely because government spends money.
  - MSP fixation; free electricity; agricultural-loan waiver → not classified as public investment in UPSC 2020.
- **Direct farm support** → transfer/income support received by farmer/household.
- **Indirect/input subsidy** → lowers input/service cost or operates through pricing system, e.g. fertiliser/power/irrigation concessions.
- WTO Agreement on Agriculture does **not** reduce to “direct subsidies allowed, indirect subsidies prohibited”; classification depends on trade-distorting character/boxes and commitments.

##### 8. Fertiliser pricing / subsidy

- India does **not** have a single fully market-driven retail-pricing rule for all chemical fertilisers.
- **Urea:** government-notified/administered MRP/subsidy architecture.
- **Phosphatic & Potassic (P&K) fertilisers:** **Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS)** since 2010; per-nutrient subsidy is fixed, while companies set MRP at a reasonable market-linked level under government monitoring.
- Therefore: **“retail price of chemical fertilisers is wholly market-driven/not administered by government” = false.**
- Production-chain anchors from UPSC 2020:
  - natural gas → important feedstock for **ammonia**, which feeds urea production;
  - sulphur used in phosphatic-fertiliser chain can be recovered as a **refinery by-product**.
- Soil Health Card linkage → soil-test-based nutrient status/recommendations; elimination value = **fertiliser rationalisation**, not irrigation expansion/loan assessment.

##### 9. Seeds: replacement + Seed Village

- **Seed Replacement Rate (SRR):** extent to which farm-saved seed is replaced by certified/quality seed in a crop/area cycle; higher SRR is relevant to varietal/quality-seed diffusion.
- UPSC constraint logic:
  - “no National Seeds Policy” → false;
  - “no private-sector participation in vegetable/horticultural seed” → false;
  - **demand–supply gap in quality seed for low-value/high-volume crops** → valid constraint.
- **Seed Village Concept:** organise/train farmers in quality-seed production so quality seed is available locally/nearby at appropriate time and affordable cost.
- Not equivalent to forcing all farmers to use own seed or reserving a village exclusively for certified-seed production.

##### 10. Durable scheme-demand anchors

- **PMFBY:** do not memorise as “uniform 2% premium for any crop/any season”; premium architecture varies by crop/season/category. Specified localised/post-harvest risks can be covered under scheme conditions.
- **Intensive Millets Promotion:** cluster-based demonstration of improved production, post-harvest technologies and value addition; significant focus on poorer/small/marginal/tribal farmers.
- Keep current premium rates, notified crops, caps, operational changes out of permanent payload unless separately updated.

##### 11. Land reforms

- Core post-Independence objectives → reduce exploitative/intermediary structures, improve cultivator security, widen access to land, reduce concentration/fragmentation and improve productive use.
- Four durable pillars:
  1. **Abolition of intermediaries**.
  2. **Tenancy reforms** → regulation of rent + security of tenure + conferment/recognition of ownership rights where provided.
  3. **Ceilings on landholdings + redistribution of surplus land**.
  4. **Consolidation of fragmented holdings**.
- UPSC anchor → major redistributive aim included **providing agricultural land to the landless**.
- Traps:
  - ceiling legislation was not uniformly “family-holding only” in the simplistic absolute sense tested by UPSC;
  - ceiling laws did have exemptions → “no exemptions” is false.

##### 12. Farm organisation

- **Small Farmer Large Field:** neighbouring/marginal farmers organise as a group and synchronise/harmonise selected agricultural operations on a larger operational scale.
- Distinguish from:
  - contract farming with corporate purchaser;
  - corporate lease/takeover of farmers’ land;
  - resettlement/cooperative ownership of a single common land parcel.

##### 13. Food security / NFSA — UPPCS-heavy but topic-owned extension

- **Food security is about access, not only aggregate production.** Surplus production can coexist with hunger where entitlements/access/distribution fail.
- Standard analytical pillars → **availability, access, utilisation, stability**. “Affordability” is commonly treated as economic access; **accountability is governance, not a core food-security dimension**.
- **Amartya Sen → entitlement approach**: starvation/famine can arise from failure of command over food even without aggregate food shortage.
- NFSA durable anchors:
  - up to **75% rural + 50% urban** population coverage under the statutory TPDS framework;
  - not restricted only to households labelled BPL;
  - **eldest woman ≥18** = head of household for ration-card purpose;
  - specific nutrition entitlements/focus for women and children;
  - Act = **2013**, so a 2010 enactment/enforcement claim is false.
- Avoid permanently storing current issue-price/free-grain implementation details; those are policy-layer updates.

#### B. Soil Conservation & Soil Health

##### 1. Soil-health anchors

- Soil = mineral/inorganic material + **organic matter** + water/air/biota; agricultural productivity depends on physical, chemical and biological condition.
- **Organic matter/humus** generally:
  - improves aggregate stability/structure;
  - increases water-holding capacity and infiltration buffering;
  - contributes nutrient supply/CEC and supports soil biota.
- Thus **“high organic matter drastically reduces water-holding capacity” = false**.
- Soil participates in nutrient/biogeochemical cycles, including **sulphur cycle** → “soil plays no role” = false.
- Earthworms/burrowing soil fauna generally improve aeration/aggregation and decomposition/nutrient cycling; useful UPPCS soil-health anchor.

##### 2. Erosion: mechanism + causes

- **Soil erosion** = detachment + transport/removal of soil, especially fertile topsoil, by water/wind and accelerated land-use pressures.
- Water-erosion progression concept:
  - **splash** → raindrop detachment;
  - **sheet** → relatively uniform thin-layer removal;
  - **rill** → small channels;
  - **gully** → deep channels, land fragmentation; ravines/badlands may develop.
- Major accelerators:
  - deforestation/removal of protective vegetation;
  - overgrazing;
  - cultivation leaving soil bare/down-slope;
  - concentrated runoff/poor drainage design;
  - strong wind over dry bare soil.
- UPSC trap → **terrace cultivation is a control method, not a cause**; “tropical climate” by itself is too broad to be the cause in the tested statement.

##### 3. Conservation-method logic

| Method | Mechanism / best-fit terrain-agent | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Contour ploughing | Plough parallel to contours → furrows/ridges obstruct downslope runoff | Not up-and-down slope ploughing |
| Contour barriers/bunding | Bunds/vegetative/stone barriers follow contour → intercept/slow runoff, increase infiltration | Not primarily a desert wind-barrier method |
| Terracing | Broad bench-like steps on steeper slopes → shorten slope + reduce runoff velocity | Terrace ≠ cause of erosion |
| Mulching/crop residue | Covers soil → less raindrop impact/evaporation, more moisture retention | Not merely nutrient addition |
| Shelterbelts/windbreaks | Rows of vegetation reduce wind speed | Best for wind erosion, not gully plugging |
| Strip/intercropping | Alternating crop/cover strips interrupt runoff/wind and keep cover | Mechanism depends on orientation/cover |
| Rock/check dams / gully plugging | Slow concentrated flow, trap sediment, stabilise gullies | Used after runoff concentrates |
| Afforestation/vegetative cover | Roots bind soil + canopy/litter reduce erosive energy | Deforestation reverses this protection |
| Controlled grazing | Retains protective cover and soil structure | Overgrazing accelerates erosion |

- **Contour bunding vs terracing:** bunding creates barriers along contour; terracing reshapes slope into bench/step surfaces. Do not use the terms interchangeably.

##### 4. Salinisation / sodicity

- **Salinisation** = accumulation of soluble salts in the root zone/topsoil.
- Common agricultural pathway:
  - irrigation introduces dissolved salts;
  - evaporation/transpiration removes water but leaves salts;
  - inadequate drainage/high water table + capillary rise intensify surface/root-zone accumulation;
  - arid/semi-arid climate magnifies the process.
- Consequences → osmotic stress, reduced germination/yield, toxicity/nutrient imbalance; severe salt/sodium problems can reduce infiltration/permeability.
- Precision trap:
  - **salinity** = excess soluble salts;
  - **sodicity/alkalinity** = high exchangeable Na, clay dispersion, poor structure/permeability.
- UPSC 2011 uses “salinisation” broadly; for soil-physics precision, marked structural impermeability is especially associated with sodium-dominated/sodic conditions.
- Corrective logic → drainage + leaching where feasible; sodic soils may require calcium amendments such as gypsum. Avoid treating gypsum as a universal cure for every saline soil.

##### 5. Watershed concept + management

- **Watershed/catchment:** land area from which precipitation/runoff drains to a **common outlet** (stream/river/lake/other point).
- Integrated watershed management = coordinated conservation/regeneration and judicious use of **land + surface water + groundwater + vegetation/biological resources**, with local/community participation.
- Core interventions:
  - contour/vegetative barriers;
  - check dams/gully plugging;
  - percolation tanks/recharge structures;
  - rainwater harvesting;
  - afforestation/pasture development;
  - soil-moisture conservation.
- Core outcomes:
  - reduce runoff and soil loss;
  - improve infiltration/recharge;
  - conserve moisture;
  - regenerate vegetation/productive resource base.
- **Not watershed management:** interlinking perennial and seasonal rivers.
- UPPCS historical anchor → **Hariyali** was a Central Government-sponsored watershed-development project oriented to rural water conservation and participatory local implementation.
