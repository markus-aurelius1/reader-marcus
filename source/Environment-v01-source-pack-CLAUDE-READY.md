## 0.5 High-Priority Correction Families

The complete pack-level `CORRECT` registers below are authoritative. In particular, final synthesis must preserve these correction families:

### Ecology / Species
- sulphur cycle = mostly sedimentary **with atmospheric phase**;
- ~10% energy-transfer rule = heuristic, not universal biomass law;
- no universal fixed `γ = α + β`;
- Irrawaddy dolphin not simply “freshwater”;
- snow leopard not “snow-only”;
- not all fish are nekton;
- ethanol generation depends on feedstock/process;
- old IUCN status examples are not timeless facts.

### Conservation / Governance
- Stockholm Conference = **5–16 June 1972**;
- Paris Agreement = legally binding treaty; force **4 Nov 2016**; goal = well below 2°C + pursue 1.5°C;
- GCF establishment = **2010 / COP16 Cancún**;
- Wild Life (Protection) Amendment = **2022**, effective 1 Apr 2023;
- FRA 75-year/3-generation criterion applies to OTFD definition, not all claimants;
- NGT jurisdiction is tied to Schedule-I enactments;
- Wetlands Rules 2017 → **National Wetlands Committee** + State/UT Wetlands Authorities;
- NGRBA dissolved in 2016; National Ganga Council/NMCG framework followed;
- no single universal fixed ESZ width;
- `UPPCS-2025-GS1-Q070` factual payload follows the pack's official-source correction.

### Pollution / Climate
- PM size bands corrected;
- NOₓ chiefly NO + NO₂; N₂O treated separately;
- black carbon = light-absorbing aerosol/SLCP, not GHG;
- CO toxicity = haemoglobin/oxygen-carrying mechanism;
- COD generally, not universally, higher than BOD;
- microplastics durable shorthand = <5 mm, regulatory cut-offs may vary;
- acid-rain principal anthropogenic precursors = SO₂ + NOₓ;
- GWP/lifetime values are gas/time-horizon specific;
- HFCs are not ODS;
- SRM ≠ carbon removal;
- heavy metals are not biodegradable.

Use the detailed tables in the Topic Packs for exact wording and exceptions.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Species: Traits, Habitats & Distribution

##### 1. High-yield species vocabulary

| Term | Durable meaning | Elimination trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Native/indigenous** | Occurs naturally in a region | Native ≠ endemic |
| **Endemic** | Naturally restricted to a defined geographic area | All endemic species are native there; all native species are **not** endemic |
| **Alien/exotic** | Introduced outside natural range by human action, deliberate or accidental | Alien ≠ automatically invasive |
| **Invasive alien species** | Alien species that establishes/spreads and causes ecological/economic/health harm | “Foreign” alone is insufficient |
| **Habitat** | Physical place where an organism lives | Habitat ≠ niche |
| **Niche** | Functional role + resources used + tolerance/interactions | Two species may share habitat but differ in niche |
| **Threatened (IUCN usage)** | Umbrella of **CR + EN + VU** | NT is not a threatened category |
| **Red List / Red Data** | Assesses extinction risk/status of species | Not a list of protected areas or domestic legal schedules |

#### B. Ecology & Ecosystem Functioning

##### 1. Core system logic

- **Ecosystem** = community of organisms + abiotic environment interacting as a functional unit.
- **Energy flow** → essentially **unidirectional**: Sun/chemical source → producers → consumers → heat loss.
- **Nutrients/elements** → **cycled** between biotic and abiotic pools.
- **Food chain** = feeding sequence; **food web** = interconnected chains → generally greater functional stability/redundancy.
- **Trophic level** = feeding position/functional level, not a permanent taxonomic label; omnivores may occupy multiple trophic levels.

##### 2. Ecological pyramids

| Pyramid | Rule | Common exception/trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy** | Always upright | Energy dissipates at each transfer |
| **Biomass** | Often upright terrestrially | Can be inverted in aquatic systems because phytoplankton have low standing biomass but rapid turnover |
| **Numbers** | Can vary | Tree/parasitic systems can be inverted/spindle-shaped |

- **10% law** → rough energy-transfer heuristic between trophic levels; **not “10% of organic matter must reach the next consumer level.”**
- Pyramids simplify real food webs and usually omit decomposer pathways.

##### 3. Productivity

- **GPP** = total photosynthetic energy/carbon fixation.
- **NPP = GPP − plant respiration** → energy/biomass available for growth and consumers.
- UPSC 2013 comparison: **mangroves > grasslands > lakes > open oceans** in decreasing productivity for the options asked.
- Productivity controlled chiefly by light, temperature/water and nutrient availability; in aquatic systems nutrient supply + light are decisive.

##### 4. Biogeochemical cycles

| Cycle | Major durable point |
|---|---|
| **Carbon** | Photosynthesis removes CO₂; respiration, decomposition and volcanic activity add CO₂ |
| **Nitrogen** | Atmospheric N₂ requires fixation before most biota can assimilate it |
| **Phosphorus** | Predominantly sedimentary; **rock weathering** is the principal entry source; no major atmospheric gaseous phase |
| **Sulphur** | **Mostly sedimentary** but has an atmospheric phase (SO₂/H₂S/DMS etc.) |

**Nitrogen sequence**
- Fixation: `N₂ → NH₃/NH₄⁺`.
- Nitrification: `NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻`.
- Assimilation: inorganic N → organic biomass.
- Ammonification: organic N → ammonia/ammonium.
- Denitrification: `NO₃⁻ → N₂/N₂O` under suitable low-O₂ conditions.
- High-confidence N fixers: **Rhizobium** (legume symbiosis), **Azotobacter** (free-living), **Nostoc/Anabaena** (cyanobacteria).

##### 5. Succession / niche / interactions

- **Succession** = directional community change over time.
  - **Primary** → starts without developed soil/previous community; lichens commonly pioneer bare rock.
  - **Secondary** → follows disturbance where soil/propagules remain; usually faster.
  - **Sere** = sequence of communities; **seral stage** = stage; **climax** = relatively stable mature state, not literally static forever.
- Grasslands may resist tree takeover because **water limitation + fire + grazing/disturbance** maintain the grass state.
- **Niche** = multidimensional functional position: tolerance + resource use + interactions; not merely physical address.
- **Competitive exclusion** → identical niches cannot stably coexist indefinitely under unchanged limiting conditions; niche differentiation permits coexistence.

**Interaction signs**
- Mutualism `+/+`
- Commensalism `+/0`
- Amensalism `-/0`
- Predation/parasitism `+/-`
- Competition `-/-`

##### 6. Ecosystem services

- **Provisioning** → food, fibre, freshwater, medicines.
- **Regulating** → climate/flood/disease/erosion regulation.
- **Cultural** → recreation, spiritual/aesthetic/educational value.
- **Supporting** → nutrient cycling, soil formation, primary production; older exam frameworks may place pollination differently—answer the question’s specified framework.
- **TEEB** → makes economic value of ecosystems/biodiversity visible for decision-making.

##### 7. Bioaccumulation vs biomagnification

- **Bioaccumulation** → concentration builds **within one organism over time** because uptake > elimination.
- **Biomagnification** → concentration increases **across successive trophic levels**.
- Strongest biomagnifiers: persistent, long-lived, poorly metabolized, often lipid-soluble substances.
- Trap: a chemical can bioaccumulate without showing strong trophic biomagnification.

##### 8. Sustainable development — durable layer

- **Brundtland Commission / WCED, 1987, _Our Common Future_** → popularized the classic intergenerational definition.
- **Limits to Growth (1972)** → Club of Rome; **not** the Brundtland Report.
- **Agenda 21** → global action plan from **Rio Earth Summit/UNCED, 1992**; not Johannesburg 2002 or 1995.
- **SDGs** → 17 goals, 2030 horizon.
- **Circular economy** → design out waste; keep products/materials in use via reuse/repair/remanufacture/recycling; reduce virgin-resource throughput. Omit annual “circularity %” from stable notes.

#### D. Marine Ecology

##### 1. Marine producers / food web

- **Primary producers** → phytoplankton: diatoms, cyanobacteria and other photosynthetic microbes/algae.
- **Copepods** → zooplankton consumers; **foraminifera** are heterotrophic protists (many may host symbionts) and were not counted as primary producers in UPSC 2021.
- Canonical marine chain example: **diatoms → crustaceans → herrings**.
- Marine phytoplankton/photosynthetic bacteria account for roughly **about half of global oxygen production** in the 2025 UPSC framing; much of ocean-produced O₂ is also consumed within marine systems.
- Removing phytoplankton damages both **marine food chains** and the ocean’s **carbon-sink function**.

##### 2. Upwelling

- Wind/ocean circulation brings **cold, nutrient-rich deeper water** toward surface.
- Nutrients → phytoplankton bloom/productivity → rich fisheries/food webs.
- Upwelling may also contribute to algal blooms when nutrient conditions are excessive.
- Trap: upwelling does **not** increase productivity by lifting bottom animals/decomposers as the principal mechanism.

##### 3. Coral reefs

- Coral polyps = animals, phylum **Cnidaria**; many reef-building corals live in mutualism with photosynthetic **zooxanthellae**.
- Reef-building corals generally favour **warm, clear, shallow, sunlit, saline** water with low sediment/pollution stress.
- Major Indian reef regions: **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh**; **Sundarbans is not** a major coral-reef region.
- Coral reefs support exceptionally high biodiversity/animal-phyla richness.

**Bleaching**
- Stress—especially anomalous heat—causes loss/expulsion/dysfunction of symbiotic algae/pigments → coral turns pale/white.
- Bleaching ≠ immediate coral death, but prolonged/severe stress can cause mortality.

**Ocean acidification**
- `CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻`
- More H⁺ → lower pH + reduced carbonate availability → harder for corals/calcareous plankton/shellfish to build CaCO₃.
- Acidification ≠ bleaching mechanism; the two stresses can interact.

**Biorock**
- Low electrical current across submerged metal structure promotes mineral accretion, especially CaCO₃, at the cathode.
- Coral fragments attached to the mineral-coated structure can use it as restoration substrate.

##### 4. Mangroves / blue carbon

- Mangroves: intertidal salt-tolerant woody vegetation; extensive root systems stabilize sediment and attenuate wave/tidal energy.
- **Blue carbon** = carbon captured/stored by ocean/coastal ecosystems, especially vegetated coastal systems such as **mangroves, salt marshes, seagrass**.
- Climate-resilience value: coastal bio-shield + nursery habitat + carbon storage + livelihood/fishery support.
- Trap: mangroves are salt-tolerant; describing roots as “salt-sensitive freshwater filters” is wrong.

##### 5. Marine mammal anchor

- **Dugong** = herbivorous marine mammal strongly associated with seagrass habitats; not found uniformly along the entire Indian coast.

#### E. Bioenergy & Biological Inputs

##### 1. Biofuel generations

| Generation | Main feedstock logic | High-yield point |
|---|---|---|
| **1G** | Food/sugar/starch/oil crops | Sugarcane/corn ethanol; vegetable-oil biodiesel; food-vs-fuel concern |
| **2G** | Lignocellulosic/non-food residues/wastes | Bagasse, straw, wood/agri residues → advanced ethanol/fuels |
| **3G** | Algae-based | High productivity potential; technical/economic/scaling constraints |
| **4G** | Advanced engineered biomass + carbon capture concepts | Not a core recurring PYQ; keep one-line only |

- **Ethanol is not intrinsically 2G**: generation depends on feedstock/process.
- US ethanol → predominantly **corn**; Brazil → predominantly **sugarcane**. Producer ranking is time-sensitive; feedstock contrast is durable.

##### 2. National Biofuels Policy — durable feedstock layer

- Eligible ethanol feedstock classes include:
  - sugar-containing material: sugarcane juice/sugar beet/sweet sorghum;
  - starch-containing material: corn/cassava etc.;
  - damaged/unfit food grains;
  - rotten potatoes;
  - specified surplus food grains subject to policy approval.
- UPSC 2020 set: **cassava + damaged wheat + rotten potatoes + sugar beet** were accepted; **groundnut seeds + horse gram** were not accepted in that question.
- Policy target dates/blending achievements → dynamic; omit from permanent payload.

##### 3. Biomass gasification

- Thermochemical conversion of biomass under **limited oxygen/air/steam**, producing combustible **syngas/product gas**.
- Typical combustible components include **CO + H₂** with variable CH₄/CO₂/N₂ etc.; “H₂ and CO₂ only” is false.
- Feedstocks: rice husk, coconut shells, groundnut shells, wood/agri residues.
- Cleaned gas can provide **heat** and can fuel suitable **internal-combustion engines/gensets**; “heat only” is false.

##### 4. Microbial fuel cells

- Microorganisms act as **biocatalysts**, oxidizing organic substrates and transferring electrons to generate electricity.
- Can combine **wastewater treatment + electricity generation**.
- They do not require “a variety of inorganic substrates” as the defining logic.

##### 5. Biofertilisers / biological inputs

| Input | Role |
|---|---|
| **Rhizobium** | Symbiotic N fixation in legume root nodules |
| **Azotobacter** | Free-living aerobic N fixer |
| **Nostoc / Anabaena** | N-fixing cyanobacteria; useful in wet/rice systems |
| **Azolla–Anabaena** | Water-fern/cyanobacterium association; paddy biofertiliser |
| **Mycorrhiza** | Fungus–root symbiosis → increases absorptive area, especially P/water uptake; improves stress tolerance |
| **Green manure legumes** | Plant biomass incorporated into soil; often also contributes biologically fixed N |

- UPSC 2013 biofertiliser trap: **Nostoc** qualifies; `Agaricus`/`Spirogyra` are not the intended answers.
- Mycorrhiza can improve drought tolerance, nutrient uptake, disease resistance and tolerance of adverse soil conditions.
- Algae-based biofuel is **not restricted to seas**; scale, expertise, harvesting and ecological/economic constraints are the real limitations.

##### 6. SAF feedstock logic

- Agricultural residues, corn grain, wastewater-treatment sludge and wood-mill waste can all serve as feedstocks/routes for **Sustainable Aviation Fuel** in the 2024 UPSC item.
- Core principle: SAF is defined by lifecycle/process specifications, not by one single crop.

#### F. Biodiversity: Patterns, Hotspots & Conservation

##### 1. Levels / measurement concepts

- **Genetic diversity** → variation within a species/population gene pool.
- **Species diversity** → combines **richness** (number of species) + **evenness** (relative abundance).
- **Ecosystem diversity** → variety of ecosystems/habitats/processes across a landscape/region.
- **α diversity** → local/site diversity.
- **β diversity** → turnover/change in composition between sites.
- **γ diversity** → regional/landscape diversity.
- Do **not** memorize `γ = α + β` as a universal law; relationship depends on the metric/partitioning convention.

##### 2. Diversity patterns

- Broad global pattern: **species richness generally increases from poles → tropics**.
- Along mountains, richness often declines with increasing elevation, though individual taxa can show mid-elevation peaks.
- Himalayan richness is aided by **confluence of different biogeographic zones**, strong environmental gradients and habitat heterogeneity.
- Coral reefs and tropical rainforests are both exceptionally diverse; do not infer “deep sea/desert” as highest-richness choices.

##### 3. Biodiversity hotspots

**Concept**
- Proposed by **Norman Myers**; developed/adopted operationally by **Conservation International**.
- Must satisfy **both**:
  1. `≥ 1,500 endemic vascular plant species`.
  2. `≥ 70% original natural vegetation/habitat lost` → `≤ 30% remaining`.

**Hotspots touching India**
- **Himalaya**
- **Indo-Burma** — includes much of NE India and Andaman group in hotspot treatments.
- **Western Ghats–Sri Lanka**
- **Sundaland** — Indian component: **Nicobar Islands**.

**Trap**
- Hotspot ≠ merely “high species richness”; **endemism + severe habitat loss/threat** are essential.
- BirdLife International did not originate the hotspot concept.

##### 4. Biodiversity-loss drivers

- **Habitat loss, degradation, fragmentation** → recurrent top driver.
- **Overexploitation** → hunting, logging, overfishing/collection.
- **Invasive alien species** → competition, predation, disease, hybridization, habitat change.
- **Pollution**.
- **Climate change**.
- Co-extinction / disrupted interactions can amplify losses.
- “Promotion of vegetarianism” is not a biodiversity-loss driver in the UPSC 2012 framing.

##### 5. Alien vs invasive

- **Alien/exotic** = outside natural range due human introduction.
- **Invasive alien** = alien + established/spreading + harmful.
- Common Indian high-yield invasive plants: **Lantana camara, Parthenium hysterophorus, Prosopis juliflora, Eichhornia crassipes (water hyacinth)**.
- **Prosopis juliflora** can form dense stands and suppress native biodiversity.
- Common invasive traits: rapid reproduction, effective dispersal, broad tolerance, generalist ecology, strong competitive ability.
- **ISSG** = Invasive Species Specialist Group under **IUCN Species Survival Commission**.

##### 6. In-situ vs ex-situ

| In-situ | Ex-situ |
|---|---|
| Conservation **within natural habitat/ecosystem** | Conservation **outside natural habitat** under managed conditions |
| Protects ecological interactions + evolutionary processes | Useful when wild population/habitat is critically threatened |
| National parks, sanctuaries, biosphere-reserve conservation zones, sacred/community conserved areas | Zoos, botanical gardens, captive breeding, seed/gene banks, cryopreservation |
| Generally retains larger natural gene pools | Risks domestication selection/inbreeding if small captive populations |

- **Botanical garden** → ex-situ.
- **National Park** → in-situ.
- Biosphere reserve concept is especially relevant where biodiversity conservation is integrated with sustainable traditional human use around protected core areas; detailed zoning/legal inventories belong to sibling topic.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **Native ≠ endemic ≠ naturally present after translocation.**
- **Species status ≠ legal schedule.** IUCN risk category and WPA protection are separate systems.
- **Habitat ≠ niche.**
- **Alien ≠ invasive.**
- **Threatened (IUCN) = CR + EN + VU**; NT is outside threatened grouping.
- **Energy pyramid always upright; biomass/numbers need not be.**
- **10% law → energy**, not organism count or organic-matter mass.
- **Bioaccumulation = within organism; biomagnification = up trophic levels.**
- **Phosphorus** → rock-weathering-dominated cycle; **sulphur** is only *mostly* sedimentary.
- **Upwelling → nutrients**, not “brings decomposers/fish to surface” as the primary productivity mechanism.
- **Phytoplankton/diatoms/cyanobacteria = producers; copepods = consumers.**
- **Coral = animal**; zooxanthellae = photosynthetic symbionts.
- **Bleaching ≠ acidification.** Heat stress ejects/damages symbionts; acidification reduces carbonate availability.
- **Blue carbon** = coastal/ocean ecosystem carbon, not forest/soil carbon.
- **Ethanol generation depends on feedstock**; ethanol itself is not automatically 2G.
- **Gasification ≠ combustion**; gasification uses limited oxidant to make combustible product gas.
- **Biofertiliser ≠ chemical fertiliser ≠ green manure.**
- **Moringa ≠ legume; tamarind ≠ South-Asia endemic.**
- **Papaya = giant herb**, not woody tree/shrub.
- **Palm oil tree native origin ≠ SE Asia** despite modern production concentration there.
- **Hotspot requires both endemism and habitat loss**; species richness alone is insufficient.
- **Protected-area law/location detail** belongs to sibling topic unless species habitat is the decisive demand.
- **Pollution/Climate-policy mechanisms** belong to sibling topic except where needed for trophic/marine ecology.

##### A2. UNFCCC → Kyoto → Paris

**UNFCCC**
- Adopted 1992; entered into force 1994.
- Core differentiation concept: developed-country historical responsibility/capability reflected through differentiated obligations; avoid converting CBDR into “only developed countries act”.
- COP = Conference of the Parties to UNFCCC.
- Durable COP anchors only:
  - COP3 Kyoto → Kyoto Protocol.
  - COP21 Paris → Paris Agreement.
  - COP16 Cancún → Green Climate Fund established as an operating entity of UNFCCC financial mechanism.
- Detailed yearly COP outcomes = dynamic layer unless a canonical PYQ requires them.

**Kyoto Protocol**
- Binding emission-limitation/reduction commitments for listed industrialised/economy-in-transition Parties.
- **Three flexible/market mechanisms**
  1. **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)** → Annex B/industrialised Party can use certified reductions from projects in developing countries; credits = **CERs**.
  2. **Joint Implementation (JI)** → emission-reduction/removal project between countries with Kyoto commitments; credits = **ERUs**.
  3. **International Emissions Trading** → Parties with spare assigned units can sell to Parties over target.
- Mechanisms supplement, not replace, domestic action.
- Carbon-credit price is market-linked; **UNEP does not periodically fix a universal price**.

**Paris Agreement**
- Adopted **12 Dec 2015**; entered into force **4 Nov 2016**.
- Legally binding international treaty.
- Temperature goal: hold warming **well below 2°C** and pursue efforts to **1.5°C** above pre-industrial levels.
- **NDCs**: each Party communicates progressively ambitious national climate actions on a recurring 5-year cycle.
- **Enhanced transparency + global stocktake** provide collective accountability/ambition mechanism.
- **Article 6**
  - **6.2** → cooperative approaches/transfer of mitigation outcomes between Parties.
  - **6.4** → UN-supervised crediting mechanism.
  - **6.8** → non-market approaches.
- Trap: Article 6 is **not only a carbon market article**; it explicitly includes non-market cooperation.

##### A3. REDD / REDD+ / forest-carbon finance

- **REDD** = reducing emissions from **deforestation + forest degradation**.
- **REDD+** adds:
  - conservation of forest carbon stocks;
  - sustainable management of forests;
  - enhancement of forest carbon stocks.
- **UN-REDD Programme** = UN collaborative programme supporting national REDD+ readiness/implementation; do not confuse with World Bank forest-carbon facilities.
- **Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (FCPF)** = World Bank-hosted forest-carbon/REDD+ partnership.
- **BioCarbon Fund Initiative for Sustainable Forest Landscapes (ISFL)** = World Bank-managed landscape/forest-climate finance initiative.
- **New York Declaration on Forests** = voluntary/non-legally-binding multi-stakeholder declaration; not a treaty.
- **Plan Vivo** = certification/standard ecosystem for community/land-use climate projects. Canonical 2026 anchor: **Khasi Hills Community REDD+ Project** = first Plan Vivo-certified REDD+ project in India.

##### B1. Do not merge these legal/conservation categories

| Category | Legal/institutional basis | Human-use logic | Exam hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wildlife Sanctuary** | Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 | Rights are settled/regulation applies; some compatible rights/uses may continue subject to law/permission | Less rigid than NP; hunting prohibited except statutory exceptions |
| **National Park** | Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 | Stronger protection; rights settled; grazing of livestock not permitted; activities only as specifically allowed for management/public purpose under law | “No human activity whatsoever” is too absolute |
| **Conservation Reserve** | WPA | Government-owned land, often adjoining/linking PAs; State Govt after consultation with local communities | Corridor/buffer logic; management committee |
| **Community Reserve** | WPA | Private/community land voluntarily offered for conservation; community retains central management role | Community Reserve Management Committee; hunting prohibited; not governed by CWLW alone |
| **Tiger Reserve** | WPA statutory framework + NTCA | Core/critical tiger habitat + buffer/peripheral area | Statutory overlay; core is NP/WLS area kept inviolate subject to rights process; buffer allows coexistence |
| **Biosphere Reserve** | Indian conservation programme + UNESCO MAB recognition possible | Zonation expressly integrates conservation + sustainable development | **Not a WPA statutory PA category** |
| **Ramsar Site** | International designation under Convention on Wetlands | Wise-use obligation; domestic legal regime depends on Indian law/notification | Ramsar designation ≠ National Park/WLS status |

##### B2. National Park vs Wildlife Sanctuary — elimination matrix

**Common**
- Both are in-situ conservation areas under WPA.
- Hunting of protected wildlife prohibited except statutory exceptions.
- State authorities manage/protect; legal rights/process matter before final notification.

**National Park**
- Higher restriction on resource use than WLS.
- Livestock grazing not permitted.
- Boundary/rights enjoy stronger legal protection; do not use old oversimplified “State Legislature alone can alter” wording without checking current statute.
- A National Park may contain ecological, faunal, floral, geomorphological or zoological importance.

**Wildlife Sanctuary**
- Can accommodate regulated rights/uses after settlement; grazing/movement can be regulated.
- “Sanctuary = unrestricted human use” is false.

**Trap**
- UPSC 2012 biomass-use question: do not generalise from Biosphere Reserve/Ramsar to statutory NP restrictions.

##### B3. Conservation Reserve vs Community Reserve

**Conservation Reserve**
- Government-owned land.
- Particularly useful for landscapes adjacent to NPs/WLS or ecological corridors.
- State Government declaration after consultation with local communities.
- Managed through a Conservation Reserve Management Committee.

**Community Reserve**
- Private/community land where community/individuals voluntarily seek conservation.
- State Government constitutes a **Community Reserve Management Committee** as managing authority.
- Hunting prohibited under WPA.
- Community/private rights do not vanish merely because of notification; changes in land use are legally constrained.
- **UPSC 2023 trap:** Chief Wildlife Warden is **not** the sole governing authority; “all traditional agriculture automatically allowed/prohibited” is an overstatement—read statutory management/land-use constraints.

##### B4. Tiger Reserve architecture

- **Project Tiger** launched 1973; conservation scheme, now implemented through statutory NTCA framework.
- **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)** = statutory body under WPA.
- Tiger Reserve has:
  1. **Core / Critical Tiger Habitat (CTH)** — areas of National Parks/Sanctuaries identified on scientific/objective criteria, intended to be kept inviolate for tiger conservation after legally required rights/relocation process.
  2. **Buffer / Peripheral Area** — surrounding landscape with lesser degree of habitat protection; habitat supplement/corridor/coexistence role.
- **Tiger Reserve ≠ always identical to one NP.** It can include one or more PA/forest/other landscape units.
- **Critical Tiger Habitat (WPA) ≠ Critical Wildlife Habitat (FRA).**
- **M-STrIPES** = Monitoring System for Tigers—Intensive Protection and Ecological Status; patrolling/monitoring tool.
- Dynamic current counts/rankings = exclude from permanent pack.

##### B5. Biosphere Reserve

**Purpose**
- Conservation of biodiversity.
- Ecologically/socio-culturally sustainable development.
- Logistic support: research, monitoring, education/training.

**Zones**
- **Core** → strictly protected; conservation priority.
- **Buffer** → surrounds/adjoins core; research, education, restoration, controlled compatible uses.
- **Transition** → settlements/economic activity under sustainable-development cooperation.

**UNESCO MAB**
- Man and the Biosphere Programme began 1971.
- UNESCO World Network recognition is not the same thing as Indian legal declaration.
- A Biosphere Reserve may contain NPs/WLS; BR itself is not automatically a WPA category.

##### B6. Wetlands: durable ecological payload

- Wetlands = interface ecosystems where hydrology creates waterlogged/saturated conditions supporting characteristic soils/biota.
- **Hydrophytes** = water/waterlogged-adapted plants.
- **Hydric soils** = soils formed under prolonged saturation/low-oxygen conditions.
- High-value ecosystem services:
  - flood moderation/storage;
  - groundwater recharge/discharge functions depending on site;
  - shoreline/erosion control;
  - nutrient retention/transformation;
  - sediment trapping;
  - pollutant removal/water purification;
  - carbon storage;
  - nursery/breeding habitat;
  - high biological productivity.
- UPSC “**kidneys of the landscape**” hook → filtration/removal/transformation of pollutants/nutrients.

##### B7. Ramsar Convention

- Intergovernmental Convention on Wetlands; adopted at Ramsar, Iran, 1971.
- Core commitments:
  1. wise use of all wetlands;
  2. designate suitable wetlands for Ramsar List and maintain ecological character;
  3. international cooperation, especially shared wetlands/water systems/species.
- Ramsar Site = wetland of international importance meeting Ramsar criteria.
- Ramsar designation **does not automatically convert site into NP/WLS**.

**Montreux Record**
- Record of Ramsar Sites where ecological character **has changed, is changing, or is likely to change**, especially due to technological development, pollution or other human interference.
- It is a management/problem-attention mechanism, not a “World Heritage danger list”.
- Durable Indian anchors historically used in exam prep: **Keoladeo National Park** and **Loktak Lake**.
- Do not memorise changing global counts for permanent notes.

##### B8. Indian Wetlands Rules

- **Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017** superseded 2010 Rules.
- Institutional structure:
  - State/UT Wetlands Authorities;
  - **National Wetlands Committee** under Rule 6, chaired by Secretary, MoEFCC.
- Core regulatory logic for notified wetlands: prohibit/strictly control conversion, incompatible industries, waste dumping, untreated effluent discharge and other activities affecting ecological character.
- **Trap:** “National Wetland Authority” is not the correct current central body name under the 2017 Rules.

#### C. Indian Environmental Laws & Institutions

##### C1. Constitutional anchors

- **Article 48A (DPSP)** → State shall endeavour to protect/improve environment and safeguard forests and wildlife.
- **Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty)** → every citizen shall protect/improve natural environment incl. forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife; compassion for living creatures.
- Forests and protection of wild animals/birds shifted to **Concurrent List** by 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976.
- UPPCS trap: protecting natural environment is a Fundamental Duty; protecting monuments is primarily a separate constitutional/statutory obligation and should not be merged with 51A(g).

##### C3. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986

- Umbrella central environmental legislation.
- Broad Central Government power to:
  - coordinate actions of authorities;
  - lay standards for environmental quality/emissions/discharges;
  - restrict/condition industrial siting/operations;
  - prescribe safeguards for hazardous substances;
  - establish/recognise environmental laboratories;
  - issue binding directions including closure/regulation in statutory framework.
- EPA is the enabling basis for numerous rules/notifications: EIA framework, CRZ, hazardous/waste rules, ESZ notifications, wetland rules, etc.

**High-yield bodies under EPA framework**
- **Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)** → constituted under EPA powers; regulates/control groundwater development/management through notified framework.
- **GEAC** → biotechnology/environment appraisal committee constituted under Rules made under EPA, 1986.
- Historical **NGRBA** was constituted under EPA; dissolved 7 Oct 2016.
- Current Ganga architecture: **National Ganga Council** + **NMCG** authority/implementation system under 2016 River Ganga Authorities Order.
- **Trap:** CPCB was **not** created by EPA.

##### C4. CPCB / SPCBs

- **CPCB** = statutory body constituted under **Water Act, 1974**.
- **SPCBs** likewise statutory pollution-control boards under Water Act architecture.
- Air Act, 1981 extends pollution-control functions into air-pollution domain.
- CPCB roles include national coordination/technical guidance, standards/monitoring and pollution-control functions; SPCBs handle state-level consent/enforcement architecture.
- **UPSC 2018 trap:** “CPCB created by executive order” = false.
- **NGT vs CPCB:** NGT adjudicates environmental disputes/relief; CPCB is a pollution regulator/technical board.

##### C5. Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 — current schedule architecture

**Current architecture after Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022 (effective 1 Apr 2023)**
- **Schedule I** → animal species receiving the highest level of protection.
- **Schedule II** → other protected animal species.
- **Schedule III** → specified protected plants.
- **Schedule IV** → specimens/species listed under CITES appendices for the Act’s CITES implementation architecture.
- Old six-schedule scheme, including **Schedule VI plants**, belongs to historical PYQs and must not be copied as current law.

**Hunting**
- Hunting of scheduled wild animals is generally prohibited.
- Statutory exceptions require the competent wildlife authority and specific legal grounds; mere fear/apprehension is not enough.
- Self-defence and other narrowly defined circumstances have separate legal treatment.

**Wild animals/property**
- Certain wild animals, trophies, articles etc. become government property under statutory conditions.
- Avoid blanket philosophical wording that “all wildlife is solely government property” without the statutory context.

**Major WPA institutions**
- **National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)** — statutory board; chaired by Prime Minister.
- **State Boards for Wildlife** — chaired by Chief Minister.
- **Central Zoo Authority (CZA)** — statutory zoo-recognition/regulation body.
- **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)** — statutory tiger-conservation authority.
- **Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB)** — statutory wildlife-crime coordination/enforcement body.
- Director of Wildlife Preservation (Centre) + Chief Wildlife Wardens (States/UTs).

**Historical PYQ handling**
- `UPSC-CSE-2020-GS1-Q081`: old **Schedule VI** protected plants — cultivation required licence; keep only as historical question-date law.
- `UPSC-CSE-2017-GS1-Q091`: old Schedule I meant highest protection.
- Final synthesis must show **current 4 schedules**, with old schedule questions marked historical.

##### C6. Forest Rights Act, 2006

**Purpose**
- Recognises and vests forest rights of **forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes (FDST)** and **Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFD)** who meet statutory conditions.

**Nodal ministry**
- **Ministry of Tribal Affairs**, not MoEFCC.

**Core rights**
- habitation/self-cultivation rights over occupied forest land, subject to Act;
- ownership/access/use/disposal of **minor forest produce (MFP)**;
- grazing/fishing/water-body/community-use rights;
- habitat rights for PVTGs and pre-agricultural communities;
- community forest-resource right to protect, regenerate, conserve/manage;
- relief/development rights subject to conservation safeguards;
- traditional knowledge/biodiversity-linked community rights.

**Title ceiling**
- Recognition for land under actual occupation; individual forest-right land cannot exceed **4 hectares**.
- FRA does not authorise fresh forest-land grabbing.

**Eligibility distinction — critical correction**
- **FDST:** member/community of Scheduled Tribe who primarily resides in and depends on forests/forest land for bona fide livelihood needs; relevant occupation/residence cut-off under Act.
- **OTFD:** must show primary residence/dependence over **three generations = 75 years** before statutory cut-off.
- **Do not apply the 75-year rule identically to FDST.**

**Claims process**
- **Gram Sabha initiates** process of determining nature/extent of individual/community forest rights.
- Claims proceed through statutory sub-divisional/district committees.

**Bamboo**
- FRA treats bamboo as **minor forest produce**; ownership/access rights over MFP are a recurring UPSC eliminator.

**Critical Wildlife Habitat (CWH)**
- FRA concept for areas of **National Parks and Sanctuaries** that, on scientific/objective criteria, are required to be kept inviolate for wildlife conservation and are notified after prescribed rights/rehabilitation safeguards.
- **CWH ≠ Critical Tiger Habitat/Core of a Tiger Reserve.**

##### C7. Biological Diversity Act, 2002

**Purpose**
- Conservation of biological diversity.
- Sustainable use.
- Fair/equitable benefit sharing.
- Implements India’s CBD/ABS obligations through domestic institutional architecture.

**Three-tier institutions**
1. **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)** — national level.
2. **State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)** — state level.
3. **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** — local-body level.

**BMC**
- Every local body constitutes BMC for conservation, sustainable use and documentation of biodiversity/traditional knowledge.
- NBA/SBB consult BMCs on relevant use of biological resources/associated knowledge within BMC jurisdiction.
- BMC may levy **collection fees** for commercial access/collection of biological resources in its jurisdiction.
- **People’s Biodiversity Register (PBR)** = local biodiversity/associated knowledge documentation instrument facilitated by BMC system.

**Nagoya link**
- BMC/ABS structure is a direct domestic bridge to Nagoya Protocol objectives.

**Correction**
- Do **not** retain blanket line “Indian citizens and entities have free access to biological resources.” Access/intimation/approval obligations vary by person/entity/resource/use and have been amended.

**2023 amendment**
- Dynamic/current statutory layer; details should be refreshed when final notes are synthesised.
- Permanent core should retain institutional architecture + ABS/PBR/BMC functions.

##### C8. National Green Tribunal

- Established by **NGT Act, 2010**.
- Specialised tribunal for civil environmental cases involving a substantial question relating to environment arising from implementation of **Schedule-I enactments**.
- Guided by principles of **natural justice**, not strictly bound by CPC.
- Can grant relief/compensation/restitution within statutory jurisdiction.
- Statutory design seeks expeditious disposal; Act sets a six-month endeavour.
- Appeal from NGT order → **Supreme Court** under the Act.

**Schedule-I exam anchor**
- Water Act 1974.
- Forest (Conservation) Act 1980.
- Air Act 1981.
- Environment (Protection) Act 1986.
- Public Liability Insurance Act 1991.
- Biological Diversity Act 2002.
- Historical Water Cess Act 1977 also appears in original Schedule (later repealed separately).

**Trap**
- Better formulation than “NGT covers every environmental law except IFA/WPA/FRA”: **jurisdiction is tied to Schedule-I statutes**.

##### C9. Forest conservation / CAMPA

**Forest-conservation law**
- Central approval architecture restricts dereservation/diversion of legally covered forest land for non-forest purposes.
- Current short title after amendment: **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980**.
- Godavarman jurisprudence historically broadened operational understanding of “forest”; avoid freezing litigation-specific wording without current check.

**Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016**
- National + State Compensatory Afforestation Funds.
- National + State **CAMPA** management/planning authorities.
- Statutory fund flows include compensatory-afforestation monies, NPV and related transfers.
- Canonical UPSC anchor:
  - CAMPA exists at **both National and State levels**.
  - People’s participation is **not universally mandated for every compensatory-afforestation programme** by the Act.

##### C10. Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ) / ESA

**ESZ**
- Notified under **Environment (Protection) Act/Rules** around protected areas/other ecologically sensitive landscapes.
- Functional logic: transition/shock-absorber zone; land-use/activities classified as prohibited, regulated or permissible/promoted.
- ESZ is **not equivalent to adding the land to a National Park/WLS**.
- No universal permanent rule that every ESZ is exactly 10 km or exactly 1 km; notification/court directions and site context matter.
- Typical prohibited/high-risk activities: commercial mining/stone crushing, highly polluting industries, major incompatible projects, waste/hazardous discharges etc.; many livelihood activities are regulated rather than automatically banned.

**Western Ghats ESA**
- Landscape-scale Ecologically Sensitive Area proposal/notification process, distinct from PA-specific ESZ.
- Exact village/area/notification status is a **dynamic statutory-current layer**; keep outside permanent source payload except as an example.

##### C11. Wetlands Rules, 2017

- Made under EPA framework.
- Superseded **Wetlands Rules, 2010**.
- Central body = **National Wetlands Committee**; states/UTs have Wetlands Authorities.
- Regulatory focus = maintaining ecological character; prohibit conversion/encroachment/incompatible waste and effluent practices, with site-specific integrated management.
- `UPSC-CSE-2019-GS1-Q040` asked the then historical 2010 Rules; final notes should explicitly label the rule vintage.

##### C12. Waste-management rules: only durable PYQ anchors

**Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016**
- Source segregation by waste generators is core.
- Applicability is broader than only “notified urban local bodies + industrial townships”.
- Rules contain criteria for waste-processing/landfill siting and management.
- Canonical UPSC 2019 eliminator: **exact/elaborate landfill/site criteria**.

**E-waste**
- **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)** appeared as the central producer-responsibility feature in **E-Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2011**.
- EPR = producer responsibility for end-of-life channelisation/environmentally sound management.
- Current target percentages/portal rules are dynamic; do not freeze unless separately current-affairs tested.

**Plastic Waste**
- EPR + source responsibility are durable concepts; exact thickness/annual target rules are amendment-sensitive.
- Keep current numerical thresholds outside permanent source pack unless a canonical PYQ demands them.

##### C13. Ganga governance

- **NGRBA**: historical authority under EPA architecture; UPSC 2014/2016-era questions can validly ask it.
- NGRBA dissolved **7 Oct 2016**.
- Replaced by **National Council for Rejuvenation, Protection and Management of River Ganga (National Ganga Council)** under River Ganga Authorities Order, 2016.
- **NMCG** = implementation/authority structure for Ganga rejuvenation.
- Never rewrite old PYQ as if NGRBA still exists; preserve question-date fact + current correction.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Pollution & Pollutants

##### 1. Core classification

- **Pollution:** undesirable physical/chemical/biological change in air/water/soil that harms organisms, ecosystems or use-value.
- **Primary pollutant:** emitted directly—CO, SO₂, NO, primary PM, VOCs, NH₃, metals.
- **Secondary pollutant:** formed in the environment—tropospheric O₃, PAN, much secondary PM, sulfuric/nitric acid; **smog** is a mixture/condition, not one single chemical.
- **Point source:** identifiable discharge (stack, drain, effluent pipe). **Non-point:** diffuse runoff/deposition, especially agriculture/urban runoff.
- **Persistence matters:** chemicals resistant to degradation + lipophilic/poorly excreted → bioaccumulation; concentration can rise across trophic levels → biomagnification.

##### 2. Air pollutants — source / mechanism / elimination value

| Pollutant | Major sources | High-yield effect / trap |
|---|---|---|
| **CO** | incomplete combustion; vehicles, biomass/fossil fuel | binds haemoglobin strongly → reduces O₂-carrying capacity; **not** a principal acid-rain precursor |
| **SO₂ / SOₓ** | sulfur-bearing coal/oil; power plants; smelting/refining | respiratory irritant; oxidises to sulfuric acid/sulfate; acid rain + secondary PM |
| **NOₓ = chiefly NO + NO₂** | high-temperature combustion; vehicles/power/industry; soils | photochemical smog/O₃; nitric acid; secondary nitrate PM. **Do not equate N₂O with conventional NOₓ** |
| **N₂O** | soils/fertiliser/manure; microbial nitrification-denitrification | long-lived GHG; contributes to stratospheric ozone chemistry; not the usual urban NOₓ pollutant |
| **Ground-level O₃** | **secondary**: NOₓ + VOC photochemistry + sunlight | oxidant; asthma/respiratory injury; peaks under sunny photochemical conditions; “bad ozone” |
| **PM₁₀** | road/construction/mining dust, coarse combustion particles | aerodynamic diameter ≤10 µm; inhalable |
| **PM₂.₅** | combustion + secondary sulfate/nitrate/ammonium/organics | ≤2.5 µm; penetrates deep lung; fine fraction can cross alveolar barrier more readily |
| **VOCs incl. benzene** | fuel evaporation/exhaust, solvents, industry, tobacco smoke, wood burning | ozone/smog precursors; benzene = carcinogenic exposure concern |
| **NH₃** | fertiliser, livestock/manure, waste | reactive nitrogen; forms secondary ammonium nitrate/sulfate PM |
| **Black carbon** | incomplete combustion: diesel, biomass, open burning | light-absorbing **aerosol/short-lived climate pollutant**, not a GHG; atmospheric warming + snow/ice darkening/albedo loss |
| **Fly/coal ash** | coal combustion | PM + Si/Al/Fe/Ca phases + trace toxic elements; may contain As/Pb/Hg; reusable but leaching/dust must be controlled |
| **Magnetite-rich particles** | brakes/engines, combustion incl. power plants | fine magnetic particles; exposure concern; do not assume domestic appliance/telephone sources without evidence |

##### 3. Industry-emission matrix

- **Coal thermal power:** CO₂ + SO₂ + NOₓ + PM/fly ash; ash can contain **As, Pb, Hg**.
- **Steel:** PM + SOₓ + NOₓ + CO + CO₂; slag generated; coke-making can add VOCs/PAHs.
- **Copper smelting:** SO₂ important; slag may leach heavy metals. UPSC trap: “lethal CO quantities” is not the defining concern.
- **Furnace oil:** heavy refinery product used by industries/boilers/power; sulfur content → SOₓ emissions.
- **Cement:** two CO₂ channels—fuel combustion + **calcination** (`CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂`) during clinker production; limestone + silica/alumina-bearing material used.
- **Biomass/crop-residue burning:** emits CO, CH₄, PM/black carbon, VOCs and NOₓ; can generate/raise secondary O₃. Sulfur emissions depend on fuel sulfur and are not the defining biomass pollutant.

##### 4. Smog + acid rain

| Classical / sulfurous smog | Photochemical smog |
|---|---|
| cool, humid, stagnant; coal smoke + SO₂ + PM | warm, sunny; NOₓ + VOCs photochemistry |
| reducing character | oxidising character |
| sulfurous aerosol/fog | O₃ + PAN + aldehydes/other oxidants |

- **PAN:** strong eye irritant; secondary photochemical pollutant.
- **Acid rain:** precipitation/deposition more acidic than natural rain; **main anthropogenic precursors = SO₂ + NOₓ → H₂SO₄ + HNO₃**.
- Natural dissolved CO₂ forms weak carbonic acid and helps explain normal rain pH around **5.6**; do **not** treat CO₂ as the principal anthropogenic acid-rain cause.
- Effects: soil/base-cation leaching; mobilisation of toxic Al; lake/stream acidification; vegetation stress; corrosion of limestone/marble/metals.
- **Wet deposition:** acids in rain/snow/fog. **Dry deposition:** acidic gases/particles deposited without precipitation.

##### 5. AQI / standards — durable layer

- **India AQI (CPCB): 8 pollutants** → **PM₁₀, PM₂.₅, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃, Pb**.
- **Not in the AQI basket:** CO₂, CH₄.
- **WHO 2021 Global AQG high-yield values** (health guidelines, **not legally binding standards**):
  - PM₂.₅: **annual 5 µg/m³; 24-h 15 µg/m³**.
  - PM₁₀: **annual 15 µg/m³; 24-h 45 µg/m³**.
- Ozone high episodes favour photochemical/sunny conditions, not “inclement weather”.
- Permanent pack should remember the **framework + UPSC-tested WHO values**; do not hard-code every revisable national standard.

##### 6. Water-pollution indicators

- **DO:** dissolved oxygen available in water; low DO commonly accompanies organic loading/eutrophication. Avoid one universal “healthy” threshold—temperature, salinity, species and ecosystem matter.
- **BOD:** O₂ consumed by microorganisms while aerobically decomposing **biodegradable organic matter** under specified test conditions. **Higher BOD → heavier biodegradable organic load, usually lower DO.**
- **COD:** oxygen-equivalent required for chemical oxidation of oxidisable material; captures a broader fraction than BOD. **COD is typically higher than BOD**, but avoid “always” as an absolute analytical rule.
- **Eutrophication:** nutrient enrichment—mainly biologically available **N + P**—→ algal/macrophyte growth → shading + decomposition → O₂ depletion/hypoxia → fish kills/dead zones. Anthropogenic acceleration = **cultural eutrophication**.
- Sources: fertiliser/manure runoff, sewage, nutrient-rich effluent, urban runoff.

##### 7. Heavy metals / groundwater contaminants

| Contaminant | Major source/association | High-yield effect / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead (Pb)** | smelting, batteries/recycling, paints, old pipes, some e-waste | neurodevelopmental + systemic toxicity; pencils are graphite, not a lead-poisoning source |
| **Mercury (Hg)** | artisanal/small-scale gold mining; coal combustion; industrial release | neurotoxic; methylmercury biomagnifies; no exposure level should be casually called “safe” |
| **Cadmium (Cd)** | batteries, smelting, phosphate fertiliser/e-waste | renal/bone toxicity; persistent |
| **Arsenic (As)** | geogenic groundwater; mining/smelting/coal/industrial pathways | chronic arsenicosis; groundwater contamination can be natural, not only industrial |
| **Fluoride** | geogenic groundwater | fluorosis at excess exposure |
| **Nitrate** | fertiliser/manure/sewage | infant methemoglobinaemia (“blue-baby syndrome”) pathway |
| **Uranium** | geogenic + mining/industrial contexts | can contaminate groundwater; chemical nephrotoxicity is important |

- Heavy metals are **elements**: they cannot be biodegraded into nothing. Treatment can precipitate, immobilise, adsorb, chelate, transform oxidation state, phytoextract or physically remove them.

##### 8. Emerging contaminants / product associations

| Substance | Exam anchor | Durable fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Nanoparticles** | 2014, 2022 | exist naturally and anthropogenically; metallic-oxide nanoparticles occur in products/cosmetics; high reactivity/small size can enable environmental and biological interactions |
| **Microbeads** | 2019 | intentionally manufactured small plastic particles; **primary microplastics**; marine ingestion/ecosystem concern |
| **BPA** | 2021 | monomer/key component associated with **polycarbonate** plastics and some epoxy resins |
| **Triclosan** | 2021 | antimicrobial historically used in **personal-care/toiletry** products; persistence/exposure concern |
| **PFAS** | 2024 | large fluorinated family used for water/grease/stain resistance; very persistent; detected in water/food/packaging; several PFAS bioaccumulate |
| **Plastic-containing gum base** | 2024 | many chewing gums use synthetic polymeric gum bases → litter/microplastic concern |
| **Pesticides (e.g., DDT; carbofuran etc.)** | 2019/UPPCS | persistence/toxicity varies; DDT is classic persistent/non-biodegradable pollutant; do not generalise one pesticide's properties to all agrochemicals |

- **Microplastic:** use durable scientific shorthand **plastic particles <5 mm**; primary = manufactured at small size (e.g., microbeads, pellets/fibres), secondary = fragmentation of larger plastic. Regulatory size cut-offs can differ.

##### 9. Reactive nitrogen / soil-resource links

- **Reactive nitrogen (Nr):** biologically/chemically active N species other than N₂—NH₃/NH₄⁺, NOₓ, NO₃⁻, N₂O, organic N.
- Sources: fertiliser/soil microbial processes, livestock/poultry manure, combustion.
- Cascading effects: PM₂.₅ formation, eutrophication, acidification, ozone formation, N₂O climate forcing.
- **Resource extraction:** mining/sand mining can alter groundwater levels, turbidity, habitat and release metals/acid drainage; keep ecological effects here, pure resource geography elsewhere.

#### B. Climate Change: Science, Mitigation & Policy

##### 1. Greenhouse physics

- Incoming solar radiation is predominantly **short-wave**; Earth emits **long-wave infrared (terrestrial radiation)**.
- Surface/atmosphere energy balance: GHGs absorb and re-emit outgoing IR → natural greenhouse effect. Anthropogenic increases strengthen radiative forcing.
- Atmosphere is heated substantially **from below by terrestrial long-wave radiation**, not simply more by direct incoming solar radiation.
- **Major climate-forcing gases:** CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, tropospheric O₃, halogenated gases; water vapour is the dominant natural GHG but primarily a **feedback** to anthropogenic warming at global scale.
- **Black carbon is not a greenhouse gas**; it is a light-absorbing aerosol/SLCP.
- **GWP:** heat-trapping effect relative to CO₂ over a specified time horizon (commonly 100 y). Values are **gas-specific and horizon-specific**—avoid one timeless family ranking.
- **Atmospheric lifetime:** CO₂ has no single lifetime; CH₄ is roughly decadal, N₂O roughly centennial, many fluorinated gases last decades to millennia. Do not memorise a false universal sequence.

##### 2. Methane / N₂O high-yield pathways

- **CH₄:** strong GHG, shorter-lived than CO₂; major anthropogenic sources = fossil-fuel systems, livestock enteric fermentation/manure, rice cultivation, landfills/wastewater.
- Paddy methane: anaerobic flooded soils favour methanogens. **Alternate wetting and drying/SRI-type water management can lower CH₄** by interrupting prolonged anoxia.
- **N₂O:** microbial nitrification/denitrification in fertilised/manured soils; long-lived GHG and stratospheric ozone-relevant nitrogen species.
- **Methane hydrates/clathrates:** methane trapped in water-ice lattice, especially marine sediments/permafrost regions; warming may destabilise some deposits → positive feedback. Atmospheric methane is oxidised over roughly a decade-scale timespan, ultimately contributing CO₂ + water.

##### 3. Carbon fertilisation

- **Carbon fertilisation effect:** elevated atmospheric CO₂ can raise photosynthetic carbon fixation and biomass, especially in many C₃ plants.
- **Not unlimited / not universal:** response constrained by water, nutrients (especially N/P), light, temperature, species, acclimation and disturbance.
- Elevated CO₂ often reduces stomatal opening → can improve plant water-use efficiency, but this **does not cancel heat/drought/ecosystem damage**.
- Elimination rule: “more CO₂ → all plants permanently grow better” = false.

##### 4. Carbon sinks / stores

- **Sink:** absorbs more carbon than it releases over a defined period. **Store/reservoir:** holds carbon; may become a source if disturbed.
- **Blue carbon:** carbon captured/stored by oceans and especially coastal ecosystems such as mangroves, salt marshes and seagrasses.
- **Peatlands:** waterlogged conditions slow decomposition → exceptionally carbon-dense organic soils; drainage/fire can rapidly convert sink/store to source.
- **Congo Basin:** location anchor for the world’s largest known **tropical peatland complex** tested by UPSC 2024.
- Ocean phytoplankton + physical/biological pumps contribute to ocean carbon uptake; destruction of primary producers weakens sink and food-web functions.

##### 5. Mitigation taxonomy — do not mix these

| Category | Core mechanism | Examples | Key limitation/trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Emissions avoidance/reduction** | prevent GHG release | renewables, efficiency, methane control | does not remove legacy atmospheric CO₂ |
| **Point-source CCS** | capture concentrated CO₂ before release + durable storage | power/cement/steel flue gas → saline formation/depleted reservoir | capture ≠ atmospheric removal unless linked to biogenic carbon |
| **CCUS** | capture + use and/or storage | concrete curing, chemicals, synthetic fuels | utilisation is not necessarily permanent sequestration |
| **CDR / negative emissions** | remove CO₂ already in atmosphere + durable storage | DACCS, afforestation, soil carbon, biochar, BECCS, enhanced weathering, ocean alkalinity | permanence, land/energy/material/ecosystem constraints |
| **SRM / radiation modification** | reduce net incoming energy / alter radiation balance | stratospheric aerosol injection, cloud brightening; cirrus thinning alters outgoing long-wave trapping | **does not remove CO₂**; acidification continues; governance/termination risks |

##### 6. CDR / sequestration techniques

- **Geological storage:** depleted oil/gas reservoirs, deep saline formations, unmineable/abandoned coal seams can store injected CO₂ under suitable geology.
- **Enhanced rock weathering:** finely ground silicate rocks (e.g., basalt) accelerate CO₂-consuming weathering; eventual bicarbonate/carbonate storage. Constraints: mining/grinding energy, dust, trace metals, measurement permanence.
- **Ocean alkalinity enhancement:** increase seawater alkalinity so more atmospheric CO₂ can be taken up and stored as bicarbonate/carbonate; ecological/geochemical side effects require caution.
- **Direct Air Capture (DAC):** chemical sorbents remove dilute CO₂ from ambient air. Climate benefit requires low-carbon energy + **durable storage (DACCS)**; captured CO₂ may also feed chemicals/plastics/food uses or combine with green H₂ for synthetic fuels, but short-lived products re-release carbon.
- **Biochar:** pyrolysed biomass, relatively stable carbon-rich material; can improve water retention/soil properties. Climate value depends on feedstock, process and permanence.
- **Soil carbon:** zero/reduced tillage, cover/residue management etc. can raise soil organic carbon under suitable conditions; saturation/reversibility matter.
- **BECCS:** biomass takes up atmospheric CO₂ → energy conversion + capture/storage; potentially net-negative but land/water/biodiversity constraints.

##### 7. Geoengineering / SRM

- **Stratospheric aerosol injection (SAI):** reflective sulfate-forming particles increase planetary albedo → cooling; analog to volcanic aerosol effect. Does **not** solve ocean acidification.
- **Marine cloud brightening:** increase cloud droplet number/reflectivity over marine low clouds; uncertain regional effects.
- **Cirrus cloud thinning:** aims to reduce high-cloud long-wave trapping so more outgoing terrestrial radiation escapes; technically distinct from classic sunlight-reflection SRM but belongs climate-radiation engineering.
- **Termination shock:** abrupt cessation after sustained SRM could yield rapid rebound warming.

##### 8. Carbon markets / credits

- **Carbon credit:** tradable unit representing a quantified emission reduction/removal—commonly **1 tonne CO₂-equivalent** under a specified standard.
- **Compliance market:** created by law/regulation (cap-and-trade, baseline-and-credit, regulated offsets). **Voluntary market:** buyers purchase credits outside a mandatory cap.
- **Allowance ≠ offset:** allowance = right to emit under a cap; offset/credit = certified reduction/removal relative to a baseline/methodology.
- Carbon price emerges from market/tax/rule design; **UNEP does not periodically fix a universal carbon-credit price**.
- **Kyoto-era mechanisms:** emissions trading, Joint Implementation, Clean Development Mechanism—historical source of “carbon credit” exam framing.
- **Paris Agreement Article 6:**
  - **6.2:** cooperative approaches / internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs).
  - **6.4:** UN-supervised crediting mechanism.
  - **6.8:** **non-market** approaches.
- Full treaty/COP architecture belongs the conventions sibling topic; retain only market mechanics here.

##### 9. Social Cost of Carbon (SCC)

- Monetary estimate of the **marginal long-term damage caused by an additional tonne of CO₂ emitted in a given year**.
- Aggregates climate damages across time/sectors; highly sensitive to discount rate, damage functions, socioeconomic pathway and geography.
- **SCC ≠ carbon tax ≠ market carbon price**. SCC is an estimated damage metric; policy prices may be set differently.

##### 11. Ozone depletion vs climate forcing

- **Stratospheric O₃ (“good ozone”):** absorbs harmful UV, concentrated in stratosphere. **Tropospheric O₃:** secondary air pollutant + GHG.
- Classic ODS are long-lived halogenated compounds that reach stratosphere and release **Cl/Br radicals** under UV: CFCs, halons, carbon tetrachloride, methyl chloroform, methyl bromide, HCFCs etc.
- **Antarctic ozone hole mechanism:** cold isolated **polar vortex** → polar stratospheric clouds (PSCs) provide reaction surfaces that activate chlorine/bromine reservoirs → sunlight returns in spring → catalytic O₃ destruction. Antarctica generally has colder/more stable vortex than Arctic.
- **CFC legacy uses:** refrigeration/air-conditioning, foam blowing, aerosol propellants, solvent/electronics cleaning.
- **HCFCs:** lower ODP than CFCs but still ODS + GHGs.
- **HFCs:** **zero/negligible ODP; not ODS**, but many have high GWP. Used in refrigeration, aerosols, foam blowing and some fire-suppression applications; “lubricant” is not the tested generic use.
- **Montreal Protocol:** controls/phases out ODS. **Kigali Amendment:** phases down HFCs because of climate warming, not because they deplete ozone.

#### C. Remediation & Waste Management

##### 1. Bioremediation

- Use of microorganisms/biological processes to transform, detoxify or remove contaminants; often accelerates natural biodegradation.
- Best suited to **biodegradable organic pollutants**—petroleum hydrocarbons, some solvents/organics, sewage constituents.
- **In situ:** treat contaminated material in place—biostimulation (nutrients/O₂), bioaugmentation (selected microbes), bioventing/biosparging variants.
- **Ex situ:** excavate/pump and treat elsewhere—landfarming, composting, engineered bioreactors.
- **Genetic engineering can design/optimise microbes** for specific degradation pathways.
- **Heavy-metal trap:** microbes cannot “biodegrade” Cd/Pb/Hg/As atoms. They may alter oxidation state, precipitate, immobilise, methylate/demethylate, biosorb or bioaccumulate them; removal still requires containment/separation.
- **Oilzapper:** hydrocarbon-degrading microbial consortium/bioremediation approach for oily sludge and oil spills; exam anchor = eco-friendly remediation, not oil exploration/fire control.

##### 2. Phytoremediation

- Plants + rhizosphere used to manage contaminants in soil/water.
- **Phytoextraction:** take up contaminants into harvestable biomass.
- **Phytostabilisation:** immobilise contaminants in soil/root zone; reduces mobility/erosion.
- **Rhizofiltration:** roots absorb/adsorb contaminants from water.
- **Phytodegradation/rhizodegradation:** plant enzymes/root-associated microbes break down organics.
- Wetlands/riparian vegetation can trap sediment/nutrients and reduce runoff; contaminated plant biomass may itself need safe disposal.

##### 3. Biofilters / MBR / MFC / activated carbon

| Technology | Core process | PYQ elimination point |
|---|---|---|
| **Biofilter** | microbes grow as biofilm on porous medium; contaminants contact biofilm | in aquaculture, helps treat organic waste and **nitrifies NH₃/NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻**; does not “increase phosphorus as fish nutrient” |
| **Membrane bioreactor (MBR)** | biological activated-sludge treatment + membrane solid/liquid separation | **wastewater treatment**; high-quality effluent, small footprint; membrane fouling/energy are constraints |
| **Microbial fuel cell (MFC)** | electroactive microbes oxidise substrates and transfer electrons to electrode | can treat wastewater while producing electricity; living organisms act as catalysts |
| **Activated carbon** | high-surface-area **adsorption** | removes many organic pollutants/odours and can adsorb metals depending chemistry; can be produced from carbon-rich wastes; **adsorption ≠ biodegradation** |

##### 4. Wastewater-treatment ladder

- **Preliminary:** screens/grit removal → large solids/sand.
- **Primary:** sedimentation/physical separation → suspended solids + part of BOD removed.
- **Secondary:** biological oxidation (activated sludge/trickling filters/bioreactors) → biodegradable organics/BOD reduction; nitrification may occur.
- **Tertiary/advanced:** nutrient removal, filtration/membranes, activated carbon/advanced oxidation as needed, disinfection → fit-for-purpose reuse/discharge.
- **Anaerobic digestion:** stabilises organic sludge/wet waste → biogas (CH₄-rich) + digestate; not the same as incineration/pyrolysis.

##### 5. Waste hierarchy / segregation

- Preferred sequence: **prevent/refuse → reduce → reuse/repair → recycle/compost → recover energy → dispose**.
- Source segregation is foundational: wet biodegradable fraction is better suited to composting/biomethanation; recyclable dry fraction to material recovery; high-calorific non-recyclable fraction may feed RDF/WtE; hazardous fractions need separate treatment.
- **Assimilative capacity:** sustainable waste generation/discharge must remain within environment’s capacity to absorb/process it without unacceptable degradation.

##### 6. Thermal / waste-to-energy technologies

| Process | Oxygen condition | Main products / use | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Incineration** | excess O₂; combustion | heat/electricity + flue gas + bottom/fly ash | reduces volume, **not pollution/GHG-free**; needs flue-gas control + ash disposal |
| **Pyrolysis** | no/very little O₂ | char + oils/tars + combustible gas | thermal decomposition, not ordinary combustion |
| **Gasification** | limited O₂/steam | syngas rich in CO/H₂ | partial oxidation; gas cleaning required |
| **Plasma gasification** | extremely high-temperature plasma | syngas + vitrified/inert-like slag | high energy/capital; not simply incineration |
| **Biomethanation / anaerobic digestion** | biological, oxygen-free | biogas + digestate | best for segregated wet organic waste |

- WtE suitability depends strongly on **segregation, moisture, inert fraction and calorific value**. Mixed wet Indian municipal waste is a poor combustion feed unless pre-processed.
- **PET:** recyclable into other products; incineration does not eliminate greenhouse-gas emissions.

##### 7. Industrial by-product reuse

| By-product | Useful applications | Critical caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly ash** | bricks/blocks; partial cement/concrete replacement; roads/embankments; mine filling/soil stabilisation under controls | trace metals + fine-dust/leaching risk; composition is **not only SiO₂ + CaO** |
| **Steel slag** | road base/aggregate; cement/clinker; selected soil amendment | alkaline/metals; suitability/leaching must be assessed |
| **Copper slag** | road/construction aggregate, abrasive/substitute material in suitable applications | can leach heavy metals; reuse requires characterisation/standards |
| **Biochar** | soil amendment, water retention, carbon storage | feedstock/process quality and contaminants determine benefit |

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **AQI ≠ GHG inventory:** CO₂/CH₄ matter for climate but are not in India’s 8-pollutant AQI basket.
- **Tropospheric O₃ vs stratospheric O₃:** same molecule, opposite environmental role—pollutant/GHG below; UV shield above.
- **NOₓ vs N₂O:** NO + NO₂ drive smog/acid rain; N₂O is primarily a long-lived GHG/stratospheric-ozone concern.
- **ODS vs high-GWP refrigerant:** CFC/HCFC/halons can deplete ozone; HFCs generally do **not**, though many strongly warm climate.
- **SRM vs CDR:** sulfate aerosols/cirrus thinning alter radiation; DAC/enhanced weathering remove CO₂.
- **CCS vs CDR:** fossil point-source CCS avoids an emission; it is not automatically removal of atmospheric CO₂.
- **Carbon price vs SCC:** market/tax price is policy/market outcome; SCC estimates marginal damage.
- **Bioremediation vs metals:** microorganisms can transform/immobilise metals, not biodegrade elemental atoms away.
- **Adsorption vs biodegradation:** activated carbon traps pollutants on surfaces; it does not biologically destroy them.
- **BOD vs COD vs DO:** BOD/COD are oxygen-demand indicators; DO is oxygen actually present.
- **Bioaccumulation vs biomagnification:** within organism/over time vs increase across trophic levels.
- **Fly ash/slag = resource + hazard:** “reuse” does not imply chemically harmless; leaching/dust remain design constraints.
