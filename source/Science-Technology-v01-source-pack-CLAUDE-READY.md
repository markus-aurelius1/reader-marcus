### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Physics & Everyday Science

##### A1. Measurement, SI & quick formula layer

**Seven SI base quantities/units**

| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| length | metre | m |
| mass | kilogram | kg |
| time | second | s |
| electric current | ampere | A |
| thermodynamic temperature | kelvin | K |
| amount of substance | mole | mol |
| luminous intensity | candela | cd |

- Current SI is defined through fixed numerical values of seven defining constants; 2019 revision changed the conceptual definitions of **kg, A, K, mol**.
- High-yield derived units:
  - force → newton `N = kg·m·s⁻²`
  - pressure → pascal `Pa = N/m²`
  - work/energy → joule `J = N·m`
  - power → watt `W = J/s`
  - frequency → hertz `Hz = s⁻¹`
  - electric charge → coulomb `C = A·s`
  - potential → volt `V = J/C`
  - resistance → ohm `Ω = V/A`
- `F = (9/5)C + 32`; `C = (5/9)(F−32)`.
- Electric potential = **scalar**; electric field = vector.
- Fuse → series safety device; heating effect of current; wire/alloy needs **low melting point** and suitable/high resistivity so excess current melts it.

##### A2. Pressure, heat, phase & everyday mechanisms
- Pressure `P = F/A`.
- Liquid pressure increases with depth; Pascal principle → pressure transmitted through confined fluid.
- **Pressure cooker:** higher pressure → higher boiling point → faster cooking. Regulated pressure depends mainly on **vent opening + load/weight**, not flame temperature.
- Evaporation causes cooling: highest-energy molecules escape; faster with high temperature, larger area, moving dry air; **slower at high humidity**.
- Water:
  - expands on freezing; ice less dense than liquid water;
  - maximum density near **4°C** → lakes freeze from top.
- Thermos/vacuum flask:
  - vacuum suppresses conduction/convection;
  - reflective/silvered walls reduce radiation;
  - stopper limits conduction/convection.
- Friction:
  - rolling friction generally < sliding friction;
  - ball bearings replace sliding with rolling.
- Capillarity:
  - surface tension + adhesion/cohesion + contact angle;
  - narrower tube → larger capillary rise (other factors fixed);
  - kerosene-lamp wick/blotting paper = capillary action;
  - drinking through straw = pressure difference, **not capillarity**;
  - xylem ascent also involves cohesion–tension/transpiration, not capillarity alone.

##### A3. Waves, sound & optics
- Sound = mechanical wave → requires material medium; no propagation in vacuum.
- Typical speed ordering: **solid > liquid > gas**.
- In gases, speed depends strongly on temperature/composition; higher air temperature generally → higher sound speed.
- Decibel = logarithmic sound-level unit; `UPPCS-2018-Q072` reference answer: rustling leaves ≈ **20 dB**.
- Light can propagate through vacuum.
- Refraction: bending due to change in speed across media; frequency unchanged at interface, wavelength/speed change.
- Total internal reflection: denser → rarer medium, incidence angle > critical angle; key principle of optical fibre.
- Rainbow: **refraction + dispersion + internal reflection** in water droplets.
- Twinkling of stars: atmospheric refraction through changing-density layers.
- Red/orange sunrise/sunset: preferential scattering of shorter wavelengths leaves longer wavelengths dominant along long atmospheric path.

##### A4. Electromagnetic spectrum
**Increasing frequency/energy; decreasing wavelength:**

`Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → Ultraviolet → X-ray → Gamma`

- `c = fλ`; photon energy `E = hf`.
- Gamma has highest frequency/energy among common listed bands.
- Visible light ≈ **380–700 nm** (convention varies slightly).
- UV/X/gamma carry progressively greater ionising capability; not all UV is equally ionising.
- Semiconductor examples: **Si, Ge**; conductivity between conductors/insulators and controllable by doping/conditions.

##### A5. Fundamental forces + particle basics

| Interaction | Relative idea | Acts on / role | Standard Model? | Carrier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| strong | strongest at subatomic scale | binds quarks; residual force binds nucleons | yes | gluon |
| electromagnetic | long range | electrically charged particles | yes | photon |
| weak | very short range | beta decay / flavour change | yes | W±, Z |
| gravity | weakest microscopically, long range | mass-energy | **not incorporated in Standard Model** | hypothetical graviton |

- Matter fermions:
  - quarks: `up, down, charm, strange, top, bottom`;
  - leptons: `e, μ, τ` + three neutrinos.
- Neutrinos: electrically neutral; very small non-zero mass; weakly interacting.
- **Higgs boson:** excitation of the Higgs field; central to Brout–Englert–Higgs mass-generation mechanism. **Not a gauge force-carrier** like photon/gluon/W/Z.
- Antimatter:
  - corresponding antiparticle has same mass, opposite electric charge where applicable and opposite relevant quantum numbers;
  - matter–antimatter annihilation converts mass to energy/other particles;
  - antihelium searches help test cosmic antimatter/evolution questions.
- IceCube:
  - cubic-kilometre-scale neutrino detector embedded deep in Antarctic ice;
  - detects Cherenkov light from secondary charged particles;
  - astrophysical-neutrino astronomy + indirect dark-matter searches.

##### A6. Gravity & orbit
- Satellite in orbit is in **continuous free fall**.
- Gravity supplies centripetal acceleration; sufficiently large tangential velocity makes the body keep “falling around” Earth.
- Circular-orbit speed `v = √(GM/r)`; decreases with orbital radius.
- Escape velocity `vₑ = √(2GM/R)` for ideal non-rotating body; independent of projectile mass.

##### A7. Sensors
- Sensor = transducer detecting physical/chemical quantity and producing usable signal.
- **Accelerometer:** acceleration/tilt/free fall → airbags, device orientation, laptop fall detection.
- **Gyroscope:** angular velocity/orientation stability.
- **Proximity:** presence/distance without direct contact.
- **Image sensor:** light → electrical/digital signal.
- **Gas sensor:** gas concentration/composition response.
- **Radar:** emits radio/microwave signal and analyses return:
  - range from time delay;
  - radial velocity via Doppler shift;
  - uses: weather, air/sea navigation, speed measurement, collision avoidance, remote sensing.

#### B. Biology: Cells, Microbes, Plants & Evolution

##### B1. Cell basics
- Cell = basic structural + functional unit of cellular life.
- Classical cell theory:
  - Schleiden + Schwann → organisms composed of cells/cell products;
  - Virchow → cells arise from pre-existing cells.
- **Cellular organisms use DNA as hereditary genomic material.** RNA genomes are characteristic of many viruses; do not generalise “cell genome may be DNA or RNA”.
- Plasma membrane → phospholipid bilayer + proteins; selectively permeable.
- Transport:
  - diffusion → down concentration gradient;
  - osmosis → water movement across selectively permeable membrane;
  - active transport → against electrochemical gradient using energy.

##### B2. Prokaryote vs eukaryote

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| nucleus | no membrane-bound nucleus; nucleoid | membrane-bound nucleus |
| membrane-bound organelles | absent | present |
| chromosome | usually one main circular chromosome | multiple linear chromosomes |
| ribosomes | smaller (70S) | cytosolic 80S; mitochondria/chloroplast retain bacterial-type ribosomes |
| division | binary fission | mitosis/meiosis |
| examples | bacteria, archaea | protists, fungi, plants, animals |

- Bacterial wall usually **peptidoglycan**; **Mycoplasma lacks cell wall**.
- Plant wall → mainly cellulose; fungal wall → mainly chitin.
- All cells possess plasma membrane; cell wall is **not universal**.

##### B3. Organelles — exam-safe function map

| Structure | Function / trap |
|---|---|
| nucleus | DNA/chromosomes; transcriptional control; nuclear envelope in eukaryotes |
| nucleolus | rRNA synthesis + ribosome assembly |
| mitochondrion | aerobic respiration/ATP; double membrane; own circular DNA |
| chloroplast | photosynthesis; chlorophyll; own circular DNA |
| ribosome | protein synthesis; non-membranous |
| rough ER | secretory/membrane protein synthesis |
| smooth ER | lipid synthesis, detoxification, Ca²⁺ storage roles |
| Golgi | modifies/sorts/packages proteins/lipids |
| lysosome | intracellular degradation |
| peroxisome | oxidative metabolism; H₂O₂ handling |
| vacuole | storage/osmoregulation; large central vacuole typical plant cell |
| centrosome/centrioles | microtubule organisation; prominent in animal cells |

**Endosymbiotic logic:** mitochondria/chloroplasts retain bacterial-like traits → double membranes, circular DNA, own division/ribosomes; best treated as evidence for prokaryotic ancestry.

##### B4. Plant vs animal cell
- Plant: cellulose wall, plastids/chloroplasts, large central vacuole, plasmodesmata.
- Animal: no cell wall/chloroplast; centrosome/centrioles typical; smaller vacuoles.
- Both: plasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus (in typical eukaryotic cells), mitochondria, ER, Golgi, ribosomes.

##### B5. Microorganisms & viruses
- Bacteria → prokaryotic.
- Fungi → eukaryotic; yeasts often unicellular, moulds filamentous.
- Cyanobacteria → photosynthetic **prokaryotes**; some fix nitrogen.
- Virus:
  - acellular infectious agent;
  - nucleic acid genome = **DNA or RNA** for a given virus;
  - protein capsid; some enveloped;
  - obligate intracellular replication → cannot independently reproduce on ordinary cell-free synthetic media.
- **Bacteriophage = virus infecting bacteria** → direct counter to “viruses cannot infect bacteria”.
- Viruses can infect animals, plants, fungi and microbes.
- Viroid → small infectious RNA, no protein coat; chiefly plant pathogens.
- Prion → infectious misfolded protein; no nucleic acid genome.
- Lichen → fungal partner + photosynthetic partner (green alga and/or cyanobacterium); sensitive to certain air pollutants → bioindicator.
- Mycorrhiza → fungus + plant root mutualism; improves nutrient/water acquisition.
- Rhizobium–legume nodules → symbiotic biological N fixation.
- Azolla–cyanobacterial association → paddy biofertilizer use.

##### B6. Plant physiology
**Photosynthesis**
- Overall concept: light energy → chemical energy stored in organic molecules.
- Inputs: CO₂ + water; light/chlorophyll; oxygen released derives from **water splitting**.
- Chloroplast:
  - thylakoid/grana → light reactions;
  - stroma → carbon-fixation reactions.
- Avoid rigid “X% of global photosynthesis” figures unless directly required.

**Transport**
- Xylem → water + mineral transport, predominantly root → shoot.
- Phloem → translocation of sugars/organic solutes from source to sink; direction depends on source–sink relation.
- Girdling/ring-barking removes phloem while xylem may remain initially functional → roots lose carbohydrate supply.
- Transpiration → water loss, mainly through stomata; creates tension supporting ascent of sap.

**Plant hormones — UPPCS+**
- auxin → cell elongation/apical dominance/tropisms/rooting.
- gibberellin → stem elongation/seed germination.
- cytokinin → cell division/delays senescence.
- abscisic acid (ABA) → stress response/stomatal closure/seed dormancy.
- ethylene → fruit ripening + senescence/abscission roles.

**Carnivorous plants**
- Nepenthes/pitcher plant photosynthesises normally.
- Insects mainly supplement mineral nutrition, especially nitrogen, in nutrient-poor habitats; carnivory ≠ heterotrophic replacement of photosynthesis.

**Plant-disease spread**
- vectors/routes can include infected seed/plant material, insects, wind, rain splash, soil/water, tools/humans, birds/animals.
- Absolute “plant diseases spread only through X” statements are unsafe.

##### B7. Classification & evolution
- Taxonomic hierarchy: **Kingdom → Phylum/Division → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**.
- Binomial nomenclature: `Genus species`; genus capitalised, species lowercase; italicised in print.
- Whittaker five-kingdom model (historical exam framework): **Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia**.
- Treat five-kingdom scheme as a historical classification model, not the final word of modern phylogenomics.

**Evolution**
- Natural selection acts on **heritable variation**; populations evolve across generations.
- Variation sources include mutation and recombination.
- Homologous structures → shared ancestry/divergent evolution signal.
- Analogous structures → similar function without close structural ancestry; convergent evolution.
- Fossils + comparative anatomy + molecular evidence support evolutionary relationships.
- Denisovans → archaic human lineage known from genetic/fossil evidence; not dinosaurs/geological period.

#### C. Chemistry & Materials in Daily Life

##### C1. Matter / change / solution basics
- **Physical change:** no new substance; composition unchanged though state/shape may change.
- **Chemical change:** new substance(s) with new composition/properties.
- Examples:
  - melting ice → physical;
  - crystallising NaCl from solution → physical;
  - souring milk → chemical.
- Element = one atomic-number type.
- Compound = elements chemically combined in fixed stoichiometric relation.
- Mixture = physical combination; composition variable.
- Homogeneous mixture = uniform composition → solution.
- Heterogeneous mixture = non-uniform phases/components.
- Sublimation: solid ↔ gas without liquid intermediate under relevant conditions; camphor/naphthalene/dry ice are common exam examples.

##### C2. Acids, bases, salts
At ~25°C:
- acid → pH `<7`; increases hydronium/H⁺ activity in water; blue litmus → red.
- neutral → pH `7`.
- base → pH `>7`; red litmus → blue.
- pH scale is logarithmic; one unit = tenfold change in H⁺ activity approximately in dilute aqueous systems.

**Common-name / formula quick table**

| Common name | Formula / identity | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| baking soda | `NaHCO₃` | sodium hydrogen carbonate; baking/antacid/fire-extinguisher uses |
| washing soda | `Na₂CO₃·10H₂O` | hydrated sodium carbonate |
| quicklime | `CaO` | calcium oxide |
| slaked lime | `Ca(OH)₂` | calcium hydroxide |
| limestone/chalk/marble | `CaCO₃` | calcium carbonate forms |
| gypsum | `CaSO₄·2H₂O` | hydrated calcium sulphate |
| plaster of Paris | `CaSO₄·½H₂O` | partial dehydration product of gypsum |
| blue vitriol | `CuSO₄·5H₂O` | copper sulphate pentahydrate |
| common salt | `NaCl` | sodium chloride |
| vinegar acid | acetic/ethanoic acid | weak organic acid |
| laughing gas | `N₂O` | nitrous oxide |
| dry ice | `CO₂(s)` | solid carbon dioxide |

##### C3. Water chemistry & purification
- Water molecule is **polar/dipolar** → excellent solvent for many ionic/polar substances.
- Hardness mainly due Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ salts:
  - temporary → bicarbonates; can be reduced by boiling;
  - permanent → e.g. sulphates/chlorides; requires ion exchange/other treatment.
- Purification mechanism map:

| Method | Mainly does | Does **not** automatically do |
|---|---|---|
| sedimentation/coagulation | remove suspended matter/flocs | reliably remove dissolved salts/pathogens alone |
| filtration | suspended particles, depending filter scale | all dissolved ions |
| activated carbon | adsorb organics, odour/taste compounds, many contaminants | sterilise everything / desalinate |
| chlorination/ozonation | disinfection/oxidation | remove all dissolved salts |
| UV | **inactivate microorganisms** | remove turbidity, salts or all odours by itself |
| reverse osmosis | pressure-driven membrane removal of many dissolved ions/solutes | inherently provide residual disinfectant |

- Adsorption ≠ absorption: adsorption = accumulation on surface; activated carbon works mainly through adsorption and very high surface area.

##### C4. Electrochemistry / batteries — durable minimum
- Electrochemical cell converts chemical ↔ electrical energy through redox reactions.
- During **battery discharge**:
  - anode = oxidation;
  - cathode = reduction;
  - electrons flow external circuit **anode → cathode**.
- Electrolyte conducts ions; separator prevents direct electrode contact while allowing ionic transport.
- Primary cells → intended single-use; secondary cells → rechargeable.
- Lead-acid: Pb/PbO₂/H₂SO₄ system; rechargeable.
- Li-ion: Li⁺ shuttles between host materials; graphite commonly anode; cathode chemistry varies.
- Route EV cathode-mineral/supply-chain/current battery-tech questions to **Energy Technologies**; keep only redox architecture here.

##### C5. Polymers & plastics
- Polymer = macromolecule made from repeating monomer-derived units.
- Polymerisation:
  - addition/chain-growth examples → polyethylene, PVC;
  - condensation/step-growth examples → nylon/polyester families.
- Natural polymers: proteins (amino-acid polymers), cellulose/starch/glycogen (glucose-derived polysaccharides), nucleic acids (nucleotide polymers), natural rubber (polyisoprene).

| Type | Heating behaviour | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| thermoplastic | softens/remoulds on heating | PE, PP, PVC, PET (thermoplastic polyester) |
| thermoset | cross-linked; does not remelt cleanly | bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde, many epoxies |

**PET**
- polyester used in bottles/fibres.
- fibres can blend with cotton/wool.
- bottles can be recycled into other products.
- Incineration still produces CO₂/other emissions → “no GHG on burning” is false.

**BPA**
- associated especially with polycarbonate plastics/epoxy-resin chemistry.

**PFAS**
- highly persistent fluorinated substances used in many consumer/industrial applications.
- persistence + mobility/exposure + possible bioaccumulation make absolute “easily degraded” claims false.
- Regulatory/current-health details remain dynamic.

**Hidden-plastic trap**
- plastics/polymers occur in filters, lenses, tyres/composites; “looks non-plastic” ≠ plastic-free.

##### C6. Explosives
- Explosive → material that can undergo very rapid exothermic reaction/decomposition, producing gases/heat/pressure.
- High explosive: detonation (supersonic reaction front/shock).
- Low explosive: deflagration (subsonic flame front).
- **CL-20, HMX, LLM-105** → high-energy/insensitive energetic-explosive family context; not refrigerants.
- TNT/dynamite/nitroglycerine associations are useful UPPCS recall; do not confuse explosive chemical with propellant/fuel simply because both release energy rapidly.

##### C7. Cloud seeding / artificial rain
- Objective: alter cloud microphysics to encourage precipitation where suitable clouds already exist.
- Common agents: **silver iodide**, dry ice; hygroscopic salts in some warm-cloud methods.
- Silver iodide acts as an ice-nucleating material due crystal-structure similarity to ice.
- `UPSC-2025-Q085`: canonical pair = **silver iodide + potassium iodide**.
- Cloud seeding cannot manufacture rain from cloudless/dry atmosphere; suitable moisture/cloud conditions remain necessary.
- Climate-geoengineering proposals such as stratospheric sulphate injection/cirrus thinning are **not the same thing** as ordinary cloud seeding.

### 5A.1 Pathogens and transmission — minimum map

| Agent | Core property | Typical transmission logic | High-yield examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virus | Acellular; obligate intracellular replication | droplets/airborne, faeco-oral, blood/body fluids, vectors, direct contact | influenza, measles, polio, HIV, hepatitis viruses, dengue, Zika |

#### Transmission traps
- **Vector-borne ≠ contagious person-to-person.**
- **Aedes** → dengue, Zika, chikungunya, yellow fever.
- **Anopheles (female)** → malaria.
- **Culex** → Japanese encephalitis; important vector for lymphatic filariasis in India.
- **Tsetse fly** → African sleeping sickness → **not mosquito-borne**.
- HIV/Hepatitis B → blood/body fluids/sexual/vertical routes; not casual touch.
- Tattooing/unsafe needles → blood-borne infection risk.
- TB → airborne droplet nuclei/aerosols; not “vector-borne”.
- Tetanus → wound contamination; no person-to-person spread in normal epidemiology.

| Hepatitis C | HCV, RNA virus | mainly blood | **no vaccine** |
| Influenza | influenza viruses, RNA | droplets/aerosols | seasonal vaccines exist |
| H1N1 influenza | influenza A subtype | respiratory | “swine flu” association |
| Dengue | dengue virus | **Aedes** | vector control central |
| Zika | Zika virus | **Aedes**; sexual/vertical also possible | same broad mosquito genus as dengue |
| Malaria | *Plasmodium* protozoa | female **Anopheles** | vector ≠ Aedes/Culex |
| Filariasis | filarial worms | mosquitoes; Culex important in India | lymphatic disease |
| Diphtheria | *Corynebacterium diphtheriae* | droplets/contact | **toxoid** vaccine |
| Tetanus | *Clostridium tetani* | contaminated wounds | **toxoid** vaccine |
| TB | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* | airborne | BCG protects particularly against severe childhood forms |
| Hib disease | *Haemophilus influenzae* type b | respiratory droplets | can cause meningitis/pneumonia/sepsis; Hib conjugate vaccine |
| Pneumococcal disease | *Streptococcus pneumoniae* | respiratory | PCV protects against invasive disease |
| Smallpox | variola virus | historically respiratory/contact | **eradicated globally** |

#### Eradication vs elimination
- **Eradication:** permanent worldwide reduction to zero natural incidence → routine control measures no longer needed.
- **Elimination:** zero/reduced transmission in a defined geography → continued surveillance/control required.
- **Smallpox:** eradicated globally.
- Do not equate “disease absent in one country” with global eradication.

### 5A.3 Immunity

#### Innate immunity
- Present from birth; **non-specific**; rapid.
- Barriers: skin/mucosa, physiological/chemical barriers, phagocytic/cellular mechanisms, cytokine/interferon responses.
- No antigen-specific long-term memory comparable to adaptive immunity.

#### Adaptive/acquired immunity
- **Antigen-specific + memory**.
- **B lymphocytes** → differentiate into plasma cells → antibodies; memory B cells.
- **T lymphocytes**
  - helper T cells coordinate immune responses;
  - cytotoxic T cells kill infected/abnormal cells;
  - memory T cells support faster secondary response.
- **B/T cells belong primarily to adaptive immunity**, not innate-barrier classification.

#### Active vs passive immunity

| Feature | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| What is received | antigen / vaccine / infection | ready-made antibodies |
| Host makes antibodies? | yes | no |
| Memory | usually yes | no |
| Onset | slower | immediate/rapid |
| Duration | longer | shorter |
| Examples | vaccines | rabies Ig, tetanus Ig, antivenom, therapeutic monoclonal antibodies |

### 5A.4 Vaccine platforms

| Platform | What is delivered | Key property / trap |
|---|---|---|
| Live attenuated | weakened replicating pathogen | strong immune response; not “killed” |
| Inactivated | killed/non-replicating pathogen | cannot replicate; often needs multiple doses/boosters |
| Subunit | selected antigen(s) | no whole pathogen |
| Toxoid | inactivated bacterial toxin | diphtheria, tetanus |
| Polysaccharide | purified capsular polysaccharide | weaker response in young children for some antigens |
| Conjugate | weak polysaccharide antigen linked to carrier protein | stronger T-dependent response + memory; useful in infants |
| Recombinant-vector | harmless/attenuated viral or bacterial vector carries gene encoding antigen | genetic engineering; vector delivers genetic instructions |
| mRNA | mRNA encoding antigen | translated in cytoplasm; does **not** need to enter nucleus |
| DNA | plasmid DNA encoding antigen | DNA must reach nucleus for transcription; antigen made by host cells |

#### Platform elimination anchors
- Covishield/Sputnik-type logic → vector platform.
- Moderna/Pfizer-type logic → mRNA platform.
- Sinovac-type logic → inactivated/whole-virus platform.
- Brand associations are examples only; **platform mechanism is permanent content**.

### 5A.5 Monoclonal antibodies
- Population of antibodies with the **same specificity**, conventionally derived from a single B-cell clone / engineered equivalent.
- Bind a specific antigen/epitope.
- Uses: diagnostics, neutralisation, receptor/ligand blockade, targeted therapy, immune modulation.
- **Passive immunotherapy ≠ vaccination**: ready-made binding protein is supplied; recipient need not generate durable immune memory.
- “Man-made protein” is broadly acceptable in exam framing, but therapeutic mAbs are biologically engineered immunoglobulins.

### 5A.6 Wolbachia
- *Wolbachia* = intracellular bacterium naturally present in many insects.
- In **Aedes** mosquitoes, selected Wolbachia strains can reduce the mosquito’s ability to transmit dengue/Zika/chikungunya viruses.
- Public-health method may release Wolbachia-carrying mosquitoes to spread the trait through local populations.
- Trap: technique targets **vector competence**, not the human virus directly.

### 5A.7 Antimicrobial resistance (AMR)

#### Definition
- Microorganism survives/grows despite an antimicrobial that would normally inhibit/kill it.
- **Antibiotic resistance** is one subset of AMR; antimicrobials also include antivirals, antifungals, antiparasitics.

#### How resistance emerges/spreads
- spontaneous mutation;
- horizontal transfer of resistance genes;
- antimicrobial exposure creates **selection pressure**;
- resistant organisms survive/reproduce;
- transmission spreads resistant strains.

#### Major drivers
- unnecessary/incorrect antimicrobial use;
- wrong drug/dose/duration;
- self-medication/non-prescription access;
- routine or inappropriate use in livestock/agriculture;
- poor infection prevention/sanitation;
- weak diagnostics → empirical overuse;
- antimicrobial residues/waste in environment.

#### Biofilms
- Microbial communities embedded in self-produced extracellular matrix.
- Form on **medical implants** and **food-processing surfaces**.
- Can impede drug penetration + harbour slow-growing/persistent cells + facilitate gene exchange → increased tolerance/resistance.
- Biofilm ≠ single free-floating bacterial cell.

#### Control logic
- stewardship: right drug + dose + duration + indication;
- diagnostics;
- vaccination/infection prevention;
- hygiene/sanitation;
- surveillance;
- One Health: human + animal + environmental sectors.

### 5A.8 Microbiome / probiotics / prebiotics
- **Microbiome:** community of microorganisms + their collective genetic material/ecological interactions in a habitat.
- **Probiotic:** live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit.
  - commonly bacteria (*Lactobacillus*, *Bifidobacterium*) and some yeasts (*Saccharomyces*);
  - probiotic organisms need not be permanent native residents of the gut.
- **Prebiotic:** selectively utilised substrate, commonly non-digestible dietary fibre, that benefits host microorganisms.
- **Synbiotic:** probiotic + prebiotic combination.
- Trap: probiotic ≠ antibiotic; prebiotic ≠ live microorganism.

### 5A.9 Food-safety toxin
- **Aflatoxins** → toxins produced mainly by *Aspergillus* moulds in improperly stored grains/oilseeds/nuts.
- Heat-stable enough that normal cooking does not reliably destroy them.
- Important chronic risk: hepatotoxicity/carcinogenicity.
- Trap: toxin may be **microbial product** without the mould itself remaining viable.

### 5A.10 Nutrition — compact permanent recall

#### Vitamins

| Vitamin | Main anchor | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|
| A (retinol/retinoids) | vision, epithelial integrity | night blindness/xerophthalmia |
| B1 (thiamine) | carbohydrate metabolism | **beriberi** |
| B2 (riboflavin) | redox coenzymes | cheilosis/angular stomatitis |
| B3 (niacin) | NAD/NADP | pellagra |
| B9 (folate) | one-carbon transfer/DNA synthesis | megaloblastic anaemia; neural-tube-risk link |
| B12 (cobalamin) | DNA synthesis + nervous system | megaloblastic anaemia/neurologic signs |
| C (ascorbate) | collagen synthesis, antioxidant | **scurvy** |
| D | Ca/P homeostasis | **rickets** in children; osteomalacia in adults |
| E | antioxidant | deficiency rare; neurologic/haemolytic effects possible |
| K | clotting-factor activation | bleeding tendency |

#### UPPCS source/association anchors
- Vitamin C-rich → amla/citrus.
- Vitamin D → sunlight + dietary sources such as fatty fish/cod-liver oil.
- B1 deficiency repeatedly linked to heavily polished-rice diets.
- Vitamin K → coagulation.
- Vitamin A/retinol → vision.

#### Antioxidants
- Limit oxidative damage by neutralising/reacting with oxidants/free radicals.
- Do not define health benefit by “activating vitamin-synthesis enzymes”.

#### Trans fats
- Industrial trans fats classically associated with **partially hydrogenated oils**.
- Increase cardiovascular risk profile; “trans-fat-free” does not mean “animal-fat-free”.

### 5A.11 Human physiology — UPPCS-ready minimum

#### Digestion
- Mouth: salivary amylase begins starch digestion.
- Stomach: HCl creates acidic medium; **pepsin** begins major protein digestion.
- Pancreas secretes digestive enzymes into small intestine:
  - trypsin/chymotrypsin → proteins/peptides;
  - pancreatic amylase → carbohydrate digestion;
  - lipase → fats.
- Small intestine = major digestion + nutrient absorption.

#### Pancreas
- **Exocrine:** digestive enzymes/bicarbonate via ducts.
- **Endocrine islets:**
  - beta cells → **insulin**;
  - alpha cells → glucagon.
- Diabetes mellitus commonly involves inadequate insulin production/action.

#### Blood
- Plasma + formed elements: RBCs, WBCs, platelets.
- Haemoglobin in RBCs → major oxygen carrier + red colour.
- Platelets → haemostasis/clot formation.
- Vitamin K → synthesis/activation of several clotting factors.
- ABO inheritance: A and B codominant; O recessive; basic blood-group genetics overlaps Genetics.

#### Heart / circulation
- Four chambers: RA, RV, LA, LV.
- Pulmonary artery → deoxygenated blood from RV to lungs.
- **Pulmonary veins → oxygenated blood from lungs to LA**.
- Left ventricle → systemic circulation.

#### Lungs
- **Alveoli** = primary gas-exchange surfaces.

#### Nervous system
- Medulla/brainstem → autonomic functions including heart-rate/respiratory control.
- Cerebellum → balance/coordination.
- Avoid statement “the brain controls X” as if one undifferentiated region performs every function.

#### Major endocrine glands — one-hop recall
- Pituitary → multiple tropic hormones; “master gland” shorthand.
- Thyroid → T3/T4; metabolism; calcitonin.
- Parathyroid → PTH; raises blood Ca²⁺.
- Adrenal cortex → cortisol/aldosterone; medulla → adrenaline/noradrenaline.
- Pancreatic islets → insulin/glucagon.
- Testes → testosterone; ovaries → oestrogen/progesterone.
- Posterior pituitary releases oxytocin/ADH synthesised in hypothalamus.

## B. Genetics & Biotechnology

### 5B.1 Genetic vocabulary

| Term | Minimum fact |
|---|---|
| DNA | hereditary information; double-stranded helix in typical cellular genomes; A,T,G,C |
| RNA | usually single-stranded; U replaces T; several functional types |
| Gene | functional DNA sequence whose product is RNA/protein; basic unit of heredity |
| Genome | complete genetic material of an organism/cell |
| Chromosome | DNA + associated proteins packaged as a discrete unit |
| Chromatin | DNA–protein complex; condenses into visible chromosomes during division |
| Allele | alternative form of a gene/locus |
| Genotype | genetic constitution |
| Phenotype | observable trait resulting from genotype + environment |
| Mutation | heritable/changeable alteration in genetic sequence |
| SNP | single-nucleotide variation at a genomic position |

#### Central dogma — exam-safe
- DNA —**transcription**→ RNA —**translation**→ protein.
- Reverse transcription exists in retroviruses: RNA → DNA.
- **mRNA** carries protein-coding information; do not say “all RNA directly codes for protein”.
- tRNA carries amino acids; rRNA is structural/catalytic part of ribosome.

#### Genetic code
- Codon = nucleotide triplet.
- 64 codons total.
- **61 sense codons encode 20 amino acids; 3 stop codons.**
- Degenerate: multiple codons can specify same amino acid.
- Nearly universal, with known exceptions (e.g. some mitochondrial codes).

### 5B.2 Mendelian inheritance — UPPCS minimum

#### Mendel
- **Law of segregation:** paired alleles separate during gamete formation.
- **Independent assortment:** alleles at different loci assort independently when effectively unlinked; linkage is the key exception.
- Dominant allele can mask recessive allele in heterozygote.

#### Beyond simple dominance
- **Codominance:** both alleles expressed → ABO: \(I^A\) and \(I^B\).
- **Multiple alleles:** ABO has \(I^A, I^B, i\).
- **Incomplete dominance:** heterozygote intermediate phenotype.
- **Sex-linked inheritance:** genes on sex chromosomes show characteristic inheritance patterns.
- Mitochondrial inheritance: **typically maternal**.

#### Chromosomal disorders
- Down syndrome → trisomy 21.
- Turner syndrome → 45,X.
- Klinefelter syndrome → 47,XXY.
- Numerical chromosome abnormalities often arise from nondisjunction.

### 5B.3 Recombinant DNA technology

#### Core tools
- **Restriction endonuclease** → sequence-specific DNA cutting; many produce sticky/blunt ends.
- **DNA ligase** → joins DNA fragments.
- **Vector** → carries foreign DNA; plasmids are common.
- **Plasmid** → small, usually circular extrachromosomal DNA replicon in bacteria; can carry selectable markers.
- **Host cell** → replicates/expresses introduced DNA.
- **Selectable marker** → helps identify transformed cells.

#### Minimal workflow
1. identify/isolate gene/DNA;
2. cut source DNA + vector;
3. separate/prepare required fragment;
4. ligate insert into vector;
5. introduce vector into host;
6. select transformed cells;
7. clone/express gene;
8. recover/purify product.

#### PCR
- Amplifies a selected DNA segment through cycles:
  - denaturation;
  - primer annealing;
  - extension by thermostable DNA polymerase.
- PCR **copies DNA**; it is not gene editing.
- RT-PCR can begin from RNA after reverse transcription to cDNA.

#### rDNA capability
- Gene transfer can cross species barriers.
- Classic products/applications: recombinant insulin, vaccine antigens, enzymes, GM organisms.
- “Transfer across species” ≠ whole organism becomes a clone of donor.

### 5B.4 GM / transgenic organisms
- **Transgenic/GMO:** genetic material deliberately altered using biotechnology.
- Durable crop objectives:
  - pest resistance;
  - herbicide tolerance;
  - abiotic-stress tolerance;
  - nutritional enhancement;
  - altered shelf life/quality.
- **Bt crops:** genes from *Bacillus thuringiensis* encode Cry proteins toxic to susceptible insect pests.
- Bollgard I/II = GM cotton technology.
- Golden Rice = beta-carotene/Vitamin-A precursor enrichment.
- Regulatory committees/approval status/current crop decisions → Environment/current layer, not permanent biotech core.

### 5B.5 RNA interference (RNAi)
- Post-transcriptional **gene silencing** mechanism.
- Double-stranded RNA is processed into small interfering RNAs.
- siRNA guides RISC to complementary mRNA → cleavage/repression → reduced protein production.
- Applications:
  - functional genomics;
  - gene-silencing therapeutics;
  - pest/pathogen-resistant crops.
- UPSC trap: RNAi is **not** a method for hormone-replacement therapy.

### 5B.6 CRISPR-Cas gene editing
- CRISPR = repeat-derived bacterial adaptive immune system; Cas = CRISPR-associated protein.
- CRISPR-Cas9 editing:
  1. guide RNA targets complementary DNA;
  2. Cas9 nuclease cuts target;
  3. cellular DNA repair produces deletion/insertion or template-directed change.
- Cas9 = **molecular scissors/nuclease**, not a biosensor/herbicide/gene itself.
- Applications: functional studies, crop improvement, somatic gene therapy, research models.
- **Germline editing** affects eggs/sperm/embryos and can be heritable → distinct ethical/regulatory category.

### 5B.7 Gene drive
- Genetic system biases inheritance so a chosen allele is transmitted to **more than the Mendelian ~50% expectation**.
- CRISPR-based drives can copy themselves onto the homologous chromosome.
- Potential: alter/vector populations; high ecological/containment concerns.
- Do not equate gene drive with ordinary CRISPR editing of one individual.

### 5B.8 Stem cells
- Core properties:
  - **self-renewal**;
  - differentiation into specialised cells.
- Types:
  - embryonic stem cells → pluripotent;
  - adult/somatic stem cells → usually multipotent;
  - induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSC) → reprogrammed somatic cells.
- Applications: disease models, drug screening, regenerative medicine.
- Trap: stem cells are **not confined to mammals** as a biological concept.

### 5B.9 Cloning / somatic cell nuclear transfer
- **Reproductive cloning:** create organism genetically near-identical in nuclear DNA to donor.
- SCNT:
  1. remove nucleus from oocyte;
  2. insert nucleus from somatic cell;
  3. activate reconstructed oocyte;
  4. develop embryo;
  5. implantation → reproductive cloning.
- Dolly = classic SCNT mammal; not produced by ordinary IVF.

### 5B.10 Mitochondrial replacement / “three-parent” technology
- Objective: reduce transmission of pathogenic mitochondrial DNA.
- Child receives:
  - nuclear DNA mainly from mother + father;
  - donor mitochondrial DNA from donor oocyte.
- **Pronuclear transfer (PNT):** performed **after fertilisation**, before embryo development proceeds.
- **Maternal spindle transfer (MST):** spindle/chromosomal material moved **before fertilisation**, then reconstructed oocyte is fertilised.
- Mitochondrial DNA inheritance is **normally maternal**; phrase with qualifier, not absolute “always”.
- Nuclear-gene defects causing mitochondrial disease are a separate category; MRT addresses mtDNA transmission most directly.

### 5B.11 Genomics / molecular tools

| Tool | What it measures/does | UPSC elimination anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Genome sequencing | determines DNA sequence across genome | markers, breeding, disease/host-pathogen analysis |
| Transcriptome | complete set of RNA transcripts expressed in cell/tissue/time/condition | not “editing enzymes” |
| DNA barcoding | identifies species using short standard DNA region + reference database | species identification; processed-food biological material |
| Microsatellites / STRs | short tandem repeats, highly polymorphic markers | population genetics, kinship, evolutionary relationships |
| Metagenomics | sequences genetic material directly from mixed environmental sample | avoids culturing every organism separately |
| Aerial metagenomics | environmental DNA sampling from air | not drone blood sampling |
| DNA fingerprinting | individual identification using variable DNA markers | individual ≠ species identification |

#### Common barcode loci — reference layer
- animals → mitochondrial **COI** commonly used;
- plants → **rbcL + matK** commonly used;
- fungi → **ITS** commonly used.
Use as high-yield associations; exact marker choice can vary by taxonomic group/project.

### 5B.12 Gene therapy / genetic medicine
- Broad aim: treat disease by adding, replacing, silencing or editing genetic material/function.
- **Somatic gene therapy** → target patient cells; changes not intended to be inherited.
- **Germline editing/therapy** → gamete/embryo; heritable; distinct ethical/regulatory status.
- **In vivo:** vector delivered directly into body.
- **Ex vivo:** cells modified outside body → returned to patient.
- Delivery systems:
  - engineered viral vectors;
  - lipid nanoparticles;
  - other non-viral carriers.
- Genetic medicine does **not** imply changing the entire genome.
- Gene therapy ≠ CRISPR only; CRISPR is one editing platform.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Computing & Digital Technologies

##### 5.1 Computer basics — UPPCS layer
- **Input:** keyboard, mouse, scanner, microphone, joystick, light pen.
- **Output:** monitor, printer, projector, plotter, speaker/headphones.
- **Touchscreen:** combined input + output.
- **RAM:** volatile; working memory.
- **ROM:** non-volatile; retains data without power.
- **Secondary storage:** HDD/SSD/optical/flash → non-volatile.
- **Blu-ray vs DVD:** Blu-ray → higher storage + HD support; do not memorize false “BD is twice as thick” claim.

##### 5.2 AI → ML → DL → LLM
- **AI:** broad field—machines perform tasks associated with human intelligence.
- **ML:** algorithms learn patterns from examples/data.
- **Deep Learning:** subset of ML using multi-layer neural networks.
- **Neural network:** input layer → hidden layers → output; weights updated during training.
- **Generative AI:** creates new content from learned data distributions.
- **LLM:** language model trained over large text/token corpora; predicts probable continuation/token sequence.
- **Training:** mathematical optimization minimizes prediction error/loss.
- **Bias:** no guarantee of unbiased output.
- **UPSC 2020 context:** AI accepted for industrial energy optimization, disease diagnosis, text-to-speech; not wireless transmission of electricity.
- **Trap:** capability questions are time-sensitive. Keep the **exam-year context** when using old AI PYQs.

##### 5.3 Cloud + SaaS
| Model | What user receives | User manages |
|---|---|---|
| **SaaS** | Complete application over internet | Mainly data/configuration/use |
| **PaaS** | Development/runtime platform | Application + data |
| **IaaS** | Virtual compute/storage/network | OS, apps, data |

- SaaS → on-demand, subscription/service-provider managed, cross-device access.
- UPSC 2022: UI customization + data-field changes + mobile access + webmail examples all accepted.
- Examples: webmail/productivity/collaboration tools.
- **Trap:** SaaS ≠ VPN. SaaS provides software; VPN provides a secure network path/access layer.

##### 5.4 Blockchain
- Distributed/replicated ledger/database across networked nodes.
- Block → data + cryptographic hash + reference/hash of previous block.
- Hash → fixed-length digest; small input change → different digest.
- Consensus → network agrees on valid state/transactions.
- **Not restricted to cryptocurrency.**
- Public/permissionless chains may allow applications without a central permission-giver.
- Smart contracts → code that self-executes when programmed conditions are met.
- Blockchain uses: provenance, certificates, supply chains, records, tokenized assets, digital identity.
- **UPSC 2026 key:** replicated copies + stakeholder visibility + strong anti-alteration/immutability property accepted.
- **Trap:** do not reduce “consortium blockchain” to a simplistic “blend of public and private blockchain”; UPSC 2026 rejected that formulation.

##### 5.5 Bitcoin / crypto basics
- Bitcoin → blockchain-based decentralized digital asset/payment system.
- Address-based peer transfer; no central bank tracks every Bitcoin transaction as claimed in 2016 PYQ.
- Public ledger does not necessarily reveal real-world identity directly → pseudonymous, not automatically fully anonymous.
- PoW: computational validation/mining; PoS: validator stake-based consensus.
- Crypto regulation/tax/status → dynamic Economy/Policy layer; not permanent tech core.

##### 5.6 Web3
- UPSC-owned properties: greater user control of data; blockchain-based networks possible; collective/decentralized operation.
- Typical stack: blockchain/distributed ledger + dApps + smart contracts + tokens/identity.
- **Do not force a universal “Web 1→2→3→4→5” taxonomy.**
- “Semantic Web” and “decentralized Web3” usages overlap historically but are not a single uncontested ladder; note writer should follow PYQ wording.

##### 5.7 NFTs + tokenization
- **NFT:** unique cryptographic token on blockchain.
- Can represent digital or physical-asset claims/metadata.
- **Non-fungible:** not interchangeable at equivalence like identical currency units.
- RWA tokenization → represent real-world asset/rights as blockchain-based digital tokens.
- UPSC 2026 treats 24×7 access/access expansion as valid properties.
- Omit speculative price/investment discussion from permanent core.

##### 5.8 Cyber security primitives
- **Malware:** malicious software umbrella.
- **Ransomware:** malware encrypts/blocks access and demands payment.
- **Exploit:** technique/code abusing a vulnerability; EternalBlue is an example tied to WannaCry.
- **Firewall:** filters network traffic using security rules; blocks unauthorized access.
- **Encryption:** transforms plaintext using cryptographic key(s).
- **Hash:** one-way digest for integrity checking.
- **Digital signature:** private-key signing + public-key verification → authenticity/integrity/non-repudiation.
- **Digital certificate:** trusted electronic credential binding identity to a public key.
- **PKI:** infrastructure of certificates, certificate authorities, keys, policies/processes supporting trusted public-key cryptography.
- **Trap:** digital signature ≠ digital certificate; UPSC 2019 tested this directly.

##### 5.9 IoT + wearables + edge
- IoT → physical/smart devices with sensors/actuators + connectivity + data exchange/automation.
- Typical flow: sense → communicate → process (cloud/edge) → act.
- M2M communication often central.
- Edge computing → process closer to data source → lower latency, less backhaul, faster local response.
- Wearables can perform location identification, sleep monitoring, hearing assistance.
- Avoid absolute claim “phones/PCs can never be IoT”; classify by system role/use.

##### 5.10 3D printing / additive manufacturing
- Digital 3D/CAD model → slicing → layer-by-layer material deposition/curing/fusion → object.
- **Additive** ≠ subtractive machining.
- Common materials: polymers, metals, ceramics, biomaterials.
- UPSC 2018 accepted applications across confectionery, bionic ears, automotive, reconstructive surgery and data-processing technologies.
- High-yield logic: complex geometry + customization + rapid prototyping + reduced material waste.

##### 5.11 Biometrics
- Physiological/behavioral identifiers used to recognize/verify persons.
- Fingerprint, iris, retina, voice all valid biometric modalities in UPSC framing.
- Facial recognition: capture → feature extraction/template → comparison/match.
- **Trap:** authentication factor may be biometric, but biometric template ≠ digital signature/certificate.

##### 5.12 AR / VR / metaverse
| Concept | Core distinction |
|---|---|
| **AR** | Real world remains visible; digital information overlays/enhances it. |
| **VR** | Immersive simulated environment replaces/blocks normal real-world view. |
| **Metaverse** | Persistent/interoperable network of 3D virtual spaces with identity/social/economic/digital-asset layer. |

- UPSC 2019: AR statements 1 false, 3 true; VR statements 2 false, 4 true.
- UPSC 2024: “interoperable network of 3D virtual worlds + virtual property rights” → metaverse.

##### 5.13 Quantum computing
- Classical computer → **bits**: 0 or 1.
- Quantum computer → **qubits**: quantum states can exist in superposition until measurement.
- **Superposition:** combination of basis states.
- **Entanglement:** correlated quantum states; useful in quantum information/communication.
- **Decoherence:** loss of usable quantum state due interaction/noise → major error source.
- Quantum advantage/supremacy → quantum system solves a selected problem beyond practical classical capability; not “quantum is faster for every task.”
- Applications: simulation, optimization, cryptography/cryptanalysis, material/drug research, secure communication.
- **QKD:** quantum-state measurement disturbance can reveal eavesdropping; it distributes keys rather than directly encrypting bulk data.
- **NQM — canonical 2026 demand:** intermediate-scale quantum computers of 50–1000 physical qubits + four thematic hubs.
- **Dynamic example only:** Majorana-1 chip; retain “quantum-computing-enabling chip concept”, not product-launch trivia.

#### B. Communication Technologies

##### 5.14 Wireless comparison matrix

| Technology | Medium / band | Typical scope | Key discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bluetooth** | Radio; 2.4 GHz ISM | Personal-area, short range | Device-to-device/peripheral; no mandatory line-of-sight |
| **Wi-Fi** | Radio; 2.4/5 GHz in 2011 PYQ framing | WLAN/local network | Higher-throughput local networking |
| **NFC** | Electromagnetic radio field; very short range | Tap/proximity | Contactless, centimetre-scale; encryption can be used |
| **RFID** | Radio tag ↔ reader | Identification/tracking | Tags/readers; short-range classification in UPSC 2022 |
| **LiFi/VLC** | Visible light | Optical wireless, short range | No RF electromagnetic interference; light cannot penetrate opaque walls |
| **VPN** | Logical encrypted tunnel over public network | Remote/private-network access | Security overlay, not a radio standard |

##### 5.15 Bluetooth vs Wi-Fi
- Both use radio; Bluetooth commonly uses 2.4 GHz.
- Wi-Fi historically uses 2.4/5 GHz (newer standards also extend beyond the old PYQ framing).
- Bluetooth → PAN/device pairing; Wi-Fi → WLAN/network access.
- Neither requires line-of-sight as a general rule.
- **Trap:** Bluetooth is not “WLAN only”; Wi-Fi is not “WWAN only.”

##### 5.16 NFC
- Very-short-range contactless communication.
- Works through electromagnetic coupling/radio field.
- Can support card emulation, peer-to-peer/data exchange, contactless payment.
- Encryption can protect sensitive exchanges.
- **Trap:** “works even at one metre” → false in UPSC 2015.

##### 5.17 RFID
- Tag stores/transmits identifier/data; reader interrogates/receives it.
- Passive/active implementations vary in range.
- Uses: inventory, asset tracking, access/logistics.
- For UPSC 2022, RFID belongs to **short-range device/technology** grouping.
- Do not memorize dubious universal range figures.

##### 5.18 LiFi / VLC
- Visible-light-based wireless data communication; LEDs can modulate light faster than human perception.
- VLC spectrum in UPSC 2020: **375–780 nm**.
- Short-range optical wireless communication.
- Faster data transfer than Bluetooth accepted by UPSC.
- No **radio-frequency** electromagnetic interference.
- Light confinement by walls can improve spatial reuse/privacy, but fixed speed/range tables are not durable.
- **Trap:** VLC is not “long-range optical wireless”.

##### 5.19 Mobile generations / LTE / VoLTE / 5G
- Broad evolution: 2G digital voice → 3G mobile data → 4G/LTE broadband IP → 5G higher capacity/lower latency/massive device support.
- LTE commonly marketed as 4G.
- VoLTE → voice carried as IP over LTE; enables 4G data continuity during voice service.
- **UPSC 2019:** LTE is not “3G”, VoLTE is not “advanced 3G”; LTE not simply data-only and VoLTE not simply voice-only.
- 5G conceptual features:
  - low/mid/high spectrum bands;
  - lower latency;
  - higher throughput/capacity;
  - more simultaneous devices;
  - massive MIMO/beamforming/small cells;
  - network slicing;
  - edge-computing synergy.
- Avoid exact advertised speed/coverage figures in permanent notes.

##### 5.20 VPN
- Extends/creates protected private-network access over public Internet.
- Commonly encrypts traffic between endpoints/tunnel gateways.
- Remote-access and site-to-site designs.
- **Trap:** VPN ≠ SaaS/cloud resource pool; VPN is a connectivity/security mechanism.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

### A. Space Technology & Applications

#### A1. Orbital mechanics — must-know
- Orbit = continuous free fall: Earth’s gravity supplies **centripetal acceleration**.
- **Circular orbit:** orbital radius + speed magnitude constant; **velocity changes direction continuously**.
- **Elliptical orbit:** radius varies; satellite is fastest near **perigee**, slowest near **apogee**.
- **LEO:** below ~2,000 km → Earth observation, crewed stations, many constellations; short orbital periods.
- **MEO:** between LEO and GEO → common navigation/GNSS region.
- **GTO:** highly elliptical transfer orbit used to move payloads toward geosynchronous/geostationary altitude.
- **Geosynchronous:** orbital period matches Earth’s rotation; need not be circular/equatorial.
- **Geostationary = special geosynchronous orbit:** circular + equatorial + prograde; appears fixed over one longitude; altitude ~**35,786 km** above equator.
- **Polar orbit:** inclination near 90° → successive passes can cover most of Earth.
- **Sun-synchronous orbit (SSO):** near-polar orbit whose orbital plane precesses so the satellite crosses a location at nearly the same **local solar time** → comparable illumination; ideal for repeated imaging/remote sensing.

**Elimination trigger:** `22,236` is approximately GEO altitude in **miles**, not kilometres.

#### A2. Launch vehicles

| Vehicle | Core use | Stable architecture |
|---|---|---|
| **PSLV** | LEO / polar / SSO; strongly associated with Earth-observation & many scientific missions | 4 stages: solid → liquid → solid → liquid |
| **GSLV Mk II** | heavier payloads toward GTO, especially communication-class missions | 3 stages; indigenous **cryogenic upper stage** |
| **LVM3** (earlier GSLV Mk III) | Indian heavy-lift launcher; GTO + large LEO missions | 2 solid S200 boosters + L110 liquid core + C25 cryogenic upper stage |

- **Solid propulsion:** storable/simple/high thrust; difficult to throttle/shut/restart precisely.
- **Liquid propulsion:** controllable/throttleable; greater plumbing/storage complexity.
- **Cryogenic stage:** very low-temperature propellants; high efficiency; **do not attach this to PSLV**.
- **Expendable** launcher used once; **reusable** system recovers/reuses selected stages/vehicle components.
- Ramjet/scramjet = **air-breathing** engines; rockets carry oxidizer and can operate in vacuum.

#### A3. Satellite navigation / PNT
- Navigation satellites provide **Positioning, Navigation and Timing (PNT)**.
- **Global GNSS:** GPS—USA; GLONASS—Russia; Galileo—EU; BeiDou—China.
- **Regional systems:** NavIC/IRNSS—India; QZSS—Japan.
- Navigation is not only “maps”: timing underpins telecom synchronisation, banking/financial timing, power-grid operation and other infrastructure.

##### NavIC / IRNSS
- India’s independent **regional** satellite-navigation system.
- Permanent note focus: regional PNT architecture + Indian autonomy; do **not** hard-code current satellite count/accuracy/service-boundary numbers unless refreshed from an official source.

##### GAGAN
- **GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation**.
- Indian **satellite-based augmentation system (SBAS)**; developed by **AAI + ISRO**.
- Uses ground infrastructure + space broadcast of corrections/integrity information to augment GPS.
- Core outcomes: better **accuracy + integrity**, more uniform/high-quality air-traffic management.
- **Not** a standalone Indian GNSS constellation; **not** useful only to aviation.

#### A4. Remote sensing / Earth observation
- Remote sensing = acquiring information without direct physical contact, typically via reflected/emitted/scattered EM radiation.
- **Passive optical/multispectral/hyperspectral:** depends on naturally available/reflected/emitted radiation; clouds can obstruct many optical bands.
- **Radar/SAR = active:** sends microwave signal + records return; can image through cloud and day/night in many conditions.

**PYQ-proven applications**
- crop productivity assessment;
- groundwater-resource mapping;
- mineral exploration;
- vegetation chlorophyll estimation;
- land-surface temperature;
- estimation of rice-paddy greenhouse-gas emissions;
- agriculture, forestry, water resources, oceans, urban/rural planning, disaster monitoring.

**Trap:** Remote-sensing satellite ≠ telecommunications satellite merely because both are satellites.

#### A5. Stable mission architecture
- **Flyby:** passes target without entering orbit.
- **Orbiter:** remains in orbit around target body.
- **Lander:** controlled descent/soft landing on surface.
- **Rover:** mobile robotic surface platform carrying instruments.
- **Docking:** two spacecraft autonomously/actively join in orbit and function together.
- **Berthing:** attachment assisted by a robotic manipulator after proximity operations.
- **Microgravity research:** studies biological/material/physical processes under very low effective gravity; mission names are examples, not permanent content.

**Mission study rule:** memorize **body/agency + objective + distinctive payload/technology**; launch date and temporary record only if explicitly in current-affairs layer.

#### A6. Stable Indian space-institution roles
- **ISRO:** space R&D, launch/satellite/space-application development and missions.
- **IN-SPACe:** promotes/facilitates and authorises non-government/private participation in Indian space activities.
- **NSIL:** commercialisation/operational commercial deployment of Indian space products/services.
- **Antrix:** older/legacy commercial arm context; avoid mixing its historical role with current NSIL functions.

### B. Astronomy & Astrophysics

#### B1. Stellar evolution / stellar types
- **Nebula** = interstellar cloud of gas + dust.
- Gravity collapse → **protostar** → core heats → hydrogen fusion starts → **main-sequence star**.
- **Mass controls evolution + lifetime.** More massive/luminous stars burn nuclear fuel much faster → **shorter**, not longer, lifetimes.

**Broad pathways**
- Lower/intermediate mass → red giant → outer layers expelled / planetary nebula → **white dwarf**.
- Massive star → supergiant → core-collapse supernova → **neutron star or black hole** depending on final core/remnant conditions.
- **Chandrasekhar limit (~1.4 M☉):** limit for white-dwarf support by electron degeneracy; **not** a simple initial-star-mass branch rule.
- **Brown dwarf:** substellar object insufficiently massive to sustain ordinary hydrogen fusion → “failed star” shorthand.

**Definition traps**
- **Cepheid:** pulsating variable star whose brightness changes periodically.
- **Nebula:** gas/dust cloud — not a periodically brightening star.
- **Pulsar:** rapidly rotating, strongly magnetised **neutron star** whose beam can be observed as periodic pulses.

#### B2. Black holes + gravitational waves
- **Black hole:** region where gravity is so strong that beyond the **event horizon** signals/light cannot escape to distant observers.
- **Event horizon** = causal boundary; **singularity** = theoretical GR description of central extreme curvature, not an observable surface.
- Stellar-mass black holes can form after collapse of massive stars; supermassive black holes occupy a different mass/origin regime.
- **Gravitational waves (GW):** propagating distortions/ripples in spacetime produced by changing asymmetric mass distributions; compact-object mergers are strong sources; propagate at light speed in GR.

##### Detectors
- **LIGO:** ground-based laser interferometer → measures tiny differential changes in arm lengths.
- **LISA/eLISA concept:** multiple spacecraft in very large triangular formation linked by lasers → space-based interferometry for GW.
- Black-hole/neutron-star mergers → GW signal; do not confuse with gravitational lensing.
- **Gravitational lensing:** gravity bends light from background sources; separate phenomenon/tool.

#### B3. Cosmology — durable minimum
- **Redshift:** spectral shift to longer wavelength; cosmological redshift is associated with expansion/recession.
- **Cosmic microwave background / microwave background + redshift** are PYQ-tested evidence related to an expanding/Big-Bang universe.
- **Hubble constant:** parameter describing present cosmic expansion rate.
- **Dark matter:** inferred via gravitational effects such as galaxy motions, lensing and large-scale structure; not ordinary luminous matter.
- **Dark energy:** name for the component associated with accelerated cosmic expansion.
- **Antimatter:** antimatter nuclei/particles can inform tests of matter–antimatter asymmetry and cosmic evolution; do not assign unrelated mineral/oil applications.

#### B4. Solar activity + space weather
- **Solar wind:** continuous outflow of charged plasma from Sun, mainly protons + electrons.
- **Solar flare:** rapid release of electromagnetic radiation/energy from active solar regions.
- **CME:** large expulsion of magnetised plasma from solar corona; related to but distinct from a flare.
- Earth’s **magnetosphere** deflects/traps many charged particles; aurora occurs when energetic particles interact with upper atmosphere, especially at high latitudes.

**Major solar-storm effects — PYQ tested**
- GPS/navigation degradation/failure;
- power-grid disturbance/damage;
- intense aurora at unusually low latitudes;
- satellite drag/orbit disturbance + satellite-electronics risk;
- HF/shortwave communication disruption, especially polar routes.

**Not standard direct effects:** tsunami; widespread forest fires.

#### B5. Solar-system bodies + planetary defence
- **Asteroid:** small rocky/metal-rich body; many concentrated in main belt between Mars and Jupiter.
- **Comet:** volatile/icy + dust/rock; heating near Sun can create coma + tail.
- Tail presence is a strong comet clue; “comets are mostly between Venus and Mercury” is false.
- **NEO:** asteroid/comet whose orbit brings it into Earth’s orbital neighbourhood; classification ≠ proof of impact threat.
- Planetary defence = detection → tracking → orbit/impact assessment → mitigation/deflection; named missions remain examples.
- **Trojan asteroid:** shares a planet’s orbit around Sun near stable Lagrange regions, classically L4/L5.

#### B6. Exoplanet habitability
- **Goldilocks / habitable zone:** circumstellar region where surface conditions on a suitable planet may permit **liquid water**.
- “In habitable zone” ≠ “habitable” ≠ “inhabited.” Atmosphere, pressure, composition, stellar activity and other factors matter.
- Mars = conventional nearby life-search candidate in basic exam questions; individual “lake/life evidence” claims remain dynamic.

#### B7. Astronomical distance units
- **Astronomical Unit (AU):** mean Earth–Sun distance ≈ **1.496 × 10¹¹ m**; useful mainly within Solar System.
- **Light-year:** distance light travels in vacuum in one year ≈ **9.46 × 10¹⁵ m**; unit of **distance**, not time.
- **Parsec:** distance at which 1 AU subtends 1 arcsecond; **1 pc ≈ 3.26 ly**.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Energy Technologies

##### 5A.1 Hydrogen: production, use, fuel cells

**Hydrogen colour logic — remember process, not colour alone**

| Type | Durable production logic | Carbon handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Green H₂** | water electrolysis powered by renewable electricity | no process CO₂ from fossil feedstock |
| **Blue H₂** | fossil-fuel reforming/gasification + carbon capture/storage | substantial CO₂ captured; not “green” |
| **Grey H₂** | fossil-fuel reforming without carbon capture | CO₂ released |

**Green-H₂ uses repeatedly tested**
- direct combustion / industrial heat;
- blending with natural gas;
- **fuel cells** → electricity;
- chemical/industrial feedstock → fertilizer/ammonia, refining;
- potential reductant/energy carrier for steel;
- energy storage / grid balancing where hydrogen is used as an energy carrier.

**Fuel cell ≠ battery ≠ combustion engine**
- Fuel cell: **electrochemical conversion**; keeps generating while reactants are supplied.
- Hydrogen at anode → protons + electrons (catalyst-assisted in PEM fuel cell).
- Proton-exchange membrane → passes protons; blocks electrons/gases.
- Electrons → external circuit → **DC electricity**.
- Cathode: oxygen + protons + electrons → **water**; heat also released.
- Hydrogen FCEV tailpipe → water vapour/warm air; **tailpipe-zero-carbon does not automatically mean lifecycle-zero-carbon** — upstream H₂ production matters.
- Fuel cells can range from small-device applications to stationary power and vehicles; reject “buildings only”.

**FCEV powertrain — minimum recall**
`H₂ tank → fuel-cell stack → DC electricity → inverter/power electronics → traction motor → wheels`
- Small traction/buffer battery may still be present; do **not** write “FCEV has no battery”.
- Regenerative braking can recover kinetic energy electrically.

##### 5A.2 Solar energy + Distributed Energy Resources

**Photovoltaic vs solar thermal**

| Feature | Photovoltaic (PV) | Solar thermal |
|---|---|---|
| Primary conversion | light → electricity | sunlight → heat |
| Immediate electrical output | **DC** from PV cell/module | electricity only after thermal/mechanical conversion where used for power generation |
| Typical trap | “PV directly gives AC” → false | “solar thermal directly gives DC” → false |

**Net metering**
- Billing/accounting mechanism: consumer-generator exports surplus rooftop-solar electricity to grid → receives credit/offset against imports, subject to applicable regulatory design.
- Concept ≠ subsidy; concept ≠ solar tariff-setting authority.

**Distributed Energy Resources (DERs)**
- Small/modular resources located close to load or on distribution side; can generate, store or flex electricity.
- Reconstructed UPSC-2024 list: **battery storage + biomass generators + fuel cells + rooftop solar PV** → all fit DER logic.
- Do not turn a one-year scheme into the definition of DER.

**Solar-powered systems**
- Solar electricity can drive different electric pump types; reject claims that it is inherently restricted to only surface or only centrifugal pumps.
- “First/largest solar park/airport” = dynamic wrapper; retain only if separately maintained in current-affairs notes.

##### 5A.3 Batteries + EVs

**Li-ion cell — core architecture**

| Component | Exam-useful fact |
|---|---|
| **Anode** | oxidation during discharge; graphite commonly used; Li intercalates between graphite layers |
| **Cathode** | reduction during discharge; chemistry strongly affects energy density, safety, cost |
| **Electrolyte** | carries Li⁺ ions between electrodes; typically lithium salt in organic solvent in conventional Li-ion cells; should not conduct electrons internally |
| **Separator** | porous membrane; keeps electrodes apart; lets ions pass; prevents internal electronic short circuit |

**Direction trap**
- **Discharge:** Li⁺ moves anode → cathode; electrons go through external circuit → useful current.
- **Charge:** external power drives Li⁺ cathode → anode.

**Cathode materials — high yield**
- `LFP` = lithium iron phosphate → high thermal/chemical stability, long cycle life, cobalt-free; generally lower energy density than high-nickel chemistries.
- `NMC` = nickel-manganese-cobalt oxide → higher energy density; cobalt/nickel cost/supply/safety trade-offs.
- 2025 trap: **Cobalt + Lithium + Nickel** can be cathode constituents; **Graphite** is normally associated with anode.

**Solid-state direction**
- Conventional liquid organic electrolytes can be flammable.
- Solid electrolytes are pursued mainly for safety + higher-performance potential; do not assume all “solid-state” batteries are commercially dominant/currently identical.

**EV powertrain**
- BEV: battery → DC; inverter → controlled AC for common traction motor; motor → torque; reduction gear → wheels.
- Regenerative braking: traction motor acts as generator → kinetic energy → electrical energy → battery.
- Core subsystems: traction battery, BMS, inverter/power electronics, motor, DC–DC converter, thermal management.

**EV-type distinctions**
- **BEV:** external charging; no ICE propulsion.
- **HEV:** ICE + motor; battery charged internally by engine/regenerative braking; no plug required.
- **PHEV:** ICE + larger externally chargeable battery; can operate limited distance in electric-only mode.
- **MHEV:** electric motor assists ICE but cannot normally propel vehicle independently.
- **REEV:** wheels primarily electric; small ICE acts as generator rather than direct wheel-drive source.
- **FCEV:** electricity generated onboard from H₂ fuel cell; electric traction motor.

##### 5A.4 Nuclear energy

**Three different nuclear-energy mechanisms**

| Mechanism | What happens | Chain reaction? | Typical application tested |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fission** | heavy nucleus splits | can be self-sustaining/controlled in reactor | nuclear power reactors |
| **Fusion** | light nuclei combine | different plasma/fusion conditions | Sun/stars; ITER pathway |
| **Radioisotope decay** | unstable isotope decays spontaneously, releases heat | **no fission chain reaction** | RTG / nuclear battery |

**Heavy water**
- Exam-tested function: **moderator** → slows fast neutrons to energies favourable for sustaining controlled fission in suitable reactor designs.
- Do not equate “heavy water” with fuel.

**Thorium**
- UPSC-tested advantages over uranium: relatively abundant; potentially high fuel utilisation; potentially favourable long-lived-waste profile depending on fuel cycle.
- Thorium appears in **monazite**; monazite is also a rare-earth-bearing mineral.
- Avoid claiming thorium is simply a drop-in substitute for uranium in every reactor.

**Uranium enrichment trap**
- Ordinary electricity generation does **not** require uranium enriched to 60%.
- Reactor fuel requirements vary by reactor design; high enrichment figures should never be treated as a generic civilian-power requirement.

**Fusion / ITER**
- Fusion combines light nuclei; experimental fusion programmes aim at controlled net-energy-capable systems.
- ITER is a fusion experiment; its significance is future fusion-reactor capability, **not** improving fission efficiency or directly substituting thorium for uranium.

**RTG**
- Radioisotope thermoelectric generator = heat from **natural radioactive decay** → thermocouples → electricity.
- No turbine; no moving parts in the basic conversion stage.
- **Not a miniature fission reactor.**
- Used where long-duration, low-maintenance power is needed, especially spacecraft/deep-space systems.
- Pu-238 is a commonly cited RTG isotope; keep historical production-origin claims separate from the core definition.

##### 5A.5 Bioenergy / gasification / fuels

**Biomass basics**
- Biomass = recent biological material/residues used as energy feedstock; **coal is fossil**, not modern biomass.
- Common durable feedstocks: crop residues, bagasse, wood residues, organic wastes, dung/biogas feedstocks.

**Biomass gasification**
- Thermochemical conversion of solid biomass under limited oxygen/steam → combustible **producer gas/syngas**.
- Feedstocks can include coconut shells, groundnut shells, rice husk etc.
- Gas mixture is not “only hydrogen + carbon dioxide”; combustible components include CO/H₂ and may include CH₄ depending on process.
- Cleaned producer gas can fuel suitable engines/generators.

**Pyrolysis vs gasification**
- Pyrolysis: thermal decomposition with little/no oxygen → char + liquids/oils + gases.
- Gasification: partial oxidation/steam at high temperature → gas-rich product designed for fuel/chemical synthesis.
- Plasma gasification is an extreme-temperature waste-conversion variant; do not confuse it with natural-gas extraction.

**Coal gasification**
- Coal + controlled O₂/steam at high temperature → **syngas** (mainly CO + H₂ after conditioning).
- Syngas can support downstream **ethanol** synthesis/fermentation routes.
- Syngas/H₂ → ammonia → **urea** chain.
- **Nitroglycerine** is not a coal-gasification product chain; made by nitrating glycerol.

**Sugar-industry link**
- **Bagasse** → biomass fuel/cogeneration.
- **Molasses / sugarcane juice** → fermentation → ethanol.

**Biofuel feedstock logic**
- Ethanol → sugar/starch feedstocks + advanced cellulosic/residue routes.
- Biodiesel → oils/fats (e.g., non-edible oils, used cooking oil, animal fats/algal oils depending process).
- Do not merge ethanol and biodiesel feedstock chemistry.

**Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF)**
- Exam focus = **feedstock diversity**, not one crop.
- Accepted feedstock classes can include agricultural residues, grains/sugars where pathway allows, forestry/wood wastes, wet wastes such as wastewater sludge, oils/fats, municipal/industrial biogenic wastes, algae etc.
- Keep “SAF is low-carbon” as lifecycle-relative, not “zero-emission”.

#### B. Nanotechnology & Advanced Materials

##### 5B.1 Nanotechnology — core frame
- Typical nanoscale: roughly **1–100 nm** for many conventional definitions.
- Nanoscale behaviour can differ from bulk because surface-area/volume ratio rises sharply and quantum/size effects may become important.
- **Natural nanoparticles exist**; “all nanoparticles are human-made” is false.
- Exam emphasis = what a nanosystem **can actually do**, not just buzzwords.

##### 5B.2 Dimensionality / material classes

| Class | Simple mental model | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0D | nanosized in all dimensions | quantum dots / nanoparticles |
| 1D | nanoscale cross-section, extended length | nanowires / many CNT descriptions |
| 2D | nanoscale thickness, extended plane | graphene |
| 3D nanostructured | bulk object built from nanoscale features | nanocomposites / porous nanostructures |

##### 5B.3 Graphene
- Carbon allotrope/nanostructure: **single/few-atom-thick 2D sp²-bonded carbon sheet**.
- Strong + thin + electrically conductive; useful in conducting electrodes, sensors, composites, energy/electronics research.
- UPSC trap: **graphene is not made of silicon**.
- Graphene ≠ graphite: graphite = stacked graphene-like layers.

##### 5B.4 Carbon nanotubes (CNTs)
- Cylindrical graphitic carbon nanostructures; can be single-walled or multi-walled.
- High aspect ratio; high tensile strength; useful electrical/thermal properties; high surface area.
- UPSC-supported applications: **drug/antigen carriers** and **biochemical sensors**.
- 2020 key accepts that biodegradation of CNTs can occur under some biological/enzymatic conditions.
- Artificial blood-capillary use was **not** accepted as an established application in the 2020 PYQ.
- **CNT ≠ C₆₀ Buckminsterfullerene**: both are carbon nanostructures, but one is tubular and the other is a closed fullerene cage.

##### 5B.5 Nanomedicine / nanoparticles
- Targeted drug delivery → key advantage from carrier size/surface functionalisation + controlled release/localisation.
- Nanotechnology can support gene-delivery/therapy systems.
- Common platform examples in references: nanomicelles, liposomes/nanoshells, quantum dots, nanoparticles, nanotubes.
- Some metal-oxide nanoparticles are used in cosmetics (e.g., UV-blocking formulations).
- Safety is **material-, size-, dose-, coating- and exposure-dependent**; do not generalise “all nanoparticles safe/unsafe”. Environmental regulation belongs to Environment.

##### 5B.6 Hydrogels
- 3D hydrophilic polymer networks capable of retaining large amounts of water.
- Tunable porosity/chemistry → strong platform for **controlled drug delivery**.
- Can be designed for biomedical, sensing, cooling, lubrication and other engineering research, but **UPSC-2024 exact use-combination remains under VERIFY** because the supplemental explanation conflicts with its stated answer.
- Final learner note should retain only the uncontested concept + drug-delivery application until the record is resolved.

##### 5B.7 Carbon fibre
- High-strength, low-density carbon-rich fibre used mainly as reinforcement in composites.
- Major application domains: aerospace/aircraft, automobiles, sports/engineering structures.
- “Carbon fibre can never be recycled” = false; recycling/recovery routes exist, though quality/economics depend on process.

##### 5B.8 Nano application matrix — retain only durable examples

| Domain | High-yield examples |
|---|---|
| Health | targeted drug delivery; imaging/sensors; gene-delivery platforms |
| Electronics | conductive nanomaterials; quantum-dot displays; nanosensors |
| Energy | improved electrodes/catalysts; PV materials; hydrogen-production/storage research |
| Water | nanomembranes; adsorption/catalysis; sensing |
| Materials | nano-coatings; light/high-strength composites; surface modification |

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Defence Technology

##### 5A.1 Missile basics — core classification

**By trajectory / flight principle**

| Ballistic missile | Cruise missile |
|---|---|
| Rocket-powered boost; thereafter largely follows a ballistic trajectory | Powered through atmospheric flight by an engine |
| High-arching trajectory; long-range systems can leave the atmosphere and re-enter | Usually flies within atmosphere, often at lower altitude |
| Guidance may occur in boost/midcourse/terminal phases; modern re-entry vehicles may manoeuvre | Continuously guided; high manoeuvrability is typical |
| Detection/interception problem dominated by high speed, re-entry and trajectory | Detection/interception problem dominated by low altitude, terrain/sea-skimming and manoeuvre |
| Example family: Agni | Example family: BrahMos / Tomahawk |

**PYQ trap:** “ballistic = jet-propelled subsonic throughout” and “cruise = rocket only initially” → both wrong.

**By launch/target mode:** surface-to-surface; surface-to-air; air-to-air; air-to-surface; anti-tank; anti-radiation; anti-ship; submarine/ship-launched variants.

**Anti-radiation missile (ARM)**
- Homes on electromagnetic/RF emissions from hostile radar/communication emitters.
- Used for suppression/destruction of enemy air defences.
- PYQ anchor: RudraM series → air-to-surface anti-radiation role.

##### 5A.2 Hypersonic systems
- **Hypersonic:** Mach ≥ 5; a speed regime, not a single weapon class.
- **Hypersonic cruise missile (HCM):** powered atmospheric flight, usually air-breathing/scramjet concept.
- **Hypersonic glide vehicle (HGV):** rocket-boosted to altitude, then unpowered/partly controlled glide at hypersonic speed.
- Ballistic re-entry vehicles can also be hypersonic; therefore **“hypersonic” ≠ automatically “cruise missile.”**
- Main challenge: high speed + manoeuvrability + lower/flatter trajectories reduce reaction time.

##### 5A.3 FOBS and MIRV
- **FOBS (Fractional Orbital Bombardment System):** payload is placed on an orbital-like trajectory and de-orbited toward target before completing a full orbit; complicates predictable approach/detection geometry.
- **MIRV:** one ballistic missile carries multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles; separate warheads can be assigned to different targets.
- Trap: MIRV = multiple warheads, not multiple launch vehicles.

##### 5A.4 Air / missile defence — layered logic

**Kill chain:** detect → track/classify → command-and-control → assign interceptor/shooter → engage → assess.

**Layers/functions**
- **BMD:** focuses on ballistic missile interception; may use exo-atmospheric and endo/terminal interceptors.
- **Air defence:** broader set against aircraft, UAVs, cruise missiles and selected missile threats.
- **Networked defence:** sensors/radars + command centres + interceptors/guns/EW; no single missile alone constitutes the architecture.

**Named PYQ anchors**
- **THAAD** → United States; terminal/high-altitude ballistic-missile defence; hit-to-kill concept.
- **Iron Dome** → Israel; short-range air/rocket defence layer.
- **Akashteer** (reference example, not canonical UPSC/UPPCS demand here) → Army air-defence command-and-control/networking; keep only if current layer requires it.

**Trap:** country-system mismatch and “anti-missile system = anti-aircraft fighter system” conflation.

##### 5A.5 Fighter generations and aircraft roles

**Fifth-generation fighter — typical feature cluster (no universally binding definition)**
- Low-observable/stealth shaping/materials.
- Advanced AESA/LPI radar and passive sensors.
- Sensor fusion + highly integrated avionics.
- Network-centric data links / battlespace situational awareness.
- High manoeuvrability; supercruise often associated but not universal.
- Internal weapons carriage commonly supports low-observable configuration.

**PYQ 2024 reconstruction:** Rafale, MiG-29, Tejas Mk-1 → **none** are fifth-generation.

**Role distinctions from 2025 PYQ**
- **Dornier-228** → maritime patrol/surveillance aircraft.
- **IL-76** → heavy/strategic transport aircraft; **not** a supersonic combat fighter.
- **C-17 Globemaster III** → military/strategic transport aircraft.
- **IL-78** → aerial refuelling tanker (useful elimination contrast from reference PYQs).

**Elimination rule:** aircraft name alone is insufficient; memorise **role + propulsion/size class + service use**.

##### 5A.6 Naval platform taxonomy

| Platform | Core role |
|---|---|
| Aircraft carrier | Mobile airbase at sea; power projection |
| Destroyer | Large multi-role surface combatant; strong AAW/ASW/ASuW capability |
| Frigate | Multi-role escort/surface combatant, generally smaller than destroyer |
| Corvette | Smaller surface combatant, often coastal/littoral/ASW roles |
| Offshore Patrol Vessel (OPV) | Patrol/EEZ/security missions; not equivalent to frontline destroyer/frigate |
| Conventional submarine (SSK) | Diesel-electric / AIP-assisted underwater combat |
| SSN | Nuclear-powered attack submarine |
| SSBN | Nuclear-powered ballistic-missile submarine; strategic deterrence |
| Auxiliary/test vessel | Support, recovery, trials, instrumentation; not primarily a combatant |

**PYQ anchors**
- **INS Astradharini** → specialised torpedo launch/recovery and test-support auxiliary vessel; not submarine/carrier.
- **Scorpene design / Kalvari class** → conventional diesel-electric attack-submarine family in Indian service; individual commissioning dates are dynamic.
- **Sarthak** (UPPCS 2020) → Offshore Patrol Vessel; individual launch event is dynamic.

##### 5A.7 UAV / drone taxonomy

**By airframe / flight mechanics**
- **Multi-rotor:** VTOL + hover; high manoeuvrability; lower endurance.
- **Single-rotor/helicopter UAV:** VTOL + hover; heavier payload potential; mechanical complexity.
- **Fixed-wing:** efficient, long range/endurance; normally needs runway/catapult/recovery system; cannot conventionally hover.
- **Hybrid fixed-wing VTOL:** vertical take-off/landing + efficient forward flight.

**By mission/endurance**
- Tactical/mini/micro UAVs.
- MALE = Medium Altitude Long Endurance.
- HALE = High Altitude Long Endurance.
- UCAV/strike UAV = weapon-capable unmanned combat platform.
- **Loitering munition:** waits/loiters in target area, then attacks; expendable weapon, not merely a reusable ISR drone.

**Power-source trap**
- UAVs are **not battery-only**: batteries, internal-combustion engines, hybrid systems, fuel cells and other propulsion arrangements exist.

**Drone swarm**
- Multiple UAVs coordinate as a group; individual nodes can communicate/share information with others.
- Resilience/distribution is a defining advantage; coordination need not depend on one centrally piloted link.
- **Counter-UAS:** RF jamming, navigation/GNSS jamming or spoofing, kinetic interception, directed energy, cyber/electronic attack depending system.
- GPS spoofing = transmission of deceptive navigation signals; jamming = denial/degradation of signal. Do not conflate.

**Application logic**
- Agriculture spraying, hazardous-area inspection, wildlife/scientific sampling, ISR, mapping, logistics and strike are all plausible depending payload/platform.

##### 5A.8 Military explosives / energetic materials

- **High explosive:** designed for rapid detonation and destructive blast/shock.
- **Propellant:** controlled rapid combustion to generate thrust; an energetic material can be formulated for different roles, so “explosive” and “rocket fuel” are not interchangeable labels.
- **CL-20, HMX, LLM-105** → military high-energy/insensitive explosive-material family in UPSC 2025 context.
- **RDX/HMX** → nitramine high explosives.
- **Nitrocellulose (guncotton)** → nitrated cellulose; energetic material used in propellants/explosive compositions; chemistry detail belongs primarily to Chemistry & Materials.

**Trap:** a substance being energetic does not automatically make it a cruise-missile fuel or rocket propellant.

#### B. Defence Organisations, Exercises & Operational Structures

##### 5B.1 Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) + Department of Military Affairs (DMA)

**CDS — durable institutional core**
- Four-star officer; also **Secretary, Department of Military Affairs**.
- **Permanent Chairman, Chiefs of Staff Committee (CoSC).**
- **Principal Military Adviser to the Defence Minister on tri-service matters.**
- Administers tri-service organisations/agencies/commands assigned to CDS.
- Member of Defence Acquisition Council; Military Adviser to Nuclear Command Authority.
- Drives jointness in operations, logistics, transport, training, communications, maintenance/support and capability planning.
- Prioritises inter-service capital-acquisition proposals within anticipated resources.

**Critical UPSC trap**
- CDS **does NOT exercise military command over the three Service Chiefs**.
- Service Chiefs retain responsibility for matters exclusive to their services; CDS provides tri-service integration/advice.

**DMA — durable functions**
- Handles matters relating to Army, Navy and Air Force, integrated HQs and Territorial Army as assigned.
- Promotes jointness in procurement, training and staffing.
- Facilitates restructuring/joint or theatre-command architecture.
- Promotes indigenous equipment use.

##### 5B.2 Equivalent commissioned-officer ranks

| Army | Navy | Air Force |
|---|---|---|
| Field Marshal | Admiral of the Fleet | Marshal of the Air Force |
| General | Admiral | Air Chief Marshal |
| Lieutenant General | Vice Admiral | Air Marshal |
| Major General | Rear Admiral | Air Vice Marshal |
| Brigadier | Commodore | Air Commodore |
| Colonel | Captain | Group Captain |
| Lieutenant Colonel | Commander | Wing Commander |
| Major | Lieutenant Commander | Squadron Leader |
| Captain | Lieutenant | Flight Lieutenant |
| Lieutenant | Sub-Lieutenant | Flying Officer |

**UPSC 2024 trap reconstruction**
- Brigadier ↔ Air Commodore ↔ **Commodore**, not Commander.
- Major General ↔ Air Vice Marshal ↔ **Rear Admiral**, not Vice Admiral.
- Major ↔ Squadron Leader ↔ Lieutenant Commander = correct.
- Lieutenant Colonel ↔ Wing Commander ↔ Commander; Group Captain/Captain correspond to Colonel level.

##### 5B.3 Home Guards

- Voluntary force; organised/administered by States/UTs under **State/UT Home Guards Acts and Rules**, not a single Central Home Guards Act.
- Auxiliary to police for maintenance of internal security/law and order and civil-administration support.
- Can assist in disaster relief, fire-fighting, essential services, elections and civil-defence-type duties.
- **Border Wing Home Guards (BWHG)** raised in some border States; assist BSF and help prevent infiltration on international borders/coastal areas and protect vulnerable communications/assets during contingencies.

**PYQ trap:** “raised under Central Government Act/Rules” = false.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **Ballistic ≠ cruise:** ballistic does not remain jet-propelled; cruise does not become unpowered after initial boost.
- **Hypersonic ≠ one weapon type:** ballistic RVs, HGVs and HCMs can all involve hypersonic speed.
- **BrahMos ≠ ICBM:** cruise missile family; do not swap with Agni.
- **THAAD ≠ Israeli:** United States. **Iron Dome ≠ US:** Israel.
- **Aircraft role trap:** IL-76 = transport, not combat; C-17 = transport; Dornier-228 = maritime patrol.
- **Fifth-generation trap:** stealth/sensor-fusion/networking features matter; not every modern multirole fighter is fifth-gen.
- **UAV absolute traps:** not all VTOL; not all hover; not all battery-powered.
- **Loitering munition ≠ ordinary reusable UAV.**
- **GPS spoofing ≠ jamming:** deception vs denial.
- **Astradharini ≠ submarine:** auxiliary torpedo recovery/test vessel.
- **OPV ≠ frontline destroyer/frigate.**
- **CDS ≠ commander of Service Chiefs:** integration/advice role; no military command over them.
- **Home Guards ≠ central statutory force:** State/UT Acts and Rules; auxiliary role.
- **Brigadier ↔ Commodore; Major General ↔ Rear Admiral; Lt Colonel ↔ Commander.**
- **Exercise edition details are dynamic:** partner/service is durable; dates/venue change.
- **MTCR belongs strategic/WMD regime notes**, not this permanent Defence Technology node.
- **Nitrocellulose/explosive chemistry overlap:** detailed chemistry belongs General Science; retain military use only here.
