### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Climatology: Atmosphere, Heat Budget, Winds & ENSO

##### Atmosphere: composition + structure

| Layer | Approx. height | Temperature trend | PYQ anchors / elimination value |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Troposphere | ~8 km poles; up to ~18 km equator | ↓ with height; mean environmental lapse ~6.5°C/km | Most weather; water vapour/dust concentrated here; thicker at equator because stronger heating/convection |
| Stratosphere | ~12–50 km | Eventually ↑ with height | Ozone absorption of UV; stable stratification; very dry but **not absolutely cloud-free**—polar stratospheric clouds occur |
| Mesosphere | ~50–80/85 km | ↓ with height | Coldest atmospheric layer/mesopause; many meteors burn here |
| Thermosphere | ~80 km upward | ↑ sharply | Extremely rarefied; aurora/ionized region overlaps |
| Ionosphere | roughly ~80–400+ km, variable | ionized region, not a purely thermal layer | Free electrons affect/refract/return **suitable-frequency** radio waves; ozone is not the cause |
| Exosphere | outermost transition | — | Extremely rarefied; merges into space; **magnetosphere is not simply an exospheric layer** |

- Dry air near sea level: N₂ ~78%, O₂ ~21%, Ar ~0.93%, CO₂ roughly **0.04% and variable/increasing**; water vapour highly variable.
- Ozone maximum concentration lies in stratosphere; it absorbs biologically harmful UV. CFC/halogen radicals catalyse stratospheric ozone loss.
- **Qualifier trap:** “stratosphere has no water vapour/clouds” is too absolute; it has little water vapour and rare PSCs.
- Pressure and density fall rapidly with altitude.

##### Heat budget / temperature

- **Insolation** = incoming solar radiation, dominated by shortwave wavelengths.
- Earth’s surface absorbs solar energy → emits **longwave terrestrial/infrared radiation**.
- Atmosphere is heated significantly from below by:
  - absorption of terrestrial longwave by greenhouse gases;
  - sensible heat transfer/conduction near surface + turbulent mixing/convection;
  - latent heat release during condensation.
- **Greenhouse gases:** water vapour, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃ etc. absorb/re-emit selected outgoing infrared bands; N₂/O₂ are not major greenhouse gases.
- Planetary/Bond albedo ≈ **30%**, not 35% as a fixed canonical value.
- **Cloud radiative trap**
  - Low/thick clouds: high albedo → strong shortwave reflection → usually net cooling.
  - High/thin clouds: weaker shortwave reflection but effective longwave trapping → often net warming.
- **Temperature controls:** latitude/solar angle, altitude, land–water contrast, ocean currents, winds/air masses, cloud cover, aspect/slope.
- **Isotherms**
  - Broadly zonal but bend with land–sea contrast and currents.
  - January NH: continents colder, oceans warmer → isotherms generally bend equatorward over cold continental interiors and poleward over warm oceanic sectors.
  - Warm currents raise coastal temperatures; cold currents lower them.
- Tropospheric cooling with altitude mainly reflects heating from below plus adiabatic expansion of rising air as pressure falls.
- **Temperature inversion** = temperature rises with height over a layer; favoured by clear calm nights, valley cold-air drainage/subsidence etc.

##### Pressure, planetary winds and Coriolis

- Surface pressure belts, idealized:
  - Equatorial low / ITCZ
  - Subtropical highs ~30°
  - Subpolar lows ~60°
  - Polar highs
- **ITCZ:** migrating low-pressure/convergence belt near thermal equator; not fixed at the geographic equator.
- Planetary winds:
  - Trades: subtropical highs → equatorial low; NE in NH, SE in SH.
  - Westerlies: subtropical highs → subpolar lows; stronger/more continuous in SH because of ocean-dominated mid/high latitudes.
  - Polar easterlies: polar highs → subpolar lows.
- **Coriolis**
  - apparent deflection from Earth’s rotation;
  - right in NH, left in SH;
  - zero at equator, increases with latitude and speed;
  - acts perpendicular to motion → changes direction, not kinetic energy/speed by itself.
- **Jet streams**
  - narrow high-speed upper-tropospheric currents near tropopause;
  - predominantly west → east (westerly) in midlatitudes;
  - Subtropical Westerly Jet and Polar Front Jet are key westerly jets;
  - Tropical Easterly Jet = important named easterly exception.
- Pressure-gradient force initiates wind; Coriolis + friction modify direction/speed near surface.

##### Humidity, condensation, precipitation

- **Absolute humidity:** mass of water vapour per unit volume, typically g/m³.
- **Specific humidity:** mass of water vapour per unit mass of moist air, e.g. g/kg.
- **Relative humidity:** actual vapour pressure / saturation vapour pressure ×100.
  - With same moisture content: warming ↓ RH; cooling ↑ RH.
- **Dew point:** temperature to which air must cool at constant pressure/moisture to reach saturation.
- Condensation requires saturation + nuclei; products include dew/frost/fog/clouds.
- **Dew under clear nights:** stronger nocturnal radiative cooling favours surface saturation; clouds reduce radiational cooling.
- Precipitation mechanisms: convectional, orographic, frontal/cyclonic.

##### Tropical cyclones

- Core conditions:
  - warm ocean, usually SST ≥ ~26.5°C over sufficient depth;
  - moist unstable lower/mid troposphere;
  - pre-existing low-level disturbance/convergence;
  - sufficient Coriolis → rare very near equator;
  - low vertical wind shear;
  - upper-level outflow.
- **Eye:** subsiding, relatively calm, **warm-core** region; eyewall has strongest winds/convection.
- Rare South Atlantic / SE Pacific tropical cyclones: generally cooler SST/eastern-boundary upwelling, stronger shear and fewer favourable disturbances/ITCZ organization.
- Temperate/extratropical cyclones are frontal, baroclinic systems and are not warm-core tropical cyclones.

##### ENSO / Walker circulation / IOD

- **Normal equatorial Pacific**
  - easterly trades push warm surface water west;
  - western Pacific warm pool + convection;
  - eastern Pacific cold upwelling off Peru/Ecuador;
  - Walker circulation links rising west / sinking east.
- **El Niño**
  - anomalous warming of central/eastern equatorial Pacific;
  - trades/Walker circulation usually weaken;
  - thermocline flattens/deepens in east; upwelling weakens → fisheries/plankton can decline;
  - irregular recurrence, commonly ~2–7 years; not fixed to 7–8.
- **La Niña**
  - anomalous cooling central/eastern equatorial Pacific;
  - stronger trades/Walker circulation and stronger eastern-Pacific upwelling are typical.
  - not mechanically required to follow every El Niño.
- **Southern Oscillation:** atmospheric-pressure component of ENSO; ENSO is coupled ocean–atmosphere variability.
- **IOD**
  - SST-gradient mode across equatorial Indian Ocean:
    - positive: western equatorial Indian Ocean relatively warmer; southeastern/eastern sector near Indonesia relatively cooler;
    - negative: opposite.
  - Can modulate Indian monsoon/ENSO impacts but does **not** deterministically guarantee a rainfall outcome.
- **MJO ≠ IOD:** MJO = eastward-moving intraseasonal tropical convective disturbance (~30–60 day envelope); IOD = interannual Indian-Ocean SST/atmosphere dipole.
- **Permanent-note boundary:** retain concepts/sign conventions; exclude current-season forecast status.

##### Equatorial rainforest nutrient trap

- High temperature + moisture → very rapid litter decomposition.
- High rainfall → intense chemical weathering/leaching.
- Nutrients released from litter are rapidly taken up by shallow dense root systems; much ecosystem nutrient capital is held in living biomass rather than deep soil.
- Clearing removes biomass + breaks tight nutrient cycle; exposed soils leach/erode rapidly → forest recovery can be slow.
- Therefore: lush vegetation **does not imply nutrient-rich soil**.

##### High-value comparisons

- **Savanna vs Steppe**
  - Savanna = tropical; summer wet/winter dry; tall grasses/scattered trees.
  - Steppe = semi-arid grassland, commonly midlatitude/interior; shorter grasses; greater annual thermal range.
  - Do **not** assume steppe rainfall > savanna rainfall.
- **Mediterranean vs Marine West Coast**
  - Mediterranean = dry summer, wet winter.
  - Marine west coast = precipitation through year; small thermal range.
- **Equatorial vs Monsoon**
  - Equatorial = no pronounced dry season; daily convection.
  - Monsoon = seasonal reversal of winds + concentrated wet season; this monsoon comparison is retained only for elimination because monsoon itself is not one of the core owned climatic-region units here.
- **Hot desert vs Steppe**
  - Desert = arid; steppe = semi-arid transitional belt.

#### C. Earth in Space: Latitudes, Seasons & Time

##### Latitude / longitude

- **Latitude:** angular distance N/S of Equator.
  - parallels are complete circles;
  - length decreases poleward;
  - spacing ≈111 km per degree.
- **Longitude:** angular distance E/W of Prime Meridian.
  - meridians are equal pole-to-pole semicircles;
  - distance between meridians decreases from equator to zero at poles.
- **Great circle:** circle whose plane passes through Earth’s centre; shortest surface route lies along a great-circle arc.
- **Time–longitude:** Earth rotates 360° in ~24 h → 15° = 1 h → 1° = 4 min.
  - east = local solar time ahead;
  - west = behind.

##### Standard/civil time

- Ideal geometry suggests **24 one-hour zones**, but actual civil time zones are political and irregular.
- Civil offsets may be half-hour/quarter-hour; boundaries deviate greatly from meridians.
- Countries can have many zones because of longitudinal span and/or overseas territories.
- 2025 UPSC country-count trap: do not infer number of national time zones solely from mainland width.
- India: IST based on 82°30′E → UTC+5:30.

##### International Date Line

- Roughly follows 180° longitude but zigzags around states/territories.
- Crossing **westward (east → west)** → calendar advances by **one day**.
- Crossing **eastward (west → east)** → calendar moves back by **one day**.
- “Gain/lose a day” phrasing is ambiguous; use the calendar operation above.

##### Rotation / revolution / seasons

- Earth rotates **west → east**.
- **Mean solar day:** ~24 h.
- **Sidereal day:** ~23 h 56 min relative to distant stars — **not the Sun**.
- Axial tilt:
  - ~23.5° from perpendicular to orbital plane;
  - ~66.5° with orbital plane.
- Seasons require:
  - revolution around Sun;
  - axial tilt;
  - near-parallel orientation of axis through the orbit.
- Earth–Sun distance is **not** the primary cause of seasons.

##### Solstice / equinox / day length

- **June solstice:** subsolar point at Tropic of Cancer (~23.5°N).
  - NH longest day; SH shortest.
  - Arctic Circle gets about 24 h daylight on/around solstice.
  - North Pole has an extended polar day approaching six months; this does **not** apply to every latitude beyond Arctic Circle.
- **December solstice:** mirror image; subsolar point at Tropic of Capricorn.
- **Equinoxes:** subsolar point near Equator; day/night approximately equal globally.
  - precise “exactly 12 h everywhere” is physically modified by refraction and solar-disc geometry.
- Overhead Sun possible only between Tropics:
  - tropic itself: once/year;
  - interior tropics: generally twice/year.
- Day-length contrast rises poleward; equatorial day length remains close to 12 h year-round.

##### Rotation/axis shift from mass redistribution

- Redistribution of mass changes Earth’s moment of inertia and rotation parameters.
- Moving meltwater from high latitudes toward lower latitudes increases moment of inertia → can slightly slow rotation and alter polar motion/axis orientation.
- This is a mass/angular-momentum effect; solar flares do not provide the causal mass redistribution.

#### D. Geomorphology & Earth History

##### Earth interior

- **Crust**
  - continental: generally thicker, less dense, older on average;
  - oceanic: thinner, denser, basaltic, generally younger.
- **Mantle:** base at ~2,900 km; solid overall but convects over geological time.
- **Core:** Fe–Ni dominated; outer core liquid, inner core solid.
- **Lithosphere:** rigid crust + uppermost mantle; broken into plates.
- **Asthenosphere:** mechanically weak/ductile upper mantle below lithosphere; primarily solid, may contain localized partial melt; seismic low-velocity zone.
- Direct information: mines/boreholes + volcanic ejecta/material.
- Indirect: seismic waves, gravity, magnetic field, meteorites, heat flow.
- **Kola Superdeep Borehole:** terrestrial borehole on Kola Peninsula, not “deep ocean drilling”.

##### Seismic waves

| Wave | Nature | Relative speed | Medium | Core inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | compressional/longitudinal | fastest | solids, liquids, gases | refracts strongly at core boundaries; traverses liquid outer core |
| S | shear/transverse | slower | **solids only** | disappears through liquid outer core → major evidence outer core is liquid |
| Surface (Love/Rayleigh) | surface-confined | slowest | surface | usually greatest damaging motion |

- P-wave shadow zone approximately ~103°–143° from epicentre.
- S waves absent beyond roughly ~103° because liquid outer core does not transmit shear waves.
- Hypocentre/focus = rupture origin inside Earth; epicentre = surface point above focus.
- Magnitude measures source size/energy; intensity measures observed effects at a location.
- Magnitude scale trap: +1 magnitude ≈ 10× ground-motion amplitude and ~31.6× energy release.

##### Continental drift → seafloor spreading → plate tectonics

**Continental-drift evidence**
- coastline/best-fit geometry;
- continuation of rock/mountain belts across Atlantic;
- matching fossils;
- tillite/palaeoclimatic evidence;
- placer gold relationship between Ghana and Brazil;
- Gondwana sediment correlations across southern landmasses.
- Wegener’s proposed driving forces were inadequate → key historical limitation.

**Seafloor spreading**
- mid-ocean ridges = crust generation;
- youngest oceanic crust at ridge; age increases away;
- symmetric magnetic-reversal stripes about ridge;
- high heat flow/shallow earthquakes at ridges;
- deep-focus earthquakes associated with subduction zones.

**Plate-boundary matrix**

| Boundary | Lithosphere | Landforms/process | Typical seismic/volcanic pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Divergent | created | mid-ocean ridge / continental rift | shallow quakes; basaltic volcanism |
| Ocean–continent convergence | oceanic plate subducts | trench + continental volcanic arc/fold belt | shallow→deep quakes; explosive arc volcanism |
| Ocean–ocean convergence | one oceanic plate subducts | trench + island arc | shallow→deep quakes; volcanic arcs |
| Continent–continent convergence | crust thickens | major fold mountains/plateaux | strong quakes; limited arc volcanism after collision |
| Transform | no net creation/destruction | strike-slip fault | shallow earthquakes; little volcanism |

##### Rocks + weathering

- **Igneous:** cooling/crystallization of magma/lava; primary rocks.
  - intrusive: slow cooling → coarse crystals (granite, gabbro);
  - extrusive: rapid cooling → fine/glassy (basalt, rhyolite/andesite).
- **Sedimentary:** weathering → transport → deposition → compaction/cementation (lithification).
  - clastic/mechanical: sandstone, shale, conglomerate;
  - chemical: rock salt/halite, gypsum, some limestone/chert, geyserite;
  - organic/biogenic: coal, chalk, many limestones.
- **Metamorphic:** solid-state mineral/textural change by heat, pressure and fluids without full melting.
  - limestone → marble; sandstone → quartzite; shale/mudstone → slate → phyllite/schist with increasing grade; granite → gneiss under suitable conditions.
  - do **not** use “coal → diamond” as a routine metamorphic pathway.
- **Weathering**
  - physical: thermal expansion, frost action, salt crystallization, pressure release etc.;
  - chemical: solution, carbonation, hydrolysis, oxidation/hydration;
  - biological: roots/organisms/organic acids.
- Rainwater + atmospheric/soil CO₂ → carbonic acid → carbonation of susceptible minerals.
- Dissolved O₂ supports oxidation; hence rainfall can enhance both carbonation and oxidation.

##### Volcanism

- Magma = molten material below surface; lava = material erupted at surface.
- Eruptive products:
  - lava;
  - pyroclasts/tephra, ash, dust;
  - gases dominated by H₂O vapour with CO₂, SO₂ and other sulphur/nitrogen/halogen species.
- Shield volcano: fluid basaltic lava, broad gentle slopes, typically effusive.
- Stratovolcano/composite: more viscous/intermediate magma, layered lava/pyroclasts, potentially explosive.
- Fissure/flood-basalt eruptions → extensive lava plateaux.
- Volcano–earthquake belts strongly track active plate margins; intraplate hotspots are an exception.

##### Mountains

- **Fold mountains:** compression/folding at convergent settings — Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Appalachians (old fold).
- **Block/fault mountains:** uplifted crustal blocks bounded by faults — Vosges/Black Forest classic examples.
- UPSC 2024 elimination: Alps ≠ block; Vosges ≠ fold; Appalachians and Andes are fold mountains.

##### Earth history / geological time

- Hierarchy: **Eon → Era → Period → Epoch → Age**.
- Modern broad boundaries for elimination:
  - Hadean: ~4.54–4.0 Ga
  - Archean: 4.0–2.5 Ga
  - Proterozoic: 2.5–0.541 Ga
  - Phanerozoic: ~541 Ma–present
    - Paleozoic: ~541–252 Ma
    - Mesozoic: ~252–66 Ma
    - Cenozoic: 66 Ma–present
  - Quaternary: ~2.58 Ma–present
  - Holocene: ~11.7 ka–present
- Early atmosphere: essentially no free O₂; volcanic outgassing supplied H₂O/CO₂/N₂ etc.
- Photosynthetic microbes progressively oxygenated ocean/atmosphere; living organisms materially altered atmospheric composition.
- Geomagnetic reversals occur at **irregular intervals**; palaeomagnetism preserves their record.
- Use Earth-history questions for broad chronology/process; avoid obsolete textbook boundary precision.

#### E. Oceanography & Hydrosphere

##### Global water distribution

- Oceans cover ~71% of Earth’s surface.
- Roughly ~97% of Earth’s water is saline ocean water; freshwater ≈2.5–3%.
- Of freshwater:
  - ice caps/glaciers ≈69%;
  - groundwater ≈30%;
  - surface/other freshwater <1%.
- Hence:
  - **largest freshwater store** = ice/glaciers;
  - **largest non-ice readily stored freshwater reservoir** = groundwater.

##### Ocean temperature / density / salinity

- Surface temperature generally decreases equator → poles, modified by currents, winds, seasons and enclosure.
- Typical vertical thermal structure in low/mid latitudes:
  1. warm surface/mixed layer;
  2. **thermocline** = rapid temperature change with depth;
  3. cold deep layer.
- Do not apply freshwater-lake `4°C maximum density` rule as a general seawater rule; seawater density depends jointly on temperature, salinity and pressure.
- **Average open-ocean salinity:** ~35‰ (PSU practical usage).
- Controls: evaporation vs precipitation, river runoff, ice freezing/melting, currents/mixing.
  - subtropical belts: generally high salinity (strong evaporation + low precipitation);
  - equatorial belt: relatively lower than subtropics because rainfall is high;
  - polar seas: often lower surface salinity through low evaporation + ice melt/freshwater input.
- Property-gradient terms:
  - **Thermocline** → temperature
  - **Halocline** → salinity
  - **Pycnocline** → density
- A halocline means rapid salinity change with depth; direction is not part of the definition.

##### Ocean-current controls

- Direct/primary controls:
  - prevailing winds;
  - pressure-gradient/gravity from sea-surface slope;
  - Coriolis/Earth rotation;
  - temperature/salinity-driven density contrasts for deep circulation;
  - basin/coast configuration modifies pathways.
- Earth’s **revolution** is not a direct current-driving force.
- Surface gyres:
  - generally clockwise NH, anticlockwise SH under wind + Coriolis.
- Broad thermal tendency:
  - western-boundary currents often warm/poleward;
  - eastern-boundary currents often cold/equatorward;
  - use named-current knowledge rather than treating tendency as an exceptionless law.

##### Equatorial currents / fishing

- North/South Equatorial Currents driven westward by trade winds pile water in western basins.
- Resulting eastward pressure-gradient return flow contributes to **Equatorial Counter Current**.
- Productive fishing zones often occur:
  - where warm/cold currents meet;
  - in coastal upwelling zones where nutrient-rich deep water reaches photic layer.
- Current convergence may also favour fog where warm moist air crosses cold water.

##### Deep circulation

- **Thermohaline circulation:** density-driven circulation from temperature + salinity differences.
- Cold/salty high-latitude water becomes dense and sinks; deep water is redistributed through connected basins.
- Pycnocline inhibits vertical mixing where strongly developed.

##### Tides

- Tides = periodic sea-level oscillations mainly from differential lunar gravity, with solar gravity and the inertial/centrifugal response of the Earth–Moon system.
- Moon is dominant tide-generating body because tidal force depends strongly on distance.
- **Spring tide:** Sun–Earth–Moon nearly aligned (new/full moon) → larger tidal range.
- **Neap tide:** Sun and Moon approximately at right angles (quarter phases) → smaller tidal range.
- **Semi-diurnal:** two high + two low tides per lunar day; successive highs roughly **12 h 25 min** apart, not exactly 12 h or 12 h 30 min.
- Diurnal/mixed patterns depend on basin geometry, latitude and declination.
- Local tidal magnitude is strongly altered by coastline/bathymetry/funnel-shaped bays.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **Border vs coast vs opens out to:** a country can border an inland sea yet remain landlocked (Kazakhstan–Caspian).
- **Sea-border distractor:** Croatia–Adriatic, not Black; Syria–Mediterranean, not Black/Red; Jordan–Gulf of Aqaba access, not Mediterranean.
- **Route trap:** Hormuz is unavoidable for Persian Gulf ports; Bab-el-Mandeb is the Red Sea’s southern gateway; Suez is a canal, not a strait.
- **City sequence:** latitude reasoning beats memorized order; Jakarta/Singapore/Bangkok/Hanoi is a recurring template.
- **Mountain range vs region:** Atlas = NW Africa; Alps = fold mountains; Pyrenees = Spain–France; “Balkans” region ≠ only Balkan Mountains.
- **River source vs lake outflow:** Lake Baikal → **Angara**; Lena rises in the Baikal Range, not Lake Baikal.
- **River mouth:** Volga→Caspian; Zambezi→Indian Ocean; Mekong→South China Sea; Mississippi→Gulf of Mexico.
- **Lake superlatives:** distinguish surface area / freshwater / volume / depth / desert lake / navigability; avoid unqualified “largest”.
- **Polar:** Maitri+Bharati operational Antarctic; Dakshin Gangotri historical; Himadri+IndARC Arctic.
- **Producer vs reserves:** Venezuela oil reserves ≠ top oil production; Lithium Triangle resources ≠ top mine-output order.
- **Mine production vs processing/refining:** especially cobalt/REEs; question wording decides the answer.
- **Grade vs benchmark:** Brent/WTI are crude grades/benchmarks, not oilfields or companies.
- **Sweet crude:** low sulfur, not zero sulfur.
- **Battery:** graphite generally anode; lithium/nickel/cobalt can be cathode inputs depending chemistry.
- **REE count:** 17, not 13; “rare” does not mean geographically unique to China.
- **Dynamic rank trap:** coffee/steel/mineral rankings must carry a reference year.
- **Scope trap:** ecology of lakes/polar ecosystems → Environment; farming technique → Agriculture; Arctic Council/GCC/IR conflict claims → IR.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. India: Drainage, Lakes & River Projects

##### 1) Drainage logic

| Feature | Himalayan rivers | Peninsular rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Age/relief | young mountains; active uplift | old stable block |
| Water | generally perennial: snowmelt + rain | mainly rain-fed; more seasonal variability |
| Valley | deep gorges in mountains; meanders/floodplains downstream | broad/shallow valleys generally; structural control locally strong |
| Course | antecedence + river capture common | more fixed/graded courses |
| Mouth | large deltas common in Ganga–Brahmaputra; Indus delta outside India | east-flowing mostly deltas; many west-flowing short rivers form estuaries |

**Drainage-pattern diagnostics**
- Dendritic → homogeneous structure/gentle slope.
- Trellis → alternating resistant/weak strata; tributaries join main stream at near-right angles.
- Rectangular → joints/faults strongly control course.
- Radial → streams diverge from a central highland; **Amarkantak** is a classic Indian anchor.
- Centripetal → streams converge into inland basin/lake; Rajasthan examples.
- Antecedent → river predates uplift and cuts across rising relief; **Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra** are core examples.

##### 2) Indus system

- **Indus:** Tibetan Plateau near Kailash–Mansarovar region → Ladakh → Pakistan → Arabian Sea.
- Major Punjab-system rivers: **Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej**.
- **Jhelum:** Verinag spring → Kashmir Valley → **Wular Lake**.
- **Chenab:** Chandra + Bhaga meet at **Tandi**; Bara Shigri Glacier feeds Chandra system.
- **Beas:** Beas Kund/Rohtang region → joins Sutlej at **Harike**.
- **Sutlej:** Tibetan origin → enters India through **Shipki La**; antecedent/trans-Himalayan.
- **Siachen Glacier → Nubra → Shyok → Indus**.
- Confluence logic tested by UPSC: Jhelum/Ravi/Beas ultimately feed the Chenab/Sutlej–Panjnad hierarchy before Indus.

**Reservoir/project anchors**
- Bhakra–Nangal → Sutlej; Gobind Sagar reservoir.
- Pong/Pandoh → Beas system (**Pandoh is not on Ravi**).
- Baglihar/Salal → Chenab.

##### 3) Ganga system

**Headstreams / Panch Prayag**
- Bhagirathi → Gaumukh at Gangotri Glacier.
- Alaknanda → upper Garhwal, traditionally traced to Satopanth/Bhagirath Kharak glacier system.
- **Vishnuprayag:** Alaknanda + Dhauliganga.
- **Nandprayag:** Alaknanda + Nandakini.
- **Karnaprayag:** Alaknanda + Pindar.
- **Rudraprayag:** Alaknanda + Mandakini.
- **Devprayag:** Alaknanda + Bhagirathi → **Ganga**.

**Major tributaries**
- Left-bank/northern: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara/Karnali, Gandak, Kosi.
- Right-bank/southern: Yamuna, Son; Damodar reaches lower Ganga/Hooghly system.
- **Downstream of Prayagraj, west→east:** **Gomti → Ghaghara → Gandak → Kosi**.
- **Ramganga:** Garhwal/Kumaon region → enters plains → joins Ganga near Kannauj.
- **Gomti:** near Pilibhit → Lucknow → joins Ganga in eastern UP.
- **Ghaghara/Karnali:** Tibetan/Himalayan origin via Nepal; major Ganga tributary.
- **Gandak/Narayani:** Nepal Himalaya → Bihar.
- **Kosi:** trans-Himalayan/Himalayan tributary system; major avulsion/flood history.
- **Yamuna:** Yamunotri Glacier/Bandarpunch massif → joins Ganga at Prayagraj; major right-bank tributaries include Chambal, Sindh, Betwa, Ken.

##### 4) Brahmaputra system

- Tibetan course = **Yarlung Tsangpo**; eastward along Tibet.
- At eastern Himalayan syntaxis near **Namcha Barwa**, takes a dramatic bend, cuts gorge, enters India as **Siang/Dihang**.
- In Assam, major tributaries include **Dibang, Lohit, Subansiri, Kameng/Jia Bharali, Manas** etc.
- Siang/Dihang + Dibang + Lohit form the Brahmaputra in Assam usage.
- Extremely high sediment load, braided channels, shifting course/floodplain character.
- **Teesta** rises in Sikkim Himalaya and joins Brahmaputra system in Bangladesh; **Rangeet** is a tributary of Teesta.

##### 5) Peninsular rivers

**East-flowing — Bay of Bengal**
- **Mahanadi:** Sihawa highlands → Chhattisgarh–Odisha → delta.
- **Godavari:** Trimbakeshwar/Nashik region → largest Peninsular basin; major tributaries include Pranhita, Indravati, Manjira, Sabari.
- **Krishna:** near Mahabaleshwar → Maharashtra–Karnataka–Telangana/Andhra region; Bhima, Tungabhadra, Ghataprabha/Malaprabha system.
- **Kaveri/Cauvery:** Talakaveri, Brahmagiri Hills → Karnataka–Tamil Nadu → delta.
- **Pennar:** peninsular east-flowing; forms **Gandikota canyon**.
- **Brahmani–Baitarani:** Odisha coast; Bhitarkanika deltaic/mangrove region nearby.
- **Nagavali + Vamsadhara** rise in Eastern Ghats; **Subarnarekha does not originate in Eastern Ghats**.

**West-flowing / Arabian Sea**
- **Narmada:** Amarkantak → rift valley between Vindhya–Satpura → estuary in Gulf of Khambhat.
- **Tapi/Tapti:** Multai region → rift/structural trough → Gulf of Khambhat.
- **Mahi:** crosses Tropic of Cancer twice; Arabian Sea/Gulf of Khambhat.
- **Sabarmati:** Aravalli region → Gulf of Khambhat.
- **Luni:** Aravalli region → inland/saline drainage toward Rann of Kutch; lower course becomes saline.
- **Periyar** → Arabian Sea; Pennar/Palar are east-flowing.

**Narmada trap:** west flow is best explained by **rift-valley/structural control**, not merely “Central India slopes west”.

##### 10) Groundwater + interlinking — durable layer

- Groundwater availability depends strongly on **porosity/permeability + aquifer thickness + recharge**, not rainfall alone.
- Indo-Gangetic alluvium = high storage/yield but heavy abstraction; hard-rock Peninsular aquifers = more limited/fracture-controlled storage.
- Excess riverbed sand mining can lower riverbed/water-table connectivity and degrade groundwater quality/quantity.
- India has very large groundwater-irrigated area and withdrawal; retain the **structural fact**, not transient annual percentages.
- River interlinking = inter-basin transfer from perceived surplus to deficit basins; permanent layer should retain hydrological logic/trade-offs, not project-of-the-month status.

#### B. India: Physiography, Islands & Political Map

##### 1) Physiographic architecture

**Six high-yield divisions**
1. Himalayan Mountains.
2. Northern/Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Plains.
3. Peninsular Plateau.
4. Indian Desert.
5. Coastal Plains.
6. Islands.

**Young vs old contrast**
- Himalaya = young fold mountains, tectonically active, steep gradients, deep gorges, landslides, ongoing uplift.
- Peninsular Block = ancient, stable shield; residual hills, plateaus, broad valleys; locally faulted/rifted.

##### 2) Himalaya — transverse + longitudinal framework

**North→south**
- Trans-Himalaya: Karakoram, Ladakh, Zanskar.
- Greater Himalaya / Himadri: highest, most continuous; major snowfields/glaciers.
- Lesser Himalaya / Himachal: Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar etc.; many valleys/hill stations.
- Shiwalik: youngest outer belt; unconsolidated sediments; duns between Shiwalik and Lesser Himalaya.

**West→east / regional anchors**
- Kashmir/Punjab Himalaya → Himachal/Uttarakhand (Kumaon) → Nepal → Assam/eastern Himalaya.
- Eastern syntaxis: **Namcha Barwa**; western syntaxis: **Nanga Parbat** region.
- Purvanchal/eastern hills: Patkai–Naga–Manipur–Mizo/Lushai trends.

**Young-fold evidence**
- deep gorges;
- antecedent/U-turning rivers;
- parallel ranges;
- steep unstable slopes/landslides;
- earthquakes + active convergence.

##### 3) Major peaks/ranges — elimination anchors

- **Nanda Devi** → Kumaon Himalaya/Uttarakhand.
- **Namcha Barwa** → eastern Himalaya, not Garhwal.
- **Nokrek** → Garo Hills, Meghalaya; not Sikkim Himalaya.
- **Amarkantak** → Maikal/eastern Satpura–Vindhyan transition; source region for Narmada/Son.
- **Mahadeo Hills** → central India/Satpura system.
- **Mikir Hills** → Assam/NE India.
- **Seshachalam** → Eastern Ghats, Andhra Pradesh; not Western Ghats.
- **Biligirirangan (B.R.) Hills** → SE Karnataka, ecological/relief link zone near Western–Eastern Ghats; not Satpura.
- **Aravalli** → very old residual/folded system, SW–NE Rajasthan–Haryana/Delhi direction.
- **Vindhya** north of Narmada; **Satpura** south of Narmada.

##### 4) Northern Plains

- Built by Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra alluvium; flat, deep alluvial cover.
- Foothill belts:
  - **Bhabar:** coarse porous deposits; streams often disappear.
  - **Terai:** marshy/wet belt immediately south; streams re-emerge; high groundwater.
- Alluvial age:
  - **Bhangar:** older alluvium; above active floodplain; kankar/calcareous nodules common.
  - **Khadar:** newer alluvium; floodplain, frequently renewed.

##### 5) Peninsular Plateau

- Ancient crystalline block; broad division into Central Highlands + Deccan Plateau.
- Central Highlands: Malwa, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chota Nagpur connections.
- Deccan Plateau: basaltic Deccan Trap in west-central India; triangular plateau south of Narmada.
- Western Ghats/Sahyadri = higher, more continuous escarpment; strong orographic barrier.
- Eastern Ghats = lower, discontinuous, dissected by east-flowing rivers.
- 2026 UPSC logic: parts of **western coast show submergence/tectonic effects**; residual ranges such as **Veliconda and Mahendragiri** belong Peninsular Block; generalized deep V-shaped valleys are not its defining character.

##### 6) Coastal Plains

| West coast | East coast |
|---|---|
| narrow, often submergent/estuarine | broader, large deltas common |
| Konkan–Kanara/Karnataka–Malabar | Northern Circars–Coromandel |
| short swift rivers | long east-flowing rivers build deltas |
| lagoons/backwaters prominent in Kerala | Chilika/Pulicat and major deltas |

- **Chandipur, Odisha:** very large tidal recession exposes sea floor twice daily.
- **Rann of Kutch:** extensive salt-marsh landscape linked to sea-level/coastal change and arid sedimentation.

##### 7) Islands and channels

**Andaman & Nicobar**
- Bay of Bengal; tectonic/island-arc affinity; continuation toward Myanmar–Indonesia arc.
- **Ten Degree Channel:** separates Andaman group from Nicobar group.
- **Duncan Passage:** South Andaman/Little Andaman sector.
- **Great Channel:** south of Great Nicobar toward Sumatra region.
- **Barren Island:** India’s only confirmed active volcano; lies in Andaman Sea east of main Andaman chain. Do **not** place it near Great Nicobar.

**Lakshadweep**

- **Nine Degree Channel** separates Minicoy from rest of Lakshadweep group.

- **Khardung La / Chang La:** Ladakh.

#### C. India: Climate, Soils & Natural Vegetation

##### 1) Indian monsoon — durable mechanism

- India = monsoon climate: **seasonal reversal of winds**.
- Core summer mechanism:
  - strong land heating + northward ITCZ/monsoon trough;
  - cross-equatorial flow from southern Indian Ocean;
  - Somali/low-level jet + Arabian Sea moisture;
  - Bay of Bengal branch deflected by relief/Himalaya;
  - Tibetan Plateau + upper-air circulation/jet-stream changes contribute.
- SW monsoon branches:
  - **Arabian Sea branch:** heavy windward Western Ghats rainfall; sharp rain shadow inland; one stream enters Narmada–Tapi corridor; Aravalli orientation limits orographic rain in western Rajasthan.
  - **Bay of Bengal branch:** NE India/Meghalaya high rainfall; then westward along Ganga plain with rainfall generally decreasing east→west.
- **Duration of monsoon decreases from south to north** because onset is earlier in south and withdrawal later there.
- **Tamil Nadu during SW monsoon:** relatively dry because coast is parallel to Bay branch + rain-shadow of Western Ghats; substantial rain arrives with **NE/retreating monsoon**.
- **Winter NW India:** western disturbances bring rain/snow; influence/rainfall generally weakens eastward.
- **IOD:** west–east Indian Ocean SST gradient; can modulate ENSO impact on Indian monsoon. Do not define eastern pole as “eastern Pacific”.

##### 2) Local summer/pre-monsoon weather

- **Loo:** hot, dry wind over north/northwest plains; Punjab–Haryana–Rajasthan–UP–Bihar belt.
- **Nor’westers / Kal Baisakhi:** violent pre-monsoon storms of Bengal/Assam/eastern India.
- **Mango showers:** Kerala/coastal Karnataka (and adjoining south) pre-monsoon rain aiding mango ripening.
- **Blossom showers:** coffee-growing Karnataka/Kerala belt; aid coffee blossom.

##### 3) Soil formation — diagnostic framework

**Controls:** parent material + climate + relief/drainage + organisms/vegetation + time.

| Soil | Diagnostic traits | Main distribution / trap |
|---|---|---|
| Alluvial | transported; variable sand–silt–clay; generally fertile | Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains + deltas; khadar=new, bhangar=old |
| Black / regur | clayey, high moisture retention, cracks; rich Ca/Mg/iron, poor N/P; derived largely from Deccan basalt | Maharashtra–MP–Gujarat–Telangana/Deccan; **not Himalayan** |
| Red & yellow | iron oxidation gives red colour; generally low humus/N/P | eastern/southern Peninsular crystalline regions; surrounds parts of black-soil belt |
| Laterite | intense leaching under high temp + heavy rain; Fe/Al rich, N/P/K poor | Western/Eastern Ghats high-rainfall uplands, parts NE; cashew/tapioca with management |
| Arid/desert | sandy, low humus/moisture, often saline/alkaline | western Rajasthan/Gujarat; kankar at depth common |
| Forest/mountain | varies strongly with altitude/slope/vegetation | Himalayan/mountain belts |
| Saline/alkaline | soluble salts/sodium accumulation; poor drainage/evaporation | arid/semi-arid + poorly drained/irrigated tracts |
| Peaty/marshy | high organic matter, waterlogged, often acidic | Kerala marshes, Sundarbans/deltaic and other wet pockets |

**UPSC trap:** laterite is **not** rich in nitrogen/potash and is **not** characteristically developed in Rajasthan/UP.

##### 4) Natural vegetation — distribution logic

| Forest type | Climate/diagnostic | Core Indian distribution | Key species/recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical wet evergreen | very high rain, no long dry season; multi-storeyed | Western Ghats windward pockets, NE India, A&N | ebony/rosewood/mesua etc.; dense evergreen canopy |
| Tropical semi-evergreen | transition evergreen↔deciduous | Western Ghats/NE/A&N transition belts | mixed evergreen + deciduous |
| Tropical moist deciduous | ~100–200 cm rain, seasonal leaf fall | Himalayan foothills/Terai, central/eastern India, parts Ghats | **teak** dominant in many peninsular tracts; sal in north/east |
| Tropical dry deciduous | lower rain, longer dry season | broad interior peninsular/northern belt | teak/sal associates; open canopy |
| Tropical thorn/scrub | low rainfall | Rajasthan–Gujarat–SW Punjab/Haryana + rain-shadow interiors | babul/acacia/euphorbia-type xerophytes |
| Montane | changes with altitude | Himalaya + higher southern hills | subtropical → temperate conifers/broadleaf → alpine scrub/meadows |
| Littoral/mangrove | tidal saline intertidal | Sundarbans + Mahanadi/Godavari/Krishna/Kaveri deltas; A&N; Gujarat etc. | sundari/rhizophora/avicennia-type mangroves |

**PYQ anchors**
- A&N can show **mangrove + evergreen + deciduous** within one regional complex.
- Tropical wet evergreen occurs in Arunachal + Mizoram; not Himachal Pradesh.
- **Teak → tropical moist deciduous** in UPSC framing.
- Himalayan vegetation changes with altitude because temperature/moisture regimes change with elevation/aspect.
- Dynamic forest-cover rankings from ISFR: keep in a current-affairs layer only.

#### D. Uttar Pradesh Geography

##### 1) Position + boundaries

- UP lies primarily in the **Ganga–Yamuna–Ghaghara plain**, with a southern upland/plateau fringe.
- International boundary: **Nepal** in the north/northeast.
- Indian neighbours: **Uttarakhand, Haryana, NCT Delhi, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Bihar**.
- **No direct UP–Himachal Pradesh border.**
- UP’s high number of neighbours makes district/state adjacency a recurrent elimination theme.

##### 2) Physiographic regions

**A. Terai / northern foothill belt**
- Along Nepal-border districts; high groundwater, marshy/alluvial character; Sharda/Ghaghara/Rapti systems.
- Forest/wetland concentration higher than in intensively cultivated central plains.

**B. Ganga Plain**
- Dominant state region; deep alluvium.
- Useful exam subdivision:
  - Western UP / Upper Ganga–Yamuna Doab + Rohilkhand.
  - Central UP / Awadh.
  - Eastern UP / Purvanchal–Middle Ganga plain transition.
- `UPPCS-2024-GS1-Q041`: Upper Gangetic region includes central + western UP; exceptionally high irrigation/cropping intensity is a regional signature.

**C. Southern uplands**
- **Bundelkhand** in SW UP: hard-rock/upland, dissected terrain, lower/variable water availability.
- **Vindhyan/Baghelkhand fringe** in SE, especially Mirzapur–Sonbhadra: plateau/escarpment, forests, minerals/river valleys.

##### 3) Rivers of UP — map first

- **Ganga:** enters western UP after Uttarakhand; traverses state west→east.
- **Yamuna:** forms/approaches western boundary sectors; flows through/near Mathura–Agra–Etawah; joins Ganga at Prayagraj.
- **Ramganga:** northern/western UP → Ganga near Kannauj.
- **Gomti:** Pilibhit region → Lucknow → eastern UP → Ganga.
- **Ghaghara/Karnali:** major transboundary Nepal–UP river; eastern UP floodplain.
- **Rapti:** Nepal/Terai → eastern UP → Ghaghara system.
- **Sharda/Kali:** Nepal-border/terai system; joins Ghaghara system.
- **Chambal:** joins Yamuna near Etawah; ravines in SW UP–MP–Rajasthan regional belt.
- **Betwa + Ken:** Bundelkhand rivers → Yamuna; **Betwa does not join Yamuna near Prayagraj**.
- **Rihand/Kanhar:** Son-basin tributary geography in Sonbhadra/Vindhyan SE.

##### 4) Climate

- Predominantly **humid subtropical monsoon**, with continentality increasing inland.
- Hot summer; **loo** common in plains.
- SW monsoon supplies most annual rain; broad rainfall tendency **east > west**.
- Winter rain from western disturbances, more important in western/northwestern UP.
- Eastern UP = more flood-prone due Ghaghara/Rapti/Gandak-adjacent alluvial systems; Bundelkhand = recurrent moisture stress/drought vulnerability.

##### 5) Soils

- **Alluvial soils dominate**: khadar in active floodplains; bhangar on older terraces/interfluves.
- Western/central/eastern plains show variations in texture, drainage, salinity/alkalinity and flood renewal.
- **Usar/reh** = saline–alkaline patches of the plains; drainage/reclamation is key physical constraint.
- Southern Bundelkhand/Vindhyan areas have **red/yellow, mixed red-black and skeletal/upland soils** alongside alluvial valleys.

##### 6) Natural vegetation / forest pattern

- Original plain vegetation heavily transformed by cultivation/settlement.
- **Terai belt:** moist deciduous/sal-rich forest and wet grassland mosaics.
- **Southern Vindhyan/Sonbhadra–Chandauli belt:** dry/moist deciduous forest concentration.
- District-level forest percentages in 2023/2025 PYQs are **report-year facts**, not permanent payload.

##### 7) UP protected/wetland location anchors — location only

- Dudhwa landscape also includes Kishanpur/Katarniaghat protected-area complex in Terai geography; conservation mechanisms belong Environment.
- **Chandraprabha Wildlife Sanctuary → Chandauli**.
- **Sarsai Nawar Jheel → UP**.
- **Samaspur Bird Sanctuary → UP**.
- **Sur Sarovar (Keetham) → Agra, UP**.
- **Rudrasagar Lake → Tripura**, not UP.
- **Surinsar–Mansar → Jammu region**, not UP.
- **Sultanpur National Park → Haryana**, not UP.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. India: Minerals, Energy & Industry

##### A1. Mineral classification — keep categories separate

| Classification basis | Categories | High-yield point |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical/economic geography** | Metallic / non-metallic | Metallic → metal-yielding ores; non-metallic → mica, limestone etc.; fossil fuels often treated separately as mineral fuels |
| **Metallic** | Ferrous / non-ferrous | Ferrous: iron-bearing/iron-steel-linked; non-ferrous: copper, bauxite/aluminium, lead, zinc etc. |
| **Origin/resource use** | Mineral fuels / atomic-energy minerals / industrial minerals | Coal, petroleum, gas ≠ metallic minerals; uranium/thorium ≠ “fossil fuel” |
| **MMDR legal category** | Minor mineral / other (“major” in exam language) | **Legal designation, not size/value/geology.** Central Government can notify minor minerals; State Governments make rules for minor-mineral concessions |
| **Supply-security category** | Critical / strategic minerals | Defined by economic/strategic importance + supply vulnerability; list can change with policy/technology |

**Legal anchors**
- MMDR Act definition of **minor minerals** expressly includes building stone, gravel, ordinary clay, ordinary sand (subject to prescribed-use qualification) + any mineral **notified by Central Government**.
- **State Governments** have rule-making power for grant of minor-mineral concessions; they also frame anti-illegal-mining rules under the Act.
- **DMF**: statutory district-level foundation for benefit/interests of persons and areas affected by mining; **not** mineral-exploration or licensing authority.
- Individual minerals can be shifted by notification between legal categories → **year-tag legal-list PYQs**; never equate “major” with “important”.
- 2020 PYQ-era elimination: **chromite, kyanite, sillimanite = major; bentonite = minor**. Treat as PYQ-era classification; later notifications can alter lists.

##### A2. India’s broad mineral geography

**Why peninsular India dominates**
- Old crystalline/igneous-metamorphic shield → metallic minerals.
- Sedimentary basins → coal, petroleum, natural gas, limestone etc.
- Weathered plateau surfaces → bauxite/lateritic ores.
- Coastal placer concentration → heavy beach-sand minerals.

**Three classic mineral belts**

| Belt | Core region | Durable mineral association |
|---|---|---|
| **North-Eastern Plateau belt** | Chota Nagpur + Odisha Plateau + W Bengal + Chhattisgarh | Iron ore, coal, manganese, bauxite, mica; strongest mineral-linked iron-steel concentration |
| **South-Western Plateau belt** | Karnataka + Goa + adjoining TN uplands + Kerala | High-grade iron ore, manganese, bauxite, limestone; Neyveli lignite in TN; Kerala/coastal belt important for monazite-heavy minerals |
| **North-Western belt** | Aravalli Rajasthan + Gujarat | Copper, lead-zinc, building stones, gypsum, limestone/dolomite; petroleum in Gujarat sedimentary basins |

**Supplementary zones**
- Himalayan belt → scattered copper, lead-zinc, cobalt/tungsten etc.; not India’s main bulk-metal belt.
- Assam + Gujarat + western offshore + east-coast sedimentary basins → petroleum/natural gas.

##### A3. Rock system ↔ resource associations

| Rock/geological setting | Resource association | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Archaean/Dharwar crystalline rocks** | Iron, manganese, gold, copper and other metallic mineralisation | **Not petroleum** |
| **Gondwana sedimentary basins** | Main coal-bearing formations | Do not generalise as natural-gas host in the classic PYQ formulation |
| **Tertiary sediments** | Younger coal in NE; major petroleum/gas-bearing sedimentary basins | Tertiary coal generally lower rank than Gondwana bituminous/coking fields |
| **Vindhyan sedimentary rocks** | Limestone, sandstone, building material | Limestone → cement linkage |
| **Coastal placer sands** | Ilmenite, rutile, monazite, zircon, garnet, sillimanite | Occurrence is patchy, **not entire coast** |

**PYQ anchors**
- **Koderma (Jharkhand) → mica**.
- **Dharwar → metallic mineralisation**, not petroleum.
- **Gondwana → coal**, not a blanket natural-gas association.

##### A4. High-yield mine / mineral / state map

**Iron ore**
- **Odisha:** Gurumahisani/Badampahar (Mayurbhanj); Keonjhar–Sundargarh belt.
- **Jharkhand:** Noamundi, Gua/Singhbhum belt.
- **Chhattisgarh:** Bailadila, Dalli–Rajhara.
- **Karnataka:** Kudremukh; Ballari–Hospet/Sandur; Baba Budan hills.
- **Rajasthan has iron-ore occurrences/mines** → reject “Rajasthan has no iron ore”.

**Copper**
- Singhbhum (Jharkhand); Balaghat/Malanjkhand belt (MP); Khetri–Jhunjhunu/Alwar (Rajasthan).

**Mica**
- Koderma–Hazaribagh belt (Jharkhand); Nellore belt (Andhra Pradesh); Rajasthan mica belt.

**Bauxite**
- Plateau/lateritic association; Odisha is a major belt; also Gujarat, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, MP and parts of south India.
- Production ranking = **year-sensitive**; keep spatial belt, not rank.

**Manganese**
- Strong association with Dharwar-type formations; important belts in Odisha, Karnataka, MP/Maharashtra.

**Limestone**
- Widespread sedimentary resource; cement industry follows limestone belts; Vindhyan/Cuddapah-type sedimentary terrains important.

##### A5. Coal — geology, quality, fields, imports

**Coal rank sequence:** peat → lignite → bituminous → anthracite (increasing carbon/heat value broadly; decreasing moisture/volatile matter broadly).

**Indian coal structure**
- Bulk of India’s coal resources → **Gondwana** basins of peninsular India.
- **Tertiary coal** → mainly NE India; generally younger.
- Indian coal commonly tested as **high ash + low sulphur**.
- Coking coal = metallurgical coal needed for coke/steel; quality domestic coking coal is relatively constrained → persistent import requirement.

**Major field–state anchors**

| Coalfield / belt | State / region | Recall hook |
|---|---|---|
| Raniganj | West Bengal (extends belt context into Jharkhand region) | Earliest commercial coal mining in India; Damodar valley |
| Jharia | Jharkhand | Major coking-coal field; Damodar valley |
| Bokaro | Jharkhand | Damodar valley |
| North/South Karanpura | Jharkhand | Damodar system |
| Ramgarh | Jharkhand | UPPCS pair |
| Talcher | Odisha | Major Odisha coalfield; Brahmani/Mahanadi coal region context |
| Ib Valley | Odisha | Mahanadi basin |
| Korba | Chhattisgarh | Hasdeo/Mahanadi basin; thermal-power/industry linkage |
| Singrauli | MP–UP | Son/Rihand system; major coal–power complex |
| Singareni/Godavari Valley | Telangana | Godavari basin |
| Namchik–Namphuk | Arunachal Pradesh | Tertiary coal; NE |
| Mawlong | Meghalaya | **Not Arunachal Pradesh** |
| Neyveli | Tamil Nadu | Lignite |

**Coal import logic**
- “Large reserves” ≠ self-sufficiency in required grades/locations.
- Imports driven by: coking-coal quality/availability for steel; domestic supply-demand mismatch; plant-specific quality/logistics/commercial needs.
- **India continues to import coal**; “now fully self-sufficient” is an unsafe absolute.

**Nationalisation / allocation**
- Coking coal mines nationalised 1971–72; non-coking coal nationalised in 1973.
- Modern coal-block allocation uses statutory **auction/allocation mechanisms**, not a general lottery system.

**Coal Controller’s Organization — tested functions**
- Major government source of coal statistics.
- Monitors development of captive coal/lignite blocks.
- Hears objections related to notifications for acquisition of coal-bearing areas.
- Do **not** add invented function “ensures every company delivers coal to end users within prescribed time”.

**Coal gasification**
- Coal + controlled oxygen/steam → **syngas (CO + H₂)** → chemical/feedstock routes.
- Can feed production pathways for **urea/ammonia, methanol, synthetic fuels/SNG; ethanol through syngas conversion**.
- **Nitroglycerine is not a standard coal-gasification downstream product** → 2025 elimination.

##### A6. Unconventional hydrocarbons

| Resource | Where held | Extraction/formation idea | India/PYQ anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coal-bed methane (CBM)** | Methane adsorbed in coal seams | Dewatering/pressure reduction releases gas; methane-rich but not “chemically pure” by definition | Coal-bearing basins incl. Damodar/Gondwana belts |
| **Shale gas** | Low-permeability organic-rich shale | Horizontal drilling + hydraulic fracturing typically required | Cambay, Cauvery, Krishna–Godavari all valid UPSC basins |
| **Methane hydrate** | Ice-like water lattice trapping methane | Stable at high pressure/low temperature | Continental margins/seafloor + permafrost; large methane potential |

**Traps**
- Shale gas is mainly methane-rich natural gas → **not propane + butane only**.
- CBM and shale gas are both established unconventional-gas concepts in India → reject “India has CBM but no shale-gas resources”.
- Methane hydrate ≠ conventional gas field; warming/depressurisation can destabilise hydrate.

##### A7. Petroleum and natural gas — durable spatial layer

- Petroleum/natural gas favour **sedimentary basins**, not ancient crystalline shields.
- Durable producing/prospective regions: Assam–Arakan belt; Gujarat/Cambay; Mumbai offshore; Krishna–Godavari; Cauvery.
- **Refinery location** may be oilfield-linked, port-linked, pipeline-linked or market-linked; crude can move more cheaply than multiple refined products, so major refineries also locate near ports/markets.

**UPPCS refinery pairs**
- Nayara Energy **Vadinar → Gujarat**.
- Manali refinery → **Tamil Nadu**.
- Numaligarh → **Assam**.
- Tatipaka → **Andhra Pradesh**.

##### A8. Atomic + beach-sand minerals

**Beach-sand mineral suite**
- **Ilmenite + rutile → titanium** feedstocks.
- **Monazite → thorium + rare-earth elements**.
- Zircon → zirconium-bearing mineral.
- Garnet + sillimanite → important industrial heavy minerals.

**Distribution**
- Heavy-mineral placer sands occur in selected coastal stretches on **both east and west coasts**.
- Strong resource belts include Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha; occurrences also reported from other coastal states.
- **Not every Indian coastal sand contains monazite**.

**Regulatory distinction**
- Monazite is a prescribed atomic substance; processing/export is tightly controlled under the atomic-energy regulatory regime.

**Thorium vs uranium — UPSC elimination facts**
- India has relatively abundant thorium resources, much associated with monazite sands.
- Thorium-232 is **fertile**, not directly fissile; it breeds U-233 in reactor/fuel-cycle context.
- Thorium fuel-cycle arguments include resource abundance and potential waste/proliferation advantages; technological/fuel-cycle complexity remains.
- Commercial power reactors use **low-enriched uranium**, not 60% enrichment.

##### A9. Critical minerals / REEs — permanent concept only

- **Critical mineral** = mineral essential to economy/technology/defence/energy transition whose supply faces material disruption risk.
- Typical high-yield examples: lithium, cobalt, nickel, natural graphite, REEs, copper and selected strategic metals; official lists can change.
- **REEs = 17 elements** → 15 lanthanides + scandium + yttrium.
- Uses: permanent magnets, electronics, wind turbines/EV systems, defence, lasers, catalysts, aerospace/advanced technology.
- Battery elimination:
  - Li + Ni + Co commonly enter cathode chemistries.
  - **Graphite is characteristically an anode material**, not a usual cathode constituent.
- India is **not resource-rich in every identified critical mineral**; import dependence is itself a recurring policy driver.
- Current missions/auction powers/list size → keep as replaceable current-affairs layer, not fixed source-pack core.

##### A10. Mineral-linked industry location

**General factors:** raw material + power + water + market + transport + labour + capital + agglomeration + policy.

**Iron & steel**
- Weight-losing bulky inputs → classic pull toward iron ore + coking coal + limestone/dolomite + water + rail.
- Strong eastern/central crescent: Jharkhand–Odisha–Chhattisgarh–W Bengal.
- Representative logic:
  - Jamshedpur → Singhbhum ore + Jharia/West Bokaro coal + rail/water.
  - Bokaro/Durgapur → coalfield orientation.
  - Bhilai/Rourkela → ore/raw-material + regional-development logic.
- **Imported coking coal remains a key input** for Indian steel industry.

**Aluminium**
- Bauxite is bulky; smelting is **extremely power-intensive** → power availability is decisive.
- High-yield pairs: BALCO–Korba; HINDALCO–Renukoot; Hirakud aluminium complex–Odisha.
- MALCO is associated with **Mettur (Tamil Nadu)** → “MALCO–Chennai” trap.

**Cement**
- Limestone-oriented; coal/power + transport also important.

**Petroleum refining / petrochemicals**
- Refineries may be near crude source, ports, pipelines or large consuming/industrial markets.
- Petrochemicals cluster around refineries because refinery products form feedstocks.

##### A11. Power geography — only durable anchors

**Thermal**
- Coal-based plants gravitate toward coalfields/pitheads **or** ports/load centres depending fuel/logistics/water/market.
- Water requirement is high; some coastal plants use seawater.
- Ownership is mixed: Central/State/public + private.
- Do not accept absolutes such as “none in water-stressed districts” or “none privately owned”.

**Hydel / nuclear high-yield pairs**
- Shivanasamudra → Karnataka → hydropower.
- Kalpakkam → Tamil Nadu → nuclear.
- Kudankulam → Tamil Nadu.
- Kakrapar → Gujarat.
- Kaiga → Karnataka.
- Rawatbhata/Rajasthan Atomic Power Station → Rajasthan.
- Narora → Uttar Pradesh.
- Tarapur → Maharashtra.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **Major/minor mineral ≠ metallic/non-metallic ≠ critical/non-critical.** Three different classification axes.
- **Sand** can be a minor mineral; “minor” is a statutory label, not low economic importance.
- State power over minor-mineral concessions/rules **does not eliminate Central power to notify what counts as a minor mineral**.
- **DMF ≠ exploration agency**; DMF is mining-affected-area/people welfare institution.
- **Dharwar = metallic minerals**, not petroleum; **Gondwana = coal**, not blanket natural gas.
- **Monazite = thorium + REEs**; **ilmenite/rutile = Ti**; beach minerals are **not continuous along entire Indian coast**.
- **CBM ≠ shale gas ≠ methane hydrate**.
- Indian coal: **high ash + low sulphur**; coking-coal import need remains a major structural point.
- “India is self-sufficient in coal” → unsafe absolute; distinguish total coal production from grade-specific import need.
- Critical-mineral list/policy numbers are **dynamic**; REE count (**17**) is durable.
- Graphite usually **anode**, not cathode; Li/Ni/Co common cathode-side materials.
- Thermal power: “none use seawater / none in water-stressed areas / none private” → all classic absolute traps.
- West-coast ports often have natural-harbour advantages, but **not every west port is natural and not every east port is artificial**.
- Kolkata = **riverine Hooghly port**, hence silt/navigation constraints.
- **Kamarajar/Ennore** first major port registered as company; do not confuse with container-volume superlatives.
- **East–West Corridor = Porbandar–Silchar**; not Dibrugarh–Surat.
- Legacy NH numbers in old PYQs ≠ current NH numbering.
- Airport “greenfield”, “international”, “fully solar”, “largest” are separate attributes.
- Kavach = **indigenous ATP**; German-collaboration claim false.
- Atal Tunnel: **Pir Panjal true; world-longest-highway-tunnel absolute false**.
- Railway HQ: South Eastern Railway = **Garden Reach/Kolkata**, not Cuttack.
- Chabahar/INSTC/ASEAN cooperation → IR ownership; only route geography cross-referenced here.
- Fly ash, mercury, sand-mining ecology → Environment ownership; retain only mineral/power fact needed for PYQ elimination.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Population & Demography

##### 5A.1 Core demographic indicators

| Indicator | Exam-safe meaning | High-yield trap |
|---|---|---|
| **CBR — Crude Birth Rate** | live births in a year per **1,000 mid-year population** | not TFR; not “children/woman” |
| **CDR — Crude Death Rate** | deaths in a year per **1,000 mid-year population** | mortality measure, not IMR |
| **Natural growth** | **Births − Deaths** | excludes migration |
| **Actual population growth** | **Births − Deaths + In-migration − Out-migration** | migration can reverse natural-growth effect |
| **TFR — Total Fertility Rate** | average number of children/live births a woman would have over her reproductive life if prevailing age-specific fertility rates continue | not per 1,000 population |
| **Replacement-level fertility** | roughly **2.1** births per woman in standard exam usage | replacement ≠ zero population growth immediately; population momentum matters |
| **IMR — Infant Mortality Rate** | deaths of infants **below age 1** per **1,000 live births** in a given period | denominator = live births, not total population |
| **Sex ratio — India Census convention** | females per **1,000 males** | differs from conventions used in some countries |
| **Child sex ratio — Census usage** | girls per **1,000 boys** in age **0–6** | distinct from overall sex ratio |
| **Population density** | persons per unit area, usually persons/km² | absolute population ≠ density |
| **Dependency ratio** | dependent-age population relative to working-age population ×100 | age cut-offs vary by source |

**NCERT correction:** a printed NCERT line describes CBR as births per thousand “women”, but its own formula uses **mid-year population**. Use the standard definition: births per 1,000 population.

##### 5A.2 Population change

- **Three direct components:** births, deaths, migration.
- **Positive growth:** births > deaths and/or net in-migration sufficiently positive.
- **Negative growth:** deaths > births and/or net out-migration sufficiently large.
- **Migration:** changes spatial distribution and actual growth; does **not** enter natural growth.
- **Push factors:** unemployment, poor living conditions, conflict, disaster, epidemics, resource stress.
- **Pull factors:** jobs, education, health, amenities, security, better living conditions.

##### 5A.3 Demographic Transition — UPSC three-stage logic

| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Growth | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I — High fluctuating** | high | high | low/slow | agrarian; high mortality offsets high fertility |
| **II — Expanding** | initially high, later falling | falls rapidly | **high** | sanitation/health improve first → birth–death gap widens |
| **III — Low fluctuating** | low | low | low/stable | urban-industrial, literate; smaller family size |

**Sequence demanded by UPSC:** **High BR + High DR → High BR + Low DR → Low BR + Low DR.**

**Trap:** modern textbooks sometimes show 4- or 5-stage variants. For the canonical UPSC sequence question, the above **three-stage model** is the decisive frame.

##### 5A.4 Demographic dividend, dependency and ageing

**Demographic dividend**
- Potential economic gain when **working-age share rises relative to dependents**.
- UPSC 2011 used **15–64**; UPPCS 2022 used **15–59** → treat working-age boundaries as **source-specific**, not universal.
- Dividend is **potential**, not guaranteed.
- Conversion conditions → **health + education + skills + jobs + productive employment + female workforce participation + savings/investment + mobility**.
- UPSC 2013 direct anchor → **skill development**.

**Dependency ratio**
- Falls when working-age share rises faster than dependent-age share.
- A fall can create a demographic window; it is not itself proof of higher incomes/jobs.

**Population ageing**
- Main durable drivers → **fertility decline + increased survival/longevity**.
- Population pyramid → narrower base + larger older-age share.
- Likely consequences → old-age dependency ↑; pension/health/care burden ↑; labour-force growth may slow.
- **Ageing ≠ population decline**: a country can age while still growing.
- **Low fertility ≠ low birth rate in every period**: age structure matters.

##### 5A.5 Population pyramids — elimination frame

- **Expanding / triangular:** broad base → high fertility; large young cohort; rapid potential growth.
- **Stable / bell-like:** similar cohort widths through much of working ages → low/stable growth.
- **Declining / constrictive:** narrow base → low fertility; ageing; possible long-run decline.
- Read **shape**, not merely total height/size.

##### 5A.6 Malthus and population theory

**Malthus**
- Population tends to increase **geometrically/exponentially**.
- Food supply tends to increase **arithmetically/linearly**.
- Disequilibrium → checks on population growth.
- **Preventive checks:** restrain births.
- **Positive checks:** raise mortality (famine, disease, war in classical framing).

**UPPCS matching demand**

- Stable theorist anchors commonly used in this question family:
  - **Demographic Transition Theory — Warren Thompson**.
  - **Optimum Population Theory — Edwin Cannan**.
  - **Malthus — population vs food-supply theory**.

##### 5A.8 Urbanisation and settlement

**Rural vs urban**
- No universally valid global population-size threshold.
- Functional distinction is often more useful:
  - rural → primary activities dominant;
  - urban → secondary/tertiary functions dominant.

**India — Census-town logic**
- **Statutory town:** municipality/corporation/cantonment board/notified urban authority → urban by administrative status.
- **Census town:** all key demographic/occupational criteria:
  - population **≥ 5,000**;
  - density **≥ 400 persons/km²**;
  - **≥ 75% of male main working population** in non-agricultural pursuits.
- **City:** Census usage commonly treats towns with population **≥ 100,000** as cities.

**Urbanisation curve**
- Stage 1 → initial/slow.
- **Stage 2 → acceleration**.
- Later stage → deceleration/saturation as urban share becomes high.

**Suburbanisation**
- Movement/residential shift from congested city core toward peripheral/suburban areas while maintaining strong city linkages/commuting.

**Rurbanisation — UPPCS recall**
