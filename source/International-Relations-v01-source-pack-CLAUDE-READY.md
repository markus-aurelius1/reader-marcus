## 5. Core Fact Payload

### A. International Groupings & Memberships

#### A1. Grouping grammar — learn these fields

For every grouping, final notes should reduce to:

`origin/year → purpose → core institutional structure/HQ → membership rule/set → India status → one UPSC trap → ↻ current delta`

#### A2. G20 and G7

##### G20

- **Origin:** 1999, after the Asian financial crisis context; initially finance ministers + central-bank governors.
- **Leaders’ level:** annual leaders’ summit since 2008.
- **Structure:** rotating presidency; Finance Track + Sherpa Track; **no permanent HQ / permanent secretariat**.
- **UPSC trap:** distinguish grouping identity from the **G20 Common Framework**, whose debt-treatment mechanics belong Economy.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current presidency, troika, summit host/theme, priority areas.

##### G7

- **Nature:** informal grouping of seven advanced industrial democracies; EU participates in annual work.
- **Structure:** rotating presidency; no treaty-based permanent secretariat.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** chair, invited guests, annual summit host/theme.

---

#### A3. BRICS

- **Formalisation:** BRIC foreign ministers’ format in 2006.
- **First BRIC summit:** 2009, Yekaterinburg, Russia.
- **South Africa:** agreement to include in 2010; participated from 2011 → BRICS.
- **Fortaleza (2014):** key institutional milestone for **New Development Bank (NDB)** and **Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA)**.
- **NDB:** HQ Shanghai; infrastructure/sustainable-development financing; do not confuse founder with APEC.
- **CRA:** liquidity / balance-of-payments support mechanism among BRICS members.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** full-member list, partner-country list, latest entrant, annual summit theme/outcomes.

**Trap set**
- 2009 first summit ≠ Rio de Janeiro.
- NDB ≠ APEC institution.
- expansion facts must be date-stamped.

- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current member count following expansion; annual summit outcomes.

### B. UN System, Conventions & International Law

#### B1. Six principal UN organs

1. **General Assembly (UNGA)**
2. **Security Council (UNSC)**
3. **Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC)**
4. **Trusteeship Council**
5. **International Court of Justice (ICJ)**
6. **Secretariat**

**Trusteeship Council:** suspended operations in 1994; still legally exists.

---

#### B2. UN General Assembly + Credentials Committee

##### UNGA

- **193 members** in supplied reference.
- annual regular session begins in September.
- approves UN budget / member financial assessments.
- can grant **observer status to non-member states**.
- intergovernmental organisations can seek observer status.
- Permanent Observers may maintain missions at UN HQ.

##### Credentials Committee

- **Committee of the General Assembly**, not the Security Council.
- **9 members**.
- examines member-state credentials and reports to the General Assembly.
- UPSC rejected the claim that it has a fixed traditional March/June/September meeting cycle.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current committee composition — omit from permanent notes.

---

#### B3. UN Security Council

- **15 members:** 5 permanent + 10 non-permanent.
- non-permanent members: elected by UNGA for **2-year terms**; five seats turn over each year.
- **9 affirmative votes** required.
- substantive decisions require absence of a P5 veto; procedural decisions do not use the P5 veto rule in the same way.
- mandate: international peace/security; sanctions and force-authorisation powers under Charter framework.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current elected members.

---

#### B4. ECOSOC / Secretariat / ICJ / UNHRC

##### ECOSOC

- **54 members**, elected by UNGA for overlapping **3-year terms**.
- central UN forum for economic, social and environmental coordination.
- functional commissions sit within its wider architecture.

##### Secretariat

- administrative arm of UN.
- Secretary-General appointed by UNGA on recommendation of UNSC; supplied source gives **5-year renewable term**.

##### International Court of Justice (ICJ)

- principal judicial organ of UN.
- HQ: The Hague.
- **15 judges**.
- elected by UNGA + UNSC for **9-year terms**.
- no two judges of the same nationality simultaneously.
- adjudicates disputes between states; also advisory role under UN framework.

##### UN Human Rights Council (UNHRC)

- created in **2006** by UNGA, replacing Commission on Human Rights.
- **47 members**.
- three-year terms; supplied source states maximum two consecutive terms.
- global forum for human-rights review/resolutions/investigations.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current elected membership.

#### B7. UNTOC vs UNCAC

##### UNTOC — Palermo Convention

- global treaty against **transnational organised crime**.
- adopted in 2000; source gives entry into force 2003.
- supplemented by **three protocols** covering:
  1. trafficking in persons;
  2. smuggling of migrants;
  3. illicit manufacturing/trafficking in firearms.
- UNODC serves as secretariat.

##### UNCAC

- **only legally binding universal anti-corruption instrument** in supplied source.
- core pillars: prevention; criminalisation; international cooperation; **asset recovery**.
- UNODC secretariat architecture.
- **DATE CAUTION:** uploaded sources conflict on adoption / entry-into-force dates. Do not freeze dates until official verification.

##### UPSC swap trap

- **Migrant-smuggling protocol → UNTOC, not UNCAC.**
- **Asset recovery → UNCAC.**

---

#### B8. Declarations / frameworks repeatedly tested

##### Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

- broad human-rights charter framework; UPSC tests specific rights and Indian constitutional echoes.
- cross-link detailed constitutional mapping to Polity.

##### Beijing Declaration & Platform for Action

- adopted in 1995 at Fourth World Conference on Women, Beijing.
- global policy framework for women’s empowerment / gender equality.

##### Alma-Ata Declaration

- 1978 health declaration centred on **Primary Health Care / “Health for All”**.

##### International Mother Language Day

- **21 February**.
- UNESCO, **not UNICEF**, is the relevant international body in UPSC trap.

---

#### B9. UNCLOS — minimum permanent legal core

- coastal state may establish territorial sea up to **12 nautical miles** from baselines.
- foreign ships may enjoy **innocent passage** through territorial sea subject to UNCLOS conditions.
- **Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ):** up to **200 nautical miles** from baselines.
- deeper shelf/zone questions should keep legal-zone terminology exact.

##### International Seabed Authority (ISA)

- established under UNCLOS architecture.
- HQ Kingston, Jamaica.
- regulates/organises mineral-related activity in the international seabed **“Area”** beyond national jurisdiction.
- deep-seabed resources = **common heritage of mankind** principle.
- grants exploration contracts; **Global Ocean Commission does not**.
- **↻ DYNAMIC:** current membership count / contract numbers.

## 7. PYQ Trap / Elimination Checklist

### Membership traps

- India **is in EAS**, but **not ASEAN or APEC**.
- Iran/Iraq **not GCC**.
- Bangladesh/China **not** Mekong–Ganga Cooperation members.
- current expansion-sensitive lists: BRICS / ASEAN / SCO / NATO → **always date-stamp**.

### Origin / chronology traps

- BIMSTEC → **1997 Bangkok Declaration**, not Dhaka 1999.
- BRIC first summit → **2009 Yekaterinburg**.
- SCO → 2001 Shanghai; India joined later.
- NATO → 1949 Washington Treaty.
- SAARC → 1985 Dhaka Charter.

### Institution-type traps

- ESM = intergovernmental eurozone crisis mechanism, **not simply an EU agency**.
- World Toilet Organization / MSF / Amnesty / Climate Group ≠ UN agencies.
- UN-linked fund/programme/office ≠ specialised agency.
- Global Ocean Commission ≠ ISA.

### UN parentage traps

- Credentials Committee → **UNGA**, not UNSC.
- UNGA can grant observer status to non-member states.
- ECOSOC members elected by UNGA.
- ICJ judges elected through UNGA + UNSC process.

### Treaty-family traps

- migrant-smuggling protocol → **UNTOC**.
- asset recovery → **UNCAC**.
- maritime mineral licensing in “Area” → **ISA / UNCLOS architecture**.

### Agency / initiative traps

- International Mother Language Day → **UNESCO**, not UNICEF.
- EP100 → Climate Group, not IEA.
- UN-Habitat mandate is broader than “government-only” partnership.

### Maritime-law traps

- territorial sea: **≤12 nm**.
- EEZ: **≤200 nm**.
- territorial-sea sovereign rights ≠ identical to EEZ resource rights.
- licensing authority for deep seabed ≠ a generic ocean commission.

### Dynamic-layer traps

- summit host/theme/logo can be correct for one year and wrong the next.
- “latest member” is a current fact; founding history is a durable fact.
- current chair / non-permanent UNSC member / EU-Schengen-Eurozone count must be refreshed.

#### B. Nuclear & WMD Regimes

##### 3B.1 NPT / nuclear-order distinctions

- **NPT recognised Nuclear-Weapon States:** five-state category; the uploaded current source explicitly frames India’s objection as NPT legitimising nuclear weapons for only five states.
- Canonical PYQ confirms **China and France** as recognised NWS among a list containing China, France, India, Israel, Pakistan.
- **India, Israel, Pakistan** are outside the NPT framework in the uploaded current source.
- Do not infer that joining an export-control regime automatically changes NPT status.

##### 3B.2 IAEA safeguards + Additional Protocol

- **IAEA:** Vienna; autonomous organisation in the UN system; promotes peaceful nuclear use and performs safeguards/non-proliferation verification.
- **Safeguards:** PYQ-tested Indian distinction is reactors using **imported uranium/fuel vs domestic supplies**.
- **Additional Protocol (AP):** strengthens safeguards by widening information/inspection access. Uploaded Prelims source records India signing in 2009 and ratifying in 2014; for India it applies to civilian facilities voluntarily placed under safeguards.
- Trap cluster: civilian vs military facilities; safeguards vs NSG membership; fuel source vs operator/ownership.

##### 3B.3 NSG and export-control regimes

- **NSG:** membership can improve access to advanced nuclear technology; **NPT membership is not automatic**.
- **MTCR:** India membership is directly tested (2022).
- **Australia Group:** linked to reducing risk of exports assisting chemical/biological weapons proliferation.
- **Wassenaar Arrangement:** do **not** describe it as a formal OECD group with identical objective to Australia Group; this was the false construction in 2011 PYQ.
- **Synthesis requirement:** final notes should use a compact 4-regime matrix — `NSG | MTCR | Australia Group | Wassenaar` — with `domain / nature / India status / key trap`. Full institutional details absent from the current uploaded source pool should be marked for authoritative verification before final publication.

##### 3B.4 Chemical / biological / arms-control architecture

- **OPCW** implements/verifies the **Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC)**; HQ The Hague.
- CWC bans development, production, acquisition, stockpiling, transfer and use of chemical weapons and requires destruction under verification.
- OPCW is **not** an EU organisation; it monitors relevant chemical industry and assists/protects States Parties against chemical threats.
- **New START:** bilateral US–Russia strategic nuclear arms-reduction treaty; durable identity should be retained even if treaty status changes over time.
- **BWC:** present in the uploaded current-affairs source pool as a WMD convention; permanent notes should retain only treaty purpose/architecture, not anniversary/status headlines.
- Nuclear Security Summits and International Panel on Fissile Materials: neither should be casually folded into UN/IAEA institutional structure.
