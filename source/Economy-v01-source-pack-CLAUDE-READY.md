##### 2. Demand, supply and goods

- **Demand** = willingness **and ability** to buy at a given price/time; own-price movement normally inverse, ceteris paribus.
- **Supply** = willingness **and ability** to sell; own-price movement normally direct, ceteris paribus.
- **Movement along curve** → own price changes. **Shift of curve** → income, tastes, population, input costs, technology, prices of related goods, policy etc.
- **Substitutes:** price of B ↑ → demand for A ↑. **Complements:** price of B ↑ → demand for A ↓.
- **Normal good:** demand ↑ with income. **Inferior good:** demand ↓ with income.
- **Giffen good:** special inferior-good case where income effect can dominate substitution effect; upward-sloping observed demand under restrictive conditions. **Veblen effect:** conspicuous/status demand may rise with price; not the same mechanism as Giffen.
- **Price elasticity of demand** = %Δ quantity demanded / %Δ price; essentials/few substitutes/short horizon tend more inelastic; many substitutes/luxuries/long horizon tend more elastic.
- **Public-good trap:** non-rivalry + non-excludability → free-rider problem; “provided by government” alone does not define a public good.

| Financial capital | Financial claims/funds used to finance activity; not itself the produced physical asset | Distinguish from physical capital |
| Intellectual/intangible capital | IP, brands, databases/client lists, organisational know-how | UPSC 2023 |

- **Inventory** = stock of raw material, work-in-progress and unsold output at a point in time; **change in inventories** over a period is investment/GCF component.
- **Intangible investment:** brand recognition, IP, customer/client databases/lists; inventory itself is tangible/current asset, not “intangible investment”.
- **Circular economy:** design out waste, keep products/materials in use, recover/recycle/regenerate → reduces virgin-resource input and waste; can lower lifecycle emissions.

##### 4. Saving, investment, capital formation, ICOR

- **Saving** = flow: income not consumed during a period. **Wealth/assets/capital stock** = stocks at a point in time.
- **Investment / capital formation** = addition to produced assets/inventories/valuables during a period; a **flow**.
- Current national-accounts expenditure identity: **GCF ≈ GFCF + change in stocks/inventories + valuables** (plus statistical discrepancy/errors where applicable).
- **GFCF** = acquisitions less disposals of produced fixed assets + specified additions/improvements; **purchase of land itself is not GFCF** because land is a non-produced asset; structures/major improvements are fixed capital.
- **CFC (consumption of fixed capital)** = economic depreciation/use of fixed assets during accounting period. **Net fixed capital formation = GFCF − CFC.**
- **ICOR = ΔK / ΔY**; lower ICOR → more output gain per unit of additional capital → higher capital productivity. Approximate planning relation: required investment rate ≈ target growth × ICOR.
- High saving is not sufficient for high growth if savings are not mobilised/productively invested or if capital-output ratio/ICOR is high.
- Financial institutions matter by mobilising savings and allocating them to investment; “saving” and “investment” are distinct ex ante decisions even if equal in national-accounting equilibrium ex post.

##### 5. National-income accounting: exact boundaries

- **GDP** = market value of final goods/services produced **within domestic economic territory** during a period.
- **Three equivalent approaches in principle:**
  - production/value-added → sum value added;
  - income → compensation + operating surplus/mixed income + relevant production taxes/subsidies + CFC depending gross/basic-price formulation;
  - expenditure → **C/PFCE + I/GCF + G/GFCE + (X−M)** (+ discrepancies in published accounts).
- Avoid double counting: include **final output** or **value added**, not both intermediate and final value.
- Production counts when produced, not only when sold; unsold output adds inventories. Resale of an existing asset is not new GDP, but brokerage/transaction services newly produced are.

**Current Indian framework (MoSPI new series, base 2022-23):**

- **GVA at basic prices = Output at basic prices − Intermediate consumption.**
- **GDP = Σ GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies.**
- **GNI = GDP + net primary income from Rest of World.**
- **NDP = GDP − CFC; NNI = GNI − CFC.**
- Base year for current GDP constant-price series = **2022-23** (new series released 27 Feb 2026, replacing 2011-12).

##### 6. Domestic vs national; resident vs citizen

- **Domestic/GDP** criterion = production attributable to **resident producer units within the economic territory**; ownership or nationality of the enterprise is irrelevant. A foreign-owned enterprise resident in India contributes to India’s GDP.
- **National/GNI** criterion = income accruing to **resident institutional units**, not citizenship.
- Hence do **not** infer national-accounting residence from passport/citizenship; “NRI” is non-resident by definition in the relevant legal/statistical context.
- Legacy identity: **GNP/GNI ≈ GDP + net factor/primary income from abroad**; modern SNA terminology prefers **GNI** and **net primary income from ROW**.

##### 7. Gross/net; current/constant; deflators

- **Gross − CFC = Net.** Thus GDP−CFC=NDP; GNI−CFC=NNI.
- **Nominal/current-price GDP:** current quantities × current prices.
- **Real/constant-price GDP:** current production valued with base-year/volume methodology; removes price-level effect to measure volume growth.
- **Economic growth** in ordinary exam usage → rise in **real output**, preferably sustained real per-capita output/income for welfare comparisons.
- **GDP deflator (index form) = Nominal GDP / Real GDP × 100.** It covers domestically produced final output; basket/weights vary with current production structure. Imported final goods affect CPI/WPI baskets but are not domestic GDP output.
- Do not assume nominal GDP “must” exceed real GDP in every year; relationship depends on price level relative to the base year.

##### 8. “National Income” legacy terminology

- Older Indian textbooks/PYQs often use **National Income = NNP at factor cost**. Retain this as a **legacy exam identity** for old questions.
- Current official national accounts emphasise GVA at basic prices, GDP at market prices, GNI/NNI; do not mix factor-cost and basic-price terms as if identical.
- **Transfer payments** (pensions, cash transfers etc.) are redistributions, not payments for current production; excluded from GDP as transfer itself, though subsequent consumption can be part of expenditure on current output.
- **Personal disposable income** = personal income minus personal tax/non-tax compulsory payments, depending textbook definition used; not identical to national income.

##### 9. Growth vs development / sustainable development

- **Economic growth:** quantitative expansion of real output/income.
- **Economic development:** broader structural/welfare change → productivity, health, education, employment quality, poverty/inequality, institutions, capabilities, sustainability.
- Therefore real GNP/per-capita output ↑ **does not automatically imply development** if poverty/unemployment/inequality deteriorate.
- **Human capital** is both an input to development and an outcome of development spending.
- **Sustainable development:** meet present needs without compromising future generations’ ability to meet theirs; conservation does not mean zero present consumption.

##### 10. Business cycle / macro demand

- **Boom/expansion:** output, income, employment generally rising; **recession:** broad contraction/decline in economic activity; **depression:** severe/prolonged contraction; **recovery:** transition back to expansion.
- **Slowdown** = growth rate falls but output can still rise; **recession** implies contraction by the applicable criterion—do not equate “slower growth” with “GDP falling” mechanically in every usage.
- Recession → aggregate-demand support can come from expansionary fiscal policy/public projects; detailed monetary tools belong the Money/RBI topic.

#### B. Inflation & Price Indices

##### 1. Core concepts

- **Inflation** = sustained rise in the **general price level**, usually measured as % change in a price index; a one-item price rise alone is not necessarily inflation.
- **Inflation rate** = rate of change of price level. If inflation falls from 8% to 5%, prices are still rising: **disinflation**.
- **Deflation** = sustained fall in general price level (negative inflation).
- **Reflation** = deliberate policy to raise demand/prices/output from depressed/deflationary conditions.
- **Stagflation** = high inflation + weak/stagnant growth and high unemployment; breaks the simple short-run inflation–unemployment trade-off intuition.
- **Hyperinflation** = extremely rapid, self-reinforcing price rise; avoid arbitrary universal threshold unless question specifies one.
- **Shrinkflation** = quantity/size falls at same sticker price → effective unit price rises.

##### 2. Causes

| Type | Mechanism | Typical triggers |
|---|---|---|
| Demand-pull | Aggregate demand > productive capacity | expansionary fiscal/monetary policy, income/credit boom, strong private demand |
| Cost-push | Unit production cost ↑ → firms raise prices/reduce supply | wages without productivity, fuel/raw materials, taxes on inputs, logistics |
| Imported inflation | Imported inputs/final goods cost more in domestic currency | global commodity shock, currency depreciation |
| Structural/bottleneck | Persistent supply rigidities constrain response | poor logistics, storage/distribution gaps, capacity constraints |
| Monetary | Money/credit grows much faster than real output, sustaining demand | monetised deficit/new-money creation |
| Expectations/spiral | Expected inflation feeds wage/price setting | wage-price spiral, adaptive expectations |

- Food inflation is often supply-sensitive and volatile; monetary tightening cannot directly produce food/fuel, though it can restrain second-round demand/expectations.
- **Most-inflationary financing (UPSC recurring): creation of new money** because it expands nominal purchasing power without prior withdrawal of equivalent purchasing power from existing savers.

##### 3. Base effect

- Year-on-year inflation compares current index with the **same period of previous year**.
- Abnormally high previous-year base → current inflation can look low even with high current prices; abnormally low base → current inflation can look high.
- Base effect changes the **measured rate**, not the current price level itself.

##### 4. CPI vs WPI vs GDP deflator — current framework

| Feature | CPI (Rural/Urban/Combined) | WPI | GDP deflator |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it tracks | Retail prices paid by households for consumption goods **and services** | Wholesale/bulk transaction prices of **goods** | Price change of domestically produced final output implicit in national accounts |
| Main publisher | NSO, MoSPI | Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry | NSO/MoSPI via national accounts |
| Current base | **2024=100** | **2022-23** | GDP constant-price base **2022-23** |
| Services | Yes | No | Yes, if domestically produced final output |
| Imports | Imported consumption goods can enter household basket/prices | Imported goods can affect relevant wholesale prices | Directly excludes imported output from domestic production; imports affect expenditure identity separately |
| Weights | Fixed for a CPI series until revision | Fixed for a WPI series until revision | Composition/implicit weights vary with production/expenditure |
| Primary use | Cost of living/retail inflation; inflation-targeting reference | Wholesale goods-price movement/producer-input environment | Broad domestic-output price measure |

- **CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL** → Labour Bureau, Ministry of Labour & Employment; do not confuse with NSO CPI-R/U/Combined.
- **Headline CPI** = all components of chosen CPI measure. **Core inflation** = headline excluding volatile components, commonly food and fuel/energy; core is analytical, not a separate statutory household basket.
- CPI transaction prices can embody **indirect taxes/subsidies** that alter consumer prices; **direct personal income tax is not a CPI item**.
- WPI is **not the same as Producer Price Index (PPI)**; saying WPI simply measures “what producers receive” is too broad.

##### 5. Effects / redistribution

- **Real purchasing power of fixed nominal income** falls when prices rise faster than income.
- Poor households are often more exposed to food inflation because necessities take a larger income share.
- For a **fixed-rate nominal debt**, unexpected inflation reduces real value of repayments → **debtor gains, creditor/lender loses**; unexpected deflation reverses it.
- Approximate Fisher relation: **real interest rate ≈ nominal interest rate − inflation** (ex post); expected inflation tends to be incorporated into nominal rates rather than making nominal rates automatically fall.
- Savers in fixed nominal deposits lose real return when inflation rises faster than deposit rate; inflation-linked instruments can protect real value.
- Firms/producers are **not automatic winners**: outcome depends on selling-price power vs input/wage/interest-cost rise.
- Unexpected inflation redistributes wealth and distorts long-term contracting; high/volatile inflation increases uncertainty.

##### 6. Control / institutional responsibility

- **RBI / monetary channel:** restrain aggregate demand, credit/liquidity and inflation expectations through monetary policy. Detailed repo/CRR/OMO mechanics belong Money/RBI topic.
- **Government fiscal channel:** lower excess demand via expenditure/tax stance where appropriate; avoid monetised financing.
- **Government supply channel:** release/import essential goods, lower relevant duties/taxes, improve logistics/storage/competition, remove bottlenecks—especially when inflation is supply-driven.
- India’s statutory framework assigns the **primary objective of monetary policy to price stability while keeping growth in mind**; the inflation target is notified by Government in consultation with RBI using CPI. Exact numeric target-period updates are dynamic and are not frozen here.

- Rolling plan has a current annual plan + medium-term plan revised each year + longer perspective component; flexibility is its defining advantage.

##### 4. 1991 crisis and reform logic

- Backdrop: macro/fiscal and **balance-of-payments crisis**, low reserves and external financing stress → short-run stabilisation + structural reform.
- **Stabilisation:** short-run demand/macro correction → fiscal restraint, inflation/BOP management, immediate external-financing adjustment.
- **Structural adjustment:** longer-run supply/institutional change → industrial deregulation, trade/investment reform, financial/tax/public-sector reforms.

##### 5. LPG — compact meaning

| Globalisation | Greater integration with global trade, capital, technology and production networks | trade liberalisation, lower import barriers, FDI/FPI opening, exchange/trade reforms |

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Banking & Financial Inclusion

##### 1. Bank: core balance-sheet logic

| Item | Bank treatment | Elimination cue |
|---|---|---|
| Customer deposits | **Liability** | bank owes depositor |
| Borrowings | Liability | bank owes lender |
| Capital/reserves | Liability/equity side | owners’ funds/buffer |
| Loans & advances | **Asset** | borrower owes bank |
| Investments incl. G-Secs | Asset | earn interest/value |
| Cash/balances with RBI/other banks | Asset | liquidity |
| Money at call/short notice lent | Asset | short-term claim |

- Bank spread ≈ lending yield − funding/deposit cost; credit losses + operating cost reduce profitability.
- **Interest Coverage Ratio (ICR) = earnings available for interest / interest expense** → higher = stronger ability to service interest; lower = higher credit risk.

##### 2. Scheduled bank vs non-scheduled bank

- **Scheduled bank:** included in Second Schedule, RBI Act; subject to applicable statutory reserve requirements and eligible for central-bank facilities under RBI rules.
- “Scheduled” ≠ public sector; scheduled banks can be public/private/foreign/cooperative/RRB categories subject to law.
- Avoid obsolete capital-threshold trivia unless specifically asked; category and RBI relationship matter more.

##### 3. Commercial bank vs NBFC — high-yield matrix

| Feature | Bank | NBFC |
|---|---|---|
| Lending/investment | ✅ | ✅ |
| Demand deposits | ✅ | **❌** |
| Deposit-taking | Current/savings/time as permitted | only specifically authorised deposit-taking NBFCs may accept prescribed **non-demand** public deposits |
| Payment & settlement system / own cheque | ✅ | **normally ❌**; NBFC is not a bank merely because it lends |
| DICGC on deposits | eligible bank deposits subject to DICGC law/limit | **❌** for NBFC deposits |
| RBI registration | banking licence/statutory framework | RBI registration generally required **but statutory/regulatory exemptions exist** for specified categories |
| CRR/SLR as banks | ✅ as applicable | **not bank CRR/SLR**; separate prudential/liquid-asset rules may apply |
| LAF | eligible banks/PDs as RBI specifies | ordinary NBFCs **do not get bank-style LAF access** |

- **50–50 principal-business test** is used by RBI in NBFC classification: financial assets + income from financial assets are central to determining financial business.
- RBI uses **scale-based regulation** for NBFCs; do not use the base-note shortcut “small NBFC < ₹200 cr → State Govt”.
- HFC regulation moved to RBI; SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA/MCA/States regulate specified financial entities/activities, creating registration exemptions from RBI NBFC provisions where law so provides.

##### 4. Differentiated banks

**Payments Banks (PB)**
- Objective → payments/remittances + small savings + inclusion.
- Accept **demand deposits subject to regulatory cap**; provide payment/remittance services.
- **Cannot undertake normal lending/credit business** from own balance sheet as a commercial bank; no conventional credit card lending.
- Can issue debit/ATM-type cards and distribute permitted third-party financial products subject to rules.
- Trap: PB ≠ SFB; “bank” in name does not mean full universal-bank lending powers.

**Small Finance Banks (SFB)**
- Full bank: deposits + lending.
- Inclusion-oriented portfolio: small borrowers/small businesses/farmers; RBI sets PSL/small-loan portfolio requirements.
- Can issue bank payment products subject to general banking regulation.

**RRBs**
- Statutory rural banking architecture; originally ownership **Centre 50% : State 15% : Sponsor Bank 35%**.
- Core clientele → rural/agricultural/small borrowers; sponsor-bank link.
- RBI regulatory role + NABARD supervisory/development role.

**Local Area Banks**
- Private local banks with geographically restricted operation; RBI-regulated banking entities.
- Low PYQ yield; retain only identity + local mobilisation/lending purpose.

##### 5. Cooperative credit architecture

**Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs)**
- Cooperative entity + banking business → cooperative-law registrar/central registrar aspects **and RBI banking regulation/supervision**.
- Banking Regulation Act provisions extended to cooperative banks; 2020 amendments strengthened RBI powers.
- Can issue equity/preference-type capital instruments subject to law/RBI rules → UPSC 2021 trap.

**Rural short-term cooperative credit**
- State Cooperative Bank → District Central Cooperative Bank → **PACS**.
- DCCB finances/links PACS; PACS is grassroots credit society.
- **PACS is not a bank** → cannot be treated as an RBI-regulated cheque-issuing bank merely due “credit society” label.
- NABARD supervises/refinances major rural-cooperative/RRB architecture subject to statute; RBI retains relevant banking-regulatory powers.

##### 6. Financial inclusion architecture

- **Lead Bank Scheme (LBS):** district-level coordination; a lead bank surveys credit needs, coordinates banks/government/development agencies, prepares district credit planning architecture.
- **Service Area Approach:** branch/area responsibility for planned rural credit; historically operated under LBS.
- **Business Correspondent (BC):** bank agent/outlet for last-mile services → deposits/withdrawals/remittances/account services as authorised; **BC is not an independent bank**.
- **Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account:** low-entry/basic account architecture; supports inclusion.
- **SHG–Bank Linkage:** NABARD-led bank-linkage architecture (not an SBI-origin programme); group savings + bank credit; peer monitoring/social collateral reduces information/transaction problems. Group members share collective responsibility/social pressure for repayment; RRBs and scheduled commercial banks participate in SHG financing.
- **Microfinance:** small-value financial services to low-income clients; can include credit/savings/insurance/remittance depending institution/regulation—UPSC tests broad concept, not “microcredit only”.
- **WaterCredit (2021 PYQ anchor):** Water.org model applying microfinance to household water/sanitation needs; not a WHO–World Bank initiative; objective is access through repayable finance rather than continuing subsidy dependence.
- **PMJDY:** mass access to bank account + payment/benefit-transfer ecosystem; remember architecture, not changing account counts.
- **KCC:** revolving/short-term farm and allied credit facility; can support crop inputs, working capital/maintenance and specified household/post-harvest needs under scheme rules.
- **MUDRA:** refinance/development support for micro units through banks/NBFCs/MFIs; not a retail bank directly opening demand-deposit accounts.
- **IREDA (2015 PYQ anchor):** Government financial institution/company with NBFC status; detailed renewable-energy mandate belongs to Infrastructure/Energy.
- **TReDS:** electronic platform for financing/discounting MSME trade receivables/invoices by financiers. **M1xchange = TReDS platform**; it facilitates invoice/Bill of Exchange discounting, not a CRA or ordinary collateral-loan company.
- **RBI FI-Index:** three sub-indices = **Access + Usage + Quality**. Weight/current index value = dynamic; omit from permanent recall unless asked.

##### 7. Priority Sector Lending (PSL)

- RBI-directed credit-allocation framework to identified sectors/borrowers where credit access has policy priority.
- Common durable buckets: agriculture; MSMEs; export credit; education; housing; social infrastructure; renewable energy; weaker sections—exact eligibility/sub-targets evolve.
- **PSLC:** certificate transfers **achievement/shortfall accounting**, not the underlying loan or credit risk itself.
- Bank lending under PSL is not automatically concessional; interest is normally determined under applicable bank/rate rules unless a separate subsidy scheme exists.
- Shortfall can be channelled to specified funds as RBI directs (e.g. RIDF architecture historically).

##### 8. Deposit insurance — DICGC

- DICGC = wholly owned subsidiary of RBI.
- Covers eligible deposits of insured banks, including eligible commercial/cooperative-bank deposits; premium paid by insured bank, not depositor.
- Statutory coverage limit is **per depositor per bank**, aggregating eligible accounts in same right/capacity; current rupee ceiling is a replaceable statutory fact.
- **NBFC deposits are not DICGC-insured.**

##### 9. Bank regulation / foreign banks / PSBs

- RBI powers derive from RBI Act + Banking Regulation Act and allied laws: licensing/private-bank entry, prudential norms, branch regulation, inspection/supervision, amalgamation/reconstruction powers within statutory scheme, etc.
- **PSBs:** ownership/statutory structure creates Government + RBI roles; do not assume RBI has identical corporate-governance/removal/licensing powers over PSBs as over private banks.
- **Foreign-bank WOS rule tested 2024:** minimum paid-up voting equity capital applies (₹5 billion in RBI scheme); board rule is **not less than 50% Indian nationals/NRIs/PIOs, subject to at least one-third being Indian nationals resident in India**. This exact wording defeats the simplified “50% Indian nationals” statement.

##### 10. PSB governance: BBB → FSIB

- **Banks Board Bureau (BBB):** historical body; recommended candidates for senior PSB appointments and advised on governance/business/capital matters. RBI Governor was **not** its chairman.
- **Financial Services Institutions Bureau (FSIB):** constituted **1 July 2022**; BBB assets/functions transferred. Recommends whole-time directors/non-executive chairpersons for public-sector financial institutions and advises on personnel/governance matters.
- **2018 PYQ-date anchor:** merger of SBI associate banks with SBI had occurred; the proposition that Government capital infusion into PSBs had *steadily* increased throughout the preceding decade was false. Do not convert that historical trend statement into a permanent current fact.
- PYQ rule: learn historical institution **as of question date**; current note should carry successor cross-reference.

##### 11. Basel norms — durable architecture

| Basel | Core recall |
|---|---|
| **Basel I** | minimum bank capital against mainly **credit risk**; risk-weighted assets |
| **Basel II** | 3 pillars: **minimum capital requirements + supervisory review + market discipline**; broader credit/market/operational risk treatment |
| **Basel III** | stronger **quality/quantity of capital**, capital conservation/countercyclical buffers, leverage constraint, **LCR + NSFR** liquidity standards |

- **CAR/CRAR = regulatory capital / risk-weighted assets** → higher capital buffer absorbs unexpected losses, protects depositors/financial stability.
- **Capital Conservation Buffer:** usable in stress; restricts distributions as capital approaches minimum.
- **Countercyclical Capital Buffer:** built in excessive credit-growth periods, releasable in stress.
- **LCR:** high-quality liquid assets sufficient for short severe liquidity stress (30-day concept).
- **NSFR:** stable funding against longer-term asset/funding profile.
- Trap: SLR is an Indian statutory liquidity requirement; it is **not synonymous with Basel market risk**.

##### 12. NPAs / stressed assets / resolution

- NPA = loan/advance where interest/principal remains overdue beyond RBI-prescribed asset-classification period; durable exam concept = deterioration from standard → NPA categories, provisioning and recovery.
- **Gross NPA:** total identified NPAs before netting eligible provisions/adjustments.
- **Net NPA:** gross NPA net of eligible provisions/adjustments as prescribed. Do **not** use the shortcut `GNPA = NNPA + provisioning` as universal arithmetic.
- **Interest Coverage Ratio ↓** → debt-service stress ↑.
- **AQR:** RBI balance-sheet clean-up/re-recognition exercise initiated in **2015**, forcing recognition of masked stress.
- **SARFAESI:** secured creditor can enforce qualifying security interest without first obtaining ordinary civil-court decree, subject to statutory notice/procedure; agricultural land is excluded from enforcement under the Act.
- **ARC:** acquires stressed financial assets; may issue security receipts under SARFAESI framework.
- **IBC:** time-bound insolvency/resolution framework; financial creditor / operational creditor / corporate applicant routes; waterfall priorities matter. Avoid obsolete threshold memorisation in permanent note.
- **S4A / SDR / 5:25 etc.:** historical RBI restructuring mechanisms → retain purpose only as PYQ anchors; IBC superseded much of the old scheme-centric exam utility.
- **Inter-Creditor Agreement:** coordination among lenders to resolve stressed borrowers instead of each creditor acting independently.
- “Bad bank” architecture (NARCL/IDRCL) = aggregation/resolution mechanism for legacy stressed assets; current operational numbers are dynamic.

#### B. Money, RBI & Monetary Policy

##### 1. Money — functions and legal categories

- Primary functions → **medium of exchange + unit/measure of value**.
- Secondary → store of value; standard of deferred payment; transfer of value.
- **Fiat money:** value rests on sovereign/legal authority rather than commodity content.
- **Legal tender:** legally valid means of settling debt; creditor cannot refuse within the law’s denomination/transaction limits.
- Cheque/DD/card/UPI instruction = payment instrument/claim transfer; **not itself legal tender money**.
- Currency notes have face value far above paper’s intrinsic value → fiat/token money.

**Indian issuance**
- RBI issues banknotes other than ₹1 note under RBI Act.
- Government of India mints/issues coins under the **Coinage Act, 2011**. The **₹1 note is issued by Government of India**, signed by the Finance Secretary and treated as a rupee coin/legal tender for RBI Act purposes; RBI currency material traces its special issuance to the Currency Ordinance, 1940 / Coinage Act legal-tender framework.
- Do not retain the old base-note reference “Coinage Act 1911”.

##### 2. RBI — institutional anchors

- RBI Act enacted **1934**; RBI began operations **1935**; nationalised **1949**.
- Central bank roles:
  - monetary authority;
  - currency issuer/manager (except statutory ₹1/coin exception);
  - banker/debt manager to government;
  - bankers’ bank + settlement role;
  - lender of last resort;
  - regulator/supervisor for banks and specified financial entities;
  - forex reserve/exchange-management role under FEMA architecture;
  - G-Sec/money-market regulation and payment-system oversight under relevant laws.
- **Governor + up to 4 Deputy Governors** are official directors; Central Board includes government-nominated/non-official directors as statute provides.
- Governor/Deputy Governors appointed by **Central Government** under RBI Act; do not add unsupported “PM in consultation with FM” formula to permanent notes.
- **Section 7:** Central Government may, after consultation with Governor, give RBI directions considered necessary in public interest. This power comes from RBI Act, **not Constitution**.
- Subject to Section 7 directions, general superintendence/direction of RBI affairs rests with Central Board; avoid claim “Governor has no say”.

##### 3. RBI balance sheet / income

**Major asset families**
- Foreign-currency assets; gold; Government securities; loans/advances/claims under statutory operations.

**Major liabilities**
- Notes/currency liabilities; deposits of banks/government/others; provisions/reserves.

**Income channels**
- Interest/valuation/trading income from government securities and foreign assets/currency operations are central sources.
- Printing/issuing a note is **not a fee-paying income source**; printing is an expense side item.
- RBI does not operate as a normal commercial lender to private companies.

**Seigniorage vs surplus**
- **Seigniorage:** economic gain associated with issuing non-interest-bearing/low-cost monetary liabilities relative to assets acquired; conceptually narrower than RBI’s total accounting profit/surplus.
- RBI **net income/surplus transfer** = overall income less expenditure + provisions/risk framework; do not equate it simply with seigniorage.
- **Bimal Jalan Expert Committee on Economic Capital Framework:** constituted 2018; report/recommendations 2019; governs risk-provision/surplus framework. Base dates corrected.

##### 4. Demand for money

- **Transactions demand** ↑ with nominal income/value of transactions; ↓ as velocity rises.
- **Precautionary demand** → emergency/liquidity buffer; generally income-related.
- **Speculative/liquidity demand** → holding cash vs interest-bearing bonds depends on expected interest-rate/bond-price movements.
- Bond price and market yield move **inversely**.
- **Liquidity trap:** at very low interest rates, additional money may be willingly held as cash/liquid balances rather than moving yields much lower → conventional monetary easing weakens.

##### 5. Monetary aggregates — use RBI definitions

| Aggregate | Durable definition |
|---|---|
| **Reserve Money / M0** | Currency in circulation + bankers’ deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI |
| **M1** | Currency with public + demand deposits with banking system + **other deposits with RBI** |
| **M2** | M1 + savings deposits of post-office savings banks |
| **M3** | M1 + time deposits with banking system → principal **broad money** aggregate |
| **M4** | M3 + all deposits with post-office savings banks **excluding NSC** |

- Narrow/broad labels are useful but **components** matter more.
- Currency with public ≠ currency in circulation: latter also includes cash held by banks.

##### 6. Money multiplier / credit creation

- Concept: banking system can expand deposits/credit on top of monetary base through repeated lending/deposit creation subject to leakages and prudential constraints.
- Stylised simplest model: multiplier ≈ `1 / reserve ratio` **only under restrictive assumptions**.
- With public cash preference: higher **currency–deposit ratio** → more cash leakage → multiplier ↓.
- Higher reserve requirement/desired excess reserves → lendable base ↓ → multiplier ↓.
- Greater banking habit/deposit preference → cash leakage ↓ → multiplier ↑.
- **Do not set `reserve-deposit ratio = CRR + SLR` mechanically.** SLR assets, excess reserves and behavioural reserves make the relationship more complex.

**Transaction traps**

| Transaction | M1 immediate effect | M3 immediate effect |
|---|---:|---:|
| Withdraw ₹ from demand deposit as cash | ~unchanged: DD ↓, currency public ↑ | ~unchanged |
| Deposit cash into demand account | ~unchanged: currency public ↓, DD ↑ | ~unchanged |
| Shift savings/current demand deposit → term deposit | **M1 ↓** | ~unchanged |
| Break term deposit → demand deposit | **M1 ↑** | ~unchanged |
| RBI buys G-Sec from non-bank/public | monetary base/liquidity ↑; money supply tends ↑ | ↑/expansionary |
| RBI sells G-Sec to public/banks | reserves/liquidity ↓ | contractionary |

##### 7. Monetary-policy objective + MPC

- RBI Act preamble: primary monetary-policy objective = **price stability while keeping in mind growth**.
- Flexible inflation-targeting framework: Central Government, **in consultation with RBI**, specifies CPI inflation target once every five years under statute.
- Durable target architecture: **4% CPI with ±2 percentage-point tolerance band** unless law/notification changes; treat current renewal date as dynamic.
- **MPC = 6:** RBI Governor (Chair) + RBI Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy + one RBI officer nominated by Central Board + 3 Central Government appointees.
- Meets at least 4 times/year; decision on **policy rate** is binding on RBI.
- MPC does **not** directly vote every CRR/SLR/regulatory setting.

##### 8. Quantitative tools — direction map

| Tool/action | Immediate banking-system effect | Broad macro direction |
|---|---|---|
| Repo/policy rate ↑ | marginal central-bank liquidity cost/market rates ↑ | credit/demand ↓ → disinflationary |
| Repo ↓ | rates/funding cost ↓ | credit/demand ↑ |
| CRR ↑ | balances locked with RBI ↑ | lendable resources/multiplier ↓ |
| CRR ↓ | reserves freed | lending/liquidity ↑ |
| SLR ↑ | larger prescribed liquid-asset holding | lendable flexibility generally ↓ |
| SLR ↓ | resources freed | credit capacity generally ↑ |
| RBI buys G-Secs (OMO) | reserves/base money ↑ | liquidity ↑; yields ↓ tendency |
| RBI sells G-Secs | reserves ↓ | liquidity ↓; yields ↑ tendency |
| SDF absorption ↑/more attractive | banks park surplus at RBI | liquidity/overnight rate tightening tendency |
| MSF borrowing | emergency overnight liquidity against eligible SLR securities | ceiling/safety-valve function |

##### 9. CRR vs SLR

**CRR**
- prescribed share of NDTL maintained as **cash balance with RBI**; not “cash with bank itself”.
- non-earning statutory balance in standard framework.
- direct reserve/liquidity lever; exact rate is dynamic.

**SLR**
- prescribed share of NDTL maintained by bank in eligible liquid assets such as cash/gold/unencumbered approved securities as law/RBI rules specify.
- held by bank, not simply deposited as CRR with RBI.
- G-Secs in SLR earn market returns; exact SLR rate is dynamic.

##### 10. LAF / Repo / SDF / MSF / Bank Rate

- **LAF:** RBI liquidity operations injecting/absorbing liquidity; contains overnight/term repo/reverse-repo operations plus standing facilities.
- **Repo:** collateralised liquidity injection against eligible securities; policy repo = MPC benchmark rate.
- **SDF:** uncollateralised liquidity-absorption standing facility.
  - RBI Act Section 17 was amended in **2018** to enable it.
  - operationalised **April 2022**.
  - replaced fixed-rate reverse repo as **floor of LAF corridor**.
- **MSF:** overnight liquidity to eligible banks by dipping into SLR portfolio within RBI limit; penal/safety-valve facility; **ceiling** of corridor.
- Durable corridor mnemonic: **SDF floor — Repo centre — MSF ceiling** (spreads/rates can be changed).
- **Bank Rate:** statutory/reference central-bank rate, currently aligned operationally with MSF; do not rely on old “long-term RBI lending rate” alone.

##### 11. OMO / sterilisation / MSS

- **Outright OMO:** RBI purchase/sale of government securities → durable reserve/liquidity change unless offset.
- **Operation Twist:** simultaneous purchases/sales across maturities to influence yield curve; no need to memorise episode dates.
- **Sterilisation:** neutralising domestic-liquidity effect of forex intervention/capital flows.
  - Example: RBI buys foreign currency → rupees injected; then sells G-Secs/uses absorption tool → rupees withdrawn.
- **MSS:** Government securities issued for sterilisation with proceeds sequestered for specified purpose; RBI + Government architecture.
- Trap: sterilisation is a **purpose/effect**, not one single instrument.

##### 12. Transmission

- Policy impulse chain: `repo/overnight rates → money-market rates → deposit/funding cost → lending rates + bond yields → borrowing/consumption/investment → output + inflation`.
- Transmission can be weakened by high NPAs, rigid deposit rates, excess liquidity, market structure, administered rates, weak credit demand.
- **MCLR:** internal benchmark designed to improve transparency/fairness and responsiveness to marginal funding cost.
- External benchmark-linked lending may strengthen transmission in specified retail/MSME loans; benchmark details are regulatory current layer.

##### 13. Lender of last resort

- Central bank supplies temporary liquidity to otherwise solvent/eligible banks facing acute liquidity stress to prevent panic/systemic contagion.
- ≠ routine cheap finance to industry.
- ≠ blanket bailout of insolvent entities.

##### 14. CBDC / Digital Rupee

- **CBDC (e₹):** digital form of sovereign currency issued by RBI; **direct liability of RBI**.
- Legal-tender/fiat-money character; convertible with cash/commercial-bank money according to design.
- Retail CBDC can be token/wallet based; programmability/offline capabilities are design features.
- **Not inherently indexed to inflation.** ₹100 e₹ has same nominal rupee value as ₹100 cash.
- **UPI ≠ CBDC:** UPI is an instant payment interface moving **commercial-bank deposit money**; CBDC is the money itself/central-bank liability.
- e₹ ≠ **e-RUPI** voucher (NPCI prepaid purpose-specific digital voucher).

#### C. Financial Markets & Instruments

##### 1. Money market vs capital market

| Basis | Money market | Capital market |
|---|---|---|
| Core horizon | short-term liquidity, typically ≤1 year | medium/long-term capital, generally >1 year + equity |
| Purpose | liquidity/working-capital/treasury management | investment/capital formation |
| Examples | call/notice money, T-bills, CP, CD, repo/TREPS | dated G-Secs, corporate bonds, debentures, equity, long-term funds |
| Risk | instrument-specific; short maturity ≠ risk-free | wider maturity/price/credit risk spectrum |

- Do **not** label money market “usually unsecured”: it contains both unsecured instruments (call money/CP/CD) and secured instruments (repo/TREPS), plus sovereign T-bills.

##### 2. Money-market instruments

**Call / Notice / Term money**
- Call = overnight unsecured borrowing/lending among eligible participants.
- Notice = beyond overnight up to short prescribed range; term money = longer short-term interbank horizon under RBI definitions.

**Commercial Paper (CP)**
- unsecured, negotiable short-term money-market promissory-note instrument used by eligible corporates/financial entities.
- Credit quality matters because normally **no specific collateral**.

**Certificate of Deposit (CD)**
- negotiable short-term deposit/money-market instrument issued by eligible banks/FIs under RBI framework.
- It is **not** an RBI long-term loan instrument to companies.

**Treasury Bills**
- Central Government short-term securities; issued at discount/redeemed at face value; no coupon.
- Common tenors: 91/182/364 days.
- State Governments issue **SDLs**, not T-bills.

**Repo / TREPS / CBLO**
- Repo = collateralised borrowing against securities with repurchase agreement.
- **CBLO:** UPSC 2024 classification = money market; historical CCIL collateralised instrument.
- Modern market migrated CBLO platform to **Triparty Repo / TREPS**; learn CBLO mainly as PYQ legacy term.

##### 3. Government securities

- **G-Sec:** tradable debt obligation issued by Central/State Government.
- Central: T-bills (short) + dated securities (longer). States: SDLs.
- Primary market = new issue/auction; secondary market = subsequent trading.
- RBI manages government debt/auctions under statute but Government is issuer/debtor.
- Retail investors can access G-Secs through permitted channels such as RBI Retail Direct/NDS-OM ecosystem; platform details can evolve.
- **Price ↑ → yield ↓**; **price ↓ → yield ↑**.
- Yield determinants: expected policy rates, inflation, fiscal/borrowing supply, liquidity, growth, risk premium, global yields/Fed, market demand.

##### 4. Bonds / debentures / equity — legal claim hierarchy

**Bond/debenture**
- debt claim: issuer owes interest/principal according to terms.
- Can be **secured or unsecured**; do not define “bond” as necessarily security-backed.
- Bondholder = creditor/lender, not owner.
- In insolvency/winding-up, debt claims generally rank ahead of ordinary equity, subject to security/statutory waterfall.

**Equity share**
- residual ownership claim; voting/ownership rights subject to company/share class.
- return = dividend (not guaranteed) + capital gain/loss.
- Ordinary shareholders rank residual after creditors.

**Preference share**
- preferential rights for dividend and repayment of capital over equity according to issue terms.
- May be convertible or non-convertible; **not necessarily compulsorily convertible**.

**Debenture**
- corporate debt instrument; may be secured/unsecured, convertible/non-convertible.
- Base-note statement “debentures must convert into equity” = **wrong**.

##### 5. Special bonds

**Zero-coupon bond**
- no periodic coupon; issued/trades at discount; return from redemption at face value/price appreciation.

**Convertible bond/debenture**
- debt with option/term to convert into equity.
- Conversion option has value → issuer can usually offer lower coupon than comparable straight debt.
- Equity conversion provides participation in equity-price appreciation, creating some inflation-linked upside but not guaranteed inflation insurance.

**Inflation-Indexed Bond (IIB)**
- principal/coupon-linked structure protects real purchasing power against specified inflation index.
- Benefit → investor inflation hedge; government may reduce inflation-risk premium/coupon cost.
- **No blanket tax exemption** merely because bond is inflation-indexed.

**Masala Bond**
- rupee-denominated bond issued offshore; currency risk largely borne by foreign investor rather than Indian issuer.
- Used for rupee debt financing by eligible Indian entities; IFC popularised term through rupee bonds.

**Sustainability Bond**
- proceeds finance a mix of eligible **environmental + social** projects; distinguish green-only/social-only bonds.

##### 6. Primary vs secondary market

- **Primary:** issuer raises fresh capital → IPO/FPO/right/placement/debt issue.
- **Secondary:** existing securities change hands; issuer normally does not receive sale proceeds.
- Stock exchange = organised secondary market + listing/trading infrastructure; debt segments also exist.
- **BSE / NSE:** multi-segment exchanges including equity/derivatives/debt.

##### 7. Depository system

- **Depository:** holds securities electronically and enables transfer/settlement; India’s two depositories = **NSDL, CDSL**.
- **Depository Participant:** intermediary/agent through which investor accesses depository services.
- **Demat account** ≠ trading account ≠ bank account.
- NDS-OM = RBI-operated electronic order-matching platform for government securities; **not a depository**.

##### 8. SEBI / other regulators

- **SEBI:** statutory securities-market regulator → stock exchanges, brokers/intermediaries, mutual funds, AIFs, listed-company market disclosure, credit-rating agencies, securities derivatives.
- **RBI:** banking, monetary policy, G-Sec/money-market core, forex/payment systems and specified NBFCs.
- **IRDAI:** insurance.
- **PFRDA:** pension sector/NPS architecture.
- **IFSCA:** IFSC financial services.
- Elimination rule: a product’s issuer/market can bring **multiple regulators**; avoid one-regulator absolutism.

##### 9. Gold-linked schemes — PYQ anchor

- **Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB):** government debt instrument denominated in grams of gold; issued through RBI on behalf of Government; gives gold-price-linked redemption plus specified interest under scheme terms, without holding physical gold.
- **Gold Monetisation Scheme (GMS):** seeks to mobilise idle household/institutional gold into formal financial channels.
- Shared policy logic tested in 2016 → **mobilise idle gold + reduce dependence on physical gold imports**; promoting FDI in jewellery is not the core purpose.

##### 10. Mutual fund / ETF / AIF

**Mutual Fund**
- pooled investment vehicle; investor owns fund units, not direct title to each portfolio security.
- NAV-based valuation; schemes can be equity/debt/hybrid/index etc.

**ETF**
- pooled fund whose **units trade on stock exchange intraday** like securities; often tracks an index/commodity/basket but ETF ≠ “all passive mutual funds”.

**Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)**
- privately pooled investment vehicle under SEBI AIF Regulations.
- **Category I:** socially/economically desirable/early-stage-type sectors e.g. venture-capital funds, infrastructure funds etc.
- **Category II:** PE/debt-type funds not using leverage except permitted operational needs.
- **Category III:** complex/trading strategies; hedge funds are classic example.
- UPSC 2025 trap: **bond and stock are securities**, not themselves AIFs; hedge fund/venture-capital fund are pooled vehicles.

##### 11. InvIT / REIT

- **InvIT:** pooled trust investing in infrastructure assets/projects; allows investors to participate in cash-generating infrastructure without direct project ownership.
- **REIT:** analogous pooled vehicle focused on income-generating real estate.
- Units can be listed/traded subject to SEBI rules.
- **2023 PYQ anchor:** InvITs are recognised as borrowers for SARFAESI purposes; the PYQ proposition that interest distribution was tax-exempt while dividend distribution was taxable was false. Tax distribution rules remain amendment-sensitive, so retain this as a question-date statutory anchor rather than a timeless tax rule.

##### 12. Derivatives

- Value derived from underlying asset/rate/index.
- **Forward:** customised OTC future transaction.
- **Futures:** standardised exchange-traded forward-type contract; margin/mark-to-market.
- **Option:** buyer has right, not obligation; seller/writer has obligation if exercised; buyer pays premium.
- **Swap:** parties exchange cash-flow streams; **currency swap** is a financial instrument.
- **Beta:** market sensitivity of security/portfolio; beta >1 = more sensitive than benchmark, <1 = less (direction depends sign).
- **L.C. Gupta Committee:** roadmap for derivatives trading under SEBI → 2026 PYQ anchor.

##### 13. P-Notes / Offshore Derivative Instruments

- ODI/P-note = instrument issued **overseas by an eligible registered FPI** against Indian securities held as underlying; economic exposure passed to overseas subscriber.
- End investor need not register directly as FPI, but issuance is subject to SEBI eligibility/KYC/disclosure/restriction framework.
- **Not “banned by SEBI”.** Regulatory framework has tightened and changed over time.
- Keep concept; current ODI eligibility/derivative restrictions are dynamic.

##### 14. Credit Rating Agencies (CRA)

- Assess relative credit/default risk of debt issuer/instrument; rating is **opinion, not guarantee**.
- Securities-market CRAs registered/regarded under **SEBI** framework, not RBI.
- **2022 PYQ anchors:** ICRA is a public limited company; Brickwork Ratings is an Indian CRA.
- Lower rating → normally higher yield/cost of borrowing for same conditions.
- Rating ≠ equity recommendation ≠ sovereign guarantee.

##### 15. BRSR

- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Reporting = listed-entity ESG/sustainability disclosure framework under **SEBI**, not RBI.
- Contains significant **non-financial** disclosures → environment, social and governance indicators.
- Coverage thresholds/assurance schedule are current-rule layer; do not fossilise.

##### 16. SENSEX / index basics

- SENSEX = benchmark BSE index of **30** major representative/liquid companies.
- Index level reflects weighted price movement; current constituents change.
- NIFTY 50 is separate NSE benchmark; do not treat index provider/exchange interchangeably.

#### D. Digital Payments

##### 1. Regulatory architecture

- **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007** → RBI is designated authority for regulation/supervision of payment systems in India.
- Payment-system operator needs RBI authorisation unless statute exempts.
- **RBI-owned/operated large fund-transfer systems:** RTGS and NEFT architecture is RBI-side; do **not** attribute them to NPCI.
- **NPCI:** incorporated 2008 as not-for-profit company (original Section 25; now Section 8 framework), promoted by banking system with RBI/IBA initiative; operates major retail-payment rails/products under RBI authorisation/oversight.

##### 2. RTGS vs NEFT vs IMPS vs UPI

| Feature | RTGS | NEFT | IMPS | UPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operator/system anchor | RBI | RBI | NPCI retail rail | NPCI interface/rail |
| Settlement processing | **real-time, gross, transaction-by-transaction** | **batch-based**; 48 half-hourly batches/day | instant retail transfer | instant bank-account payment built over IMPS-type infrastructure |
| Availability | 24×7×365 | 24×7×365 | 24×7 | 24×7 |
| Typical use | large-value/finality-sensitive | general account transfer | instant retail transfer | mobile/QR/P2P/P2M |
| RBI minimum | RTGS has prescribed minimum amount | no RBI minimum in NEFT | product rules | product/category limits dynamic |

- **Inward RTGS/NEFT transactions:** no customer charge; outward pricing rules can differ/evolve.
- UPSC 2025: “RTGS instantaneous, NEFT takes some batch time” = durable distinction; statement “RTGS only limited hours while NEFT 24×7” is obsolete/false.

##### 3. NPCI products — durable map

- **UPI:** instant bank-account-to-bank-account interface; supports push/pay and pull/collect, QR, virtual payment address etc.
- **BHIM:** NPCI’s UPI application/interface; UPI is the rail/interface standard, BHIM one app.
- **RuPay:** domestic card-payment network/scheme.
- **IMPS:** instant interbank retail funds transfer.
- **NFS:** shared ATM-switch/network → UPSC 2018 “links ATMs”.
- **AePS:** Aadhaar-authenticated interoperable bank transactions through BC/Micro-ATM ecosystem.
- **NACH:** bulk recurring payments/collections.
- **NETC/FASTag:** electronic toll collection.
- Current product limits/versions = dynamic; retain only system identity/function.

##### 4. UPI — traps

- UPI does **not require a pre-funded wallet** for normal linked-bank-account transfer.
- TPAP (e.g. third-party UPI app) provides front end; customer funds remain in linked bank account unless separate wallet/credit product used.
- UPI is **not legal tender/currency**; it is payment messaging/transfer infrastructure.
- Merchant receives bank money; underlying liability remains with commercial banking system.
- International UPI merchant acceptance/linkages are expanding but country list changes → **never permanent-note a fixed count**.

##### 5. Merchant Discount Rate (MDR)

- Fee/discount borne by merchant/acquirer ecosystem for accepting specified electronic/card payment; distributed among acquiring bank/network/issuer etc. according to product rules.
- It is **not** an incentive paid by bank to merchant.
- Zero-MDR mandates/incentives for specified rails/transaction sizes are policy-current layer; keep definition only.

##### 6. Payment-data localisation

- RBI 2018 directive: authorised payment-system providers must ensure **entire data relating to payment systems operated by them is stored in systems only in India**; foreign leg may also be stored abroad where required.
- Full end-to-end transaction data included.
- Compliance backed by **System Audit Report** from CERT-In-empanelled auditors, approved by Board and submitted to RBI.
- Does **not** mean payment systems must be public-sector owned/operated.

##### 7. UPI vs CBDC vs e-RUPI

| Feature | UPI | CBDC / e₹ | e-RUPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is it? | payment interface/rail | **sovereign digital currency** | prepaid purpose-specific digital voucher |
| Liability/money | commercial-bank deposit moves | **RBI liability** | issuer/prepaid-voucher claim, not sovereign currency |
| Needs bank deposit? | normal model linked to bank account | CBDC wallet/token can represent central-bank money | voucher redemption architecture |
| Legal tender? | no—UPI itself is not money | yes, as RBI CBDC | no |
| Key institution | NPCI + participant banks | RBI | NPCI platform + issuing participants/government programme |

### 6. UPPCS Extension Layer

- **Banking chronology:**
  - RRB experiment/establishment → **1975**; RRB Act → 1976.
  - NABARD → **1982**.
  - SHG–Bank Linkage Programme → **1992**.
  - Kisan Credit Card → **1998**.
- **Lead Bank Scheme:** district credit-coordination architecture; LBS full form frequently directly asked.
- **Social control of banks:** pre-nationalisation banking-policy phase; **L.K. Jha** appears in UPPCS matching-style recall.
- **Financial-inclusion historical instruments:** bank nationalisation, RRBs, village/branch expansion, SHG–bank linkage, BC architecture.
- **SENSEX:** BSE benchmark with **30** constituents; remember count, not current company list.
- **Pashu-Kisan Credit Card (Haryana, 2020)** and 2020 FPI corporate-bond limit are **question-date snapshots only**; do not convert into permanent national facts.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- Bank deposit = **bank liability**; bank loan/advance = **bank asset**.
- RBI buys G-Sec → liquidity/base money **↑**; sells → **↓**.
- Bond price **↑** ↔ yield **↓**.
- CRR = cash balance **with RBI**; SLR = eligible liquid assets **maintained by bank**.
- Modern corridor: **SDF floor — repo centre — MSF ceiling**; reverse repo no longer the standing floor.
- NBFC can lend/invest but **cannot accept demand deposits**, issue cheque on itself, or claim DICGC cover.
- Not every entity colloquially called NBFC must obtain RBI CoR: statutory/regulator-based exemptions exist.
- Payments Bank ≠ Small Finance Bank: PB normal lending **❌**; SFB lending **✅**.
- PACS ≠ bank; DCCB/State Cooperative Bank are banking institutions in cooperative credit chain.
- Basel III ≠ “SLR rules”; Basel focuses capital quality/buffers/leverage/liquidity standards.
- Higher ICR = **better** debt-service capacity, not worse.
- Gross NPA ≠ simply Net NPA + provision in all contexts.
- BBB is historical; **FSIB replaced it in 2022**.
- M1 must include **other deposits with RBI**; M3 = M1 + banking-system time deposits.
- Cash withdrawal from demand deposit does **not** automatically reduce aggregate money supply.
- Bond may be **secured or unsecured**; debenture/preference share need not be convertible.
- T-bill = Central Govt short-term discount security; State debt = SDL.
- CP = unsecured money-market instrument; CD = negotiable bank/FI deposit instrument.
- CBLO = money market **legacy**; current collateralised triparty-repo architecture = TREPS.

### 5. Core Fact Payload

#### A. Budget & Fiscal Policy

##### A1. Budget accounts — classification first

| Item | Core test | Examples / traps |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Receipt** | does **not** create liability or reduce Government asset | tax revenue; fees; dividends; interest receipts |
| **Capital Receipt** | **creates liability OR reduces asset** | borrowing; recovery of loans; disinvestment |
| **Revenue Expenditure** | normally no asset creation / liability reduction for Government | salaries; pensions; interest; subsidies; many grants |
| **Capital Expenditure** | creates/acquires asset OR reduces liability | infrastructure asset; equity acquisition; loans/advances; principal repayment |

- `TRAP →` **Interest received on loans** = revenue receipt; it does not create Government liability.
- `TRAP →` Financing method ≠ expenditure classification. Debt/equity financing by a company is not itself “capital expenditure”.
- Plan / Non-Plan expenditure = **legacy classification**, discontinued from Union Budget 2017–18 onward.

##### A2. Deficit arithmetic

- **Revenue Deficit (RD)** = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts.
- **Fiscal Deficit (FD)** = Total Expenditure − Revenue Receipts − Non-debt Capital Receipts.
  - operationally = net borrowing requirement, subject to accounting adjustments.
- **Primary Deficit (PD)** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments.
- **Effective Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Deficit − grants for creation of capital assets → retain as a historical/analytical term; do not treat old FRBM targets as current without date.
- **Traditional “Budget Deficit”** is not the principal modern FRBM headline measure; do not write that the concept was literally “abolished”.

**2025 arithmetic anchor**
- RE 80,000; RR 60,000 → **RD 20,000**.
- Borrowing/FD 10,000; interest 6,000 → **PD 4,000**.
- Correct base-note typo: RD calculation is `80,000 − 60,000`, not `80,000 − 40,000`.

##### A3. Fiscal policy

- **Expansionary:** G ↑ and/or T ↓ → aggregate demand ↑ → used against recession/unemployment; inflation/fiscal-pressure risk.
- **Contractionary:** G ↓ and/or T ↑ → aggregate demand ↓ → disinflationary.
- **Counter-cyclical:** restrain in boom; support in slowdown.
- **Pro-cyclical:** policy moves in same direction as cycle → amplifies fluctuations.
- **Crowding out:** Government borrowing ↑ → can push interest rates / absorb loanable funds → private investment ↓, other things equal.
- Deficit financing through money creation is generally the **most inflationary** financing route.

##### A4. FRBM — permanent architecture, dated targets

- **FRBM Act, 2003** → fiscal discipline, transparency, medium-term fiscal management.
- Budget-linked statutory fiscal-policy statements include the **Macro-Economic Framework Statement** and other medium-term/fiscal-strategy statements required under the FRBM framework.
- **NK Singh FRBM Review Committee** → debt anchor + escape-clause/counter-cyclical emphasis; the widely tested **60% general-government debt recommendation (40% Centre + 20% States)** is a **historical committee recommendation/PYQ anchor**, not a timeless current target.
- Old “FD must always be 3%” shorthand is unsafe as current law/policy.
- **Current-framework rule for permanent notes:** retain the debt-anchor/glide-path concept; keep live annual deficit/debt targets in a `↻ DYNAMIC` layer.

##### A5. Government funds / public debt

- **Consolidated Fund of India (Art. 266)** → Union revenues, loans raised, loan recoveries; appropriation required for withdrawal as constitutionally prescribed.
- **Public Account (Art. 266)** → money held by Government in trust/other public money; withdrawals are not voted like CFI appropriation.
- **Contingency Fund (Art. 267)** → advances for unforeseen expenditure pending legislative authorization.
- **Union borrowing: Art. 292**; State borrowing: Art. 293.
- Central-government liabilities include public debt plus other liabilities/public-account obligations.
- **Public debt:** internal + external sovereign debt.
- **Off-budget borrowing:** borrowing by public entities whose servicing ultimately burdens Government but is not transparently captured in headline Budget borrowing → fiscal-risk/transparency issue.

##### A6. Disinvestment

- Disinvestment proceeds = **non-debt capital receipt**.
- Disinvestment ≠ necessarily privatisation.
- Government may reduce equity while retaining management control.
- Do not assume proceeds are primarily for external-debt repayment.

##### A7. Minimal fiscal-federalism bridge

- **Finance Commission — Art. 280**: recommends Union–State tax devolution, grants-in-aid and specified fiscal matters.
- Keep detailed constitutional mechanics in Polity; Public Finance owns economic logic of vertical/horizontal devolution and tax effort.

#### B. Taxation

##### B1. Tax-system concepts

| Type | Memory cue |
|---|---|
| **Progressive** | average/effective burden rises with ability/income |
| **Proportional** | constant rate |
| **Regressive** | burden falls proportionately as income rises |
| **Direct tax** | legal incidence and intended burden on same taxpayer |
| **Indirect tax** | burden can be shifted through price |

- Tax incidence is an **economic** question; statutory payer ≠ necessarily final bearer.
- **Tax-to-GDP** = tax revenue relative to GDP; movement reflects growth, policy, compliance, structure and cycle—not one single cause.
- **Tax buoyancy** = % change in tax revenue / % change in GDP (includes policy + base effects).
- **Tax elasticity** = responsiveness of tax revenue to tax base/income **holding discretionary tax-policy changes aside**.

##### B2. VAT / ITC / GST

- VAT/GST logic → tax on value addition + **Input Tax Credit (ITC)** → limits cascading.
- GST is broadly **destination/consumption based**.
- Intra-State → **CGST + SGST/UTGST**.
- Inter-State/import supply → **IGST**, later apportioned/settled under statutory place-of-supply and credit rules.
- `TRAP →` IGST is **not** a mechanically fixed “50% Centre + 50% destination State” payment in every transaction.
- Exports/qualifying SEZ supplies → **zero-rated** architecture.
- `TRAP →` GST did **not** simply erase every indirect tax except customs. Customs continues; constitutionally excluded/temporarily outside GST categories and State excise/VAT domains remain relevant.
- Product-wise exemptions, thresholds and rates = `↻ DYNAMIC`.

##### B3. GST constitutional anchor

- **101st Constitutional Amendment** enabled GST framework.
- **GST Council — Art. 279A**.
- Voting architecture: decision threshold = ≥3/4 weighted votes of members present/voting; Union weight = 1/3; States collectively = 2/3.
- Detailed federalism/judicial doctrine → Polity cross-link.

##### B4. Cess vs surcharge

- **Art. 271** specifically concerns **surcharge** on specified Union taxes/duties for Union purposes.
- **Cess is distinct**; specific-purpose cesses are excluded from the divisible pool under the constitutional distribution architecture (Art. 270).
- Never write `Article 271 = cess + surcharge`.

##### B5. Direct-tax current-law anchor

- **Income-tax Act, 2025** is the current governing Act for Tax Year 2026–27 onward; the Income-tax Act, 1961 continues for earlier years/transitional proceedings.
- Older PYQs/base notes referring to the **1961 Act** remain valid **historical question context**, not current-law naming.
- UPPCS “Direct Tax Code” question = historical reform vocabulary, not current governing statute.

##### B6. Agricultural income

- “Agricultural income” has a statutory definition; **all rural/allied activities are not automatically agricultural income**.
- Poultry/wool-rearing etc. are not exempt merely because carried out in a rural/agricultural setting.
- Rural agricultural land satisfying the statutory exclusion is not treated as a “capital asset” for the relevant capital-gains provision; apply the statutory conditions, not a blanket rural-land rule.

##### B7. BEPS / international-tax anti-avoidance

- **BEPS:** profit shifted to low/no-tax jurisdictions where little economic activity/value creation occurs.
- **Transfer pricing:** pricing of transactions between associated enterprises; arm’s-length principle central.
- **GAAR:** anti-avoidance framework against impermissible avoidance arrangements.
- **Indirect transfer:** transfer of an offshore entity/interest whose value substantially derives from assets located in India can trigger Indian tax rules subject to statutory conditions.
- **Tax planning** = lawful use of provisions.
- **Tax evasion** = unlawful concealment/misstatement.
- Avoid defining all “tax avoidance” as simply “illegal but not yet prohibited”; aggressive avoidance may be formally within wording yet countered by specific/GAAR rules.

##### B8. Historical tax instruments — do not present as current

- **Equalisation Levy**
  - 2% e-commerce-supply levy removed w.e.f. **1 Aug 2024**.
  - 6% specified-services levy removed w.e.f. **1 Apr 2025**.

##### 5A.1 Bretton Woods / World Bank Group

| Institution | Durable identity | Primary client / function | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **IBRD** | One of original Bretton Woods institutions; with IDA forms the **World Bank** | Loans, guarantees, risk-management/advisory to **middle-income + creditworthy low-income governments** | **Not the concessional window** |
| **IDA** | WBG concessional arm | Poorest/low-income countries; grants + highly concessional credits | Do not confuse with IBRD |
| **IFC** | WBG private-sector arm | Investment/advisory/mobilisation for private enterprise | IFC association with Masala bonds ≠ IFC required for every Masala issue |

- **World Bank = IBRD + IDA**; **World Bank Group = IBRD + IDA + IFC + MIGA + ICSID**.
- **Global Economic Prospects → World Bank**.
- Bretton Woods twins: **IMF + IBRD/World Bank**; both headquartered in **Washington, D.C.**

##### 5A.2 IMF

- Core mandate for Prelims: international monetary cooperation + macro/financial stability; surveillance; temporary BoP financing; reserve/liquidity architecture.
- **Quota** → capital subscription, voting weight, SDR allocation share, and an important basis for access to IMF resources. Quota is denominated in **SDRs**.
- Quota subscription: roughly **25% reserve-asset portion** (SDRs or currencies specified/accepted by IMF) + **75% member’s own currency**. Do **not** equate the reserve-asset portion with “the five SDR-basket currencies”.
- **Reserve Tranche Position (historically “gold tranche”)**:
  - liquid reserve claim of a member on IMF;
  - initially linked to reserve-asset portion of quota, but varies with IMF use of member currency;
  - part of member’s international reserves;
  - drawing it is **not ordinary use of IMF credit** and is not the same as an IMF loan facility.
- **SDR**:
  - international reserve asset created by IMF; unit of account for IMF;
  - **not a currency; not a claim on IMF**;
  - potential claim on **freely usable currencies** of IMF members;
  - value based on basket: USD, EUR, CNY/RMB, JPY, GBP; **weights are dynamic → omit from permanent notes**;
  - distinguish **SDR allocation** (cumulative amount allocated) from **SDR holdings** (what member currently holds). Net interest arises when holdings differ from allocations.
- **RFI** → General Resources Account; rapid assistance to **any IMF member** with urgent BoP need.
- **RCF** → Poverty Reduction and Growth Trust; **concessional** rapid assistance to eligible low-income countries with urgent BoP need.
- Reports: **World Economic Outlook (WEO)** + **Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR)** → IMF.
- **IMFC** = ministerial-level advisory body of IMF governance; discusses international monetary/financial system and global economy.

##### 5A.3 AIIB / NDB / ADB — durable layer

- **AIIB**: multilateral development bank; sustainable infrastructure in Asia and beyond; HQ **Beijing**; membership open to IBRD or ADB members; **Board of Governors = highest decision-making body**; Board of Directors manages general operations under delegated powers.
- **NDB / BRICS Bank**: established by BRICS; HQ **Shanghai**; mobilises resources for infrastructure + sustainable-development projects in BRICS and other emerging/developing economies. Current membership/shareholding = dynamic.
- **ADB**: Asia-Pacific MDB. Programme names/hubs are current examples; e.g. 2025 Nature Solutions Finance Hub is a dated PYQ anchor, not core permanent recall.

##### 5A.4 WTO architecture

- GATT system preceded WTO; **WTO established 1995 after Uruguay Round / Marrakesh Agreement**.
- Core logic:
  - **MFN** → do not discriminate among trading partners, subject to recognised exceptions (including qualifying regional trade arrangements/developing-country preferences etc.).
  - **National Treatment** → after import clears border/internal market, do not discriminate against imported product vis-à-vis like domestic product through internal measures.
- High-yield agreement map:

| Agreement | Domain | Elimination key |
|---|---|---|
| **GATT 1994** | Trade in goods | tariff/non-tariff goods discipline |
| **GATS** | Trade in services | 4 modes of service supply |
| **TRIPS** | Intellectual property | minimum standards + WTO dispute framework |
| **TRIMs** | Investment measures affecting **trade in goods only** | tied to GATT national treatment + quantitative restrictions; not general FDI law |
| **AoA** | Agriculture | market access + domestic support + export competition |
| **SPS** | Food safety / animal & plant health measures | science/risk + non-discrimination discipline |
| **TFA** | Customs/border procedures | transparency, release/clearance, transit, simplification |

##### 5A.5 AoA boxes + Peace Clause

| Box | Nature | Limit logic |
|---|---|---|
| **Green** | no/minimal trade distortion; decoupled/general services subject to criteria | no reduction commitment/cap under AoA if criteria met |
| **Amber** | trade-distorting support incl price/product-linked support | subject to AMS commitments; de minimis generally **5% developed / 10% most developing** |
| **Blue** | direct payments under production-limiting programmes | exempt from reduction commitments; no current spending ceiling under AoA |

- **Peace Clause/public stockholding** = conditional interim protection for qualifying developing-country public-stockholding food-security programmes while permanent solution is negotiated; **not** blanket immunity for every subsidy or every breach of “10%”.
- Separate **de minimis** allowance from a universal “subsidy cap”; not all agriculture support belongs to Amber Box.

##### 5A.6 TRIMs / trade remedies / TFA

- **TRIMs applies to trade in goods only**.
- Prohibits TRIMs inconsistent with **GATT Article III (national treatment)** or **Article XI (quantitative restrictions)**; classic examples → local-content and trade-balancing/import-restriction requirements.
- It is **not a general code regulating foreign investment**.
- **Anti-dumping:** dumping assessed against **normal value vs export price** + injury/causal requirements; in India **DGTR** investigates/recommends; Finance Ministry levies duty.
- **Countervailing duty:** offsets actionable subsidisation causing injury; distinguish from anti-dumping.
- **TFA:** negotiated under WTO; concluded at **Bali 2013**; India ratified in 2016; agreement entered into force in **2017** after required ratifications.

##### 5A.7 TRIPS / GI / patent traps

- **GI is an intellectual-property right** identifying goods whose quality/reputation/characteristic is essentially linked to geographical origin.
- GI gives eligible producers/users in defined region the right to prevent misleading/non-conforming use by outsiders; it is not merely a “place-name permission”.
- India: **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**; registration **10 years, renewable**; GI Registry at **Chennai** under the Registrar of Geographical Indications/CGPDTM system.
- Patent trap: Indian Patents Act **s.3(j)** excludes plants/animals in whole or part (other than microorganisms), seeds, varieties/species and essentially biological processes for production/propagation from patentability.
- Plant varieties are protected through a **sui generis** regime, not ordinary plant-variety patenting.
- **IPAB dated trap:** it existed when UPSC 2019 asked the question; it was abolished through the 2021 tribunal reforms and appellate functions shifted to courts. Never carry the 2019 institutional statement forward as current fact.

##### 5A.8 Regional trade agreements

| Integration level | Durable meaning |
|---|---|
| **PTA** | preferential tariff concessions on selected products among members |
| **FTA** | tariffs/quantitative restrictions substantially reduced/eliminated among members; members retain own external tariffs against non-members |
| **Customs Union** | FTA + **common external tariff** |
| **Common Market** | customs union + freer movement of factors (labour/capital) |
| **Economic Union** | common market + deeper harmonisation/coordination of economic policy/institutions |

- **Rules of Origin (RoO)** determine whether a product genuinely originates in an FTA partner → prevent **trade deflection** through a low-tariff member.
- **Certificate of Origin** evidences origin for preferential tariff claim.
- CECA/CEPA/ECTA labels are **agreement-specific, not a universal legal hierarchy**. Read chapters/coverage; generally Indian “comprehensive” pacts may extend beyond goods into services/investment/cooperation.
- Dated PYQ anchors only:
  - **BTIA → India–EU**.
  - TPP/CPTPP-type arrangements = trade pacts, not “all Pacific Rim” or maritime-security alliances.
  - ASEAN FTA-partner list in a legacy PYQ must be read **as of that year**.
  - India–Australia **ECTA** = dated bilateral trade-agreement example; event-attendee details are current-layer trivia.

##### 5A.9 Global debt-governance anchor

- **G20 Common Framework for Debt Treatments beyond DSSI** → G20 + Paris Club approach for coordinated sovereign-debt treatment of eligible countries; case-specific debt restructuring with comparable treatment logic.
- EU **Stability and Growth Pact** → fiscal discipline (deficit/debt rules); not a mechanism for pooling infrastructure or technology.

#### B. External Sector: BoP, Capital Flows, Exchange Rate & Trade

##### 5B.1 BoP — use modern classification, recognise PYQ legacy terminology

**BoP:** systematic record of transactions between **residents and non-residents** over a period.

| BPM6 account | Main contents | Exam trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Current Account** | goods; services; **primary income** (interest, dividends, compensation etc.); **secondary income** (remittances/transfers) | remittances ≠ capital inflow; tourist spending in India = service export |
| **Capital Account** *(narrow modern use)* | capital transfers; acquisition/disposal of non-produced, non-financial assets | much smaller than legacy “capital account” used in older textbooks/PYQs |
| **Financial Account** | direct investment; portfolio investment; derivatives; other investment/loans/deposits; reserve assets | older UPSC questions often call these the **capital account** broadly |

- **Balance of Trade (BoT)** = merchandise exports − merchandise imports; services are excluded.
- **Current-account balance** = goods + services + primary income + secondary income.
- BoP accounting ultimately balances after financial flows/reserve movements and statistical errors/omissions.
- A **CAD** requires financing through net financial inflows and/or reserve drawdown; **capital inflows finance a CAD but are not themselves current-account credits**.
- Legacy PYQ caution (`UPSC-CSE-2011-GS1-Q010`): canonical answer treats attracting FDI/FII as helping “reduce” CAD. For conceptual notes write **finance/ease the BoP pressure from CAD**, not “FDI is part of current account”.

##### 5B.2 FDI vs FPI

- **FDI** under Indian foreign-investment rules:
  - investment in an **unlisted** Indian company, or
  - **10% or more** of post-issue paid-up equity capital on fully diluted basis in a listed Indian company;
  - once classified as FDI, subsequent fall below 10% does not automatically reclassify it as FPI.
- **FPI** = foreign investment **below 10%** in listed equity, subject to applicable framework/limits; FPIs may also invest in eligible debt securities.

| FDI | FPI |
|---|---|
| enterprise/control/lasting-interest orientation | portfolio/financial-return orientation |
| generally stickier | generally more liquid/volatile |
| non-debt equity flow when equity investment | may be equity **or debt** portfolio investment |
| can transmit management/technology/know-how | no comparable managerial control merely by being portfolio holder |

- **FDI is generally non-debt-creating**; do not confuse with NRE deposits/ordinary foreign borrowing.
- **P-notes / Offshore Derivative Instruments:** instruments issued by registered FPIs to overseas investors providing economic exposure to Indian securities without those investors directly registering as FPIs; subject to SEBI framework.
- **ADR/GDR** = depository-receipt route backed by underlying securities; classification can depend on instrument/conditions. Do not use simplistic “all DR = FPI” rule.

##### 5B.3 ECB / Masala / external debt

- **External debt** = outstanding liabilities of residents to non-residents requiring future principal/interest payment; covers government + non-government borrowers; denomination may be USD or other foreign currencies and rupees.
- **ECB** = commercial borrowing by eligible resident entities from recognised non-resident lenders under RBI/FEMA framework; may be foreign-currency or rupee-denominated depending on instrument/rules.
- **Foreign-currency ECB:** rupee depreciation raises domestic-currency debt-service burden/currency risk if unhedged.
- **Masala bond:** rupee-denominated bond issued overseas → issuer owes rupees; **currency risk largely on foreign investor**. IFC helped pioneer/popularise Masala bonds; IFC participation is **not a legal prerequisite for every issue**.
- **Trade credit:** supplier’s credit / buyer’s credit linked to imports.
- Vulnerability rises with: larger short-term external debt, high debt-service burden, currency mismatch/unhedged FX debt, low reserves/import cover, persistent CAD + volatile portfolio financing.

##### 5B.4 Forex reserves

India’s official forex reserves comprise:
1. **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)**
2. **Gold**
3. **SDR holdings**
4. **Reserve Tranche Position (RTP) in IMF**

- **Import cover** = reserve stock expressed as months of prospective/recent import payments.
- Adequacy is multi-dimensional: import cover + short-term debt/reserves + debt-service ratio + maturity profile + currency composition + access to stable forex earnings.
- Stable service exports and remittances can improve external resilience by supplying foreign exchange.

##### 5B.5 Exchange-rate system and quotations

- **Direct quote (India):** ₹ per unit foreign currency, e.g. ₹/$.
  - ₹/$ **rises** → dollar appreciates / rupee **depreciates**.
  - ₹/$ **falls** → rupee **appreciates**.
- **Floating:** market demand/supply determines rate.
- **Fixed/pegged:** authority commits to a parity/peg and must use reserves/policy controls to defend it.
- **Managed float:** market-determined most of the time; central bank intervenes to smooth disorderly volatility or pursue policy objectives.
- Terminology:
  - market-driven fall/rise → **depreciation/appreciation**;
  - official parity change under fixed/pegged system → **devaluation/revaluation**.

**Dollar demand rises:** imports, outward investment/remittance/tourism, external-debt service, capital outflow → pressure for rupee depreciation.  
**Dollar supply rises:** exports, inward remittances, tourism receipts, FDI/FPI/borrowing inflows → pressure for rupee appreciation.

##### 5B.6 NEER / REER

- **NEER** = trade-weighted average of bilateral **nominal** exchange rates against a currency basket.
- **REER** = NEER adjusted for relative price/inflation differentials with trading partners.
- RBI-index convention used by UPSC: **↑NEER index = rupee appreciation**; **↑REER = real appreciation**.
- Ceteris paribus, **↑REER → exports less price-competitive / imports relatively more attractive**; therefore “higher REER = improved competitiveness” is a classic false statement.
- Domestic inflation rising faster than partner-country inflation can make REER diverge upward from NEER.

##### 5B.7 RBI intervention + sterilisation

- Rapid rupee depreciation → RBI may **sell USD / buy INR** → supports rupee and withdraws rupee liquidity.
- Strong inflows/appreciation pressure → RBI may **buy USD / sell INR** → adds rupee liquidity.
- **Sterilisation** = offset the domestic-liquidity consequence of FX intervention.
  - If RBI buys USD and injects INR → can **sell G-secs/absorb liquidity**.
  - If RBI sells USD and absorbs INR → may inject domestic liquidity through other tools if needed.
- UPSC anchor: **OMO is a sterilisation tool**.

##### 5B.8 Convertibility

- **Current-account convertibility:** freedom to exchange currency for bona fide current transactions such as trade/services/income/transfers, subject to legal/regulatory framework.
- India has current-account convertibility; **capital-account convertibility is not full**—capital flows remain subject to differentiated limits/conditions.
- Full capital-account convertibility can increase efficiency/access but also amplify sudden-stop/capital-flight risk; it is **not automatically a crisis buffer**.

##### 5B.9 Devaluation/depreciation and trade balance

- Currency depreciation/devaluation generally:
  - makes exports cheaper in foreign-currency terms → improves **price competitiveness**;
  - makes imports costlier in domestic currency → imported-inflation pressure;
  - raises rupee burden of unhedged foreign-currency debt.
- **Trade balance need not improve immediately or necessarily**:
  - quantities respond with lags/contracts;
  - if import/export demand is inelastic, value effects can initially worsen balance → **J-curve**;
  - durable improvement depends on elasticities/supply response (Marshall–Lerner logic).
- Therefore UPSC 2021: “devaluation necessarily improves export competitiveness” can be true while “necessarily improves trade balance” is false.

##### 5B.10 Currency-crisis buffers / capital flight

- Risk-reducing buffers: adequate reserves; diversified/stable forex earnings; remittances; lower short-term external debt; manageable external debt-service; prudent currency mismatch; credible macro policy.
- Tighter major-economy monetary policy can raise returns abroad → portfolio outflows/capital flight → domestic-currency depreciation → higher servicing burden on unhedged foreign-currency ECBs.
- **Canonical caveat:** the famous 2022 UPSC item testing this chain (`Q061`) is **cancelled/inactive** in the canonical package; retain mechanism as concept, not as active PYQ count.

##### 5B.11 India trade — permanent vs replaceable layer

**Permanent:**
- merchandise trade balance; services trade; current-account linkage; export/import concentration risk; energy/gold/electronics-type import dependence as *conceptual* examples; exchange-rate competitiveness; trade-credit mechanics.
- services exports + remittances can offset merchandise deficit.

**Replace every current-affairs cycle:**
- largest export/import partner;
- largest FDI source;
- remittance rank;
- commodity-wise highest import/export;
- India’s world-export share;
- bilateral surplus/deficit rankings;
- one-year tariff/customs rates.

**2026 historical PYQ anchor:** artificially fixed rupee–sterling rate recommended by Hilton–Young Commission was adopted to support remittance flows and India’s colonial-era creditworthiness; keep as dated factual anchor, not a rule of modern exchange-rate management.

### 7. Cross-Topic / Cross-Subtopic Traps

- **World Bank ≠ World Bank Group**; World Bank = IBRD + IDA.
- **IBRD ≠ IDA:** IBRD not the highly concessional window.
- **SDR ≠ currency; Reserve Tranche ≠ ordinary IMF credit**.
- **Forex reserves = FCA + Gold + SDR + RTP**, not only three items.
- **Legacy “capital account” PYQ language ≠ BPM6 narrow capital account**; FDI/FPI/loans are modern financial-account flows.
- **FDI ≥10% listed / unlisted; FPI <10% listed**; FPI may include debt, so “FPI = equity only” is unsafe.
- **Masala = rupee-denominated offshore debt**; currency risk on investor; IFC not mandatory.
- **Depreciation/devaluation ≠ guaranteed trade-balance improvement**.
- **↑REER ≠ better competitiveness** under RBI/UPSC index convention.
- **RBI dollar sale** counters sharp rupee depreciation; **dollar purchase** can counter appreciation/add reserves; sterilisation offsets liquidity effect.
- **GI is IPR**; protected indication does not give one producer ownership of the underlying production technique.
- **TRIMs = goods only**; not general foreign-investment regulation.
- **AoA 10% developing-country de minimis ≠ universal farm-subsidy ceiling**; Peace Clause is conditional.
- **MFN does not prohibit all FTAs**; qualifying regional agreements are recognised exceptions.

#### B. Industry, Labour & Infrastructure

##### B1. Industrial-policy spine

**New Industrial Policy, 1991**
- Industrial licensing → substantially abolished; only a narrow notified set remained.
- Public-sector reservation → sharply reduced over time.
- MRTP-era asset-threshold restrictions on large firms → removed/liberalised.
- Foreign investment/technology access → liberalised.
- Exam trap → 1991 policy = **delicensing + competition + private/foreign participation**, not abolition of all regulation.

**Special Economic Zones (SEZs)**
- SEZ Act, 2005 + Rules, 2006 → framework for specially delineated zones to promote exports/investment/infrastructure and simplify approvals/customs treatment.
- Institutional architecture → Board of Approval at Centre; Development Commissioner/zone-level administration.
- **Do not freeze** number of SEZs, newly notified zones or current tax sunset provisions in permanent notes.

##### B2. IIP + core industries

- **Index of Industrial Production (IIP)** → official high-frequency **volume** indicator of industrial output; compiled/released by **NSO, MoSPI**.
- **Current base:** `2022-23 = 100` (new series first released 1 June 2026), replacing 2011-12.
- 2026 revision → updated basket/weights + wider sectoral coverage; therefore **old PYQ weights/base-year claims are historical**.
- **Eight Core Industries** → Coal, Crude Oil, Natural Gas, Refinery Products, Fertilizers, Steel, Cement, Electricity.
- Trap → **textiles is not one of the eight**. Avoid memorising old combined IIP weight or “highest-weight core industry” as a timeless fact after base revision.

##### B3. MSME — current statutory classification

**Composite criteria; same for manufacturing + services; both investment AND turnover ceilings matter. Revised w.e.f. 1 Apr 2025.**

| Category | Investment in plant/machinery/equipment | Annual turnover |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Micro** | ≤ ₹2.5 crore | ≤ ₹10 crore |
| **Small** | ≤ ₹25 crore | ≤ ₹100 crore |
| **Medium** | ≤ ₹125 crore | ≤ ₹500 crore |

- Exceeding either relevant ceiling moves the enterprise out of that category as per classification rules.
- UPSC 2023 threshold statement must be read against the law applicable at the time; **do not preserve pre-2025 thresholds as current**.
- Bank credit to MSMEs has a priority-sector linkage; exact sub-target/rule belongs to Banking notes if tested.

##### B4. CSR — Companies Act, 2013, s.135

**Applicability trigger: any one in immediately preceding FY**
- Net worth ≥ **₹500 crore**, OR
- Turnover ≥ **₹1,000 crore**, OR
- Net profit ≥ **₹5 crore**.

**Spending rule**
- Board to ensure at least **2% of average net profits of the three immediately preceding FYs** is spent on CSR (subject to statutory treatment of unspent amounts).

**Not safe as CSR**
- Activities in normal course of business, except limited statutory exceptions.
- Activities designed exclusively for employees and their families are not the general CSR contemplated by Schedule VII/rules.
- Political contributions do not count as CSR.

**Trap:** Companies **Act 2013**, not “Companies Act 2014”; spending rule is **2%**, not 1%.

##### B5. Labour architecture — historical PYQs vs current law

**Four Labour Codes are in force from 21 Nov 2025; they rationalised 29 Central labour laws.**

| Current Code | Core field | Important legacy anchors subsumed |
|---|---|---|
| **Code on Wages, 2019** | wages/minimum wages/bonus/equal remuneration | Payment of Wages; Minimum Wages; Payment of Bonus; Equal Remuneration |
| **Industrial Relations Code, 2020** | trade unions; standing orders; industrial disputes | Trade Unions; Industrial Employment (Standing Orders); Industrial Disputes |
| **Code on Social Security, 2020** | EPF/ESI/gratuity/maternity/unorganised-worker social security | EPF; ESI; Maternity Benefit; Gratuity etc. |
| **OSHWC Code, 2020** | occupational safety, factories, mines, contract/inter-state migrant labour etc. | 13 safety/working-condition laws |

**Maternity — durable test points**
- Current social-security code retains paid maternity-benefit architecture; **26 weeks** for a woman with fewer than two surviving children; **12 weeks** where she has two or more surviving children.
- Creche/work-from-home and establishment-coverage details can be tested, but use current rules rather than assuming the old 1961 Act operates independently.

**Casual-worker trap**
- “Casual” label alone does **not** automatically decide EPF/social-security coverage. Coverage depends on the applicable establishment/employee provisions; avoid “all casual workers get EPF” or “no casual worker gets EPF” absolutes.

**ILO child-labour conventions**
- **C138** → Minimum Age Convention.
- **C182** → Worst Forms of Child Labour Convention.
- India ratified both in 2017.

**UPPCS Code-on-Wages trap:** Contract Labour Act is **not** one of the four laws consolidated into the Code on Wages.

##### B6. Industrial finance / infrastructure institutions

| Institution/tool | Core role | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| **NIIF** | Government-anchored investment platform/fund manager mobilising institutional/private capital for infrastructure and related assets | Not an organ of NITI Aayog; not a regulator |
| **Global Infrastructure Facility (World Bank)** | Helps governments prepare/structure complex infrastructure PPPs and crowd in private capital | Not a retail project lender to every infrastructure project |
| **PM Gati Shakti** | GIS-based National Master Plan for integrated, multimodal infrastructure planning across ministries/sectors | Coordination/planning platform; not a standalone construction company/fund |

##### B7. IPR / manufacturing-policy anchors

#### C. Poverty, Inclusion & Employment

##### C1. Poverty concepts

| Concept | Durable meaning | Elimination cue |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute poverty** | Below a specified minimum/basic-needs standard | Fixed/normative threshold |
| **Relative poverty** | Deprivation relative to prevailing distribution/median living standard | Comparison/inequality dimension |
| **Human poverty** | Deprivation in capabilities/services/security even beyond income | Education, health, shelter, dignity, exclusion |
| **Multidimensional poverty** | Simultaneous weighted deprivations across health, education and living standards | Not reducible to `$x/day` income alone |
| **Social exclusion** | Exclusion from normal participation/access enjoyed by others; can be cause and consequence of poverty | Caste/gender/access/institutional barriers |
| **Vulnerability** | Higher probability of becoming/remain poor after shocks or because of structural disadvantage | Risk, not necessarily current BPL status |

- **Headcount Ratio (HCR)** = persons below poverty line / total population.
- Conventional Indian poverty measurement historically used **consumption expenditure**, partly because income is volatile/under-reported.
- State-specific poverty lines can differ because **price levels differ**.

##### C2. Indian poverty-estimation chronology

| Committee/approach | What to remember |
|---|---|
| **Dandekar & Rath (1971)** | Early calorie-linked poverty estimation study |
| **Y.K. Alagh Task Force (1979)** | Calorie norms; separate rural/urban poverty lines |
| **Lakdawala Expert Group (1993)** | State-specific price adjustment; calorie-linked basket with CPI updating |
| **Tendulkar Committee (2009)** | Moved away from direct calorie anchoring; broader private consumption incl. health/education; uniform reference basket logic; Mixed Reference Period use |
| **Rangarajan Committee (2014 report)** | Revised food + non-food normative requirements; higher 2011-12 thresholds; **recommendations were not adopted as the official replacement** |
| **N.C. Saxena Committee** | Rural BPL-identification criteria |
| **Hashim Committee** | Urban BPL-identification methodology |

**Current-use caution**
- Tendulkar methodology underpinned the **last official Planning Commission national poverty estimates for 2011-12**. Do **not** call the old ₹27/₹33-per-day figures a live 2026 poverty line.
- The old institution-answer “Planning Commission fixes poverty line” is historical PYQ context; Planning Commission no longer exists.

##### C3. World Bank poverty line — corrected current anchor

As of the World Bank’s June 2025 revision using **2021 PPPs**:
- **International/extreme poverty line:** `$3.00/person/day`.
- **Lower-middle-income line:** `$4.20/day`.
- **Upper-middle-income line:** `$8.30/day`.

**Override:** base-note `$1.90/day` is obsolete (it was an older PPP vintage; later `$2.15` also superseded).

##### C4. Global MPI vs India National MPI

| Feature | **Global MPI** | **National MPI — India** |
|---|---|---|
| Producer | UNDP + OPHI | NITI Aayog |
| Method | Alkire–Foster | Alkire–Foster |
| Dimensions | Health, Education, Standard of Living | Same 3 dimensions |
| Indicators | **10** | **12** |
| Poverty cut-off | Weighted deprivation score **≥ 1/3** | Weighted deprivation score **≥ 1/3 (33.33%)** |
| Measure | `MPI = H × A` (incidence × intensity) | Same |
| Extra India indicators | — | **Maternal Health + Bank Account** |

**Global 10 indicators**
- Health → Nutrition; Child Mortality.
- Education → Years of Schooling; School Attendance.
- Standard of Living → Cooking Fuel; Sanitation; Drinking Water; Electricity; Housing; Assets.

**National additions** → Maternal Health (health) + Bank Account (living standard).

**Critical correction:** a household/person is **not** MPI-poor merely because it is deprived in “3 or more indicators”; indicators have **different weights**. The criterion is the **weighted deprivation score ≥ 1/3**.

##### C5. Inequality

- **Lorenz Curve** → cumulative population share vs cumulative income/wealth share; farther from line of equality = greater inequality.
- **Gini coefficient** → 0 = perfect equality; 1 = perfect inequality (conceptual range).
- **Kuznets hypothesis** → inequality may first rise and later fall with development/structural transformation → inverted-U; hypothesis, not universal law.

##### C6. Inclusive growth

**Core** → broad-based growth where excluded groups participate in and benefit from the growth process.

High-yield characteristics:
- both **pace + pattern** of growth matter;
- access to markets/resources/opportunities;
- productive employment/structural transformation;
- regional/social inclusion;
- human-capital access (health/education/skills);
- government as enabler/provider of public goods + fair institutions.

**Trap:** inclusive growth ≠ only redistribution after growth; strengthening capital markets alone is not sufficient.

##### C7. Employment indicators

| Indicator | Formula/meaning |
|---|---|
| **Labour Force** | Employed + unemployed persons actively seeking/available for work under the chosen survey status |
| **LFPR** | Labour Force / Population in the specified survey age group × 100 |
| **WPR** | Employed persons / Population × 100 |
| **Unemployment Rate (UR)** | Unemployed / Labour Force × 100 |
| **Employment elasticity** | % change in employment / % change in output; low elasticity during high growth → “jobless growth” concern |

**Corrections**
- Do not write `UR = Employed/Unemployed`.
- Do not use one universal “working-age = 16–64” definition. Age base depends on survey/publication; PLFS commonly reports specified cohorts such as 15+ or 15–59.

##### C8. Measurement status

- **Usual Status (PS+SS)** → long reference period; principal status + subsidiary economic activity.
- **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** → shorter reference period; captures short-term/seasonal labour-market conditions better.
- **PLFS** → NSO/MoSPI; key source for LFPR/WPR/UR; produces annual and higher-frequency urban estimates under specified status concepts.
- Dynamic annual rates → **omit from permanent notes**.

##### C9. Unemployment types

| Type | Trigger/cue |
|---|---|
| **Disguised** | More workers than needed; removing some does not reduce output → marginal productivity ≈ zero |
| **Seasonal** | Work unavailable in off-season |
| **Frictional** | Search/matching/time-information gap between jobs |
| **Structural** | Skills/location/industry structure no longer matches job demand |
| **Cyclical** | Downturn/recession reduces aggregate demand/jobs |
| **Open** | Willing/available but no work |
| **Educated** | Qualifications exist but suitable employment unavailable/mismatch |
| **Voluntary** | Person chooses not to accept available work under prevailing conditions |

#### D. Development Indices & Reports

##### D1. Human-development family

| Index/report | Producer | Components / identity | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Human Development Report / HDI** | **UNDP** | Health = life expectancy at birth; Education = **mean years of schooling + expected years of schooling**; Living standard = **GNI per capita (PPP)** | Old “adult attainment + enrolment ratio” formula is obsolete |
| **Gender Development Index (GDI)** | UNDP | Female HDI relative to male HDI using same health/education/income dimensions | Measures gender gap in human-development achievements, not a separate 3-dimension GII |

##### D3. Index-component traps

**PQLI — Physical Quality of Life Index**
- Associated with **Morris D. Morris**.
- Components → infant mortality, life expectancy at age one, basic literacy.
- **Income/GDP is not a component**.

**World Bank Human Capital Index (HCI)**
- Human Capital Project metric; combines survival, schooling and health/productivity prospects.
- Do not confuse **HCI** with UNDP **HDI**.

**GHI**
- Four indicators combine both population-level undernourishment + child outcomes.
- Wasting = low weight-for-height; stunting = low height-for-age.
