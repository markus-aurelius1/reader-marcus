## Article 368 — Procedure

- Part XX · Parliament exercises {{**constituent power**}} → amend by {{**addition, variation or repeal**}}.

> [!FLOW] Bill in {{**either House**}} (minister **or private member**; {{no prior Presidential recommendation}}) → each House **separately**: {{majority of total membership **+** ≥ 2/3 of members present and voting}} → *(federal provisions)* ratification by {{**not less than one-half** of State legislatures}} → President {{**shall** give assent}}

- {{**No joint sitting**}} on deadlock.
- State legislatures can !!never!! initiate; they only ratify — by {{simple majority}}.
- Presidential assent made mandatory by → {{24th Amendment, 1971}}.

> [!TRAP] Special majority ≠ 2/3 of *total* membership. · ≥ ½ of States ≠ all States. · "No prior recommendation" ≠ discretionary veto. · Either House ≠ Lok Sabha only.

## Three Routes of Change

| Route | Requirement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple majority** *(outside Art. 368)* | Ordinary legislative majority | New States / boundaries / names (Arts. 2–4); creation / abolition of Legislative Councils; citizenship; Second Schedule; parliamentary procedure & privileges; official language; Fifth & Sixth Schedules |
| **Special majority** | Art. 368 majority in each House | Fundamental Rights; DPSP; everything not in the other two |
| **Special majority + States** | + ≥ ½ State legislatures | Federal provisions ↓ |

- Art. 368 itself provides {{**two**}} methods; simple-majority changes are a third *practical* category, {{not "amendments" for Art. 368}}.

### Needs State ratification — Art. 368(2) proviso
- {{Arts. **54, 55**}} → election of President
- {{Arts. **73, 162**}} → extent of Union / State executive power
- {{Art. **241**}} → High Courts for UTs
- {{Art. **279A**}} → GST Council
- {{Ch. IV Part V}} → Union Judiciary · {{Ch. V Part VI}} → High Courts
- {{Ch. I Part XI}} → legislative relations
- Any List in the {{Seventh Schedule}}
- {{Representation of States in Parliament}}
- {{Art. 368 itself}}

> [!TRAP] Art. **162** (State executive power) needs ratification; Art. **158** (conditions of Governor's office) does !!not!!.

## Basic Structure Doctrine

- {{**Judicial doctrine**}} — the Constitution has no textual definition or list; grows case by case.
- **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** → Parliament may amend **any** provision, incl. FRs, but cannot {{damage / destroy the basic structure}}.
- Judicial review + limited amending power are themselves basic features.

| Cluster | Elements recognised |
|---|---|
| Identity | Supremacy of Constitution; sovereign-democratic-republican character; secularism; federalism; unity & integrity |
| Institutions | Separation of powers; parliamentary system; rule of law; judicial review; independence of judiciary; limited amending power |
| Democratic / rights core | Equality; free & fair elections; freedom & dignity of individual; essence of FRs; effective access to justice |
| Judicial powers | SC — Arts. 32, 136, 141, 142; HC — Arts. 226, 227 |
| Social | Welfare state / social justice; **harmony between FRs and DPSP** |

> [!TRAP] "Parliament cannot amend basic-structure provisions" → wrong; it can amend, not destroy. · Basic structure is !!not!! an enumerated constitutional list — nor are "judicial review" / "basic structure" defined in the text.

## Judgment ↔ Amendment Chain

| Step | Proposition |
|---|---|
| **Shankari Prasad (1951)** | {{Amendment ≠ "law" under Art. 13 → Parliament can amend FRs}}; 1st Amdt upheld |
| **Sajjan Singh (1964–65)** | {{Reaffirmed}} |
| **Golak Nath (1967)** | {{Amendment = "law" → Parliament cannot abridge FRs}} |
| **24th Amdt (1971)** | {{Answer to Golak Nath}}: Arts. 13(4), 368(1),(3); constituent power stated; President shall assent |
| **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** | {{24th upheld; FRs amendable subject to basic structure}} |
| **39th Amdt (1975) → Indira Nehru Gandhi (1975)** | {{Bid to insulate PM's election dispute — struck down}} |
| **42nd Amdt (1976)** | {{Art. 368(4),(5): no judicial challenge + unlimited amending power}} |
| **Minerva Mills (1980)** | {{368(4),(5) invalid; limited amending power, judicial review, FR–DPSP harmony = basic structure}} |
| **Waman Rao (1980)** | {{24 April 1973 cut-off for Ninth Schedule}} |
| **I.R. Coelho (2007)** | {{Ninth Schedule ≠ blanket immunity}} |
| **99th Amdt (2014) → NJAC case (2015)** | {{Struck down; independence of judiciary = basic structure}} |

> [!TRAP] The PM-election exclusion was the **39th**, not the 44th; the 44th *omitted* Art. 329A. · Avoid "FR > DPSP" shorthand — *Minerva Mills* protects their **balance**.
