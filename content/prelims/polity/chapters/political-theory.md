## State, Nation & Sovereignty

- **State** = {{population + definite territory + organised government + sovereignty}} (classic four).
- **Sovereignty** → supreme authority internally **+** independence from external control.
- **Nation** → socio-cultural / historical community; need **not** possess sovereign statehood.
- UPSC best-fit State → permanent community + definite territory + independence from external control + organised government.

> [!TRAP] International recognition is !!not!! one of the four essential elements. · State ≠ Nation.

## Constitution vs Constitutionalism

| Constitution | Constitutionalism |
|---|---|
| Basic legal-political framework (a document) | Principle: **limited government + Rule of Law** |
| Creates / allocates authority | **Limits** authority; controls *how* power is exercised |
| Can exist under authoritarian rule | Needs effective restraints on arbitrary power |

- Chief purpose of a Constitution → {{**define AND limit** governmental power}}.
- ==Constitutional government = limited government== → State authority restricted in the interest of individual liberty.
- Dispersal / decentralisation of power + institutional restraints → support constitutionalism.
- Constitutionalism = antithesis of arbitrary power.

> [!TRAP] Having a constitution ≠ constitutionalism. · Constitutional government ≠ merely elected / popular government.

## Parliamentary vs Presidential Government

| Axis | Parliamentary | Presidential |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | Nominal head + real political executive (PM + CoM) | President = head of State **and** government |
| Legislature–executive | Fusion / co-operation | Institutional separation, with checks & balances |
| Responsibility | Executive responsible to legislature | Not collectively responsible to legislature |
| Tenure | Survives while it retains confidence | Fixed tenure |
| Removal | Loss of confidence / no-confidence | Impeachment-type removal |
| Ministers | Usually legislators | US secretaries sit outside Congress |
| Lower House | Can be dissolved | President cannot dissolve House of Representatives |
| **Exam marker** | ==responsibility / accountability== | ==separation + fixed tenure== |

- India → CoM collectively responsible to {{**Lok Sabha** — `Art. 75(3)`}}.
- Non-MP minister may continue for {{**6 consecutive months** — `Art. 75(5)`}}.
- Main advantage of parliamentary system → **executive responsibility**; government removable by Parliament.
- Collective responsibility = bedrock principle of Cabinet government.
- PM heads the government — single-party **or coalition** — and must command / retain Lok Sabha confidence.

> [!TRAP] Parliamentary ≠ mere election. · Head of State ≠ necessarily head of government. · A minister need not be an MP at every instant (6-month window). · PM is !!not!! necessarily "leader of the majority party" (coalitions). · Presidential ≠ "strict" isolation of organs.

### India ≠ Britain

| | Britain | India |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Parliamentary / Cabinet | Parliamentary / Cabinet |
| Parliament | **Sovereign** | Limited by written Constitution, federal division, FRs, judicial review |
| Head of State | Hereditary | {{Elected — republic}} |

> [!UPPCS]
> - Parliamentary government first developed in → {{Great Britain}}.
> - India is "parliamentary" **because** → executive is responsible to the legislature.

## Liberty, Equality, Rights & Duties

### Liberty
- Best-fit → {{opportunity / conditions for **full development of the individual**}}.
- **Negative** liberty → freedom *from* coercion. **Positive** liberty → enabling conditions for development.
- Law ↔ liberty: absence of law does **not** produce liberty; ordered liberty needs lawful restraint.
- Best institutional safeguard of liberty in a liberal democracy → {{**separation of powers**}} (itself part of [basic structure](@amendments-basic-structure#basic-structure-doctrine)).
- Liberty is qualified, never absolute.

> [!TRAP] Liberty ≠ absence of all restraint ≠ licence ≠ unrestricted choice.

### Equality
- Core → {{**absence of special privileges**}}; equality of status / opportunity; like cases alike.
- Equality ≠ mechanical identical treatment of unequal situations. Articles 14–18 → [Fundamental Rights](@fundamental-rights#right-to-equality-articles-14-18).

### Rights ↔ Duties
- ==Rights are correlative with duties== → every right creates corresponding obligations.
- Rights ≠ purely personal claims independent of society.

## Rule of Law & Due Process

### Rule of Law
PYQ-tested features:
1. Limitation of powers
2. Equality before law
3. Liberty and civil rights

- !!NOT!! a feature → "people's responsibility to the Government".
- Rule of law ≠ unrestricted parliamentary sovereignty.

**Dicey** → absence of arbitrary power · equality before ordinary law · primacy of individual rights (British formulation).

> [!EXC] India: individual rights flow **from the Constitution** → Dicey's third limb does not apply literally.

> [!UPPCS] Rule of Law ↔ **effective access to justice** for weaker sections; **PIL** tested as one mechanism.

### Due process vs procedure established by law
- `Art. 21` text → {{"**procedure established by law**"}} — no express American-style due-process clause.
- Due Process of Law (UPSC best-fit) → {{principle of **natural justice**}}.
- Natural justice → unbiased decision-maker + fair hearing + fair procedure.

> [!FLOW] **A.K. Gopalan (1950)** — narrow: any legally prescribed procedure suffices *(historical)* → **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** — procedure must be {{**just, fair and reasonable**}}, not arbitrary *(current law)*

> [!TRAP] "Procedure established by law" (text) ≠ "due process of law" — yet post-*Maneka* the procedure must still be fair, just, reasonable.

## Democracy & Political Ideology

- **Popular sovereignty** → ultimate authority rests with the people.
- India → representative / indirect democracy.
- Democratic virtue (UPSC) → activates the intelligence and character of ordinary citizens.

| Direct-democracy device | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Referendum | Electorate decides a proposal / question |
| Initiative | Citizens initiate a legislative proposal |
| Recall | Voters remove a representative before term ends |
| Plebiscite | Popular vote on a major political / territorial question |
