## Supreme Court — Judges

- Number of judges → fixed by {{**Parliament by law**}}.
- Appointed by President. Qualification → citizen + {{HC judge **5 years**}} / {{HC advocate **10 years**}} / {{distinguished jurist}}.
- No minimum age. Retirement → {{SC **65**; HC **62**}}. Resigns to → {{President}}.
- Senior-most judge as CJI → convention, not Art. 124 text.
- Acting CJI → appointed by {{President}}.
- Ad hoc judge → CJI requests an HC judge; consults that HC's Chief Justice + {{prior consent of President}}.
- Retired judge sitting (`Art. 128`) → CJI's request + {{**President's prior consent** + the judge's consent}}.
- Retired SC judge → cannot plead / act in any court in India.

> [!DYN] Sanctioned strength 34 = CJI + 33 (since 2019).

## Removal & Independence

| Constitution — `Art. 124(4)` | Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968 |
|---|---|
| Grounds → {{**proved misbehaviour or incapacity**}} | Notice by {{**100** LS / **50** RS members}} |
| Each House, {{same session}} | Speaker / Chairman {{may admit **or refuse**}} |
| {{Majority of total membership + ≥ 2/3 present and voting}} | 3-member committee → {{SC judge + HC Chief Justice + distinguished jurist}} |
| President issues the order | |

- Constitution says "removal", not "impeachment"; misbehaviour / incapacity not defined.
- Same procedure for HC judges.

**Independence** → security of tenure · salaries {{charged on CFI}}, not reducible except in {{Financial Emergency}} · conduct not discussed in legislatures except on removal · SC controls its own staff · jurisdiction cannot be curtailed · post-retirement practice bar.
- Independence of judiciary = basic structure.

## Jurisdiction & Powers

- **Art. 131 — original** → Centre v State(s); Centre + State v State(s); State v State; dispute about a {{**legal right**}}.
- !!Not!! under Art. 131 → citizen v government; {{inter-State river-water disputes (Art. 262)}}; pre-Constitution treaties; Finance Commission matters.
- **Art. 136** → discretionary, not an appeal of right.
- **Art. 143** → opinion on question of law / fact of public importance; {{not binding on President}}.
- "Guardian of the Constitution" → from interpretation, FR enforcement, judicial review.

> [!TRAP] Art. 141 (precedent) ≠ Art. 142 (complete justice). · Art. 137 review ≠ judicial review. · SC has no general superintendence over all courts — Art. 227 is an HC power.

### Judicial review
- Testing constitutionality of legislative + executive action; anchors → {{Arts. 13, 32, 226}}; part of basic structure.
- Reviews legality, not policy wisdom. Amendments reviewable for basic structure.
- High Courts {{**can**}} strike down Central laws.

> [!TRAP] `Art. 131A` (SC-exclusive power over Central laws — 42nd Amdt) was {{omitted by the 43rd Amdt}}. No such exclusivity today.

### Contempt
- Power → Constitution (Arts. 129, 215). Definitions → {{**Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**}} (after {{H.N. Sanyal Committee}}).
- **Civil** → {{wilful disobedience of order / breach of undertaking}}.
- **Criminal** → {{scandalises court, prejudices proceedings, obstructs justice}}.
- Defences → innocent publication; fair & accurate reporting; fair criticism.

> [!TRAP] The Constitution does !!not!! define civil / criminal contempt.

**Free legal aid — s.12 (any one)** → SC / ST · victim of trafficking / begar · {{**woman or child**}} · person with disability · victim of mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake, industrial disaster · industrial workman · person in custody · income below ceiling.

> [!TRAP] Senior citizens / OBCs / transgender persons are !!not!! blanket s.12 categories. · The income test applies only to the income category.

> [!DYN] Income ceilings vary — SC matters ₹5 lakh; States fix their own (older PYQ figure ₹1 lakh).

> [!VERIFY] Uttar Pradesh legal-aid income ceiling — UPSLSA shows ₹3 lakh, NALSA's table shows ₹1 lakh. Source inconsistency unresolved; no figure adopted.

### Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008
- In force {{2 Oct 2009}}; Law Commission 114th Report.
- State Government {{**may** establish, in consultation with the HC}} → not mandatory.
- *Nyayadhikari* = Judicial Magistrate First Class level; seat at intermediate panchayat HQ; {{mobile court}}.
- Jurisdiction → {{**civil + criminal**}}; summary trial; conciliation first.
- Not bound by Evidence Act → natural justice.
- Not applicable → Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, Sikkim, specified tribal areas.
- Appeals → criminal to {{Sessions Court}}; civil to {{District Court}}.

## Tribunals

- {{Part XIV-A — **42nd Amdt, 1976**}}.

| | `Art. 323A` | `Art. 323B` |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | {{Administrative — public service matters}} | {{Tax, forex, labour, land reform, urban ceiling, elections, foodstuffs, rent}} |
| Made by | {{**Parliament only**}} | {{Parliament **or** State Legislature}} |
| Hierarchy | No | May be created |

- Administrative Tribunals Act, 1985 → CAT / SAT / JAT; guided by natural justice, not CPC.
- {{**L. Chandra Kumar (1997)**}} → HC (226 / 227) and SC (32) review cannot be excluded; tribunal orders go to an HC Division Bench.
- Many tribunals are purely statutory → {{NGT — National Green Tribunal Act, **2010**}}; natural justice, not CPC.

- PIL → {{judge-made}}; relaxed *locus standi*; not in Constitution / statute.
