## Ninth Schedule

- `Art. 31B` + Ninth Schedule → {{**1st Amendment, 1951**}}; background → land reform / zamindari abolition laws.
- A law enters the Schedule only {{through a **constitutional amendment**}}.
- Cut-off → {{**24 April 1973**}} (Kesavananda date).
  - **Waman Rao** → post-cut-off inclusions subject to basic structure.
  - **I.R. Coelho (2007)** → post-cut-off insertions reviewable if they damage basic structure.

> [!TRAP] Ninth Schedule protection is !!not!! blanket immunity from judicial review.

## Tenth Schedule — Anti-Defection

- Added by {{**52nd Amendment, 1985**}}; strengthened by {{**91st Amendment, 2003**}}.

| Member | Disqualified when |
|---|---|
| Party member | Voluntarily gives up membership (formal resignation **not** needed) · or votes / abstains against party direction without prior permission **and** not condoned within {{15 days}} |
| Independent | {{Joins any party after election}} |
| Nominated | Joins a party {{**after 6 months** of taking seat}} (free to join within 6 months) |

- **Merger exception** → {{≥ **2/3** of the legislature party agree}}.
- Old {{**1/3 split** exemption — deleted by 91st Amdt}}.
- Presiding-officer exception → may leave party on election as Speaker / Chairman and rejoin after demitting office.
- Decided by → {{**Speaker / Chairman**}}; subject to judicial review (*Kihoto Hollohan*).
- No deadline in the Schedule; *Keisham Meghachandra Singh (2020)* → ordinarily within {{3 months}}.
- 91st Amdt also → CoM ceiling 15%; defector cannot be made minister. → [Union Executive](@union-executive#prime-minister-and-council-of-ministers)

> [!TRAP] Decision ≠ President / Governor. · 1/3 was a **split**, never a merger. · "< 2/3 switching = defection" is wrong shorthand — individual grounds apply on their own; 2/3 is only the merger exception. · The Schedule !!does!! use the words "political party".
