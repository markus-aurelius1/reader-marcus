## Government of India Act, 1919 {#act-1919}

- Basis → Montagu Declaration {{1917}} + Montagu–Chelmsford Report.
- {{**Dyarchy in provinces**}} only.

| Reserved | Transferred |
|---|---|
| Police, justice, land revenue, irrigation | Local government, education, health, agriculture |
| Governor + councillors | Ministers responsible to the legislature |

- Centre → bicameral: {{Council of State + Legislative Assembly}}; direct election, narrow franchise.
- Separate electorates extended → Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans.
- Central and provincial subjects, budgets separated.
- High Commissioner for India in London.
- Review after ten years → Simon Commission.
- Women → provinces could extend the vote.

> [!TRAP] 1909 introduced separate electorates; 1919 extended them. · The Centre stayed non-responsible.

## Government of India Act, 1935 {#act-1935}

| Provided | Operated? |
|---|---|
| All-India Federation | {{Never}} — princes did not accede |
| Dyarchy at the Centre | {{Never}} |
| Provincial autonomy | {{From 1937}} |
| Federal Court | 1937 |

- Three lists → Federal, Provincial, Concurrent; residuary → {{Governor-General}}.
- Bicameral legislatures in six provinces → Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, UP.
- Seats reserved for women, labour, depressed classes.
- {{Instrument of Instructions}} → antecedent of the Directive Principles.
