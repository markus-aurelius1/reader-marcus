## Money & Legal Tender {#money-and-legal-tender}

- Primary functions → {{medium of exchange + measure of value}}. Secondary → store of value, standard of deferred payment, transfer of value.
- **Fiat money** → value rests on sovereign authority, not commodity content.
- **Legal tender** → {{a creditor cannot refuse it in settlement of a debt}}, within legal limits.
- Cheque / DD / card / UPI instruction → payment instrument. !!Not!! legal tender.

| Issue | Authority |
|---|---|
| Banknotes (other than ₹1) | {{RBI — RBI Act}} |
| Coins | {{Government of India — `Coinage Act, 2011`}} |
| **₹1 note** | {{Government of India; signed by the Finance Secretary}}; treated as a rupee coin |

> [!TRAP] "Coinage Act 1911" is wrong. · RBI does not print notes "against the guarantee of government bonds" one-for-one — note issue is a balance-sheet system.

Gresham's Law → [Thinkers](@economic-thinkers#greshams-law)

## RBI — Institution {#rbi-institution}

- RBI Act {{**1934**}} → began {{**1935**}} → nationalised {{**1949**}}.
- Roles → monetary authority · currency issuer · banker and debt manager to government · {{bankers' bank}} · lender of last resort · regulator of banks and specified financial entities · forex manager (FEMA) · G-Sec / money-market regulator · payment-system overseer.
- Governor + up to {{4}} Deputy Governors → appointed by {{the **Central Government** under the RBI Act}}.
- {{**Section 7**}} → Central Government may give RBI directions in the public interest, {{after consulting the Governor}}.
- General superintendence → **Central Board** (subject to the Act and such directions).
- **Bankers' bank** → holds banks' reserves, lends for liquidity, advises on monetary matters.
- **Lender of last resort** → {{temporary liquidity to solvent banks under acute stress}}; prevents contagion.

> [!TRAP] RBI's powers and Section 7 directions flow from the **RBI Act**, not the Constitution. · "PM in consultation with FM appoints the Governor" → not the statutory formula. · Lender of last resort ≠ cheap finance to industry ≠ bailout of an insolvent bank.

## RBI Balance Sheet & Income {#rbi-balance-sheet}

| Side | Items |
|---|---|
| Assets | Foreign-currency assets, gold, government securities, loans / advances |
| Liabilities | {{Notes in circulation}}, deposits of banks and government, reserves / provisions |

- Income → {{interest and trading income on government securities + foreign-currency operations}}.
- Printing notes → a **cost**, not a fee-earning source.
- RBI does not lend commercially to private companies; does not manage pension funds for income.
- **Seigniorage** → gain from issuing non-interest-bearing money against earning assets. Narrower than RBI's surplus.
- Surplus transfer = income − expenditure − provisions under the Economic Capital Framework.
- **Bimal Jalan Committee** (Economic Capital Framework) → constituted {{2018}}, reported {{2019}}.

> [!TRAP] RBI net profit ≠ seigniorage.

## Demand for Money {#demand-for-money}

| Motive | Depends on |
|---|---|
| Transactions | Nominal income / value of transactions |
| Precautionary | Contingency buffer; income |
| Speculative | Expected interest rate / bond prices |

- {{Bond price ↑ ↔ yield ↓}}.
- **Liquidity trap** → at very low rates extra money is held as cash → monetary easing loses force.

## Monetary Aggregates {#monetary-aggregates}

| Aggregate | Components |
|---|---|
| **M0 — Reserve Money** | {{Currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI}} |
| **M1 — narrow** | {{Currency with public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI}} |
| **M2** | M1 + savings deposits of post-office savings banks |
| **M3 — broad** | {{M1 + time deposits with banks}} |
| **M4** | M3 + all post-office savings-bank deposits ({{excluding NSC}}) |

- Liquidity order → {{currency > demand deposits > savings deposits > time deposits}}.
- Currency in circulation = currency with public **+ cash held by banks**.

### Transaction traps

| Transaction | M1 | M3 |
|---|---|---|
| Withdraw cash from demand deposit | {{Unchanged}} | Unchanged |
| Deposit cash in demand account | {{Unchanged}} | Unchanged |
| Demand / savings deposit → fixed deposit | {{**↓**}} | Unchanged |
| Break fixed deposit → demand deposit | {{**↑**}} | Unchanged |
| RBI buys G-Secs | ↑ | ↑ |
| RBI sells G-Secs | ↓ | ↓ |

- Money supply ↑ → RBI purchase of G-Secs · government borrowing from RBI (monetised deficit).

## Money Multiplier {#money-multiplier}

- Banks create deposits on top of the monetary base through repeated lending.
- Simple model → multiplier ≈ 1 ÷ reserve ratio (restrictive assumptions only).

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Banking habit ↑ / cash leakage ↓ | {{↑}} |
| Currency–deposit ratio ↑ | {{↓}} |
| CRR / reserve requirement ↑ | {{↓}} |
| Banks hold excess reserves | ↓ |

> [!TRAP] Reserve–deposit ratio ≠ CRR + SLR mechanically. · Statutory reserve requirements **limit** credit creation.

## Monetary Policy Framework {#monetary-policy-framework}

- Objective (RBI Act) → {{price stability, keeping in mind growth}}.
- Flexible inflation targeting → {{Central Government, in consultation with RBI, sets the CPI target once every five years}}.
- Target architecture → {{4% CPI, ± 2 percentage points}}.
- **MPC = {{6}}** → Governor (Chair) + Deputy Governor (monetary policy) + one RBI officer nominated by the Central Board + {{3 Central Government appointees}}.
- Meets ≥ {{4}} times a year; decides the **policy (repo) rate** → binding on RBI.

> [!TRAP] MPC does !!not!! vote on CRR / SLR or regulatory settings.

> [!DYN] Current repo / SDF / MSF / CRR / SLR rates and the date of the latest target renewal.

## Policy Tools — Direction Map {#policy-tools}

| Action | Banking system | Macro direction |
|---|---|---|
| Repo ↑ | Funding cost / market rates ↑ | Credit, demand ↓ → disinflation |
| Repo ↓ | Rates ↓ | Credit, investment ↑ |
| CRR ↑ | More balances locked with RBI | Lendable resources, multiplier ↓ |
| SLR ↓ | Resources freed | Credit capacity ↑; lending rates may fall |
| RBI **buys** G-Secs (OMO) | Reserves ↑ | {{Liquidity ↑, yields ↓}} |
| RBI **sells** G-Secs | Reserves ↓ | {{Liquidity ↓, yields ↑}} |
| Bank Rate ↑ | Signal | Tight money |

### CRR vs SLR

| | CRR | SLR |
|---|---|---|
| Held as | {{Cash balance **with RBI**}} | {{Cash, gold, approved securities — **held by the bank**}} |
| Base | % of NDTL | % of NDTL |
| Return | None | G-Secs earn interest |

### Corridor

- **LAF** → RBI's liquidity operations: repo / reverse-repo + standing facilities.
- **Repo** → collateralised lending to banks; the policy rate.
- **SDF** → {{uncollateralised absorption}}; enabled by {{2018}} amendment (s.17), operational {{April 2022}}; ==floor of the corridor==.
- **MSF** → overnight funds against SLR securities within a limit; penal; ==ceiling==.
- {{**SDF floor — Repo centre — MSF ceiling**}}.
- **Bank Rate** → aligned with MSF.

> [!TRAP] Reverse repo is no longer the standing floor. · CRR is not "cash kept by the bank itself". · Expansionary stance = lower policy / reserve rates, not a higher MSF. · Bank Rate and OMO are monetary tools; public debt and public revenue are fiscal.

## OMO, Sterilisation & Transmission {#omo-sterilisation-transmission}

- **OMO** → {{RBI's purchase / sale of government securities}}.
- **Operation Twist** → simultaneous buy and sell across maturities → shapes the yield curve.
- **Sterilisation** → offsetting the liquidity effect of forex intervention → RBI buys dollars (rupees injected) → sells G-Secs (rupees absorbed). A purpose, not one instrument.
- **MSS** → government securities issued for sterilisation; proceeds sequestered.

> [!FLOW] Repo → overnight rates → money-market rates → deposit / funding cost → lending rates + bond yields → consumption, investment → output + inflation

- Transmission weakened by → high NPAs, rigid deposit rates, weak credit demand.
- **MCLR** → internal benchmark → {{transparency and better transmission}}.
- Lower interest rate → investment expenditure ↑.
- G-Sec yields respond to → RBI stance, inflation, short rates, {{global rates / US Fed}}.

Forex intervention in practice → [External Sector](@external-sector#rbi-intervention)

## Digital Rupee (CBDC) {#cbdc}

- **e₹** → digital sovereign currency issued by RBI → {{direct liability of RBI}}; legal tender.
- Convertible with cash and bank money; programmability / offline use are design features.
- Cross-border CBDC use need not route through USD / SWIFT in principle.

> [!TRAP] e₹ is !!not!! inflation-indexed — ₹100 e₹ = ₹100 cash. · e₹ ≠ e-RUPI (prepaid voucher). · UPI moves bank deposits; e₹ is the money itself → [comparison](@digital-payments#upi-cbdc-erupi).
