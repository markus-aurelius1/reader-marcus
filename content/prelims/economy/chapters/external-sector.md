## BoP Accounts {#bop-accounts}

- **BoP** → systematic record of transactions between {{residents and non-residents}} in a period.

| Account (BPM6) | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Current** | Goods · services · {{primary income}} (interest, dividends, wages) · {{secondary income}} (remittances, transfers) |
| **Capital** (narrow) | Capital transfers; non-produced non-financial assets |
| **Financial** | {{FDI, portfolio, derivatives, loans / deposits, reserve assets}} |

- {{**Balance of Trade** = goods exports − goods imports}}; services excluded.
- Current account = trade balance + **invisibles** (services, income, transfers).
- Foreign tourists spending in India → {{export of services}}.
- BoP balances after financial flows, reserve change, errors and omissions.
- **CAD** → financed by net capital inflows or reserve drawdown.

> [!TRAP] Older questions call FDI + portfolio + loans the "capital account" — in BPM6 that is the **financial** account. · Remittances = current account, not capital inflow. · Foreign assets / SDRs are not current-account items. · FDI / FPI inflows **finance** a CAD; they are not current-account credits.

## FDI vs FPI {#fdi-vs-fpi}

| | FDI | FPI |
|---|---|---|
| Test | {{Unlisted company, or ≥ 10% of a listed company}} | {{< 10% of a listed company}} |
| Aim | Control, lasting interest | Financial return |
| Stability | Sticky | Volatile |
| Nature | {{Non-debt-creating}} | Equity **or** debt |
| Brings | Management, technology | Capital only |

- Once FDI, a fall below 10% does not reclassify it.
- FCCBs, GDRs, conditional institutional investment → can count as FDI. {{NRE deposits}} → debt-creating, not FDI.
- ADR / GDR → depository receipts on underlying shares.

P-notes, FPI instruments → [Financial Markets](@financial-markets#derivatives-and-p-notes)

> [!TRAP] "All FPI is debt" → wrong. · "All depository receipts are FPI" → wrong.

## External Debt & ECB {#external-debt}

- **External debt** → liabilities of residents (government + private) to non-residents. {{Neither mostly sovereign nor wholly in dollars}}.
- **ECB** → commercial borrowing from non-resident lenders under FEMA / RBI rules.
- Foreign-currency ECB + rupee depreciation → {{repayment burden ↑}} if unhedged.
- **Masala bond** → rupee bond issued abroad → {{currency risk on the investor}}.
- Trade credit → buyer's / supplier's credit for imports.

Bond types → [Special bonds](@financial-markets#special-bonds)

## Forex Reserves {#forex-reserves}

- Components → {{**FCA + Gold + SDRs + Reserve Tranche Position**}}.
- {{**Import cover**}} → months of imports the reserves can pay for.
- Adequacy → import cover, short-term debt ÷ reserves, debt-service ratio, maturity mix.

SDR, reserve tranche → [IMF](@international-institutions#imf)

> [!TRAP] Reserves have four components, not three.

## Exchange Rate {#exchange-rate}

| Regime | Rate set by |
|---|---|
| Floating | {{Demand and supply of forex}} |
| Fixed / pegged | Authority; defended with reserves |
| Managed float | Market + central-bank smoothing |

- ₹/$ ↑ → rupee {{depreciates}}. ₹/$ ↓ → rupee {{appreciates}}.
- Market move → depreciation / appreciation. Official change of a peg → {{devaluation / revaluation}}.
- Dollar demand ↑ → imports, outward remittance, debt service, capital outflow → rupee weakens.
- Dollar supply ↑ → exports, remittances, tourism, FDI / FPI inflows → rupee strengthens.
- Currency's value → fundamentals, political stability, economic potential. Not fixed by the World Bank.

### NEER / REER
- **NEER** → trade-weighted average of nominal rates.
- **REER** → {{NEER adjusted for relative inflation}}.
- ↑ NEER = rupee appreciation. {{↑ REER = real appreciation → exports **less** competitive}}.
- Higher domestic inflation → REER rises above NEER.

> [!TRAP] "Higher REER = better competitiveness" → false. · In a flexible system the central bank does not set demand and supply.

## RBI Intervention {#rbi-intervention}

| Situation | RBI |
|---|---|
| Rupee falling fast | {{Sells dollars}} → absorbs rupees |
| Strong inflows / appreciation | {{Buys dollars}} → injects rupees |

- **Sterilisation** → offset the rupee-liquidity effect → {{OMO}} → [Monetary policy](@money-rbi-monetary-policy#omo-sterilisation-transmission).
- Against a sliding rupee → curb non-essential imports, promote exports, rupee-denominated borrowing abroad, easier ECB.

> [!TRAP] Expansionary monetary policy does !!not!! defend a falling rupee.

## Convertibility {#convertibility}

- **Convertibility** → freedom to convert rupees into foreign currency and back.
- India → {{current account: convertible}}; {{capital account: partial}}.

> [!TRAP] Full capital-account convertibility is not a shield against a currency crisis — it can amplify capital flight.

## Devaluation & Crisis Buffers {#devaluation-and-buffers}

- Devaluation / depreciation → exports cheaper abroad; imports costlier → imported inflation; foreign-currency debt heavier.
- Trade balance → {{need not improve}}: lags + inelastic demand → **J-curve**; needs elastic demand (Marshall–Lerner).

> [!FLOW] US Fed tightens → portfolio outflow → rupee depreciates → unhedged foreign-currency ECB costlier to service

| Lowers crisis risk | Does not |
|---|---|
| Adequate reserves | Higher government expenditure |
| {{IT / service export earnings, remittances}} | Full capital-account convertibility |
| {{Less short-term external debt}} | Reliance on volatile portfolio flows |

## India's Trade {#india-trade}

- Services exports + remittances → offset the merchandise deficit.
- Structural import dependence → energy, gold, electronics, edible oil.
- Remittances → current account (secondary income).
- Tax treaties shape FDI routing (Singapore / Mauritius).
- Hilton–Young Commission → fixed rupee–sterling rate, to aid remittances and India's creditworthiness (colonial).

> [!DYN] Largest trade partner, FDI source, remittance rank, top export / import commodity, India's share of world exports, bilateral surplus rankings, tariff rates.
