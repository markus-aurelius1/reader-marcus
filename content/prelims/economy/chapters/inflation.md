## Core Terms {#core-terms}

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Inflation** | Sustained rise in the **general** price level (% change in a price index) |
| **Disinflation** | {{Inflation rate falls — prices still rising}} (8% → 5%) |
| **Deflation** | {{Sustained fall in the general price level}} (negative inflation) |
| **Reflation** | Deliberate policy to lift demand / prices from depressed conditions |
| **Stagflation** | {{High inflation + stagnant growth + high unemployment}} |
| Hyperinflation | Extremely rapid, self-reinforcing rise |
| **Shrinkflation** | {{Smaller quantity at the same price}} → unit price ↑ |

> [!TRAP] Price **level** ≠ inflation **rate**. · One item getting costlier ≠ inflation. · Disinflation ≠ deflation. · No universal numeric threshold for hyperinflation.

## Causes {#causes}

| Type | Mechanism | Triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand-pull** | Aggregate demand > productive capacity | Expansionary fiscal / monetary policy, credit boom, rising purchasing power |
| **Cost-push** | Unit cost ↑ → prices ↑ / supply ↓ | Wages without productivity, fuel, raw material, input taxes |
| **Imported** | Imports costlier in rupees | Global commodity shock, {{currency depreciation}} |
| **Structural / bottleneck** | Supply rigidities | Poor logistics, storage / distribution gaps, capacity limits |
| **Monetary** | Money / credit grows faster than real output | {{Monetised deficit — new money}} |
| Expectations | Expected inflation feeds wage and price setting | Wage–price spiral |

- Price level rises with → money supply ↑ · aggregate output ↓ · effective demand ↑.
- Food inflation → demand / income shifts **+** supply-chain constraints. Not one cause.
- ==**Most inflationary way to finance a deficit → creation of new money**== (adds purchasing power without withdrawing any from savers) → [Deficit financing](@budget-fiscal-policy#fiscal-policy).

> [!TRAP] Higher interest rates are a **response** to demand-pull inflation, not its cause. · Monetary tightening cannot produce food or fuel — it restrains second-round demand and expectations.

## Base Effect {#base-effect}

- Year-on-year inflation compares the index with the **same period last year**.
- High base last year → current rate looks {{low}}. Low base → looks {{high}}.
- Changes the **measured rate**, not today's price level.

## CPI vs WPI vs GDP Deflator {#cpi-wpi-deflator}

| | CPI (Rural / Urban / Combined) | WPI | GDP deflator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tracks | Retail prices of household consumption — goods **and services** | Wholesale / bulk prices of **goods** | Prices of all domestically produced final output |
| Publisher | {{**NSO, MoSPI**}} | {{**Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT** — Commerce & Industry}} | NSO (national accounts) |
| Services | {{Yes}} | {{No}} | Yes |
| Imports | In the basket | Affect wholesale prices | {{Excluded — not domestic output}} |
| Weights | Fixed for a series | Fixed for a series | Vary with production |
| Use | Cost of living; ==inflation-targeting reference== | Wholesale goods-price trend | Broadest domestic price measure |

- {{**CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL → Labour Bureau**}}, Ministry of Labour & Employment.
- **Headline** = full CPI. **Core** = headline minus volatile food and fuel → analytical, not a separate statutory basket.
- Headline inflation for India's inflation target → {{**CPI-Combined**}}.
- Food weight → higher in CPI than in WPI.

> [!TRAP] RBI's target is !!not!! WPI-based. · WPI ≠ Producer Price Index ("what producers receive" is too broad). · CPI does !!not!! include direct taxes — indirect taxes / subsidies show up through prices.

> [!DYN] Base years: CPI (R / U / C) **2024 = 100** · WPI **2022-23** · GDP **2022-23**. Earlier CPI series was 2012 = 100. Basket weights change with each series — not kept here.

## Effects — Winners & Losers {#effects}

| Who | Unexpected inflation |
|---|---|
| Fixed-rate **debtor / borrower** | {{Gains — repays in cheaper money}} |
| Fixed-rate **creditor / lender**, bondholder | {{Loses}} |
| Fixed nominal income, fixed-deposit saver | Loses real value |
| Poor households | Hit harder — necessities are a larger share |
| Producers | Not automatic winners — depends on pricing power vs input cost |

- {{**Real interest rate ≈ nominal rate − inflation**}} (Fisher).
- Expected inflation ↑ → nominal rates tend to **rise**, not fall.
- Unexpected deflation → reverses the debtor / creditor result.
- Inflation-indexed instruments protect real value → [IIB](@financial-markets#special-bonds).

> [!TRAP] "Inflation ↑ → nominal interest rate ↓" is wrong as a rule. · The debtor gains only on **fixed-rate nominal** contracts and **unexpected** inflation.

## Control {#control}

| Channel | Who | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Monetary | RBI | Restrain demand, credit, liquidity, expectations → [tools](@money-rbi-monetary-policy#policy-tools) |
| Fiscal — demand | Government | Lower excess demand; avoid monetised financing |
| Fiscal — supply | Government | Release / import essentials, {{cut duties and taxes on fuel or imports}}, fix logistics and storage |

- Statute → primary objective of monetary policy = {{price stability, keeping growth in mind}}.
- Target → notified by {{the Central Government in consultation with RBI}}, on CPI → [MPC](@money-rbi-monetary-policy#monetary-policy-framework).
- Reducing money in circulation → helps contain demand-side inflation.

> [!TRAP] Inflation control ≠ RBI alone. Supply-side and fiscal steps belong to the government.
