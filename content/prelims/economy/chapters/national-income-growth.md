## Economic Systems & Sectors {#systems-and-sectors}

| Concept | Core | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Market economy | Households / firms / prices decide; private ownership dominant | "No government at all" → too absolute |
| Command economy | State / planning authority allocates and owns | ≠ every welfare state |
| Mixed economy | {{Public + private sectors; market + state intervention}} → India | India neither purely socialist nor capitalist |
| Economic activity | Produces goods / services from scarce resources, with measurable economic value | {{Voluntary social service}} → socially valuable yet **non-economic** |

| Sector | What | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Extraction / production from natural resources | Farming, {{dairy, mineral exploration}}, mining |
| **Secondary** | Transformation / manufacturing + construction | {{Weaving}}, factories |
| **Tertiary** | Services | {{Storage}}, transport, trade, finance, administration |

- **Real sector** → production / flow of goods and services. **Financial sector** → {{bank lending, bond issuance}}.
- Public sector's roles → public utilities · economic and social overheads · balanced regional development · distributive goals.

> [!TRAP] Storage / transport of farm output = **tertiary**, not primary. · A service linked to agriculture stays a service.

## Demand, Supply & Goods {#demand-supply-goods}

- **Demand** = willingness !!and!! ability to buy; own price ↑ → quantity demanded ↓ (ceteris paribus).
- **Supply** = willingness and ability to sell; own price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑.
- ==Movement along the curve → own price. Shift of the curve → income, tastes, population, input cost, technology, related-good prices, policy.==

| Related-good / income change | Effect on demand for A |
|---|---|
| Price of **substitute** B ↑ | {{Demand for A ↑}} |
| Price of **complement** B ↑ | {{Demand for A ↓}} |
| Income ↑ — **normal** good | {{↑}} |
| Income ↑ — **inferior** good | {{↓}} |

- **Giffen good** → special inferior good; {{income effect outweighs substitution effect}} → demand rises with price (restrictive conditions).
- **Veblen effect** → status / conspicuous demand rises with price. Different mechanism from Giffen.
- **Price elasticity of demand** = %Δ quantity demanded ÷ %Δ price.
  - More inelastic → essentials, few substitutes, short horizon.
  - More elastic → luxuries, many substitutes, long horizon.
- **Public good** → {{non-rival + non-excludable}} → free-rider problem.

> [!TRAP] "Provided by government" does !!not!! define a public good. · Giffen ≠ Veblen.

## Capital — Types {#capital-types}

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Produced assets used in production | — |
| **Fixed** | Repeated use; not used up in one cycle | {{Machine, computer, farmer's plough}} |
| **Working / circulating** | Used up or transformed in production | {{Yarn, petrol, raw material}} |
| **Human** | Education, health, skills, experience embodied in people → productivity, future earnings | Intangible wealth, not machines or funds |
| **Social** | {{Trust, norms, networks, cooperation}} easing collective action | ≠ physical / human / financial |
| Financial | Claims / funds that finance activity | Not itself a produced asset |
| **Intangible / intellectual** | {{Brand recognition, IP, client lists / databases}}, know-how | Inventory is !!not!! intangible |

- **Inventory** = stock of raw material + work-in-progress + unsold output at a point of time. **Change** in inventories over a period → investment.
- Investment in human capital (education, health) → higher productivity → ==future returns==.
- Human resources ↔ development → **two-way**: skills raise output; development raises capability.
- **Circular economy** → design out waste, keep materials in use, recycle / regenerate → less virgin input + less waste → {{can lower GHG emissions}}.

## Saving, Investment & ICOR {#saving-investment-icor}

- ==**Saving** = flow== (income not consumed in a period). **Wealth / capital stock** = stock (at a point of time).
- **Investment / capital formation** = addition to produced assets, inventories, valuables in a period → **flow**.
- {{**GCF = GFCF + change in stocks + valuables**}} (± statistical discrepancy).
- **GFCF** = acquisition less disposal of **produced** fixed assets + specified improvements.
- **CFC** (consumption of fixed capital) = depreciation. {{Net fixed capital formation = GFCF − CFC}}.
- {{**ICOR = ΔK ÷ ΔY**}} → lower ICOR = more output per unit of extra capital = higher capital productivity.
  - Required investment rate ≈ target growth × ICOR.
- Saving → growth only if savings are mobilised and productively invested → financial institutions matter.
- Saving and investment → distinct decisions *ex ante*; equal *ex post* in the accounts.

> [!TRAP] Saving is !!not!! a stock. · GCF ≠ GFCF. · **Purchase of land ≠ GFCF** — land is a non-produced asset; construction and major improvements are. · High saving + high ICOR → weak output response.

> [!FLOW] Saving → mobilised by financial institutions → investment / capital formation → output growth (scaled by capital productivity = 1 ÷ ICOR)

## National Income — Accounting {#national-income-accounting}

- **GDP** = market value of **final** goods and services produced within the **domestic economic territory** in a period.

| Method | Adds up |
|---|---|
| Production / value added | Value added by all producers |
| Income | Compensation of employees + operating surplus / mixed income + relevant taxes less subsidies + CFC |
| Expenditure | {{**PFCE + GCF + GFCE + (X − M)**}} |

- Count **final output** or **value added** — never both (double counting).
- Production counts when produced → unsold output = inventory addition.
- Resale of an existing asset → not GDP; {{brokerage / service on the sale}} → GDP.
- **Transfer payments** (pensions, cash transfers) → redistribution, not production → {{excluded from GDP}}.

### Current Indian framework

- {{**GVA at basic prices** = Output at basic prices − intermediate consumption}}.
- {{**GDP = Σ GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies**}}.
- {{**GNI = GDP + net primary income from the rest of the world**}}.
- **NDP = GDP − CFC** · **NNI = GNI − CFC** → ==Gross − CFC = Net==.

> [!TRAP] `GDP = ΣGVA` is incomplete — add net product taxes. · Headline GVA is at **basic prices**, not factor cost; the two terms are not interchangeable.

> [!DYN] GDP constant-price base year = **2022-23** (MoSPI new series, released 27 Feb 2026; replaced 2011-12).

### Legacy terms (older questions)

- "National Income" = {{**NNP at factor cost**}}.
- GNP ≈ GDP + net factor income from abroad → modern name **GNI**.
- **Personal disposable income** = personal income − personal taxes / compulsory payments. ≠ national income.

## Domestic vs National · Nominal vs Real {#domestic-national-real-nominal}

| Pair | Test |
|---|---|
| **Domestic** (GDP) | Production by resident producer units **inside the economic territory** — ownership / nationality irrelevant |
| **National** (GNI) | Income accruing to **resident institutional units** |
| **Nominal** | Current quantities × current prices |
| **Real** | Valued at base-year prices → volume growth |

- Residence = centre of economic interest. {{Not citizenship / passport}}.
- Foreign-owned firm resident in India → India's **GDP**.
- {{**GDP deflator = Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP × 100**}} → covers all domestically produced final output; weights change with production.
- Imported final goods → in CPI / WPI baskets, !!not!! in the GDP deflator.
- Economic growth → rise in **real** output (preferably real per-capita).

> [!TRAP] Nominal GDP need not exceed real GDP every year — depends on prices relative to the base year. · A current-price series can rise while real growth is not steadily rising. · GDP growth and per-capita income need not rise "steadily" year after year.

Price indices in detail → [CPI vs WPI vs deflator](@inflation#cpi-wpi-deflator)

## Growth vs Development {#growth-vs-development}

| | Growth | Development |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Quantitative rise in real output / income | Structural + welfare change |
| Covers | GDP, per-capita income | Productivity, health, education, job quality, poverty, inequality, institutions, sustainability |

- ==Real GNP / per-capita income ↑ does **not** imply development== if poverty, unemployment or inequality worsen.
- Growth factors → capital accumulation, technology, labour / population, specialisation. {{"Technocrats / bureaucrats"}} → not a standard growth factor.
- Capital formation → a direct growth driver; needs saving + institutions to mobilise it.
- **Sustainable development** → {{meet present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs}}. Conservation ≠ zero present consumption.
- Growth is commonly accompanied by **some inflation** — not deflation / stagflation by definition.

Measuring development → [HDI family](@indices-reports#human-development-family) · [Poverty concepts](@poverty-employment#poverty-concepts)

## Business Cycle {#business-cycle}

| Phase | Mark |
|---|---|
| Boom / expansion | Output, income, employment rising |
| **Recession** | Broad contraction of activity |
| Depression | Severe, prolonged contraction |
| Recovery | Back toward expansion |

- **Slowdown** = growth rate falls, output may still rise. ≠ recession (contraction).
- Recession remedy → support aggregate demand → {{public-project expenditure / expansionary fiscal policy}} → [Fiscal policy](@budget-fiscal-policy#fiscal-policy).
