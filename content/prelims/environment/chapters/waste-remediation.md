## Bioremediation {#bioremediation}

- Using microbes to break down or detoxify pollutants. Best for {{organic}} pollutants — oil, sewage, solvents.
- **In situ** → treated in place (add nutrients / oxygen / microbes). **Ex situ** → dug out and treated elsewhere.
- Microbes can be genetically engineered for a task.
- **Oilzapper** → bacteria that eat {{oil sludge and oil spills}} (TERI).
- **Bio-decomposer** → {{fungal}} mix that rots paddy stubble into manure.

> [!TRAP] Microbes cannot destroy heavy metals such as cadmium or lead — they can only change their form or trap them.

## Phytoremediation {#phytoremediation}

| Type | Plant does |
|---|---|
| **Phytoextraction** | Takes metal up into shoots (then harvested) |
| **Phytostabilisation** | Holds pollutant in the root zone |
| **Rhizofiltration** | Roots filter polluted water |
| **Phytodegradation** | Breaks down organics |

## Treatment Technologies {#treatment-technologies}

| Technology | How | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Biofilter** | Microbe film on a porous bed | In fish farms → turns {{ammonia into nitrate}} |
| **Membrane bioreactor** | Biological treatment + membrane filter | {{Wastewater}} treatment |
| **Microbial fuel cell** | Microbes release electrons | Cleans wastewater + makes electricity → [Microbial fuel cells](@bioenergy-bio-inputs#microbial-fuel-cells) |
| **Activated carbon** | Huge surface area → {{adsorption}} | Removes organics, odour, some metals; made from carbon-rich waste |

- Sewage treatment → **primary** (settle solids) → **secondary** (microbes cut BOD) → **tertiary** (nutrients, disinfection).
- **Anaerobic digestion** → wet waste → biogas + slurry.

> [!TRAP] Adsorption (sticks to a surface) ≠ biodegradation.

Purifying drinking water → [Water & purification](@prelims/science-tech/chemistry-daily-life#water-purification)

## Waste Hierarchy & Thermal Routes {#waste-to-energy}

- Order → {{refuse → reduce → reuse → recycle / compost → recover energy → dispose}}.
- Segregation at source → wet waste to compost / biogas; dry to recycling.
- Waste must stay within nature's **assimilative capacity**.

| Process | Oxygen | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Incineration** | Plenty | Heat + flue gas + ash — {{not emission-free}} |
| **Pyrolysis** | {{None}} | Char + oil + gas |
| **Gasification** | Limited | Syngas |
| **Plasma gasification** | Very high temperature | Syngas + glassy slag |
| **Biomethanation** | None (biological) | Biogas |

- Pyrolysis and plasma gasification → waste-to-energy routes.
- **PET** → recyclable.

Rules on waste → [Waste rules](@environmental-laws#waste-rules)

## By-Products as Resources {#byproduct-reuse}

| By-product | Used for | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly ash** | {{Bricks, cement, roads}}, embankments | Has trace heavy metals; not just silica |
| **Steel slag** | Road base, cement, soil conditioner | Check leaching |
| **Copper slag** | Road and construction aggregate | Can leach heavy metals |
| **Biochar** | Soil improvement, carbon storage | |

- Greener roads → copper slag, cold-mix asphalt, geotextiles.

> [!UPPCS] Biodegradable → wool, paper, food waste. Non-biodegradable → plastic, glass, metals, DDT.
