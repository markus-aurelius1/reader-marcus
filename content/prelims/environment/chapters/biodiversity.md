## Levels & Measures {#levels}

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Genetic** | Variation within a species |
| **Species** | {{Richness}} (how many) + {{evenness}} (how balanced) |
| **Ecosystem** | Variety of habitats and ecosystems |

| Measure | Scale |
|---|---|
| **Alpha** | Within one site |
| **Beta** | {{Change between sites}} |
| **Gamma** | Whole region |

> [!TRAP] "Gamma = alpha + beta" is !!not!! a universal formula.

## Patterns {#patterns}

- Species richness {{rises from poles to tropics}}.
- Falls with rising altitude.
- **Himalaya** is rich because several {{biogeographic zones meet}} there.
- Richest ecosystems → tropical rainforests and coral reefs.
- Biodiversity → basis of soil formation, pollination, food, medicine.

Climate behind the gradient → [Climatic regions](@prelims/geography/climatic-regions#identification-matrix)

## Hotspots {#hotspots}

- Idea → {{Norman Myers}}; used by **Conservation International**.
- Both conditions needed:
  1. at least {{1,500}} endemic vascular plants;
  2. at least {{70%}} of original vegetation lost.

| Hotspot touching India | Indian part |
|---|---|
| **Himalaya** | Whole Indian Himalaya |
| **Indo-Burma** | NE India, Andaman Islands |
| **Western Ghats – Sri Lanka** | Western Ghats |
| **Sundaland** | {{Nicobar Islands}} |

Where these ranges lie → [Himalaya](@prelims/geography/india-physiography#himalaya) · [Western Ghats](@prelims/geography/india-physiography#peninsular-plateau)

> [!TRAP] High species count alone is not enough — endemism **and** threat are required. · BirdLife International did !!not!! invent hotspots.

## Loss Drivers {#loss-drivers}

- {{Habitat loss and fragmentation}} → the biggest driver.
- Over-exploitation · invasive alien species · pollution · climate change · co-extinction.
- **Sixth mass extinction** → the current, human-caused one.

## Alien vs Invasive {#invasive-species}

- **Alien / exotic** → brought outside its natural range by people.
- **Invasive** → alien + spreads + causes harm.
- Indian invaders → {{Lantana, Parthenium, Prosopis juliflora, water hyacinth}}.
- Invasive traits → fast breeding, easy dispersal, wide tolerance.
- **Invasive Species Specialist Group** → under the {{IUCN Species Survival Commission}}.

> [!TRAP] Alien ≠ invasive. · Common myna is native to India — invasive only elsewhere.

## In-situ vs Ex-situ {#in-situ-ex-situ}

| | In-situ | Ex-situ |
|---|---|---|
| Where | In the natural habitat | Outside it, under care |
| Examples | {{National parks, sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, sacred groves}} | {{Zoos, botanical gardens, seed and gene banks, cryopreservation}} |
| Strength | Keeps interactions and evolution going | Last resort for critically low populations |
