## Water on Earth {#water-distribution}

- Oceans cover ~{{71%}} of the surface. About {{97%}} of all water is saline.
- Freshwater ≈ 2.5–3% of all water:

| Store | Share of freshwater |
|---|---|
| **Ice caps and glaciers** | ~{{69%}} → largest freshwater store |
| **Groundwater** | ~{{30%}} → largest store outside ice |
| Lakes, rivers, soil, air | < 1% |

India's groundwater → [Groundwater](@india-drainage#groundwater)

## Temperature, Salinity & Density {#temperature-salinity}

- Surface temperature falls from Equator to poles; modified by currents and winds.
- Vertical layers (low and mid latitudes) → warm mixed layer → **thermocline** → cold deep water.
- Average salinity ≈ {{35 parts per thousand}}.

| Belt | Surface salinity | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Subtropics** | {{Highest}} | Strong evaporation, little rain |
| Equator | Lower than the subtropics | Heavy rain |
| Polar seas | Low | Little evaporation, ice melt |

| Zone of rapid change in… | Name |
|---|---|
| Temperature | {{Thermocline}} |
| Salinity | {{Halocline}} |
| Density | {{Pycnocline}} |

> [!TRAP] Maximum salinity is !!not!! at the Equator. · Halocline = rapid **change** in salinity — it need not increase with depth. · "Densest at 4°C" is a freshwater rule; seawater density depends on temperature, salinity and pressure.

## Ocean Currents {#ocean-currents}

Drivers:
- Prevailing winds · Coriolis (Earth's **rotation**) · sea-surface slope (gravity) · density differences (temperature + salinity).
- Coasts and basins shape the path. Earth's **revolution** is !!not!! a driver.

- Gyres → {{clockwise in the NH}}, {{anticlockwise in the SH}}.
- Tendency → western-boundary currents warm (flow poleward); eastern-boundary currents cold (flow equatorward).

| Warm | Ocean | Cold | Ocean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gulf Stream / North Atlantic Drift** | N Atlantic | **Canary** | NE Atlantic |
| **Brazil** | S Atlantic | **Benguela** | SE Atlantic |
| **Kuroshio** | N Pacific | **California** | NE Pacific |
| **East Australian** | S Pacific | **Humboldt / Peru** | SE Pacific |
| **Agulhas** | SW Indian | **West Australian** | SE Indian |

> [!UPPCS] {{Agulhas and Brazil}} → warm. {{Humboldt and California}} → cold.

- Currents bend isotherms → [Isotherms](@climatology#heat-budget). Cold currents help make coastal deserts → [Hot deserts](@climatic-regions#identification-matrix).

## Equatorial Currents, Upwelling & Fishing {#equatorial-currents-fishing}

> [!FLOW] Trade winds → North and South Equatorial Currents flow west → water piles up in the west → slope drives a return flow east → **Equatorial Counter Current**.

- Rich fishing grounds:
  - where {{warm and cold currents meet}} (plankton-rich mixing; also fog);
  - coastal **upwelling** zones — nutrient-rich deep water reaches the sunlit layer.
- El Niño weakens Peru upwelling → [ENSO](@climatology#enso-iod).

## Deep Circulation {#thermohaline}

- **Thermohaline circulation** → driven by density (temperature + salinity).
- Cold, salty water at high latitudes sinks → spreads through all ocean basins.
- A strong pycnocline blocks vertical mixing.

Oceans as carbon sink, acidification → [Corals and acidification](@prelims/environment/marine-ecology#corals)

## Tides {#tides}

- Cause → Moon's gravity (main) + Sun's gravity + the inertial (centrifugal) effect of the Earth–Moon system.
- Moon dominates because tidal force depends strongly on **distance**.

| Tide | Alignment | Phase | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | Sun–Earth–Moon in a line | {{New and full moon}} | Largest |
| **Neap** | Sun and Moon at right angles | {{Quarter moons}} | Smallest |

- Semi-diurnal → two highs and two lows a lunar day; successive high tides about {{12 h 25 min}} apart.
- Funnel-shaped bays and shallow coasts magnify tides → [Chandipur](@india-physiography#coasts).

> [!TRAP] High tides are !!not!! exactly 12 hours apart.
