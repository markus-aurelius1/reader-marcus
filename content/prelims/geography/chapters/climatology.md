## Atmosphere — Composition & Layers {#atmosphere-layers}

- Dry air → N₂ ~78% · O₂ ~21% · Ar ~0.93% · CO₂ about {{0.04%, rising}} · water vapour highly variable.
- Pressure and density fall quickly with height.

| Layer | Height | Temperature with height | Anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | ~8 km poles → ~18 km Equator | Falls (~{{6.5°C per km}}) | All weather; water vapour and dust; {{thicker at the Equator}} — stronger heating and convection |
| **Stratosphere** | up to ~50 km | Rises | {{Ozone}} absorbs UV; stable, very dry → smooth jet flight |
| **Mesosphere** | ~50–80 km | Falls | {{Coldest layer}}; meteors burn |
| **Thermosphere** | 80 km + | Rises sharply | Very thin air; aurora |
| **Ionosphere** | ~80–400 km (overlaps) | — | Free electrons bend / return {{radio waves}} of suitable frequency |
| **Exosphere** | Outermost | — | Merges into space |

- Ozone → concentrated in the stratosphere. CFC / halogen radicals destroy it catalytically; polar stratospheric clouds speed this up.

> [!TRAP] "Stratosphere has no clouds or water vapour" → too absolute; it is very dry and has rare polar stratospheric clouds. · Ionosphere works through **ionisation**, not ozone. · The magnetosphere is !!not!! a layer of the exosphere.

Ozone depletion and its treaty → [Ozone](@prelims/environment/climate-change#ozone)

## Heat Budget & Temperature {#heat-budget}

> [!FLOW] Sun → **shortwave** insolation → surface absorbs → emits **longwave** (infrared) → greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit → atmosphere heated **from below**.

- Atmosphere is heated by → terrestrial longwave radiation · conduction and convection near the ground · latent heat released on condensation.
- Radiation, conduction and convection are **separate** modes of heat transfer.
- Greenhouse gases → water vapour, CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, O₃. N₂ and O₂ are !!not!! greenhouse gases.
- Planetary albedo ≈ {{30%}}.

| Cloud | Shortwave | Longwave | Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low, thick** | Reflects strongly | — | {{Cooling}} |
| **High, thin** | Lets sunlight through | Traps outgoing heat | {{Warming}} |

- Temperature controls → latitude, altitude, land–sea contrast, ocean currents, winds, cloud, slope.
- **Isotherms** → roughly east–west, bent by land–sea contrast and currents.
- January, Northern Hemisphere → continents cold, oceans warm → isotherms bend {{equatorward over land}}, {{poleward over oceans}}. The warm Gulf Stream pushes them poleward over the North Atlantic.
- **Temperature inversion** → temperature rises with height; clear calm nights, valleys.

Greenhouse gases and warming → [Greenhouse science](@prelims/environment/climate-change#climate-science)
- Dust in the air → more in the subtropical and temperate dry belts than at the Equator or poles.

## Pressure Belts, Winds & Coriolis {#pressure-winds}

- Belts → Equatorial low (ITCZ) · Subtropical highs ~30° · Subpolar lows ~60° · Polar highs.
- **ITCZ** → migrates with the Sun; not fixed on the Equator.

| Wind | From → to | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Trades** | Subtropical high → Equatorial low | NE in NH, SE in SH; pile warm water on the western side of oceans |
| **Westerlies** | Subtropical high → Subpolar low | {{Stronger in the SH}} — open ocean, few land barriers |
| **Polar easterlies** | Polar high → Subpolar low | |

**Coriolis**
- Deflects moving air {{right in the NH, left in the SH}}.
- {{Zero at the Equator}}, increases towards the poles.
- Changes **direction only** — never speed.
- Wind is started by the pressure gradient; Coriolis and friction then modify it.

**Jet streams**
- Narrow, fast currents near the tropopause; mostly {{west → east}}.
- Subtropical Westerly Jet + Polar Front Jet. Exception → {{Tropical Easterly Jet}}.

> [!TRAP] "All jet streams are westerly" and "Coriolis increases wind speed" are both wrong.

## Humidity, Condensation & Dew {#humidity}

| Term | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute humidity** | Water vapour per unit volume of air | {{g/m³}} |
| **Specific humidity** | Water vapour per unit mass of air | {{g/kg}} |
| **Relative humidity** | Actual vapour ÷ saturation vapour × 100 | % |
| **Dew point** | Temperature at which air becomes saturated | °C |

- Same moisture content → warming {{lowers}} RH, cooling {{raises}} it.
- Condensation needs saturation + nuclei → dew, frost, fog, cloud.
- Dew forms best on {{clear, calm nights}} — the ground loses heat fast. Clouds keep the ground warm → less dew.
- Rain types → convectional · orographic · frontal / cyclonic.

## Tropical Cyclones {#tropical-cyclones}

Needs:
- Warm sea → {{about 26.5°C or more}}, to a good depth.
- Moist, unstable air + a pre-existing disturbance.
- Enough Coriolis → none very near the Equator.
- {{Low vertical wind shear}} + outflow aloft.

- **Eye** → calm, sinking air, {{warmer than its surroundings}}. **Eyewall** → strongest winds and rain.
- Rare in the **South Atlantic and SE Pacific** → cool water (cold currents, upwelling), strong shear, ITCZ rarely reaches there.
- Temperate cyclones → frontal systems; not warm-core.

Winds applied to India → [Monsoon](@india-climate-soils-vegetation#monsoon)

## ENSO, IOD & MJO {#enso-iod}

**Normal Pacific** → trades push warm water west → warm pool and rain near Indonesia / Australia; cold upwelling off Peru. **Walker circulation** = rising air in the west, sinking in the east.

| | El Niño | La Niña |
|---|---|---|
| Central / eastern Pacific | {{Warmer than normal}} | {{Cooler than normal}} |
| Trades / Walker cell | Weaken | Strengthen |
| Upwelling off Peru | {{Weakens}} → plankton and fish decline | Strengthens |
| Recurrence | Irregular, about {{2–7 years}} | Does not have to follow an El Niño |

- **Southern Oscillation** = the air-pressure see-saw; ENSO = ocean + atmosphere together.

**Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)**
- Difference in sea-surface temperature across the **equatorial Indian Ocean**.
- Positive IOD → {{western Indian Ocean warmer}}, eastern side (near Indonesia) cooler. Negative → reverse.
- Can strengthen or offset El Niño's effect on the Indian monsoon. No guaranteed outcome.

**MJO** → eastward-moving band of tropical cloud and rain, {{30–60 day}} cycle. MJO ≠ IOD.

> [!TRAP] El Niño is !!not!! a fixed 7–8 year cycle. · IOD's eastern pole is in the Indian Ocean, not the Pacific. · No formula of El Niño + IOD + MJO fixes monsoon rainfall.

> [!DYN] Whether this year is an El Niño / La Niña / positive-IOD year is a forecast, not a note fact — check before the exam.
