## Nano Basics {#nano-basics}

- Nanoscale → about {{1–100 nanometres}}.
- At this size → huge surface area; materials behave differently.
- Nanoparticles also occur {{naturally}} (volcanic ash, sea spray).
- Metal-oxide nanoparticles (zinc, titanium) → used in sunscreens and cosmetics.
- Safety depends on material, size and dose.

Nanoparticles as pollutants → [Emerging contaminants](@prelims/environment/pollution#emerging-contaminants)

## Carbon Materials {#carbon-materials}

| Material | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Graphene** | One-atom-thick sheet of {{carbon}} (2D) | Very strong, thin, conducts electricity → touch screens, electrodes, sensors |
| **Graphite** | Stacked graphene layers | Pencil; battery anode |
| **Carbon nanotube** | Graphene rolled into a tube | Carries drugs and antigens in the body; biochemical sensors; can biodegrade |
| **Fullerene (C₆₀)** | Closed cage ("buckyball") | Different from a nanotube |
| **Carbon fibre** | Light, strong fibre in composites | {{Aircraft, cars}}, sports gear; can be recycled |

> [!TRAP] Graphene is !!not!! made of silicon. · "Carbon fibre cannot be recycled" is false. · Nanotubes as artificial blood capillaries → not an accepted use.

## Nanomedicine & Hydrogels {#nanomedicine}

- Nano-carriers → {{targeted drug delivery}} — medicine released only where needed; also gene therapy → [Gene therapy](@genetics-biotechnology#gene-therapy).
- **Hydrogel** → polymer network that holds a lot of water → controlled drug release, wound dressing, contact lenses.

> [!VERIFY] A 2024 question listed several hydrogel uses (drug delivery plus industrial ones); the record is incomplete and sources disagree on the right combination. Only drug delivery is taken as settled.

> [!UPPCS] Graphene → a nanostructure of carbon. · Graphite → the most stable form of carbon.
