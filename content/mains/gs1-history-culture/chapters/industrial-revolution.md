## Why Britain First {#why-britain}

| Cluster | Points |
|---|---|
| **Agriculture & labour** | Agricultural Revolution → surplus food; labour released; population growth, migration |
| **Resources & technology** | Coal + iron; textile mechanisation; steam; canals → railways |
| **Markets & empire** | Cheap colonial cotton; captive markets; shipping, harbours |
| **Finance & institutions** | Post-Glorious Revolution fiscal order; public debt markets; London; stability, property incentives |
| **Ideas** | Scientific outlook; experiment; liberalism, entrepreneurship |

- Mnemonic → **A-R-C-F-I-M-T**: Agriculture · Resources · Colonies · Finance · Institutions · Markets · Technology.
- Interpretation → technology-only explanation is inadequate.

## Industrial Capitalism & Social Effects {#capitalism-society}

- Factory system → capital, machines, labour concentrated.
- Wage labour replaced much household / artisanal production.
- Industrial bourgeoisie; market dependence; industrial towns.
- Industrial competition spilled into ==imperial competition==.

| Growth side | Social cost (2015: quality of life) |
|---|---|
| Mass production → cheaper goods | Overcrowded towns |
| Jobs in factories, mines, railways | Poor sanitation, public health |
| Urbanisation | Long hours, low wages, hazards |
| Middle and business classes | Women and child labour |
| Technical education | Class differentiation |
| Wider markets | Artisans displaced; uneven regions |

- Readings → developmental (productivity, connectivity) · social critique (proletarianisation, exploitation) · colonial (same technology, different purpose depending on ownership and power).

## Japan — the Latecomer {#japan}

| | Britain / early West | Japan (2013) |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Gradual, 18th–19th c. | Compressed after {{Meiji Restoration, 1868}} |
| **State** | Enabling | ==Strong direct direction== |
| **Technology** | Largely endogenous | Selective borrowing, adaptation |
| **Firms** | Private capital | State enterprises → {{zaibatsu}} |
| **Human capital** | Gradual | Deliberate education drive |
| **Resources** | Coal, iron | Scarce → efficiency, imports |
| **Purpose** | Commercial expansion | Modernisation + national power, security |

- Takeaway → late industrialisers compress development by using the state to acquire technology, mobilise capital and build capability.

## Railways {#railways}

> [!FLOW] Steam / iron → railway → lower transport cost → larger markets → specialisation → urbanisation → mobility → national integration

| Developed economies | Colonies / developing economies (2023) |
|---|---|
| Linked industrial centres and resource regions | Could stimulate trade, integration |
| Suburban, urban growth | Often linked hinterland → port for extraction |
| Engineering, railway employment | Imported manufactures reached rural markets |
| Movement of people, ideas | Displacement, uneven benefit |

- Examples → US Transcontinental (continental integration) · {{Trans-Siberian}} (territorial integration) · Kenya–Uganda Railway (colonial commerce) · colonial Indian railways (extraction + market penetration).

Indian side → [Infrastructure — Dual Effect](@colonial-economy#infrastructure)

## India Link — Deindustrialisation {#india-link}

| Direct industrial mechanism | But the Industrial Revolution alone is insufficient |
|---|---|
| Machine goods' cost and scale advantage | Colonial tariff / trade policy |
| Handloom could not compete with mechanised imports | Loss of royal, local patronage |
| Railways widened import penetration | Revenue pressure |
| India supplied raw material | Commercialised agriculture; no protective industrial policy |

- Line (2024) → ==industrial technology created the asymmetry; colonial policy converted it into deindustrialisation==.
- Indigenous response → Tata steel, Sakchi, {{1907}}.

Full Indian chain → [Deindustrialisation](@colonial-economy#deindustrialisation)

## Rapid Recall {#rapid-recall}

- **Keywords** → factory system · industrial capitalism · mechanisation · proletarianisation · market integration · late industrialisation · deindustrialisation
- **Examples** → Meiji 1868 · zaibatsu · Trans-Siberian · Kenya–Uganda Railway · Tata at Sakchi
- **Visual** → flywheel (capital → machines → productivity → markets → profit) resting on coal / iron + labour + transport + institutions + empire
- **Intro** → The Industrial Revolution reorganised production, labour, settlement, markets and, eventually, the world distribution of power.
- **Conclusion** → Technology yields neither development nor exploitation automatically; outcomes depend on the institutions and power through which industrialisation occurs.
