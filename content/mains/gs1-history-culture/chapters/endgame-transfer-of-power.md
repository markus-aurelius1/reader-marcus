## Why the Raj Became Unsustainable {#unsustainable}

| Pressure | Points |
|---|---|
| **Imperial / global** | Britain financially, militarily exhausted; USA and USSR dominant; decolonisation fits the post-war order; less manpower for coercion; {{Attlee}}'s Labour government receptive |
| **Political** | Decades of mobilisation destroyed legitimacy; QIM showed depth; 1945–46 elections confirmed Congress and League as mass claimants; INA trial enthusiasm; no viable Indian coalition for indefinite Raj |
| **Military / coercive** | INA — Indian soldiers could be turned; sympathy inside the services; RIN mutiny; fear of military revolt meeting popular unrest |
| **Constitutional** | Cripps → Wavell → Cabinet Mission failed to reconcile Congress centralism with the League's Pakistan demand; British arbitration became part of the deadlock |

## RIN Mutiny, 1946 {#rin}

- Causes → poor food, pay, conditions · racial humiliation · demobilisation anxiety · nationalist mood after the INA trials.
- Course → {{HMIS Talwar}}, Bombay ({{February 1946}}) → ratings' strike → other naval establishments → civilian and worker solidarity.

| Why strategically significant | Why not sufficient alone |
|---|---|
| Coercive reliability questioned — empire rested on Indian soldiers, sailors | Short-lived; no all-India service coordination |
| INA → RIN: nationalism crossed into the forces | Congress and League did not endorse military rebellion |
| Congress, League, Communist flags flown together | Constitutional transfer already moving |
| Bombay workers struck in solidarity | British weakness was cumulative |
| Came as post-war policy was being decided | |
| Re-conquest by force would be too costly | |

- Verdict (2014) → not the sole cause; the most dramatic sign that the ==coercive foundation of the Raj== was uncertain.

## How British Policy Complicated It {#british-role}

1. **Delayed concession** → August Offer, Cripps gave future self-government, no present power.
2. **Minority veto** → Congress–League agreement treated as a precondition.
3. **Ambiguous grouping** → Cabinet Mission scheme read differently by each side.
4. **Changing templates** → Cripps → Wavell → Cabinet Mission → Attlee → Mountbatten.
5. **Communal institutional legacy** → separate electorates hardened identities.
6. **Abrupt acceleration** → June 1948 deadline compressed to August 1947.
7. **Boundary uncertainty** → last-minute division of Punjab, Bengal, services, army.
8. **Paramountcy lapse** → princely states left with a sovereignty problem → [Integration](@princely-states-integration#problem-mechanism).

> [!NOTE] Balance: Congress–League incompatibility and mass communalisation cannot be reduced to British manipulation — by the 1940s Indian actors had independent agency and incompatible aims.
