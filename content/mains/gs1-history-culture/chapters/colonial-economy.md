## Land Revenue & Commercialisation {#land-revenue}

| | Permanent Settlement | Ryotwari | Mahalwari |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unit** | Zamindar | Individual cultivator | Village / mahal |
| **Region** | Bengal–Bihar | Madras, Bombay | North-West, North India |
| **Date** | {{1793}} | Early 19th c. | {{1822}} onwards |
| **Demand** | Fixed permanently with zamindar | Direct, periodically assessed | Collective, revised |
| **Effect** | Intermediaries, absentee landlordism | State–peasant pressure | Collective liability |

- Common outcome → high revenue pressure, debt, land alienation, weak agricultural investment.
- Argument → forms differed; the aim was ==secure, maximised revenue==, not agrarian development.

**Commercialisation of agriculture**
- Drivers → cash revenue · export demand · planter coercion · rail–port links · British industrial needs · world prices.
- Crops → indigo, cotton, opium, jute, tea.
- Mixed impact → monetisation, gains for some merchants and rich peasants; for most → price risk, debt, food insecurity, moneylender dependence.

Settlement facts → [Land Revenue](@prelims/history-culture/british-administration-economy#land-revenue)

## Deindustrialisation {#deindustrialisation}

> [!FLOW] British industrialisation + trade asymmetry + lost patronage + machine imports → artisan displacement → return to agriculture → ruralisation / overcrowded land → fragmented holdings, disguised unemployment → less supplementary income → debt and vulnerability

- Sectors → handloom weavers, spinners, dyers, metal workers, potters, leather workers.
- 2017 point → handicraft decline was not only "industrial" — it ==transferred labour and distress into agriculture==.
- 2024 point → technology created the competitive gap; colonial tariff / trade policy, lost patronage and no protective policy turned it into deindustrialisation → [India link](@industrial-revolution#india-link).

> [!NOTE] Qualifier for a critical answer: decline was neither instant nor uniform — some specialised crafts adapted; Indian cotton and jute mills later emerged.

## Drain of Wealth {#drain}

- **Dadabhai Naoroji** → Drain Theory: poverty as a critique of colonial political economy.

| Channel | Item |
|---|---|
| **Home Charges** | Costs met in Britain from Indian revenue |
| **Personnel** | Salaries, pensions of British staff |
| **Debt** | Interest on externally raised public debt |
| **Private** | Profits, dividends, remittances |
| **Services** | Stores bought in Britain; shipping, banking, insurance |
| **Military** | Imperial wars on Indian revenue |

- Consequence → lower capital formation, demand, employment, reinvestment.
- Political significance → nationalism moved from "bad administrators" to ==colonial rule itself produces poverty==.

Drain detail → [Drain of Wealth](@prelims/history-culture/british-administration-economy#drain-of-wealth)

## Famines {#famines}

- Examples → Bengal {{1770}} · Orissa {{1866}} · Great Famine {{1876–78}} · Bengal {{1943}}.
- Two-layer answer:

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Rainfall failure, drought, crop disease, war |
| **Why catastrophe** | Heavy revenue demand · cash-crop orientation · artisan decline → over-dependence on land · debt, weak purchasing power · customary safety nets eroded · imperial military / fiscal priorities · relief limited by ideology · grain moved through markets without reaching those who could not buy |

- Line → ==climate was the trigger; colonial vulnerability was the cause==.

## Infrastructure — Dual Effect {#infrastructure}

| Colonial function | Unintended / transformative effect |
|---|---|
| Raw materials to ports | Market and geographic integration |
| British manufactures inland | Mobility |
| Troop movement | Railway labour as a new class |
| Administrative integration | Easier nationalist organisation |
| Guaranteed returns for British capital | Physical base inherited in 1947 |

- Railways from {{1853}}; plus ports, roads, telegraph, irrigation.
- Avoid both extremes → neither "gift of modernity" nor infrastructure with no developmental effect.

## Interpretations {#interpretations}

- **Naoroji, R.C. Dutt** → exploitation and drain.
- **R.P. Dutt (Marxist)** → changing forms: merchant capital → industrial / free-trade capital → finance capital.
- Balanced → policy created the ==structural asymmetry==; outcomes varied by crop, craft, region and period.

## Rapid Recall {#rapid-recall}

- **Keywords** → deindustrialisation · commercialisation · ruralisation · drain · Home Charges · one-way free trade
- **Examples** → Permanent Settlement 1793 · Bengal famine 1770 · handloom weavers · railways 1853 · Naoroji
- **Visual** → two arrows — revenue → cash crops → debt; British industry → artisans → agriculture — meeting at agrarian overcrowding + low purchasing power → drought / war shock → famine
- **Intro** → Colonialism did not only extract surplus; it reorganised production, trade and infrastructure to serve an external centre.
- **Conclusion** → The deepest legacy was structural — from diversified production to agrarian dependence and economic subordination.
