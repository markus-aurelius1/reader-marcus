## Pre-History {#pre-history}

- Congress's {{Nagpur reorganisation, 1920}} → linguistic units inside the party.
- After Partition → leadership cautious: language seemed able to reproduce separatism.
- {{Dhar Commission, 1948}} and {{JVP Committee, 1949}} → unity and administration over immediate linguistic states.

## Pressure From Below {#pressure}

> [!FLOW] Telugu mobilisation → Potti Sriramulu's fast and death (1952) → mass protests → Andhra State (1953)

- Precedent → linguistic statehood was not bestowed by the Centre; ==organised regional movements altered central policy==.
- Followed by → Samyukta Maharashtra · Mahagujarat · Punjabi Suba · later ethnic / developmental movements in the North-East and elsewhere.

## SRC & the 1956 Settlement {#src}

- States Reorganisation Commission, {{1953}} → {{Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru}} → report 1955 → States Reorganisation Act + {{7th Amendment}}, 1956.
- Rejected "one language = one state". Criteria:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| **Language / culture** | Important, not sole |
| **National unity, security** | Overriding |
| **Administrative convenience, communication** | Yes |
| **Financial / economic viability** | Yes |

- 1956 outcome → {{14 states + 6 Union Territories}}; boundaries largely rationalised; sizeable linguistic minorities remained.

Commission and Act facts → [States Reorganisation](@prelims/history-culture/post-independence-transition#states-reorganisation) · Constitutional power → [Reorganisation of States](@prelims/polity/union-states-citizenship#reorganisation-of-states)

## Why It Strengthened India {#strengthened}

- **Democratic legitimacy** → government in the people's language.
- **Identity accommodation** → regional pride needed no exit.
- **Conflict institutionalised** → agitation → commission → Parliament → boundary change.
- **Federalised nationalism** → Telugu / Tamil / Marathi ==and== Indian.
- **Regional leaders entered constitutional politics** → less incentive for separatism.
- **Linguistic-minority protections** → no rigid nation-state model.
- Paradox → "dividing India by language" consolidated it: identities got constitutional homes within the Union.
- Phrase → constitutional accommodation over coercion.

## A Continuous Process {#continuous}

| Reorganisation | Dominant driver |
|---|---|
| **Maharashtra–Gujarat, 1960** | Language, popular mobilisation |
| **Nagaland, 1963** | Ethnicity, security |
| **Haryana, 1966** | Language + regional politics |
| **Meghalaya and others** | Tribal / ethnic identity |
| **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Uttarakhand, 2000** | Development + geography + identity |
| **Telangana, 2014** | Regional identity + perceived developmental imbalance |

- Line (2022) → 1956 was ==not an endpoint but a federal technique==; language was never the only principle.
- Limits → linguistic minorities remained inside every state; new sub-regional and developmental demands keep arising.
