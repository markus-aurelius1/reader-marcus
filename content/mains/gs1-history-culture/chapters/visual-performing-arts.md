## Sculpture Traditions {#sculpture}

| Tradition | Identifiers | Material / technique | Use in answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harappan** | Small-scale realism; seals | {{Lost-wax}} bronze, terracotta, steatite | Dancing Girl → metallurgy; seals → trade, belief |
| **Mauryan** | Monumental, polished; pillar capitals; Yaksha–Yakshi | Polished sandstone, monolithic | Sarnath lions → sovereignty, Dhamma; court vs popular art |
| **Bharhut–Sanchi** | Narrative relief; ==aniconism== | Railing / torana relief | Buddha as wheel, tree, footprints, empty throne |
| **Gandhara** | Anatomy, wavy hair, deep drapery | {{Grey schist}}, stucco | Buddhist content + Hellenistic / Roman / Central Asian form |
| **Mathura** | Frontal, full-bodied; Buddhist + Jain + Hindu | {{Spotted red sandstone}} | Indigenous Yaksha line |
| **Amaravati** | Animated, crowded narrative | {{White limestone}} | Southern Buddhist idiom |
| **Gupta / Sarnath** | Restraint, serenity, proportion | Fine sandstone | Classical synthesis; ideal Buddha type across Asia |
| **Pallava** | Relief fused with rock | Rock-cut → structural | Descent of Ganga, Mahishasuramardini |
| **Chola** | Grace, rhythm, balance | Lost-wax bronze | Nataraja; portable processional icons |
| **Chandella (2025)** | Rhythmic bodies, surasundaris | High-relief | "Breadth of life" — divine, sensual, martial, everyday in one programme |

- Synthesis line → Indian sculpture ==absorbed, localised and re-signified== external forms; not "foreign vs indigenous".

**Gandhara (2014, 2019) — answer points**
- Kushana-age north-west = cultural crossroads.
- Anthropomorphic Buddha; Apollo / Hercules-like modelling; halo; Iranian / Central Asian dress.
- Content stays Buddhist → form borrowed, meaning Indian.
- Cross-fertilised with Mathura → Gupta idiom.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Lion** | Sovereignty, courage; Ashokan Dhamma; Durga / Narasimha |
| **Bull** | Nandi–Shaiva devotion; strength, fertility, agrarian prosperity |
| **Lotus** | Purity from the material world; divine seat; Buddhist / Hindu / Jain |
| **Dharmachakra** | Dhamma; moral-cosmic order |
| **Yaksha–Yakshi, tree, shalabhanjika** | Folk fertility cults absorbed |
| **Mithuna** | Auspiciousness, fertility; worldly-to-sacred continuum |

- **Nataraja** → damaru: {{creation}} · agni: {{destruction}} · abhaya: protection · Apasmara: {{ignorance}} · lifted foot: liberation · flame ring: cosmic process.

**Temple as visual archive (2022)**
- Occupations → artisans, warriors, dancers, musicians, merchants.
- Gender → ornament, domestic life, performers, courtly women.
- Material life → dress, jewellery, hair, weapons, instruments.
- Hierarchy → scale, attire, setting.
- Leisure → dance, music, hunting, wrestling, erotic life.
- Power → royal, processional, martial imagery.
- Limit → selective, convention-bound; patrons idealise — an image is not a photograph.

**Material arts beyond sculpture**
- **Coins** → metal + portrait + legend + symbol = polity + religion + aesthetics.
- **Gupta coins (2017)** → king sacrificing, hunting, playing music + deities → legitimacy as miniature art.
- **Seals** → script + animals + commodity authentication → iconography joins economy.
- **Bronze** → Dancing Girl → Chola lost-wax perfection.

## Painting {#painting}

| Tradition | Markers | Themes | Patronage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prehistoric rock art** | Mineral pigment, superimposition | Hunt, animals, dance | Community |
| **Ajanta mural** | Plaster, flowing line, modelling | Jatakas, court scenes | Monastic + elite |
| **Pala** | Palm-leaf, fine line | Buddhist deities | Eastern monasteries |
| **Western Indian / Jain** | Angular figures, red ground | *Kalpasutra* | Merchant–monastic |
| **Mughal** | Naturalism, portrait, perspective | Court, hunt, flora-fauna | Imperial kitabkhana |
| **Rajasthani** | Flat brilliant colour, bold line | Krishna, Ragamala | Rajput courts + Bhakti |
| **Pahari** | Basohli bold; Kangra lyrical | Radha–Krishna, *Gita Govinda* | Hill courts |
| **Deccani** | Rich colour, elongated figures | Court, music | Bijapur, Golconda |
| **Company → Bengal** | European naturalism → revivalism | Occupations → nationalist themes | Colonial patrons → nationalist institutions |

- **Shadanga** (six limbs) → rupabheda, pramana, bhava, lavanyayojanam, sadrisya, varnikabhanga.

**Mughal painting — model of synthesis**
- Akbar's kitabkhana → organised atelier.
- Persian convention + Indian colour, movement, narrative.
- Jahangir → empirical naturalism; {{Ustad Mansur}} flora and fauna.
- Portrait / darbar → hierarchy and sovereignty made visible.
- European contact → perspective, shading.
- Works → *Hamzanama, Razmnama, Akbarnama, Padshahnama*.
- Line → court art + propaganda + natural history + laboratory of synthesis.

**Rock art as social evidence (Bhimbetka)** → hunting: collective organisation · honey: subsistence · cattle: pastoral shift · group dance: community · weapons: technology · pigments: material knowledge.

**Folk identifiers (one line each)** → Madhubani: dense line, ritual themes · Pattachitra: Jagannath scrolls · Kalamkari: Srikalahasti / Machilipatnam textile · Warli: geometric stick figures · Gond: patterned flora-fauna · Phad: Rajasthan scroll · Kalighat: urban satire.

> [!FLOW] Natya Shastra → rasa + bhava + abhinaya + mudra + movement + music → aesthetic experience

- **Nritta** → pure rhythm. **Nritya** → expressive dance. **Natya** → drama.

**Tandava (2013)**
- Shiva's dance = philosophy in movement → creation → preservation → destruction → concealment → grace.
- Energetic mode; contrast → {{Lasya}}.
- Nataraja turns the idea into bronze; Chidambaram joins sculpture, dance and temple.
- Point → Indian forms are ==intermedial== — theology in text, stone, music and movement at once.

| | Hindustani | Carnatic |
|---|---|---|
| **Context** | Persian / court / Sufi interaction | Temple / Bhakti continuity |
| **Forms** | Dhrupad, Khayal, Thumri | Kriti, Varnam |
| **Idiom** | Gharana | Bani / sampradaya |
| **Mechanism** | Indo-Persian synthesis within raga–tala | Bhakti vernacularisation + systematic raga / mela |

- Bhakti contributors → Purandara Dasa, Annamacharya, Mirabai, Surdas, Kabir.

**Theatre** → synthetic art: literature + mime + dance + music + costume.
- Koodiyattam (living Sanskrit theatre) · Yakshagana (Karnataka) · Bhaona / Ankiya Naat (Assam) · Ramlila / Raslila · Nautanki · Tamasha · Jatra · Therukoothu · Bhavai · Dastangoi.
- Significance → preservation + moral education + social comment + regional identity + fusion.

## Patronage & Debates {#patronage-debates}

> [!FLOW] Patron → institution / site → artisan / performer → technique / style → theme → audience → transmission

| Patron | Sculpture | Painting | Performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Religious / monastic** | Stupas, icons | Ajanta, Pala / Jain manuscripts | Temple dance, Bhakti music |
| **Royal** | Mauryan pillars, Chola bronzes | Mughal / Rajput / Pahari | Court music, Kathak |
| **Merchant / community** | Stupa donations | Jain manuscripts, folk | Folk theatre |
| **Modern institutions** | Museums | Art schools | Academies, festivals |

- Patronage changes scale, subject, material, audience and vocabulary — not just finance.
- **Synthesis, not imitation** → Gandhara, Mughal painting, Hindustani music.
- **Sacred ≠ socially isolated** → religious art preserves dress, gender, leisure, hierarchy.
- **Classical–folk continuum** → folk motifs enter codified forms; classical flows back.

- **Keywords** → aniconism · anthropomorphism · narrative relief · cire-perdue · tribhanga · Shadanga · kitabkhana · rasa–bhava · abhinaya · gharana

- **Intro** → From stone and bronze to pigment and movement, India repeatedly turned philosophy and social experience into visible, performed form.
- **Conclusion** → Endurance lies less in stylistic purity than in continuity through adaptation — new patrons, materials and influences within recognisable grammars.
