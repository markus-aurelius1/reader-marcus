## WWI — Causation {#ww1-causes}

**Balance-of-power explanation (2024)**
- Unified Germany's economic and military rise upset the European equilibrium.
- Germany challenged British naval supremacy.
- {{Triple Alliance}} vs {{Triple Entente}} → balancing institutionalised.
- Russia–Austria rivalry in the Balkans.
- Moroccan crises → Franco-German imperial rivalry.
- Alliances turned a regional crisis into a continental war.

**Beyond balance of power — MAIN**

| | Content |
|---|---|
| **M — Militarism** | Arms race, war plans, faith in rapid offensive; {{Schlieffen Plan}} |
| **A — Alliances** | Security mechanism became a transmission belt for war |
| **I — Imperialism** | Colonial rivalry sharpened distrust |
| **N — Nationalism** | French revanchism; Balkan / Serbian nationalism |
| **Trigger** | Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, Sarajevo, {{1914}} |

- Judgement → balance of power explains the ==structure of insecurity==; nationalism, imperial rivalry, militarism and crisis decisions explain why it produced war.

Imperial rivalry background → [New Imperialism — Why](@imperialism-migration#new-imperialism)

## Germany's Responsibility {#germany}

| | WWI | WWII |
|---|---|---|
| **German agency** | Backed Austria-Hungary; {{"blank cheque"}}; war plan widened the war; naval build-up alarmed Britain | Explicit Nazi revisionism, expansionism; Versailles limits dismantled; attack on {{Poland, 1939}} |
| **Shared / enabling** | Austro-Russian Balkan rivalry; Serbian nationalism; French, Russian alliance politics; imperial competition; general arms race | Punitive, unstable Versailles; Depression; League failure; appeasement; unresolved grievances |
| **Verdict (2015)** | Systemic responsibility with important German agency | Much stronger German / Nazi agency within an enabling crisis |

## Interwar Democratic Crisis {#interwar}

> [!FLOW] WWI trauma + weak settlements + economic crisis + social fear → disillusion with parliamentary democracy → authoritarian alternatives

| Dimension (2021) | Content |
|---|---|
| **Economic** | Depression → unemployment, insecurity; democracies seemed unable to recover fast |
| **Ideological** | Fascism / Nazism offered order, nationalism, anti-communism; communism a rival system |
| **Institutional** | League lacked enforcement; failed in {{Manchuria}} and {{Ethiopia}} |
| **Diplomatic** | Appeasement enabled revisionist expansion |
| **Social-psychological** | War-weariness, pacifism, reluctance to confront aggression |

| | Fascism | Nazism |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Ultra-nationalist authoritarianism | Fascism + racial-biological hierarchy |
| **State** | Nation / state supreme | Führer-state + racial community |
| **Economy** | Private property under state direction | Same + rearmament, autarky |
| **Method** | One party, propaganda, corporatism, violence | One-party terror + racial persecution |
| **Distinctive** | Primacy of the state | {{Aryan supremacy, antisemitism, Lebensraum}} |

- Common conditions → post-war grievance · fear of communism · fragile democracy · economic distress · mass propaganda.
- Markers → Mussolini {{1922}} · Hitler {{1933}} · Munich {{1938}}.

**Appeasement — why**
- War-weariness, pacifist opinion.
- Belief that Versailles was too harsh.
- Fear of communism → Germany as counterweight.
- Depression-era constraints; US isolationism; League failure.
- Misreading Hitler's aims.
- Evaluation → real short-term constraints, but concessions without credible deterrence ==strengthened revisionist calculation==.

## Great Depression — Policy Instruments {#depression}

> [!FLOW] Financial crash → bank failures, credit contraction → falling demand, output → unemployment → trade collapse → political radicalisation

| Approach (2013) | Instruments |
|---|---|
| **Monetary** | Devaluation; leaving rigid gold-standard limits; easier money |
| **Fiscal** | Deficit spending, public works |
| **Banking** | Regulation, deposit and financial stabilisation |
| **Welfare** | Employment and relief programmes |
| **Trade** | Tariffs, imperial preference blocs |
| **USA** | {{New Deal}} — relief, recovery, reform |
| **Germany** | Public works + rearmament + state direction |
| **Ideas** | Legitimacy for {{Keynesian}} demand management |

- Significance → weakened laissez-faire · expanded the economic state · intensified protectionism, economic nationalism · fed radical parties.

## Remaking of Boundaries {#boundaries}

| After WWI | After WWII |
|---|---|
| German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman, Russian empires collapsed | Germany divided, occupied |
| Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Austria, Hungary created / reconstituted | Poland shifted west; Soviet territorial gains |
| Wilsonian self-determination applied unevenly → stranded minorities | Japanese empire dismantled; Korea divided |
| Ottoman lands → League {{mandates}}: new borders, incomplete self-determination | European overseas empires → rapid decolonisation |
| League of Nations | United Nations replaced the League |

- Point → wars changed not just borders but the ==principle and hierarchy of the international order==.

## Rapid Recall {#rapid-recall}

- **Keywords** → balance of power · security dilemma · militarism · collective security · revisionism · appeasement · totalitarianism · economic nationalism
- **Examples** → Sarajevo 1914 · Schlieffen Plan · Manchuria / Ethiopia · Munich 1938 · New Deal
- **Visual** → Versailles → Depression → extremism → appeasement → WWII
- **Intro** → The World Wars were crises of the European state system, imperial rivalry and competing visions of political order — not isolated explosions.
- **Conclusion** → They redistributed global power — from a Europe-centred balance to superpower rivalry, international institutions and decolonisation.
