## 1965 War {#war-1965}

- Background → Kashmir unresolved after 1947–48 · Rann of Kutch tension · Pakistan read India's post-1962 position as an opening.
- {{Operation Gibraltar}} → infiltration to raise insurgency in Kashmir.
- {{Operation Grand Slam}} → thrust at Akhnoor.

> [!FLOW] Infiltration → Indian response across the ceasefire line → India opens the international border toward Lahore / Sialkot → full conventional war → UN ceasefire, September 1965

Food-security side of 1965 → [Jai Jawan Jai Kisan](@planning-agrarian-change#green-revolution)

## Tashkent Agreement {#tashkent}

| Why Tashkent | Point |
|---|---|
| **Military** | No decisive result on the battlefield |
| **UN** | Pressure for ceasefire |
| **Economic** | Both economies strained |
| **Cold War** | USSR sought a diplomatic role in South Asia |

- Signed {{10 January 1966}}; Soviet mediation.
- Terms → withdrawal to pre-war positions · diplomatic relations restored · prisoners exchanged · non-interference · renunciation of force.

| Success | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Ended the war, normalised relations | Kashmir and the security dilemma unresolved |
| Showed Soviet mediation | Return to status quo; Shastri's death at Tashkent gave it lasting political symbolism |

- Line → Tashkent turned ==military stalemate into diplomatic de-escalation, not political reconciliation==.

## Bangladesh 1971 — Roots & Compulsions {#bangladesh-1971}

- Structural roots in Pakistan → geographic separation + Bengali demographic majority + linguistic-cultural discrimination + political-economic domination by the West wing.
- {{1970}} election → Awami League (Sheikh Mujibur Rahman) won a majority → transfer of power blocked.
- {{25 March 1971}} — Operation Searchlight → repression → about {{10 million}} refugees into India.

| India's compulsion | Logic |
|---|---|
| **Humanitarian** | Large-scale violence in East Pakistan |
| **Refugee burden** | Fiscal, social pressure on eastern India |
| **Security** | Two-wing Pakistan flanked India |
| **Domestic stability** | Camps, conflict threatened border states |
| **Political** | A democratic mandate denied |
| **Strategic** | A friendly Bangladesh transforms the eastern front |

## 1971 — Strategy & Outcome {#strategy-outcome}

1. Political and diplomatic support to the Bengali cause.
2. Help to the {{Mukti Bahini}}.
3. Indira Gandhi's international campaign.
4. {{Indo-Soviet Treaty, August 1971}} → insurance against a US–China–Pakistan alignment.
5. Direct war delayed until conditions were favourable.
6. Pakistan's air strikes of {{3 December}} (Operation Chengiz Khan) → full-scale war.

- Outcome → air, land, naval superiority in the east · Dhaka isolated · surrender on {{16 December 1971}} · about {{93,000}} prisoners · Bangladesh independent.
- International → US tilt to Pakistan, {{Seventh Fleet}} toward the Bay of Bengal · Soviet diplomatic cover, Security Council vetoes.
- Lesson → ==military preparation, diplomatic alignment and timing== were inseparable.

## 1965 vs 1971 {#comparison}

| | 1965 | 1971 |
|---|---|---|
| **War** | Limited, inconclusive | Decisive victory |
| **Settlement** | Tashkent, Soviet mediation | Simla, Indian strategic superiority |
| **Result** | Status quo | South Asian map redrawn |
| **Core issue** | Kashmir unresolved | Bangladesh created; Pakistan divided |
| **Diplomacy** | Managed a stalemate | Consolidated a battlefield outcome |

- **Simla Agreement, {{1972}}** → bilateral settlement, peaceful relations, Line of Control framework; aftermath of 1971, not a final resolution.

## Interpretation {#interpretation}

- Avoid → "pure humanitarian intervention" or "pure geopolitical expansion".
- Stronger → convergence of humanitarian catastrophe + refugee burden + democratic legitimacy + national-security interest + favourable timing.
- Bilateral and strategic analysis today → [GS2 International Relations](@mains/gs2-international-relations).
