## Problem & Mechanism {#problem-mechanism}

- Lapse of British paramountcy left about {{565}} princely states outside provincial administration → danger of fragmentation.

> [!FLOW] Persuasion → Instrument of Accession → standstill / administrative arrangements → merger / unionisation → constitutional integration

**Patel – V.P. Menon strategy**
- {{States Department}} → dedicated machinery.
- Transitional guarantees → privy purses, property, status.
- Practical arguments → geography, defence, communications, economic interdependence.
- Diplomacy preferred; ==coercive capacity kept in reserve== (Hyderabad).
- Accession first on {{defence, external affairs, communications}}.

## Administrative & Socio-Cultural Problems {#problems}

| Administrative | Socio-cultural / political |
|---|---|
| Fragmented territory, enclaves (Kathiawar, Rajasthan) | Ruler–population mismatch in some states |
| Different laws, bureaucracies, courts | Long autonomy → fear of losing regional identity |
| Unequal revenue capacity, development | Princes, landed elites losing privilege |
| Separate currencies, systems — Osmania currency, Hyderabad | Communal tension tied to accession (Junagadh, Hyderabad) |
| Remote, poorly connected states | Uneven political mobilisation → uneven democratic transition |
| Many documents to harmonise while handling Partition, refugees, Kashmir | |

## Key Cases {#cases}

| State | Problem | Outcome | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Junagadh** | Muslim ruler, Hindu-majority; acceded to Pakistan | Indian administration; {{1948 plebiscite}} for India | Geography + popular legitimacy |
| **Hyderabad** | Nizam sought independence; Razakar violence | {{Operation Polo, September 1948}} | Limits of persuasion |
| **Jammu & Kashmir** | Maharaja sought autonomy; tribal invasion from Pakistan | Instrument of Accession, {{26 October 1947}} | Integration became an international-security issue |
| **Travancore, Bhopal, Jodhpur** | Hesitation, other preferences | Negotiation | Prevented cascading fragmentation |

## Second-Stage Integration {#second-stage}

- Accession alone ≠ nation-building.
- Smaller states merged into viable units.
- Democratic institutions replaced personal monarchical sovereignty.
- Privy purses → a ==transitional bargain==; abolished by the {{26th Amendment, 1971}}.
- Leads into → [States Reorganisation](@states-reorganisation#pre-history).

## Interpretation {#interpretation}

- Neither simply "Patel's iron hand" nor purely voluntary union.
- Synthesis → ==constitutional instruments + elite bargains + popular legitimacy + calibrated force==.
