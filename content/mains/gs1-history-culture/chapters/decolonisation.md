## Why Decolonisation Accelerated {#drivers}

> [!FLOW] European exhaustion + nationalist mobilisation + mass politics + legitimacy crisis of empire + international pressure + Cold War → decolonisation

- European powers weakened economically, militarily after WWII.
- War mobilised colonial subjects; exposed the gap between freedom rhetoric and imperial rule.
- Organised nationalist parties grew.
- Educated elites turned Western political vocabulary into demands for self-rule.
- Workers, peasants, veterans widened the movement.
- International climate hostile to formal empire.
- Cold War both hastened withdrawal and complicated transitions.

## Malaya — A Difficult Transfer {#malaya}

| Problem (2017) | Content |
|---|---|
| **Plural society** | Colonial labour patterns created distinct Malay, Chinese, Indian communities; ethnicity overlapped with occupation, wealth, representation |
| **Security** | Communist insurgency — {{Malayan Emergency}}; Britain tied independence to security and viable institutions |
| **Ethnic bargain** | Malay political primacy vs large non-Malay communities; ethnic economic inequality |
| **Territorial / federal** | Formation of Malaysia → disputes with neighbours; Singapore separated in {{1965}} |
| **Legacies** | Ethnic tension, religious questions, indigenous rights, unequal colonial economy, dominant-party politics |

## Suez Crisis, 1956 {#suez}

> [!FLOW] Nasser's nationalism → West withdraws Aswan Dam finance → Suez Canal nationalised → Britain + France + Israel collude → invasion → US financial / diplomatic pressure + UN action → withdrawal

**Why a final blow to Britain's self-image (2014)**
- Could not sustain military action against US opposition.
- Run on sterling exposed economic weakness; needed IMF / US support.
- Prime Minister {{Anthony Eden}} resigned, January 1957.
- Marked the shift to a ==US–Soviet dominated order==.
- Anti-colonial nationalism defeated coercive restoration of imperial privilege.
- Line → Suez was less the beginning of British decline than its dramatic public revelation.

## Post-Colonial State Formation {#state-formation}

| Problem | Content |
|---|---|
| **Political** | Institutions built for control, not representation; weak parties; coups, authoritarian drift |
| **Territorial** | Inherited colonial borders; groups split or lumped together |
| **Social** | Citizenship to be built across ethnicity, religion, language |
| **Economic** | Commodity dependence; unequal land / resources; reliance on former metropole |
| **International** | Cold War rivalry, proxy alignments |
| **Administrative** | Few experienced indigenous personnel after rapid transfers |

- Central contradiction → ==independent state ≠ consolidated nation==.
- Contrast case → India's constitutional consolidation → [Consolidation Matrix](@early-republic#consolidation).

## Interpretations {#interpretations}

| Thesis | Claim |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | Nationalist elites organised and negotiated independence |
| **Structural** | Empires became financially, militarily, morally unsustainable after WWII |
| **Mass politics** | Elites succeeded only when tied to workers, peasants |

- Synthesis → imperial decline opened the door; organised nationalism forced and shaped the exit.
