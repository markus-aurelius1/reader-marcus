## Gandhian Method {#method}

- **Satyagraha** = moral principle + political technique + mass pedagogy.

| Element | Function |
|---|---|
| **Satya + Ahimsa** | Opponent converted, not destroyed; means inseparable from ends |
| **Tapasya (self-suffering)** | Moral asymmetry between satyagrahi and state |
| **Active resistance** | Non-cooperation withdraws consent; civil disobedience breaks unjust law |
| **Mass discipline** | Peasants, women, workers, students join without military organisation |
| **Local → national** | Champaran (indigo), Kheda (revenue), Ahmedabad (mill dispute) as political education |
| **Symbolic politics** | Khadi, charkha, salt → swaraj as everyday experience |
| **Struggle–truce–struggle** | Agitation → negotiation → constructive consolidation → renewed struggle |
| **Controlled escalation** | Boycott → non-cooperation → civil disobedience |
| **Organisation** | {{Nagpur, 1920}} — linguistic PCCs, local committees, low fee → mass machine |
| **Politics + social change** | Untouchability, communal harmony, village reconstruction, dignity of labour |

> [!FLOW] Local grievance → satyagraha + symbol → mass participation → repression / legitimacy crisis → negotiation or withdrawal → constructive work, cadre consolidation → next higher stage

## NCM → CDM → QIM {#ncm-cdm-qim}

| | Non-Cooperation 1920–22 | Civil Disobedience 1930–34 | Quit India 1942 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Context** | Punjab wrongs, Khilafat, reform disillusion | Simon Commission; Lahore Purna Swaraj | War, Japanese advance, Cripps failure |
| **Demand** | Swaraj (elastic) | Purna Swaraj | Immediate British withdrawal |
| **Technique** | Withdraw cooperation | Break colonial law | Mass revolt after leaders' arrest |
| **Forms** | Boycott of courts, schools, councils, cloth; titles returned | Salt law, tax and forest-law defiance, picketing | Strikes, underground, communications disrupted |
| **Symbol** | Khadi / charkha | Salt | "Do or Die" |
| **Participation** | Breakthrough; strong Muslim role via Khilafat | More women, peasants, regions; weaker Muslim role | Students, peasants, workers, socialists; decentralised |
| **Regional** | Awadh, Malabar, Andhra | {{Vedaranyam}}, Khudai Khidmatgars (NWFP), Gujarat | {{Ballia, Tamluk, Satara}} parallel governments |
| **End** | Withdrawn after {{Chauri Chaura}} | Gandhi–Irwin Pact; resumed; ended 1934 | Repressed; underground into 1944 |
| **Significance** | First nationwide Gandhian movement | Politicised colonial law; British negotiated | Empire could not recover legitimacy |

- Progression → NCM: deny cooperation → CDM: deny legitimacy of law → QIM: ==deny legitimacy of the British presence==.
- Why salt worked → a universal daily grievance tied swaraj to ordinary life.

Events and dates → [Khilafat & Non-Cooperation](@prelims/history-culture/gandhian-mass-movements#non-cooperation) · [Civil Disobedience](@prelims/history-culture/gandhian-mass-movements#civil-disobedience)

## Quit India, 1942 — Answer Frame {#quit-india-frame}

| Cause | Point |
|---|---|
| **Political** | August Offer, Cripps failed — no immediate transfer |
| **Strategic** | Japanese advance exposed British vulnerability |
| **Psychological** | Retreat from Asia punctured prestige |
| **Economic** | Wartime inflation, shortages |
| **Ideological** | Subordination while Britain "fought fascism" |
| **Gandhi's calculation** | Withdrawal would reduce Japan's incentive to attack |

- Demand → end of British rule, provisional government, mass civil disobedience; separate instructions to officials, soldiers, peasants, students, princely subjects.

**Nature**
- Leaderless after the {{9 August}} arrests → local initiative.
- Mixed repertoires → peaceful protest + underground + sabotage in places.
- Alternative sovereignty → parallel governments.
- Young socialists → Jayaprakash Narayan, Lohia; Aruna Asaf Ali; {{Usha Mehta}} (Congress Radio).
- Uneven geography → Bihar, eastern UP, Maharashtra, Midnapore, parts of Karnataka.

**Results**
- Immediate aim not achieved; Congress organisation crushed.
- Yet Indian cooperation could no longer be assumed; stable pre-war rule implausible.
- Prepared ground for INA → post-war upsurge → RIN.
- Nuance → both the culmination of Gandhian politics and its most decentralised phase.

Chronology → [Quit India](@prelims/history-culture/gandhian-mass-movements#quit-india)

## Constructive Programme {#constructive-programme}

- Line → political struggle was the visible superstructure; constructive work built the ==social infrastructure==.

| Programme | Political function |
|---|---|
| **Khadi / charkha** | Swadeshi, self-reliance, daily participation |
| **Village industries** | Alternative to colonial-industrial dependence |
| **Anti-untouchability** | Freedom tied to social emancipation |
| **Hindu–Muslim unity** | Keep the coalition broad |
| **National / basic education** | Less dependence on colonial institutions |
| **Sanitation, women's uplift, adult education** | Civic discipline; wider constituency |
| **Hindustani** | Mass communication |
| **Panchayat work** | Training in self-government |

> [!FLOW] Constructive work → cadre retention in the lull → social penetration → ideological discipline → readiness for the next movement

## Limits & Debates {#limits-debates}

- Village-centric economics contested by Nehru, Bose, socialists.
- Anti-caste agenda contested by Ambedkar.
- Communal unity weakened in the 1930s–40s.
- Congress mobilised classes together but could not always reconcile their interests.
- Control over violence weakened as mobilisation widened — visible by 1942.

| Reading (2015) | View |
|---|---|
| **Nationalist** | Gandhi turned elite agitation into mass anti-imperial struggle |
| **Structural / Cambridge** | Imperial decline came from material forces, not Gandhi alone |
| **Synthesis** | Gandhi explains the ==scale, legitimacy and repertoire==; war and imperial decline explain why Britain lost capacity and will |

- Relevance (2018) → means–ends consistency · non-violent dissent · trusteeship · decentralisation · communal harmony → [GS4 Ethics](@mains/gs4-ethics).
