## Why the Company Won {#company-won}

- **Professional army** → drill, command, logistics.
- **Sepoy army** → Indian manpower, European organisation.
- **Stable finance** → Bengal after Plassey ({{1757}}), Buxar ({{1764}}), Diwani ({{1765}}).
- **Unified corporate command** vs shifting coalitions, personalised armies.
- **Diplomacy before battle** → succession disputes, Residents, treaties, puppet regimes.
- **Sequential defeat of rivals** → Mysore, Marathas, Sikhs separately.
- **Sea power + commercial network** → strategic depth.
- **Subsidiary Alliance** (Wellesley) → dependent allies without annexation.
- **Doctrine of Lapse** (Dalhousie) → completed consolidation.

> [!FLOW] Military-fiscal loop: territory → revenue → larger army → more territory

> [!FLOW] Commercial foothold → political intervention → Bengal revenues → standing army → alliances / wars → territorial revenue → administrative state

- Map → Bengal → Mysore → Maratha region → Punjab (annexed {{1849}}).

## Company to Colonial State {#state-building}

> [!FLOW] 1773 Regulating Act → 1784 Pitt's Act → 1813 → 1833 → 1853 Charter Acts → 1858 Crown → 1861 / 1892 Councils Acts → 1909 → 1919 → 1935

| Theme | Content |
|---|---|
| **Parliamentary supervision** | 1773, 1784 |
| **Trader → governing corporation** | Monopoly ended in stages; {{1833}} ended the commercial role |
| **Centralisation** | Governor-General of India; central legislation |
| **Company → Crown** | GoI Act 1858 |
| **Bureaucratic + legal state** | ICS, district collector, codified law, courts |
| **Controlled representation** | Councils widened; sovereignty stayed imperial |
| **Communal representation** | Separate electorates, {{1909}} |
| **Provincial experiment** | Dyarchy (1919) → provincial autonomy (1935) |

**Legacy of the 1935 Act (2015)** → provincial autonomy · federal scheme, division of subjects · Public Service Commissions · Federal Court · office of Governor · detailed administrative provisions · emergency architecture.
- Line → independent India ==democratised and nationalised an inherited administrative skeleton==.

Act detail → [Government of India Act, 1935](@prelims/history-culture/constitutional-development#act-1935)

## Colonial Education {#education}

- State aims → cheap subordinate staff · English as elite medium · downward filtration · cultural legitimacy.

> [!FLOW] Charter Act 1813 → Orientalist–Anglicist debate → Macaulay's Minute 1835 → Wood's Despatch 1854 → universities 1857 → Hunter Commission 1882 → Universities Act 1904

- **Macaulay's Minute (2026)** → year {{1835}}; Anglicist victory; English education for a small class.
- Contradiction → neglected mass, vernacular, female and technical education, yet produced an intelligentsia exposed to rationalism, liberty and constitutionalism.
- Line → ==an instrument that escaped its creators== — institutions meant to stabilise rule became arenas of anti-colonial criticism.

Milestones → [Education](@prelims/history-culture/british-administration-economy#education)

## Interpretations {#interpretations}

- **John Seeley** → conquest largely unintended, "absent-minded".
- Balanced → early acquisitions protected commerce; later expansion deliberate and imperial.
- Synthesis → ==state-building without self-government== — real integration and institutions, built around control, revenue and imperial interest.
