## Why Resistance Recurred {#causes}

> [!FLOW] Colonial penetration → disruption of the existing political economy → dispossession → resistance

| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Agrarian extraction** | High revenue, rigid collection, eviction, new intermediaries |
| **Dispossessed elites** | Zamindars, poligars, chiefs, rulers lost land and authority |
| **Commercial penetration** | Moneylenders, traders → debt, land alienation |
| **Artisanal decline** | Imports + lost court patronage |
| **Tribal intrusion** | Settled agriculture, outsiders ({{dikus}}), revenue rules, forest control broke communal land relations |
| **Administrative-judicial** | Alien courts and property concepts displaced custom |
| **Cultural anxiety** | Missionaries; interference, real or perceived |
| **Military** | Pay, service conditions, overseas service, caste / religious sensitivities |

## Resistance Bank & Character {#resistance-bank}

| Rising | Where, when | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanyasi–Fakir** | Bengal–Bihar, c. 1763–1800 | Famine + revenue distress; Hindu–Muslim participation |
| **Chuar / Jungle Mahal** | Bengal | Revenue disruption, tribal dispossession |
| **Polygar** | South India | Dispossessed military-landed chiefs |
| **Vellore Mutiny** | {{1806}} | Early military protest over social-religious interference |
| **Paika** | Odisha, {{1817}} — Bakshi Jagabandhu | Loss of military-land privileges |
| **Khasi** | North-East, 1829–33 | Road-building, outsiders |
| **Kol** | Chotanagpur, {{1831–32}} | Land to outsiders, moneylenders, new courts |
| **Faraizi** | Bengal, 1830s–50s | Religious mobilisation + tenant resistance |
| **Santhal Hul** | {{1855–56}} — Sidhu, Kanhu | Moneylenders, zamindars, land alienation |

**Tribal answer (2023)** → impact: land alienation, dikus, debt, forest restrictions, alien courts → response: armed risings (Kol, Santhal, Khasi, Bhil) defending customary rights.

**Common character**
- Localised, tied to immediate grievance.
- Traditional leadership — chiefs, zamindars, religious figures.
- ==Restorationist== → recover customary rights, not build a new order.
- Intense popular participation; militant methods (treasuries, revenue offices, moneylenders).
- Weak horizontal coordination; modern nationalism only embryonic.

> [!TRAP] Similar revolts do !!not!! prove a common national impulse — common colonial pressure produced similar forms.

Uprising facts → [Tribal & Civil Uprisings](@prelims/history-culture/tribal-peasant-uprisings#tribal-uprisings)

## 1857 as Culmination {#culmination}

> [!FLOW] Repeated local grievance → accumulated anti-colonial experience → convergence of soldiers + peasants + dispossessed elites in 1857

1. Same agrarian roots — revenue, eviction, debt.
2. Same displaced elites — chiefs, taluqdars, rulers.
3. Same artisanal distress.
4. Tribal precedent — Santhal Hul just before.
5. Military precedent — Vellore, repeated sepoy mutinies.
6. Shared target — Company institutions.
7. Same modes — treasuries, records, police stations.
8. Memory of resistance survived repression.
9. Scale change — military + civil + agrarian + princely discontent fused.
10. Discontinuity — earlier revolts lacked geographic convergence and a supra-regional symbol.

- C.A. Bayly → 1857 as many resistances converging, not one movement.

## 1857 — Causes, Nature, Failure {#revolt-1857}

| Cause | Points |
|---|---|
| **Political** | Doctrine of Lapse (Jhansi); Awadh annexed {{1856}}; Nana Saheb's pension; Mughal dignity eroded |
| **Economic** | Heavy assessment, eviction; taluqdars displaced; debt; handicraft decline |
| **Social-religious** | Missionary fear; reform laws read as interference → "religion in danger" |
| **Administrative-racial** | Exclusion from high office; racial arrogance |
| **Military** | Pay, promotion, overseas service; few European officers; {{Enfield cartridge}} as trigger |

- Course → Barrackpore → Meerut ({{10 May 1857}}) → Delhi → Bahadur Shah Zafar as symbol.

| Centre | Leaders |
|---|---|
| **Delhi** | Bahadur Shah Zafar, {{Bakht Khan}} |
| **Kanpur** | Nana Saheb, Tantia Tope |
| **Lucknow** | {{Begum Hazrat Mahal}} |
| **Jhansi** | Rani Lakshmibai |
| **Bihar** | {{Kunwar Singh}} |
| **Bareilly** | Khan Bahadur Khan |
| **Faizabad** | Maulvi Ahmadullah Shah |

| More than a mutiny | Less than a national war |
|---|---|
| Mass civilian participation (Awadh) | South, east, west largely outside |
| Cross-class — taluqdars to artisans | Major princes loyal |
| British rule itself the target | No national organisation or command |
| Bahadur Shah as supra-regional symbol | Aims varied — estates, rulers, custom |
| Hindu–Muslim cooperation | No programme for a post-British state |
| Revenue, judicial, police offices attacked | Educated middle class detached |

| Historian | View |
|---|---|
| **Lawrence, Seeley** | Sepoy mutiny |
| **V.D. Savarkar** | War of Independence |
| **R.C. Majumdar** | Neither first, nor national, nor a war of independence |
| **S.N. Sen** | Not organised national revolt; wider than a mutiny |

- Synthesis → ==a mutiny in origin, a popular rebellion in several regions, anti-colonial in consequence== — not yet a modern pan-Indian movement.

**Why it failed** → limited territory · uneven social support · no unified command · no future programme · inferior arms · British telegraph and transport · imperial reinforcements · Sikh / Gurkha troops · princely help · symbolic, weak centre.

Leaders and centres → [Revolt of 1857](@prelims/history-culture/tribal-peasant-uprisings#revolt-1857)

## 1857 as Policy Watershed {#watershed}

| Dimension | Shift after 1857 |
|---|---|
| **Government** | GoI Act {{1858}} — Company → Crown |
| **Executive** | Secretary of State + Council; Viceroy |
| **Princes** | Annexation moderated; states became conservative allies |
| **Religion / reform** | Queen's Proclamation — non-interference |
| **Landlords** | Taluqdars cultivated as counterweights |
| **Army** | More Europeans; artillery in European hands |
| **Recruitment** | {{"Martial races"}}; division and counterpoise |
| **Strategy** | Divide-and-rule more systematic |
| **Race** | Deeper distrust and social distance |

- Line (2016) → 1857 did not liberalise the Raj; it made it ==socially cautious, politically conservative, racially hardened and calculated in using Indian divisions==.
