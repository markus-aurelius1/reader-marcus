## Why Nationalism Emerged {#origins}

| Factor | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Administrative unification** | One framework, laws, bureaucracy, communications → imperial tools became nationalism's infrastructure |
| **Economic unification + exploitation** | Shared fate; drain, deindustrialisation, revenue seen as ==systemic== |
| **Modern education** | Liberty, equality, democracy, representative government |
| **English** | Linked elites across regions |
| **Press, literature** | National public sphere |
| **Reform, rediscovered past** | Self-confidence; answer to "inferiority" |
| **New middle class** | Lawyers, teachers, journalists as organisers |
| **Racial discrimination** | {{Ilbert Bill}} controversy; civil-service bias |
| **Repression** | Lytton's Arms Act, Vernacular Press Act ({{1878}}); Curzon |

**Organisational apprenticeship**

| Association | Importance |
|---|---|
| **East India Association** ({{1866}}, Naoroji) | Indian question before British opinion |
| **Poona Sarvajanik Sabha** | Bridge between government and public |
| **Indian Association** ({{1876}}, Surendranath Banerjea, Ananda Mohan Bose) | Wider mobilisation; civil-service agitation |
| **Madras Mahajan Sabha** (1884) · **Bombay Presidency Association** (1885) | Regional feeders |

- Pre-Congress campaigns → Indianisation of services, Arms Act, Vernacular Press Act, plantation labour, Ilbert Bill, ICS age limit.
- **INC, 1885** → scattered associations became a permanent all-India arena: political education, common demands, unity across region and religion, idea of nationhood.
- Safety-valve debate → don't overinvest: nationalists used Hume as a {{"lightning conductor"}} for forces already developing.

## Moderates — Achievements {#moderate-achievements}

- Period {{1885–1905}}. Leaders → Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gokhale, W.C. Bonnerjee, Wacha, Surendranath Banerjea.
- Core → liberalism · constitutionalism · gradualism · political education · economic nationalism.
- Methods → resolutions, petitions, press, councils, British opinion ("prayer–petition–protest" understates them).

| # | Achievement (2021) |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Indian political nation** — elites spoke for India, not a province |
| 2 | **Political education** — representation, budgets, rights |
| 3 | **Economic critique** — poverty structurally tied to colonial rule (Naoroji, R.C. Dutt) |
| 4 | **Drain Theory** — Home Charges, salaries, debt interest, remittances, military cost |
| 5 | **Economic nationalism** — lower land revenue, salt burden, military spending; tariff protection, industry |
| 6 | **Constitutional pressure** — Indian Councils Act {{1892}} |
| 7 | **Councils as platforms** — budget criticism |
| 8 | **"No taxation without representation"** |
| 9 | **Administrative reform** — Indianisation, simultaneous ICS exams, judiciary–executive separation |
| 10 | **Civil liberties** — speech, press, association |
| 11 | **Critique of imperial wars** on Indian revenue |
| 12 | **Institutional continuity** — organisation, press, vocabulary inherited by Extremists and Gandhi |
| 13 | **Ideological delegitimisation** — Raj no longer a reformable benefactor |
| 14 | **Secular national platform** |
| 15 | **Prepared mass politics** — colonialism had first to be intellectually explained |

- Synthesis → they did not create a mass struggle; they created its ==ideology, organisation, leadership and vocabulary==.
- Bipan Chandra → 1858–1905 as the {{"seed time"}} of Indian nationalism.

## Moderates — Why They Lost Conviction {#moderate-limits}

| Internal limits (2017) | Changed environment |
|---|---|
| Narrow base — educated urban middle class | Famines, plague sharpened discontent |
| Little peasant / worker mobilisation | Racial arrogance exposed British liberalism |
| Vocabulary remote from ordinary people | Repression answered constitutional criticism |
| Excess faith in British justice | Curzon — Calcutta Corporation, Universities Act, Bengal partition |
| Slow, repetitive methods; modest demands | Japan defeats Russia ({{1905}}) |
| No mass organisation for pressure | New leaders — Tilak, Lajpat Rai, Bipin Pal, Aurobindo |

> [!TRAP] Do !!not!! write "Moderates failed". Write: immediate concessions modest; long-term nation-building foundational; the limit was inability to turn consciousness into organised mass pressure.

| Reading | View |
|---|---|
| **Immediate-performance critique** | Cautious, elite, institutionally weak |
| **Long-term nationalist reading** | Nation-builders, not failed revolutionaries |

## Swadeshi Transition {#swadeshi}

> [!FLOW] Moderate frustration + Curzonian authoritarianism + Bengal Partition + militant leadership → Swadeshi breakthrough

> [!FLOW] 1905 Partition → Swadeshi / Boycott → 1906 Calcutta (Swaraj, Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education) → 1907 Surat Split → decline by 1908 → Partition annulled 1911

- **Four pillars** → Swadeshi · Boycott · National Education · Passive Resistance / {{atma-shakti}}.
- Methods → bonfires of foreign cloth, picketing, samitis, national schools, indigenous enterprise, songs, festivals, press.
- Symbols → Vande Mataram · Tagore's *Amar Sonar Bangla* · Rakshabandhan · Ganapati / Shivaji festivals.
- Spread → Tilak (Maharashtra) · Lajpat Rai, Ajit Singh (Punjab) · V.O. Chidambaram Pillai (Madras).
- Economic nationalism moved from ==explaining exploitation to practising resistance==.

**Why it is the crucial transition**
1. Petition → direct popular action.
2. Elite politics → wider mobilisation (students, women, workers).
3. Administrative reform → Swaraj.
4. Economic critique → economic action.
5. Leaders → volunteer networks.
6. Culture as political communication.
7. Constructive nation-building — enterprise, national education.
8. Sacrifice gained prestige; revolutionary nationalism grew alongside.
9. Later Gandhian techniques — boycott, non-cooperation, constructive work — rehearsed.
10. Colonial ideological hegemony weakened.

**Limits and decline** → repression · no durable all-India machinery · leaders arrested / deported · Surat Split · peasants little mobilised · passive resistance under-organised · revivalist symbols could alienate some.
- Verdict → a "leap forward"; ==first rehearsal of mass nationalism== — nearly every later stream present in embryo.

## Stage Comparison {#comparison}

| | Pre-1857 revolts | 1857 | Moderates | Swadeshi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scale** | Local | Regional convergence | All-India organisation | Wider agitation |
| **Leadership** | Traditional | Sepoys, princes, local elites | Educated middle class | Militant nationalists, activists |
| **Goal** | Restoration | Roll back Company order | Reform → self-government | Swaraj |
| **Method** | Armed rising | Armed rebellion | Constitutional agitation | Boycott, passive resistance |
| **Mass role** | Local | High in some regions | Limited | Much wider |
| **Role in history** | Resistance tradition | Policy watershed | Ideological foundation | Rehearsal for mass politics |
