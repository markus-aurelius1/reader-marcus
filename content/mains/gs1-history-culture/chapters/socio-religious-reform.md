## Why Reform Emerged {#causes}

- Colonial encounter ==+== internal social critique — not one alone.
- Western education → rationalism, liberalism, humanism.
- Missionary criticism → defence and reinterpretation of indigenous religion.
- Print → books, pamphlets, newspapers, polemic.
- Urbanisation + new educated middle class.
- Rediscovery of Vedas, Upanishads, India's past.
- Caste exclusion, untouchability → reform from below.
- "Women's question" → central to debates on civilisation and progress.
- Associations → a public sphere beyond court and religious authority.

## Reform Spectrum {#spectrum}

| Current | Examples | Impulse |
|---|---|---|
| **Rational-reformist** | Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj | Religion through reason; social reform |
| **Radical-intellectual** | Young Bengal | Free thought, liberty |
| **Revivalist-reformist** | Arya Samaj | Vedic authority + social reform |
| **Spiritual service** | Ramakrishna Mission | Universalism + service |
| **Muslim modernist** | Aligarh | Modern education |
| **Anti-caste / subaltern** | Phule's Satyashodhak, Narayana Guru | Challenge hierarchy itself |

**Brahmo Samaj — Rammohan Roy**
- Atmiya Sabha {{1814}} → Brahmo Sabha / Samaj {{1828}}.
- Monotheism · reason over blind scriptural authority · against idolatry, ritualism, caste.
- Campaign against sati · women's property · modern education · freedom of the press.
- Phrase → "religious reform as social and intellectual modernisation".

**Young Bengal — Derozio, Hindu College**
- Free thought, Enlightenment / French revolutionary ideas, liberty, equality, women's education.
- Limit → intellectually radical, ==socially shallow== — small circle, no organisation, no link with peasants.

| 2021 contrast | Young Bengal | Brahmo Samaj |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | Radical questioning | Sustained institution-building |
| **Reach** | Narrow, short-lived | Wider, durable |

## Phule & Subaltern Reform {#phule}

- Jotirao + Savitribai Phule → schools for girls and lower castes.
- Attack on Brahmanical domination; {{Satyashodhak Samaj, 1873}}.
- Simple non-Brahmin ceremonies; {{Gulamgiri}} on caste exploitation.
- Peasants, widows, the poor; dignity and self-organisation of the oppressed.

> [!FLOW] 2025 frame — classes touched: Shudra–Atishudra → women → peasants → widows → poor → lower-caste students

- Shift → from "reforming high-caste society" to ==questioning hierarchy itself==.

## Awakening → National Consciousness {#awakening-nation}

> [!FLOW] Rational enquiry → critique of inherited authority → print / public debate → educated opinion → cultural self-respect → ideas of rights and equality → associations → national identity and organised politics

- English + vernacular press → inter-regional debate.
- Reinterpreted past → answer to claims of Indian inferiority.
- Reform normalised the vocabulary of rights, equality, representation, public criticism.
- Education produced the group that staffed associations and the early Congress.
- Social and economic debate traced problems to a shared colonial system.
- Line (2019) → reform did not automatically produce nationalism; it supplied ==vocabulary, networks and a critical public sphere==.

Next step → [Rise of Nationalism](@rise-of-nationalism#origins)

## Limits & Interpretation {#limits}

- Urban middle-class bias; uneven regional reach.
- Many attacked untouchability without rejecting varna.
- Women's reform often paternalistic — "better wives and mothers".
- Glorified antiquity could exclude lower castes, feed revivalist exclusivism.
- Muslim, Dalit, non-Brahmin trajectories → ==no single Indian Renaissance==.
- Literacy restricted the public sphere.
- Created national consciousness, not mass nationalism.

| Reading | View |
|---|---|
| **"Renaissance"** | Awakening, rationalism, self-criticism, cultural recovery |
| **Critique** | Colonial setting, regionally uneven, socially selective, tied to caste / religious identity |
| **Use** | "Indian social-intellectual awakening" — not a copy of Europe's Renaissance |
