## New Imperialism — Why {#new-imperialism}

- Meaning → intensified territorial expansion from the later 19th century to WWI; Britain and France joined by Germany, Italy, Japan, Russia, USA.

| Driver | Points |
|---|---|
| **Economic** | Second Industrial Revolution → raw materials; protected markets; surplus capital seeking investment; finance capital; cheap labour; post-{{1873}} downturn |
| **National-strategic** | Empire as prestige and power; Germany's {{Weltpolitik}}; great-power competition; bases, sea routes |
| **Ideological** | Racial / civilisational justification; "civilising mission", White Man's Burden |
| **Political** | Imperial success reinforced domestic nationalism |

- Mnemonic → **M² + P² + I** = Markets / Materials + Power / Prestige + Ideology.
- Verdict → ==simultaneously economic and nationalistic==: industrial capitalism gave the incentive; national rivalry turned expansion into territorial conquest.

## Scramble for Africa & Artificial Borders {#africa-borders}

> [!FLOW] Industrial demand + prestige + strategic rivalry → scramble → Berlin Conference → partition → externally drawn boundaries

- {{Berlin Conference, 1884–85}} → rules for partition; inter-imperial accommodation, not African political geography.
- Borders ignored ethnic, linguistic, political communities → some groups split across states, others bundled into one unit.
- Colonial states built for control and extraction, not representative nation-building.

**Long-term consequences (2013)**
- Weak fit between state boundaries and social communities.
- Cross-border ethnic populations.
- Competition for state power and resources.
- Secessionist and territorial conflicts.
- Hard to build a common national identity.
- Colonial administrative and economic spatial patterns persist.

> [!NOTE] Qualifier: imposed borders mattered greatly, but outcomes also depended on local agency, institutions and regional variation.

After independence → [Post-Colonial State Formation](@decolonisation#state-formation)

## Indentured Labour {#indenture}

| Why created (2018) | Nature |
|---|---|
| Abolition of slavery → plantation labour shortage | Formally contractual |
| Need for cheap, disciplined farm labour | Highly unequal power relations |
| Indian poverty, famine, job scarcity | Recruitment, debt, contracts limited real freedom |
| Rural Indians had agricultural experience | Tied India to plantation colonies across the empire |
| Colonial administration organised recruitment, transport | |

- Destinations → Mauritius, Caribbean (Guyana, Trinidad), Fiji, Natal / South Africa.

**How identity survived**
- Language, dialects passed down.
- Religious institutions, festivals rebuilt community.
- Food, marriage, family practices.
- Music, oral tradition → hybrid diasporic forms.
- Not unchanged reproduction → ==creolisation, hybridisation==.
- Became lasting political-cultural communities in host societies.
- Line → identity survived through adaptation, not isolation.

Diaspora in society → [GS1 Society](@mains/gs1-society)

- Imperial mobility circulated → students · workers · political exiles · print · revolutionary ideas · examples of resistance.

| Global development (2014) | Effect on India |
