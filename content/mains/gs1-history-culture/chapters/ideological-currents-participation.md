## Left & Socialist Currents {#left}

- Sources → Russian Revolution · colonial exploitation, worker / peasant distress · radical young Congressmen · organised labour and peasant movements.

| Strand | Marker |
|---|---|
| **Nehru** | Socialism + anti-imperialism + internationalism |
| **Bose** | State planning, industrialisation, militant anti-imperialism |
| **Congress Socialist Party** | {{1934}} — Narendra Dev, Jayaprakash Narayan, Lohia |
| **Communists** | Class mobilisation of workers, peasants |
| **AITUC** | {{1920}} |
| **All India Kisan Sabha** | {{1936}} — Sahajanand Saraswati |

**How the Left widened nationalism**
- Put ==class== into anti-colonialism.
- Tied independence to land, wages, redistribution.
- Pushed the future-state debate toward planning, public ownership, social justice.
- Added anti-fascist internationalism.
- Supplied underground organisers in Quit India.

| Tension | Gandhi | Left |
|---|---|---|
| **Society** | Cross-class unity, trusteeship | Class conflict |
| **Economy** | Decentralisation | Planning, industrial transformation |

## Social Bases — "Many Voices" {#social-bases}

| Group | Contribution | Examples | Qualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Women** | Supporters → picketers → satyagrahis → underground leaders | Sarojini Naidu (Dharasana), Kamaladevi, Usha Mehta, Aruna Asaf Ali, Kalpana Dutt, Bina Das; Rani of Jhansi Regiment | Did not dissolve patriarchy |
| **Peasants** | Made nationalism rural and massive | Champaran, Kheda, Awadh Kisan Sabha, Eka, Bardoli, AIKS | Agrarian and national demands could diverge |
| **Workers** | Strike pressure, class politics | Ahmedabad, AITUC, Bombay 1946 | Congress cautious on class conflict |
| **Students** | Boycott, underground, revolutionary recruits | NCM, Swadeshi, QIM | Exposed to repression |
| **Tribals** | Forest / customary rights fused with anti-colonialism | Rampa, forest satyagrahas | Autonomous local agendas |
| **Muslims** | Khilafat gave NCM inter-community reach | Ali brothers, Azad, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan | Declined with communal polarisation |
| **Business** | Finance, swadeshi, later planning | FICCI, Bombay Plan | Feared radical labour |
| **Princely-state peoples** | Nationalism beyond British provinces | AISPC | Congress intervention cautious |

- 2019 line → the Gandhian era's strength was not homogenisation under Gandhi but incorporating actors whose aims often exceeded the Congress programme (Nehru, Patel, Azad, Bose, Bhagat Singh, Sarojini Naidu).

**Foreign participants (2013) — by function**
- **Annie Besant** → Home Rule League; *New India*, *Commonweal*; Congress president.
- **C.F. Andrews** → moral ally; took Indian grievances to British and world opinion.
- **Sister Nivedita** → education, cultural-national revival.
- **Mirabehn** → constructive work, international moral support.
- **Satyanand Stokes** → joined Non-Cooperation.
- **Theosophists** → defended Indian philosophy against colonial "inferiority".
- Value → undercut the claim that nationalism was racial hostility; gave ==international legitimacy==, networks, journalism.

## Bose–Gandhi · Gandhi–Tagore {#leader-debates}

| | Gandhi | Bose (2016) |
|---|---|---|
| **Means** | Ahimsa integral to ends | Armed struggle permissible |
| **Strategy** | Mass civil resistance + negotiation | Militant mobilisation + foreign military alliance |
| **WWII** | No Axis partnership | Sought Axis help |
| **State** | Decentralised Gram Swaraj | Strong central state |
| **Economy** | Village industry, trusteeship | Planning, industrialisation |
| **Tempo** | Long moral transformation | Seize the moment |
| **Legacy** | Delegitimised empire by non-cooperation | INA shook the army's reliability |

- Synthesis → same goal, dignity and mobilisation; differed on ==means, tempo, state structure, economic modernity==.

| | Gandhi | Tagore (2023) |
|---|---|---|
| **Nationalism** | Necessary instrument of self-rule | Wary of aggressive, narrow nationalism |
| **Universalism** | Self-rule leads to fraternity | Humanity prior to nation |
| **Swadeshi** | Self-reliance, moral resistance | Supportive, then critical of coercive boycott |
| **Education** | {{Nai Talim}} — craft, vernacular, moral training | {{Santiniketan / Visva-Bharati}} — freedom, creativity, nature, intercultural learning |
| **Machine** | Suspicious of industrial civilisation | Receptive to science, exchange |
| **Shared** | Human dignity, rural regeneration, critique of colonial education | Same |

- Frame → ==debate, not antagonism==; deep mutual respect.

## Interpretations {#interpretations}

- Three lenses → Congress-led national movement ↔ autonomous class / subaltern movements ↔ coalition of overlapping nationalisms.
- Position → none is exhaustive; durability came from aggregating regional, class, caste, gender and ideological aspirations around anti-colonial sovereignty.

## Rapid Recall {#rapid-recall}

- **Keywords** → horizontal expansion · plural nationalism · social coalition-building · Karachi Resolution · class in anti-colonialism · international legitimacy
- **Examples** → Bhagat Singh–HSRA · CSP 1934 · AIKS 1936 · Usha Mehta's radio · Annie Besant's Home Rule
- **Visual** → Swaraj widening in layers: political → social justice → redistribution → worker / peasant rights → planning → gender → secular republic
- **Intro** → The Gandhian age was not one of ideological uniformity; its strength lay in the coexistence of Gandhian, socialist, revolutionary, peasant, labour and feminist impulses.
- **Conclusion** → Freedom came through no single ideology — the movement won because it was at once mass and plural.
